# Bitcoin Falls Below $70,000 for First Time Since August

Bitcoin fell 3.84% to $68,867.33 on Friday morning, dropping below $70,000 for the first time since August, according to TheStreet's market coverage. The Motley Fool's quote bar later showed the coin at about $68,316, down roughly 4.5% on the day. Over $1.3 billion in crypto futures positions were liquidated in the previous 24 hours, TheStreet said, which is the market's way of announcing that someone else's leverage has been repossessed.

The decline came on a day when the stock market was not exactly cheerful either, but bitcoin managed to fall by more than the S&P 500 did. The index lost 0.40%, according to the Fool. Bitcoin was therefore roughly ten times as volatile as the thing it was invented to be an alternative to. This is one of those facts that the crypto industry tends to describe as "early".

## Why now

TheStreet attributed part of the move to "a surging U.S. dollar", and the rest of the macro tape was not hospitable to anything that pays no yield. The Fool's midday report had the 10-year Treasury yield at about 5.10%, and Eurasia Business News said it was heading toward 5.15%. At those levels a Treasury bond offers a government-backed return, while a coin offers a conviction.

The inflation data did not soften the case for caution. According to Xinhua's report on the Labor Department release, producer prices for October rose 0.3% on the month, a little below the 0.4% economists had expected, but were up 6.5% from a year earlier, the biggest annual gain since November 2022. Consumer prices for October had already come in 0.1 percentage point above forecasts on both the headline and core measures, according to Eurasia Business News.

The Federal Reserve is leaning the same way. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on CNBC's Squawk Box that the central bank "has definitely got work to do", as quoted by TheStreet, and added that inflation is rising. Markets are pricing a quarter-point rate increase at the December 8-9 meeting, with a half-point move above a 10% probability, per CME FedWatch figures cited by the same outlet.

## The company bitcoin keeps

The weekly movers tell the second half of the story. Eurasia Business News listed Strategy, the company best known for owning a great deal of bitcoin, among the biggest decliners of the week, down about 13.43%. A stock whose business model is holding one asset tends to behave like that asset with a prospectus attached.

Gold offered the comparison nobody in the crypto community enjoys. According to Eurasia Business News, gold settled at $3,883.40 an ounce on Friday, up $7 or 0.18%, trading in a range of $3,867.40 to $3,924. The Fool's midday report had it slightly lower at that point, so the picture is of a metal that went almost nowhere. In a week when digital gold lost a round number, physical gold lost nothing in particular, which is a form of performance.

None of this settles the argument. Bitcoin has dropped by more than this before and recovered. Because it had stayed above $70,000 since August, the break of a round number is more psychological than structural, and the next few sessions will show which. Round numbers matter to people who set stop-losses at them, which is why more than a billion dollars of futures disappeared in a day.

## The reading

The cleanest way to read Friday is as a pricing exercise rather than a verdict, and the most boring explanation is usually the one that turns out to be right. When the risk-free rate climbs toward 5%, the premium demanded for everything that is not risk-free climbs with it. Equities pay that premium in the form of earnings. Bitcoin pays it in the form of a lower price, delivered promptly and without commentary.

The coin's defenders will say that its long-term case is unchanged. They may be right. The short-term case is that a currency with no central bank is being repriced by a week of central bank commentary, which is a lesson in who actually sets the weather. Next week brings October retail sales on Tuesday and the minutes of the Fed's October meeting on Wednesday, according to Xinhua. Bitcoin holders will read them with the same attention as everyone else, and with considerably more to lose.
