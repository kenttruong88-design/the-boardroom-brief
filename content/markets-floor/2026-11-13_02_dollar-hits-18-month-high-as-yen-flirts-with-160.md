# Dollar Hits 18-Month High as Yen Flirts With 160

The dollar rose for a third straight session on Thursday to its highest level in 18 months, according to the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index cited in Saxo Bank's Asia market note, which said the gauge gained 0.2%. The dollar-yen pair briefly traded above 160 before closing near 159.84, which is close enough to a round number to make a finance ministry reach for its phone.

## A bond market doing the heavy lifting

The currency move had a bond market behind it. The yield on the 10-year Treasury note finished at 5.10%, up 0.10 percentage point on the day, with an intraday high of 5.12%, according to Yahoo Finance. Saxo described it as the first close above 5% since October 22. The 30-year yield rose 9 basis points to 5.449%, and the 5-year and 7-year yields each rose 15 basis points, to 4.97% and 5.04% respectively, per Saxo. The 2-year sat near 4.77%, and because the 30-year rose less than the 5-year, the curve flattened, a pattern bond desks call a bear flattening and everyone else calls a bad afternoon.

The 30-year auction did not help. The Treasury sold $25 billion of 30-year bonds at a high yield of 5.441%, with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.36, and the sale tailed slightly, Saxo reported. A tail means the bonds cleared at a higher yield than dealers had expected when the auction was announced, which is an expensive way to learn what the market thinks.

## The Fed hike that keeps getting bigger

What lifts the dollar in this setting is the expected path of the Federal Reserve. Saxo said markets now price about 88 basis points of tightening by mid-2027, up from 78 basis points before Tuesday's hot consumer price report. The bank quoted Nomura as saying the market is "clearly contemplating a 50bp hike at the December meeting." Yahoo Finance quoted Chicago Fed president Austan Goolsbee as saying "We're at a precarious moment," and San Francisco Fed president Mary Daly as saying "My bias is that further tightening will be needed."

When two Fed presidents sound that way in a single week, the currency market tends to take them at their word. Higher expected rates make dollar assets more attractive to hold, and the dollar rises to say so.

## The yen's difficult position

The weakest part of the picture is Japan. Saxo noted that the pair's brief move above 160 revived talk of intervention. The yen is on the losing side of a simple arithmetic: US yields rise, and capital tends to go where the yield is. The finance ministry can sell dollars, but it cannot easily change the yield gap that makes buying dollars attractive.

Elsewhere the Australian dollar fell 0.57% to 0.6808 against the US currency, per Saxo. Gold, which pays no interest and therefore feels every rise in yields, fell as much as 3.4% on the day to below $3,860, a one-year low according to the same note, and was around $3,880 on Friday morning. Yahoo's closing tile for gold looked almost flat, at $3,886.70, which shows how far it climbed back from the low.

## The equity side

Stocks did not enjoy the afternoon. The S&P 500 closed down 0.35% at 7,592.40, with Nvidia's leadership upheaval adding to the pressure from yields. A 10-year yield above 5% is a discount rate that makes the earnings of distant years look smaller, and growth shares are the usual casualty. The Russell 2000, a gauge of smaller and more rate-sensitive companies, fell 1.66%.

## The closing observation

Currency analysts like to say the dollar is the least ugly in a contest of ugly currencies. Thursday supported the theory. The dollar rose not because of anything dramatic in the US economy but because the cost of money is expected to keep rising, and everyone else's cost of money is expected to rise by less. Japan's finance ministry now has a choice between talking and spending. History says it starts with talking, then checks the price of the yen against its own patience, and then, when 160 is clearly broken, discovers it has reserves after all.

The rest of the market faces a simpler question. If 5.10% on the 10-year is the new floor for the moment, then every other asset that was priced on the assumption of 4% is now a sentence in need of a rewrite.
