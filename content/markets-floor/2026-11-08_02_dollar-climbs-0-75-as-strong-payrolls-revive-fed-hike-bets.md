# Dollar Climbs 0.75% as Strong Payrolls Revive Fed Hike Bets

The US dollar index rose 0.75% to 102.32 on Friday, according to Investrade's closing recap, after October payrolls came in at 165,000 against a consensus of roughly 65,000. It was the dollar's highest close in more than a week, and the move arrived on a day when the currency's main rivals were busy supplying their own reasons not to be bought.

## A scoreboard with one winner

The euro fell 79 pips to 1.1142 against the dollar, per Investrade, in a week that ranged from 1.1105 to 1.1273, according to the Substack weekly wrap by Daniel San. The yen sat at 158.49 per dollar, within a weekly range of 157.26 to 158.80, with some support from hawkish Bank of Japan minutes. The Canadian dollar closed near 1.4125, up about 1% on the week for the US currency. Mitrade's desk headlined the damage elsewhere: sterling "cracks 1.3400" on the payrolls, and the Australian dollar fell to its lowest since March.

None of these is a collapse. All of them are a currency trader's way of saying that if the Federal Reserve is going to raise rates in December, the other side of the pair had better have a persuasive answer. Most of them did not.

The payrolls report was strong at every level one cares to inspect. Private payrolls rose 159,000 against a forecast of about 64,000. Unemployment was 4.1%, according to the weekly wrap, from 4.2% the previous month. Hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year, ahead of the 2.9% economists had pencilled in. Revisions added to the effect: September now stands at 60,000 rather than 29,000, and August at 186,000 rather than 133,000.

Fed funds futures moved accordingly. The probability of a December hike stood at about 75% on Friday evening, up from roughly 67% on Thursday evening, per the Substack wrap. New York Fed president John Williams did nothing to arrest the drift. Inflation is "too high for too long," he told a conference in Frankfurt on Friday, according to Reuters coverage carried by TMGM. The Fed held at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28, with two dissenters wanting to move.

## Everyone else is also hiking, which is the problem

The interesting feature of this dollar rally is that it is not obviously a story about the US being unusually strong. It is a story about everyone being roughly equally inflation-prone and the US releasing its data first. The Bank of England raised its rate by 25 basis points to 4.00% on Thursday, in an 8 to 1 vote with one member preferring a larger move, according to Newsquawk. Eurozone inflation reached 4.0% in October, Euronews reported, with markets pricing about an 84% chance of a European Central Bank hike in December. The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate to 4.85% earlier in the week, though Reuters noted it had dropped language signalling further increases.

A hiking cycle in which every central bank is hiking is not a currency story so much as a competition over who sounds least tired. Friday's payrolls simply gave the dollar a head start. The yen, meanwhile, remains the structural outlier: the Bank of Japan's policy rate is 1.25%, per the weekly wrap, and the currency changes hands near 158.

The equity market, which spent Thursday in a state of undress, recovered some dignity. The S&P 500 closed at 7,376.86, up 0.32% on the day but down 2.68% on the week. One wrap reported that Japan's Nikkei 225 fell about 6% on Friday to 57,435, though that figure comes from a single source and should be treated with the caution one applies to any number of that size.

## What to watch

The next test arrives Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, when October consumer prices are due, with consensus at 0.3% for the headline and 0.2% for the core. A reading in line leaves the December hike roughly where payrolls put it. A cooler one invites a retracement. A hotter one would make the dollar's best Friday in a week look like a warm-up act.

Currencies, as the old line goes, are priced relative to each other. This week the dollar was relatively less disappointing, which in the foreign exchange market counts as a mandate.
