# Nvidia Slides 3.4% as CEO Scrutiny Meets a Wounded Chip Sector

Nvidia shares slid 3.4% on Monday, according to a Reuters headline carried by investingLive, as questions over Chief Executive Jensen Huang's future landed on a stock that had already lost 9.3% in a single session last Thursday. The Reuters line tied the move to scrutiny of Huang "amid foreign intelligence probe", which is a lot of drama for a ticker that had hoped to spend the week recovering.

## The probe, such as it is

The scrutiny traces to a New York Times investigation published on 29 October. As summarised by The Hill, the Times reported that US spy agencies had found that Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based data-centre company Megaspeed, funnelled information to Beijing while building a close relationship with Jensen Huang. Tom's Guide, summarising the same report, notes that there is no public evidence that Jensen Huang knew of, or took part in, any operation.

Nvidia disputes the framing. A company spokesperson told The Hill that the diversion allegations against the regional cloud were "thoroughly investigated and debunked last year", and that Huang's dealings with the executive were "not unusual or unexpected". The Times also reported that President Trump was briefed on the intelligence about a year ago and took no further action. The White House did not immediately respond to The Hill's request for comment.

None of this is a finding of wrongdoing by Nvidia or its chief executive, and this column is not suggesting one. It is, however, the sort of story that markets are prepared to price before the facts arrive, because the alternative is waiting for the facts.

## A stock with no cushion

The facts of last Thursday are easier to establish. According to the Associated Press, Nvidia fell 9.3% on 5 November and was by far the heaviest weight on the S&P 500, which dropped 4.4% to 7,353.73. That left the chipmaker down 2.6% for the year, after annual gains of roughly 39%, 171% and 239% over the previous three years. A stock that has compounded like that does not carry much margin for error, and it was not carrying any on Thursday.

The Nasdaq composite fell 5.5% to 24,315.00, leaving it 11.9% below its record set last month, the AP said. AMD dropped 17.3% and CoreWeave 9.8%. The wider damage was considerable. Reuters put the value destroyed across S&P 500 companies at $3 trillion, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor index plunged more than 12%. All 11 S&P sectors closed lower, and CNBC reported that information technology fell 7.30%, its worst day since 16 March 2020. The Cboe VIX closed at 28.18, and volume ran at 26.9 billion shares against a 20-session average of 17.7 billion.

Viraj Patel of Vanda Research told Reuters that retail investors, who ordinarily buy dips, had largely stopped doing so in individual names. When the marginal buyer leaves the room, a 3.4% slide is not a rounding error. It is a vote.

## Why now

The AP attributed Thursday's rout to worries about the AI data-centre build-out, high borrowing costs, and the possibility that Democrats' midterm wins could lead governments to force pauses in data-centre construction. Rate expectations are not helping. Investing Live, citing CME FedWatch, put the odds of a December Fed hike at around 80%, up from roughly 70% before Friday's payrolls report. A growth stock priced for abundance does not enjoy being introduced to the cost of money.

Note the order of events. The Times report was already in circulation on 29 October. The chip sector's real trouble arrived a week later, on a day the AP chiefly blamed on spending worries and politics. The probe is therefore a candidate for the headline, not necessarily the cause of the move. Monday's slide may be investors adding a governance discount to a valuation discount, or it may be simple follow-through. The tape does not say, and the tape rarely does.

US consumer price data is due on Tuesday, and ten Federal Reserve speakers are scheduled this week. If the quarter-point question is going to be reopened, it will be reopened in front of a chip sector that has just learned what a bad day feels like.

The market's relationship with Nvidia has always been a bet that the company will keep being right. This week it is being asked whether the people around it will keep being unremarkable. Seldom has a stock been asked to price an absence of news so urgently.
