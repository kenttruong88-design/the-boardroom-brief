# Nvidia Beats Forecasts by $6.7 Billion, Falls 2.9% Anyway

Nvidia closed at $150.96 on Wednesday, down 2.89%, a day after reporting quarterly revenue that beat analyst forecasts by roughly $6.7 billion, according to Reuters market data. The shares have now lost 13.11% over five days. Market capitalisation stands at about $3.75 trillion, which is still a figure most countries would be happy to call a GDP.

*Photo: Rafael Minguet Delgado via Pexels*

The results themselves were, by any ordinary standard, excellent. Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion against expectations of $109.15 billion, and adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share against $2.47 expected, according to Yahoo Finance. It guided fourth-quarter revenue to between $127.4 billion and $132.6 billion, against an analyst consensus of $125.25 billion, and lifted its fiscal 2028 growth outlook to 75% from 70% three months earlier.

The market's first reaction was appropriate. Shares rose almost 4% in premarket trading. By the cash open the rally had narrowed, and by lunchtime Yahoo Finance was describing the stock as down on the day. The Associated Press recorded the same arc: up 2.7% at the open, then a slide through zero. Few trades have reversed so quickly since the last time someone said "just one more slide" in a board meeting.

## The line the numbers do not contain

The explanation sits in neither the income statement nor the guidance. It sits in the corner office. Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on November 12, and the chief financial officer, Colette Kress, is serving as interim CEO while the board searches. Kress told analysts that "our momentum is real", and the figures supported her. The question the market kept asking was who, exactly, would be running the momentum in 2028.

Oppenheimer's Rich Schafer described the situation as a leadership vacuum, which he noted nature and investors both abhor. Seaport's Jay Goldberg was more specific, saying he did not know that any internal candidate was capable of running the whole company. Neither remark appears on a balance sheet, but both appeared in the share price.

Nvidia is not a small contributor to the index. Its slide helped pull the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 1.11% to 50,198.17 and the S&P 500 down 0.23% to 7,323.63, Reuters reported. The Nasdaq Composite managed a gain of 0.42%, to 24,429.88, which is the sort of divergence that tells you the damage was concentrated rather than general. Over five days the S&P 500 is down 3.88%, the Dow down 4.36% and the Nasdaq down 4.07%.

## The neighbours

Nvidia's customers and rivals were sorted by mood rather than by merit. By mid-morning, according to the AP, Broadcom was up 2.3% after an early gain of 3%, while CoreWeave, whose business is renting out Nvidia hardware, was down 2.6%. In a sector where everyone's revenue is somebody else's capital expenditure, a stock falling on good news is not a verdict on one company so much as a poll on the whole arrangement.

Elsewhere, the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.08% in the morning, down from 5.10% on Tuesday, and Brent crude fell 1.4% to $91.85 a barrel, the AP said. Investors expect the Federal Reserve to raise rates next month after holding in October. The dollar and the yield are, in other words, offering the chip sector the sort of competition for capital that it is not accustomed to.

The after-hours tape had offered an early clue. Kiplinger's live coverage recorded a gain of about 4% in extended trading on Tuesday, the sort of move that usually gets a trading desk to order lunch at its desk. It did not survive contact with the Wednesday session. Gross margin of 74.9% and data-center revenue of $107.6 billion, according to Kiplinger, were not enough to override the lingering question of who signs the next product roadmap.

## What it means

A beat-and-raise is meant to be the cleanest signal a company can send. On Wednesday it was received as one input among several, and not the loudest. Investors have not stopped believing in the demand for accelerated computing. They are simply pricing the possibility that the person who built the franchise is not coming back, and that the board has hired a consultant rather than a successor.

The conclusion for traders is uncomfortable but tidy. Nvidia delivered the best quarter in its history and lost almost 3% of its value in a day. The earnings were never the risk. The org chart was.
