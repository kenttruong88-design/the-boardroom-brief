# S&P 500 Gains 1.3% as Gold Falls Below $4,000

The S&P 500 rose 1.3% on Wednesday, to roughly 7,620, recovering most of the previous week's losses, according to The Associated Press. The Nasdaq composite gained 1.6% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 0.5%. Gold, the traditional refuge of the nervous, fell about 1.5% to roughly $3,980 an ounce, closing below $4,000, according to the Rio Times.

*Photo: Rafael Minguet Delgado via Pexels*

The rally has a feature that should be noted before anyone frames it. American bond markets were closed on Wednesday for Veterans Day, according to market reports. The Treasury market is the thing that had spent the week making equity investors uncomfortable: the 10-year yield crossed 5% during Tuesday's session before settling at 4.992%, and the two-year yield rose roughly 18 basis points on the day, its sharpest one-day increase since April 2025, per the same reports. On Wednesday, with the source of the discomfort unavailable for trading, stocks found they felt much better.

## The numbers behind the mood

None of the inputs had changed overnight. October consumer prices, released on Tuesday, rose 0.4% on the month against an expected 0.3%, with the core measure up 0.3% against an expected 0.2%. A Federal Reserve rate increase in December remains the central scenario; the prediction market Polymarket put the odds of a quarter-point hike at roughly 85%, according to the Rio Times, with a further 10% or so on something larger.

The Cboe Volatility Index settled at about 17.5, a level that in a calmer year would have been called mildly elevated and this year is called a return to normal. It is a measure of how far the baseline has moved that a reading in the high teens now reads as reassurance.

Oil offered some help. US crude fell 1.2% to $85.48 a barrel and Brent fell 1.2% to $91.65, according to AP, which has been the direction of travel this week. Cheaper crude is the cleanest argument that Tuesday's inflation reading is a ceiling rather than a floor. It is also an argument that has now been made, and abandoned, several times this month.

## Gold, reconsidered

The weaker note was struck by gold. Metal that had been the beneficiary of every fear this autumn lost about 1.5% in a session where fear was, on paper, receding. The explanation circulating in market commentary was arithmetic: with a Treasury paying 5%, an ounce of gold that pays nothing has to justify its storage costs. A 5% Treasury, as one summary put it, is tough competition. Gold fell on a day that the Treasury market was closed, which is either a tribute to the Treasury market's reach or evidence that the argument had been won earlier and was merely being filed.

## Thursday, and the usual reminder

Asia, which does not observe Veterans Day, did not follow Wall Street. Japan's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.1%, South Korea's Kospi was roughly flat, Taiwan's Taiex fell 1.2%, Australia's ASX 200 lost 0.4% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.9%, while Shanghai edged up 0.2%, per AP. The dollar bought 159.74 yen and the euro $1.1077.

The reason for the regional gloom was Nvidia, whose shares fell 8.6% in after-hours trading, AP reported, on reports that its board had met to discuss Chief Executive Jensen Huang. The company is the single largest weight in the artificial-intelligence trade, and its shares had already come under pressure earlier in the week. An index that has just recovered a week of losses in a single afternoon now has to explain why its largest component has just surrendered a large part of the afternoon.

The calendar offers little respite. Producer prices are due on Friday, with economists looking for a 0.4% monthly rise, according to the Rio Times, and Thursday's 30-year auction will show whether the long end of the curve still has buyers at these levels. A rally built on a closed market is, by construction, untested until the market opens.

None of this is a forecast. It is an inventory. Equities gained on a day Treasuries were closed, gold fell on a day it was supposed to rise, and Asia sold what Wall Street had bought. Chris Weston of Pepperstone summarised the week's logic in a note carried by AAP on Monday: "The only fundamental this week that matters is price."

The Treasury market reopens on Thursday, when a 30-year bond auction awaits. Equity investors will be able to find out then what they thought of their Wednesday.
