# Gold and Silver Slide as Bitcoin Breaks Below $60,000

Gold, silver and bitcoin fell together on Tuesday, November 24, while Brent crude rose, a combination that does not appear in any hedging textbook the author has consulted. Gold futures fell more than 2 percent, silver futures dropped 5.75 percent according to Reuters, bitcoin slipped below $60,000, and Brent settled $2.03 higher at $91.40 a barrel.

## Gold: the numbers

December gold futures settled $89.50 lower at $3,561.90 an ounce, according to Que.com's market report. NewsBeep, carrying Reuters, put the decline in futures at 2.67 percent, and HNGN described the fall as more than 2 percent. One page I read, HDFC Sky, showed gold down only 0.42 percent at $3,619.91, which appears to be a different price series, so I have set it aside.

The context matters. On November 18, Bloomberg reported through Swissinfo that spot gold had fallen 3.2 percent in a single session to $3,724.75 an ounce. Spot and futures are different instruments and are not strictly comparable, but the direction is not in dispute: the metal has dropped by roughly $160 an ounce since November 18 on the closest comparison available. On November 12, TheStreet reported gold at its lowest close since October 2025. A refuge that keeps setting new lows for the year is not a refuge. It is a position.

## Silver, which does everything gold does, faster

Silver futures fell 5.75 percent on Tuesday, per Reuters via NewsBeep. Silver is traditionally gold's more excitable cousin, with an industrial use case that makes it sensitive to growth fears as well as to fear itself. Tuesday's move was more than double gold's, which is what a leveraged version of the same trade looks like when the trade is going the wrong way.

## Bitcoin: below the line

Bitcoin traded below $60,000, near $59,500 according to Que.com and about $59,400, down nearly 3 percent, according to HNGN. Que.com also reported an overnight high above $64,000, which means the coin travelled more than $4,000 in one direction and then came back for it. On November 18, Bloomberg put bitcoin at $62,518.53 after a 2.8 percent fall. The level then was about $3,000 higher, and the market described that day as a decline.

Nobody in the stories I read attributed the move to a single cause, which is the honest position when a market is both a risk asset and an ideology.

## Oil goes the other way

Brent settled up $2.03, or 2.27 percent, at $91.40, Reuters reported. Que.com put West Texas Intermediate $1.88 higher at $82.05. On November 18, Bloomberg had WTI at $82.80, down 3.7 percent that day, so American crude is roughly where it was a week ago, having visited several other prices on the way.

The oil rise and the metals fall are the awkward pairing. Normally, an energy shock that pushes inflation expectations higher helps gold. On Tuesday, gold went down. The 10-year Treasury yield stayed near 4.9 percent, with the pages I read quoting between 4.90 and 4.93 percent, and gold pays no interest. A yield that high is the price of holding a metal that sits in a vault. When yields rise and oil rises, the metal is not asked to protect against inflation. It is asked to explain why it does not yield.

## What the day was not

It was not a risk-off day. The Dow closed above 50,000, per several accounts, and the equity market's weakness was concentrated in technology. It was not a dollar day either: the pages I read disagreed on whether the dollar index rose or fell, so I decline to build a narrative on a figure with an internal contradiction.

What the day offers instead is a portfolio lesson. Gold, silver and bitcoin are held for different stories: an old store of value, an industrial and monetary hybrid, and a speculative digital one. On Tuesday they behaved as one, with each of the three going down in response to the same thing, which was apparently the opportunity cost of owning something that does not pay a coupon while Treasuries do.

Diversification is the only free lunch in finance, the saying goes. Tuesday suggested the kitchen is having a quiet week.

Wednesday's data, including the October personal consumption expenditures report, will say whether inflation is the reason oil is rising and the metals are not. Until then, the precious metals can reflect on being safe havens in the sense that the haven is safe, provided you are not inside it.
