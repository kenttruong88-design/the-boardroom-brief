# Nvidia Falls 4.3% to $166.19 After Its CEO Quits Before Breakfast

Nvidia shares closed at $166.19 on Thursday, down 4.34%, after the company announced at 8:48 a.m. Eastern that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. At the worst point of the session the stock was down as much as 13%, according to Yahoo Finance. The market therefore spent the morning pricing a leadership vacuum and the afternoon deciding that 13% had been a little keen.

## What the filing says

The company's 8-K and accompanying press release give no reason for the departure. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim CEO and keeps her finance job. Harvey C. Jones, a director since 1993, is named the first chair of the board in the company's history. The board said it will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent role. Third-quarter fiscal 2027 results are still scheduled for Tuesday, November 17, with the call at 2 p.m. Pacific, which gives the interim arrangement three trading days to settle in before it has to explain itself to analysts.

Fortune reported separately that Huang acknowledged an extramarital relationship with a woman whom US intelligence officials had deemed a suspected Chinese spy, and that the board had opened an investigation. According to Fortune, the board said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised." Nvidia's own filing does not mention any of this. Fortune also reported that the company's first-half fiscal 2027 revenue was $96 billion, up 85% from a year earlier, which is the sort of figure that makes a resignation feel less like a rescue and more like an accident.

## The tape

Set against the broad market, the move was large. The S&P 500 fell 0.35% to 7,592.40, the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 0.36% to 52,297.09, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.39% to 25,366.09, per Yahoo Finance's closing tables. Nvidia's drop was therefore more than twelve times the S&P's. The Russell 2000 fell 1.66%, and the VIX settled at 18.70, a number that suggests investors were worried but not yet writing letters.

Elsewhere in the sector, Saxo Bank's Asia note said Broadcom and AMD outperformed on the day, which is the polite way of saying that competitors enjoyed the news. In Europe the Stoxx 600 rose 0.1% to a record 664.01, according to the same note, so the damage was a domestic matter. Saxo's Friday-morning read showed Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.6% and S&P 500 futures up 0.4%, and Cisco was higher after hours on raised guidance. The rest of the market, it seems, had been waiting for someone else to have a bad Thursday.

## The analysts reach for the pen

Truist cut Nvidia to Hold from Buy and lowered its price target to $174 from $346. Craig-Hallum made the same downgrade and moved its target to $180 from $275, writing that "Huang's departure leaves a major leadership gap that will be extremely difficult to fill." Two details deserve a moment. Truist's old target was more than double Thursday's close, which tells you how much of the price was a view on one man. And its new target, with the target sliced nearly in half, still sits about 4.7% above where the stock actually finished. A Hold, in this instance, is a Buy that has taken a day off.

Yahoo Finance's midday snapshot had the shares down 10.9% on the week, which would have been the stock's worst week in more than a year. The close was kinder than the snapshot, but the week is not over, and Friday's futures point higher for the broad market, if not necessarily for Nvidia. Former Goldman Sachs chief Lloyd Blankfein, via a post on X quoted by Yahoo, called the exit "Shakespearean," a rare case of a Wall Street veteran underselling a drama.

## The Tuesday problem

The practical issue is scheduling. On Tuesday, Nvidia reports its quarter to a market that has just marked the stock down 4.34% on the news that the person who built the company is gone. The executive presenting the numbers will be the chief financial officer. She will also be the chief executive. When an analyst asks about guidance, the person responsible for the forecast and the person responsible for the strategy will, for the first time in memory, be in total agreement.

There is a certain efficiency in that. The earnings call needs only one set of talking points, and nobody will have to explain who made the decision. The market, however, has made its own decision, and it has priced in a vacancy, not an efficiency gain.
