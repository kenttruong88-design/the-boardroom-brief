# Gold Drops 2.6% to $4,030 as Brent Settles at $91.49, Lowest Since August

Gold, the asset investors buy when they want to feel calm, fell 2.62% on Tuesday to $4,030.56 an ounce. Brent crude, the asset everyone watches when they want to feel anxious, settled at $91.49, its lowest close since August 31. Between them, the two commodities ran a short seminar on what happens when the dollar and bond yields stop cooperating.

![Gold bars stacked in a vault](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/markets-floor/2026-11-11-02-gold-falls-oil-brent-lowest-close-august-hero)
*Photo: Aurelijus U. via Pexels*

According to Euronext, citing Reuters, spot gold's drop came as US inflation data pushed Treasury yields sharply higher and lifted the dollar. The dollar index gained 0.56% to 102.25, per Cappnotes, which is the arithmetic gold dislikes most: bullion is priced in dollars, pays no interest, and becomes more expensive for foreign buyers whenever the dollar strengthens. Higher yields, meanwhile, raise the opportunity cost of owning a lump of metal that sits in a vault.

The move built through the session. TheStreet's early-trading snapshot had gold down only 0.69% at $4,086.10, with silver off 0.58% at $52.39. By the close, gold's loss was nearly four times that, which suggests that the early buyers were optimists and the late sellers had read the CPI report.

## What the CPI did

October consumer prices rose 0.4% on the month, ahead of the 0.3% forecast, with core up 0.3% against an expected 0.2%, according to TheStreet. The two-year Treasury yield rose 18.44 basis points to 4.731%, Euronext reported. The Federal Reserve meets December 8-9, and the data raised the odds that the hike delivered then will be larger than 25 basis points.

For gold, that is a problem of its own making. Gold is conventionally treated as the preferred hedge against inflation and geopolitical risk. Tuesday offered the first, in the form of a hot print, and the second, in the form of an ongoing Iran war, and gold still fell. The explanation is that the market cares less about inflation itself than about the interest-rate response to it. A hedge that loses to the central bank's reaction function is a hedge with a footnote. Investors who bought gold for protection learned on Tuesday that protection, like insurance, has exclusions, and that the exclusions are printed in the Federal Reserve's minutes.

Gold at $4,030 remains a high price by any historical standard, and a session like this one is the sort of thing long-term holders are expected to describe as healthy. A drop of 2.6% in one session is a notable move, though it is a move from a very elevated level.

## Oil keeps falling, which is the opposite of the inflation story

![Oil refinery at dusk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/markets-floor/2026-11-11-02-gold-falls-oil-brent-lowest-close-august-body)
*Photo: Oleksiy via Pexels*

The crude market ran in the opposite direction from the inflation narrative. Euronext reported that Brent settled at $91.49, down 1.2%, while West Texas Intermediate settled at $86.04, down 0.9%. Both were the lowest closes since August 31.

Fortune's price tracker offers a longer view. At 7:15 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, Brent stood at $92.98, down $4.67 from the same time on Monday and $14.30 below its level a month earlier, when it traded at $107.28. A month ago, in other words, Brent was above $107. That is a 13% decline, give or take, which would normally be described as a relief rally for consumers and instead arrived on the same day that consumer prices were reported to be rising faster than forecast.

Cappnotes attributed part of oil's softness to remarks by President Donald Trump that the Iran war could end "fairly soon." This is a secondary source for a statement we were unable to trace to a primary one, so the headline belongs to the crude market and not to the President. Traders, for their part, have learned to price the possibility of peace more readily than its arrival.

The tension is obvious. If the oil shock that pushed core inflation higher is fading, the Fed may be about to tighten into an energy price that is already retreating. Jacobsen of Annex Wealth Management put the inflation worry in Reuters this way: the oil shock "has seeped into core." Seepage, unlike spot prices, is slow to drain.

For commodity traders, Tuesday's lesson is that no single asset class has a monopoly on being the safe one. Gold fell because rates rose. Oil fell because peace talk rose. The dollar rose because everything else did the opposite. The only thing that held steady was the market's confidence that someone, somewhere, was about to be wrong.
