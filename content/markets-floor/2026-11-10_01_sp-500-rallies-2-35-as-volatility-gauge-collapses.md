# S&P 500 Rallies 2.35% to 7,549.94 as Volatility Gauge Collapses

Wall Street spent Monday undoing most of what it had spent the previous week doing. The S&P 500 closed up 2.35%, or 173.08 points, at 7,549.94, while the Nasdaq gained 2.74%, according to market data compiled by JournalArta. The VIX, the index investors consult when they wish to be told how frightened they are, fell 22.31% to 19.92.

*Photo: Alex Luna via Pexels*

The rally was broad enough to embarrass anyone who had sold on Friday. According to StockTitan's relay of CNBC's live coverage, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up more than 1,100 points, or about 2.2%, by early afternoon, putting it above its Election Day close. The Nasdaq Composite was up roughly 2.5% at the same point. Taiwan's main index had already closed up 4.94% on Monday, its biggest one-day gain since July 31, handing the semiconductor trade its opening bid before New York woke up.

## Chips, with one exception

Technology did the heavy lifting. Early on Monday, Marvell was up 7.41% and Arm up 4.98%, according to the StockTitan tally. By early afternoon AMD had gained roughly 12% and Broadcom about 8%. CNBC attributed AMD's early strength to an upgrade to outperform from Oppenheimer, which is the sort of thing that, on a quiet Monday, moves a stock by a percent or two, and on a Monday like this one apparently moves it by twelve.

Then there was Nvidia. On Sunday afternoon the company's board said it had opened an investigation into reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside lawyers, will run the review. The board said it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were harmed, and that it retains confidence in Mr Huang. The market, which is not required to share the board's serenity, sold the stock. Shares were down about 5.4% in early afternoon trading, at roughly $176.54 against a Friday close of $186.64. StockTitan's own arithmetic, resting on 24.1 billion shares outstanding, puts the paper loss at around $243 billion, which is a lot of money to lose on a day when everyone else was making it.

The divergence is the notable feature. Joachim Klement of Panmure Liberum, quoted by CNBC, said the damage appeared contained to Nvidia itself. The rest of the sector's behaviour supports him: a chipmaker's reputational problem is, for now, being treated as a chipmaker's reputational problem, and not as a verdict on artificial intelligence. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on November 17, so the board's review will be competing for attention with something more conventional, namely a number.

## Everything else went up too

Outside semiconductors, the session read like a corporate-action bulletin. NRG Energy rose 9.47% after agreeing to sell six gas plants to LS Power for $4 billion in cash. Fervo Energy gained 22.94% after bringing the first unit of its Cape Station into commercial operation, backed by a 396-megawatt power deal with Google. Greenfire Resources jumped 38.13% after Strathcona agreed to buy it. Not every stock participated: monday.com fell 5.90% after guiding fourth-quarter growth to about 14%, down from 19%, and Ionis dropped 24.62% after cutting its 2026 sales forecast for its drug Tryngolza.

Deal flow was as lively as the tape. Surgery Partners rose 12.76% after HealthSpan Partners raised its cash offer to $25 a share, following a rival bid of $28. QXO gained 20.11% after preliminary third-quarter net sales of about $4.90 billion, up 79% including two acquired companies. Vir Biotechnology rose 11.88% after a hepatitis delta treatment met its primary endpoint in a phase 3 trial. Take-Two, Occidental, KKR, Constellation and Skydance were all due to report after the bell, according to CNBC, which gives the next trading session something to do besides wait for the inflation number.

Asia kept the mood going on Tuesday. JournalArta reported the Nikkei 225 up 2.33% at 60,142.12 and Australia's ASX 200 up 1.39%. The US ten-year Treasury yield, meanwhile, barely moved, at 4.864%. Bond traders, it appears, had been invited to the party and declined.

The caveat is on the calendar. October consumer price data is due at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, and traders have spent days pricing a December rate rise on the strength of the jobs report. A rally built on relief is a rally built on the absence of bad news, which is a thin foundation, though a perfectly serviceable one for a Monday.

Volatility at 19.92 is not calm, merely less alarmed. The VIX has fallen by nearly a quarter in a day. Whether investors have stopped worrying, or have simply run out of things to sell before the inflation number, will be settled by about a quarter to nine.
