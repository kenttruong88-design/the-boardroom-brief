# Dow Closes Above 50,000 as Nvidia Gives Back 6% of Rally

The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 50,000 for the first time on Tuesday, November 24, rising about 0.85 percent, or roughly 424 points, to a little over 50,019, according to several market wrappers that agreed on the broad shape of the day if not on the last decimal. The S&P 500 slipped 0.50 percent to about 7,201. The Nasdaq Composite lost 1.48 percent to 23,733.54. The index that rose belongs to industrial companies, and the indexes that fell are the ones that own the artificial intelligence trade. Both facts arrived at the closing bell together.

## The rebound, reviewed

Nvidia shares fell about 6 percent after Monday's 31.5 percent surge, which followed the company's announcement that Jensen Huang had been reinstated as chief executive. HNGN put the prior closes at $125.53 on Friday and $165.05 on Monday. Reuters, as carried by NewsBeep, said Monday's gain added roughly $1 trillion in market value; other outlets put it at more than $900 billion. Que.com, citing Investopedia, reported that the stock fell more than 8 percent at its low on Tuesday and remains about 35 percent below its record close of October 6.

Put differently, Monday's rally was the first draft of the market's opinion, and Tuesday was the first round of edits. The market has not so much changed its mind about Nvidia as begun to audit it.

Strategists supplied the customary vocabulary. "After the biggest rally in months, you would expect some profit-taking," Gene Goldman, chief investment officer at Cetera Financial Group, told Reuters. Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG Group, said that Huang's return "assures people massively and gives hope that the data centre construction continues." He then listed what remains unresolved: "What did he know? What else did he do? How much went to China?" Nvidia's board has said it was satisfied with Huang's candour and ability to lead, according to HNGN, a reassurance that has the useful property of being unfalsifiable until the investigation concludes.

## Dell has a long day

The AI complex was not alone in giving back ground. Que.com reported that Dell Technologies fell about 12 percent in the regular session to $295.28, after rising 20 percent on Monday. After the close, Dell reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $54.9 billion, up 103 percent from a year earlier, according to the company's filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, and the shares rose about 2 to 3 percent in extended trading, per the same account. A company that doubles its revenue and is rewarded with a small after-hours gain after a double-digit daily loss has learned what the market thinks of beating a number that is already priced.

Que.com also reported that the Russell 2000 gained 0.57 percent to 2,684.94, a figure no other page I read supplied, so treat it accordingly. Elsewhere, Anthropic, which priced its initial public offering at $83, fell more than 6 percent to just over $1 below that price, Reuters reported. An issue priced above its marketed range can, it turns out, still trade at a discount, which is the sort of thing the range was supposed to prevent.

## Yields, and who is steering

The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.9 percent, with the pages I read quoting between 4.90 and 4.93 percent. That is high enough to matter for the valuation of anything whose cash flows arrive in the future, and the AI trade is made largely of such things. Krishna Guha of Evercore ISI said, according to HNGN, that markets remain uncertain about the stance of Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh. Mark Haefele of UBS Global Wealth Management, per the same outlet, sees no evidence that the investment case for AI has broken.

Two views, then, sit side by side: the case is intact, and the discount rate is not helping. Both can be true, and the tape on Tuesday suggested investors were prepared to hold both and sell a little of whichever one was most recently expensive.

## The number on the screen

A Dow above 50,000 is a round number, and round numbers are the market's version of a company all-hands: everyone gathers, someone says something about journey, and the actual work resumes the next morning. The Dow is price-weighted, which means its direction depends on the prices of its thirty members rather than their size, and it can rise while the Nasdaq falls by more than one percent, as it did on Tuesday.

The milestone is real. The mood behind it is more qualified. A market that sets a record on the same afternoon the stock behind the year's biggest rally falls 6 percent, and a company that has just doubled its revenue falls 12 percent, is not in the grip of exuberance. It is in the grip of an index construction method, which has been known to pass.

Wednesday brings the second estimate of third-quarter gross domestic product and the October personal consumption expenditures report, ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. Traders will have two data releases and a turkey to price. One of them has been in the oven since Monday.
