# Anthropic Prices at $83, Closes at $97.17 After Record IPO

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday, November 19, and closed its first session at $97.17 a share, up $14.17, or 17.1%, from its $83 offer price, according to Forbes and Yahoo Finance. It was the largest initial public offering ever recorded, and for the first half of the day it behaved accordingly.

![Stock exchange trading screens showing market data](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/markets-floor/2026-11-20-01-anthropic-prices-83-closes-97-record-ipo-hero)

*Photo: Alex Luna via Pexels*

The shares were priced at $83, above the $70 to $78 range the company had indicated earlier in the process. Anthropic and its selling stockholders sold 980 million Class A shares, of which 956,351,354 came from the company, raising about $81.3 billion, according to Forbes and Yahoo. That tops the roughly $75 billion SpaceX raised in June, the previous record. SK Hynix's $26.5 billion offering in July and Saudi Aramco's $25.6 billion in 2019 now look like bake-sale figures. A greenshoe option for a further 147 million shares remains open for 30 days.

## A fast start, a slower finish

The first trade printed at $111.50 at 11:48 a.m. Eastern, about 34% above the offer price, according to StockTitan's live coverage. Forbes put the intraday high at $115.10, and the AP reported a spike above $115. From there the stock took the stairs down. The intraday low was $92.72, and the close of $97.17 sat roughly $14 below the opening print. Volume had passed 600 million shares by 1:30 p.m. Eastern, Forbes reported, citing CNBC, which is a great many people agreeing to disagree about a price.

Anyone who bought at the open and held to the close lost money on the day. Anyone who received shares at $83 had a very pleasant Thursday. This is the traditional arrangement in IPOs, and it was observed with unusual fidelity at unusual scale.

Pre-market, traders on the crypto venue Hyperliquid had priced the stock near $96 and expected a pop of 20% to 25%, according to Yahoo's live blog. The market, in other words, had already priced the debut, and then the debut priced itself differently. The final close landed within a dollar of the Hyperliquid number, which will be cited at several conferences.

## What the company is worth depends on who is counting

Valuation was the one part of the day nobody could agree on. Yahoo and BNN Bloomberg described the company as worth about $1.5 trillion at the offer price on a fully diluted basis, counting every share and option that could exist. Forbes reported a market capitalisation of about $1.27 trillion at the close and said the figure topped $2 trillion at the opening peak. A CNN headline cited $1.76 trillion. The articles did not consistently explain which share count they were using, so this desk will not pick a number and suggests that readers hold all of them loosely.

The reasoning holds up in at least one respect. A company of this size, valued on an uncertain share count, has a market cap that changes by several hundred billion dollars depending on a footnote.

Daniela Amodei, Anthropic's president, rang the opening bell and told the crowd, "The end of this story isn't written yet." Analysts were similarly careful. Sam Grelck of Truist Advisory told CBS News he would not view the listing as a verdict on artificial intelligence, a sentence that works equally well on every outcome.

## The backdrop was less festive

The debut landed on a day when the broader market fell hard. The Dow dropped roughly 1,100 points and the S&P 500 lost 2%, according to the AP, so the most anticipated listing of the decade closed in the green while almost everything around it closed in the red. Dan Ives told Yahoo the listing was a win for the technology sector after a difficult stretch involving Nvidia, which is one way to describe a day on which the sector lost a great deal of value.

A note of disclosure: Anthropic builds the Claude models, and The Alignment Times has a professional interest in being seen to treat its stock exactly like everyone else's. It has been treated like a stock that opened 34% above its offer and closed 17% above it, which is to say, like a stock.

The lesson of day one is modest. Investors will pay a great deal for the company, then immediately begin discussing the price. The first day of trading is the cheapest moment to be certain about anything, and as of Thursday's close, the shares are worth $97.17 and no one is sure why.
