# S&P 500 Falls 1.9% as Nvidia Sheds $735 Billion Before Earnings

Wall Street ended Monday sharply lower, with the S&P 500 down 1.9%, the Nasdaq Composite down 2.5% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 1.4%, according to an Associated Press market report. Nvidia fell 4.1%, and the AP said the chipmaker has now lost $735 billion in market value since the disclosures surrounding chief executive Jensen Huang's departure. It reports quarterly results on Tuesday, which is either a catalyst or a hostage situation, depending on your position size.

## The arithmetic of a bad Monday

Schwab's morning table put Friday's S&P 500 close at 7,561.67. A 1.9% fall from there implies a close near 7,418, though that is our arithmetic and not a reported figure. The decline follows a week in which the index rose 2.5%, according to Blockhead, citing Investrade data, and in which the Nasdaq had its best week since August, according to Schwab. Monday took back a respectable share of that, and did so in a single session, which is the market's way of demonstrating efficiency.

The damage was concentrated where the enthusiasm had been. Reuters reported before the open that Nvidia was coming off its longest weekly losing streak since October 2012, with Intel, AMD, Broadcom and Micron each indicated 3.5% to 5% lower. CoreWeave was down 5.4%, and Dell and HP were each down 5.1%.

Schwab noted that the PHLX Semiconductor Index closed last week at 10,146.32. That is about 24% below its October high of 13,349.96 and under its 200-day moving average of 10,884.21. A chip index that sits a quarter below its peak and beneath its long-run average is not having a pullback. It is having a conversation, and the conversation is about price.

## The other side of the trade

The backdrop is not helping. The AP put the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.13% in its Tuesday morning wrap, against 5.15% late Friday and a low of 4.80% earlier this month. Reuters, citing CME FedWatch, said markets fully price a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, with about an 8% chance of a half-point move. Equities that were priced for falling rates are now being asked to explain themselves to rising ones.

It is not an earnings problem, at least not yet. The AP, citing FactSet, said S&P 500 profits for the third quarter are expected to be nearly 32% higher than a year earlier. The question being asked on trading desks is not whether companies made money. It is how much anyone should pay for it when a risk-free bond yields more than 5%.

There is also a supply issue. Ipek Ozkardeskaya, senior analyst at Swissquote, told Reuters that investors buying Anthropic "may sell existing technology holdings to make room." Blockhead reported that Anthropic is expected to price its IPO on Wednesday evening, with trading to begin on Nasdaq on Thursday. New technology equity is arriving in the same week that existing technology equity is looking for the exit. Even a patient market would find that scheduling awkward.

Asia took the hint on Tuesday. The AP reported that South Korea's Kospi fell 1% to 5,051.93, leaving it down more than 10% over the past month, and that Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.2% to 58,956.67, with SoftBank down 6.2% and Advantest down 4.3%. Zhiwei Zhang, chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, told the AP that investors were already worried about this risk before the trouble at Nvidia, and that the trouble has made it more serious. The AP also noted new Trump administration rules restricting remote access to advanced US AI chips and servers, which is not a sentence that calms a chip sector.

## What Tuesday has to deliver

Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results with an earnings call at 2 p.m. Pacific time, according to the company's announcement of Huang's resignation. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer who is also now the interim chief executive, will be the one talking. Schwab said Nvidia commands about 80% of the advanced AI chip market and that analysts expect revenue and earnings per share to be up about 90% from a year ago.

Those are the sort of numbers that once produced applause. This week they are being offered as evidence in a more complicated case. A company growing at roughly 90% that has lost $735 billion in value in a matter of days is being priced on something other than its quarter.

Tuesday's call will clarify whether that something is the departure of its founder, the level of interest rates, or the memory of how high the stock was in October. Our guess is all three, in proportions management cannot control and analysts cannot agree on, which is the one thing markets have always been able to price.
