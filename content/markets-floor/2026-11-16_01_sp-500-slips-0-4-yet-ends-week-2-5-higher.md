# S&P 500 Slips 0.4% Friday, Yet Ends Week 2.5% Higher

The S&P 500 fell 0.4% on Friday to close at 7,562, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.9% to 25,136, but both indices finished the week higher and ended four-week losing streaks, according to closing data compiled by Hammerstone Markets and published by Investrade on November 13. The S&P 500 gained 2.51% over the five sessions. The Nasdaq rose 2.27% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which closed at 52,348, added 2.07%.

A week in which every one of the S&P 500's eleven sectors rose is not, on its face, a week that requires an apology. And yet the bond market offered one anyway, on behalf of everyone else.

## The bond market declined to celebrate

The yield on the 10-year Treasury note ended Friday at 5.152%, up 5.1 basis points on the day and 29.4 basis points on the week, according to the same recap. That is the largest one-week rise since April 2025, and it ended a five-week run of falling yields. The two-year yield, which is more sensitive to expectations for the Federal Reserve, rose 35.2 basis points over the week to 4.872%.

Put differently: the cost of long-term money in the United States moved more in five days than it had in the better part of nineteen months, and equity investors responded by buying shares. One of these two groups has misread the situation. Historically, the bond market has the better record.

The proximate cause is a Tuesday inflation report that came in hotter than economists expected, followed by a procession of Fed officials who treated it as an invitation. Governor Christopher Waller said on November 10 that a 25 basis point hike in December is his base case, but that a 50 basis point move should be considered if October core inflation prints around 0.3% or higher, according to Kitco's account of his speech. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the central bank needs at least two more quarter-point increases and that it may be possible to move faster this winter.

Traders have noticed. The Investrade recap puts the market-implied probability of a 25 basis point hike at the December meeting at roughly 88%, with about 12% assigned to 50 basis points. In other words, the question being priced is no longer whether the Fed raises rates, but how enthusiastically.

## Who won the week

Energy, by some distance. The Energy Select Sector fund rose 5.37% on the week, ahead of industrials at 3.73%, communications at 3.72% and technology at 3.3%, per Investrade. Brent crude settled Friday at $95.84 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate at $88.56. The Baker Hughes rig count fell by three to 601.

Technology had a more complicated Friday. Tech shares fell for a second consecutive session, and Nvidia, the sector's bellwether, traded at fresh 52-week lows after its chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned on Thursday, according to the recap. The Nasdaq's Friday decline was the sharpest of the four major indices, which is a polite way of saying the AI trade is being asked to explain itself.

The Russell 2000 of small-cap stocks slipped 0.3% to 2,790.

## The week ahead, in order of difficulty

The coming week is not designed for relaxation. Nvidia reports third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday after the close, with analysts expecting earnings of $2.47 a share on revenue of roughly $108.8 billion, according to FXEmpire. Home Depot reports Tuesday morning, Target and Lowe's on Wednesday, and Walmart on Thursday. Retail sales for October land Tuesday. Minutes of the Fed's October meeting follow on Wednesday, at which point investors will learn precisely how much of the hawkish talk was already in the room.

Anthropic is also expected to begin trading on Nasdaq this Thursday, giving the market a large new stock to price at the exact moment it is struggling to price the existing ones.

The broader market breadth was healthy, if not triumphant. Every sector gained on the week, the Investrade recap said, and equity funds continued to attract money, with roughly $16.9 billion flowing into one large S&P 500 ETF in the five days to November 10. Investors, in other words, have been buying the index with their hands and watching the bond market with their eyes.

For now, the arithmetic is simple. Stocks are higher on the week, yields are sharply higher on the week, and a 10-year Treasury above 5% now offers a risk-free alternative to anyone nervous about 7,500 on the S&P 500. Equities have, for one week, ignored that competitor. They are unlikely to be permitted to do so indefinitely.
