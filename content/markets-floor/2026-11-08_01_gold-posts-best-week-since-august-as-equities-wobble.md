# Gold Posts Best Week Since August as Equities Wobble

Gold futures settled at $4,074.90 an ounce on Friday, down $38.60, or 0.94%, on the day, according to Investrade's closing recap. Spot gold nonetheless finished the week up 5.2%, its best since August, having climbed from a low of $3,869.85 on October 30 to a high of $4,146.63 on Thursday, per Mitrade's commodity desk. A buyer who caught the bottom made roughly $200 an ounce in a week. A buyer of Nasdaq futures on Wednesday had a more educational week.

## The tape, in order

The backdrop was unpleasant for almost everything else. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,376.86, which Investrade put at a weekly loss of 2.68%. The Nasdaq Composite ended at 24,579.42, down 3.37% on the week and its fourth consecutive weekly decline. On Thursday the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index lost 12.25%, after Democrats took both chambers of Congress and the market decided it did not care for the implications for artificial intelligence regulation.

Gold, an asset with no earnings, no guidance and no chief executive, performed its one advertised function. It went up while other things went down. Mitrade's Thursday note, written as Wall Street slid and Treasury yields fell, was headlined as much. The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 4.858%, according to Investrade, roughly 7 basis points lower on the week, which lowered the cost of holding a metal that pays nothing.

Then came the payrolls. The October payrolls report showed 165,000 jobs added against a consensus that Investrade and Mitrade put at 65,000 and one weekly wrap put at about 59,000. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year, against a forecast of 2.9% annually. September was revised up to 60,000 from 29,000, and August to 186,000 from 133,000.

Rate-hike pricing responded in the usual fashion. Fed funds futures showed about a 75% probability of a December increase on Friday, up from roughly 67% on Thursday evening, according to the weekly wrap published by Daniel San on Substack. The dollar index rose 0.75% to 102.32, per Investrade, its highest close in more than a week.

A stronger dollar and firmer rate expectations are the two things gold analysts reliably list as headwinds, and gold duly gave back $38.60. Silver followed at $51.94, down 83 cents, or 0.83%. The pullback was modest next to the gain, and the weekly rally survived, but the Friday session did establish that the metal's safe-haven status comes with a clause about interest rates that nobody mentions in the marketing.

## What the Fed is saying

The central bank itself did little to cool the hike talk. John Williams, president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, told a conference in Frankfurt on Friday that inflation was "too high for too long," according to Reuters coverage carried by TMGM, and added that returning it to 2% in a timely manner was essential. He did not signal the next move. The Fed held its policy rate at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favour of a hike, according to Newsquawk.

That is the awkward arithmetic for gold holders. The rally owed much to equity anxiety and a dip in yields, both of which are temporary conditions. The thing that would sustain it, a Fed that stops worrying about inflation, is not currently on offer. Gold sits roughly 1.7% below the week's high and about 5% above the October low, which is a respectable place to be and not a particularly stable one.

## Next week's test

The calendar offers an early referendum. US consumer prices for October are due on Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, with consensus at 0.3% for the headline and 0.2% for the core, following a 0.6% headline increase the previous month. Bond markets close on Wednesday for Veterans Day, so any rates reaction has to fit into the remaining sessions.

A soft print would validate the week's trade: yields down, gold up, hike odds trimmed. A hot one would put the dollar and the 10-year back in charge and test whether the 5.2% weekly gain was conviction or merely a hiding place that happened to be nearby. Gold has been described as a hedge against uncertainty. This week it was, at least, a hedge against Thursday.
