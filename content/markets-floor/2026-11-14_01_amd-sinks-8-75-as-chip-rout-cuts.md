# AMD Sinks 8.75% as Chip Rout Cuts Short a Strong Week

Advanced Micro Devices fell 8.75% on Friday to close at $534.06, a loss of $51.24, as an early rally in chip stocks reversed into a sector-wide sell-off, according to The Motley Fool. The S&P 500 slipped 0.40% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.91% to 25,136.27. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.10% to 52,347.55, which was the only thing going up that anyone noticed.

The detail worth sitting with is the week, not the day. According to Eurasia Business News, the S&P 500 finished the week up about 2.51%, the Nasdaq up about 2.27% and the Dow up about 2.07%. All three indexes ended the week higher. Friday was a bad afternoon inside a good week, which is a distinction the afternoon did not appreciate.

## A stock with a very long Friday

AMD traded between $520.01 and $571.14 during the session, against a 52-week range of $188.22 to $658.52, the Fool reported. It opened with gains and gave them all back. Volume reached roughly 48.6 million shares, about twice the average of roughly 24 million, so the exit was crowded.

The stock had earned the attention. Eurasia Business News put AMD's weekly gain at about 18.93%, and the Fool noted that the company reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion last week, with data-center sales nearly doubling from a year earlier. A company that doubles a division and then loses almost nine percent in a session is not contradicting itself. It is demonstrating what a high expectation costs when it meets a Friday.

AMD was not alone. Intel fell 2.52% to $59.66, the Fool reported. Cisco was down roughly 6% by midday, according to the same outlet, after reporting results the evening before. Nebius dropped about 12% after lowering its 2026 revenue outlook, according to TheStreet's market coverage. Nvidia closed about 2% lower at $162.85, according to the Fool, which left it down about 12.75% on the week, per Eurasia Business News. The stock's chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned on Thursday. The company's own announcement is covered elsewhere in this publication, and nobody on the floor needed it explained.

Volume tells the same story as price. A stock that trades at twice its normal pace on the way down has not been abandoned by the few. It has been reconsidered by the many, all within one session, many of them presumably the same people who bought on Monday. Momentum investors describe this as rotation. Everyone else describes it as Friday.

## The part of the screen nobody wanted to look at

Stocks were also working against the bond market. The Fool's midday report had the 10-year Treasury yield reaching about 5.10%, and Eurasia Business News said it moved toward 5.15%. A yield at that level is a standing invitation to ask why anyone would pay a premium for a company's future when the government will pay a high rate for the present.

The inflation backdrop did not help. Producer prices for October, released Friday, rose 0.3% on the month against 0.4% expected, according to Xinhua's account of the Labor Department data, which also put the annual increase at 6.5%. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on CNBC that inflation is rising and that the central bank "has definitely got work to do," as quoted by TheStreet. Markets expect at least a quarter-point rate rise at the Fed's December meeting, with a larger move a minority possibility, according to the same coverage.

Equity investors have therefore spent a week performing a familiar calculation. Cheaper money justified paying a lot for chips. Dearer money does not. Friday gave a one-day preview of the second case, with an AI-chip stock as the volunteer.

## What the tape said

It said very little that was new. The AI trade remains the market's centre of gravity, which is a polite way of saying that when it sneezes, the Nasdaq reaches for a handkerchief. A one-day loss of under one percent for the Nasdaq after a week that added more than two is not a crisis. It is a reminder that the index has a small number of landlords.

Next week brings Nvidia's earnings on Tuesday, which the company said would go ahead as previously scheduled, the first under an interim chief executive. Investors will be listening for guidance on chip demand and, one suspects, for the sound of someone asking who is in charge. The answer, as of Friday's close, is the bond market, which was not available for comment because it never is.
