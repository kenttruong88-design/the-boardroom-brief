# Bitcoin Hits $62,112 as Strategy's Coins Sit Underwater

Bitcoin fell to $62,112 on Sunday, its lowest level since July 14, and was trading around $65,000 on Tuesday, about 48% below its record of $126,080, according to a Blockhead market report citing CoinGecko. Roughly $2 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated in the move, and the Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 10, a reading that describes the mood and, usefully, nothing else.

## A week of exits

Bitcoin is down about 13% over seven days, per Blockhead. The money has been leaving through the front door. Spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds lost about $2.35 billion over two weeks, including $1.07 billion in the week to November 13, according to Farside data cited by GN Crypto. The IBIT fund has now recorded 11 straight sessions of outflows, with $185.4 million leaving on Friday alone.

The plumbing is draining too. Glassnode data cited by Blockhead show stablecoin supply down $14.2 billion over 30 days, the largest monthly decline since June 2022. Stablecoins are the cash that crypto uses to buy crypto, so a shrinking balance is less a signal than a receipt.

Recent buyers are in the red. CryptoQuant estimates their average cost basis at about $80,300, and bitcoin touched $81,105 as recently as November 4. At $65,000, that cohort is down roughly 19%, by our arithmetic. Seven sessions ago the mood was momentum. It is now accounting.

## The treasury company's treasury

The most exposed holder is also the most famous. Strategy, the company formerly known as MicroStrategy, holds 850,849 bitcoin at an average cost of $75,452 per coin, according to Blockhead. At $65,000 each coin is about $10,452 under water, which implies an unrealised loss of roughly $8.9 billion. At Sunday's low of $62,112 the figure was closer to $11.4 billion. Both numbers are our calculations, not company disclosures, and both are the sort of figure that a finance chief prefers to see in a footnote rather than a headline. The company has not, as far as the sources reviewed here show, sold any of its coins, so the loss remains a loss on paper. Paper, as any treasurer will confirm, is the format in which most losses are first discovered.

Strategy's problem is no longer only arithmetic. MSCI will remove the company from its MSCI USA, World and ACWI indexes at the close on November 30, Blockhead reported. Index deletion means that funds tracking those benchmarks must sell the stock regardless of how they feel about bitcoin, which is the purest form of price-insensitive selling.

The equities have already noticed. Schwab said bitcoin futures fell almost 7% in early trading on Monday, with Strategy down 8%, Coinbase down 7.4% and Circle down almost 8%. Those are not quiet moves for companies that describe themselves as infrastructure.

## The rest of the board

Bitcoin was not alone. Blockhead said ether was near $1,830 on Saturday, its lowest since July, and that the Ethereum Foundation sold 7,500 ETH over four days. Spot ether funds lost about $399 million last week, their seventh straight weekly outflow. XRP fell below $1 to $0.89, a two-year low.

The macro setting is unkind. Blockhead noted that the 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.14%, up 28 basis points on the week, while gold fell about 4.6%. CME FedWatch had the odds of a half-point Fed hike in December at 13.6% at one point last week, and a quarter-point move is widely expected. An asset with no cash flow is hard to value when the alternative pays more than 5% for doing nothing.

The calendar offers no obvious relief. Blockhead listed US retail sales on Tuesday morning, Nvidia's results after the close, the Federal Reserve's October meeting minutes on Wednesday afternoon and Anthropic's expected IPO pricing on Wednesday evening, followed by a Nasdaq debut on Thursday. A senior US official also told Reuters that no agreement is expected from the Trump-Xi talks at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Every one of those events can move risk appetite, and bitcoin trades on risk appetite around the clock, including the hours when nobody is at a desk to apologise for it.

Crypto has always argued that it is an alternative to the financial system. This week it has been a remarkably faithful reflection of it, only with more leverage and fewer closing hours.

The reckoning for corporate treasuries is simple to state. A balance sheet strategy that works while the price rises is called a bet, and the day it stops working the market stops calling it a strategy. Strategy's own chart now makes that case more clearly than any analyst could.
