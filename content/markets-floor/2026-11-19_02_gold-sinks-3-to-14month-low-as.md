# Gold Sinks 3% to 14-Month Low as Dollar Hits 19-Month High

Gold fell about 3% on Wednesday to $3,735 an ounce, its lowest in 14 months, as the dollar climbed 0.3% to a 19-month high, according to Reuters' end-of-day market wrap. The euro slipped below $1.10. Bitcoin, the other asset class that markets itself as protection from the monetary system, traded below $63,000.

The day's market narrative was assembled from three inputs. Nvidia's earnings produced the equity story, the minutes of the Federal Reserve's October meeting produced the rates story, and an oil sell-off produced the energy story. Of the three, the rates story did most of the work in the metals pit.

## Hawkish minutes, in plain English

The Fed held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28, but the minutes released Wednesday suggested the committee's mood was less settled than its decision. According to Reuters, the minutes leaned toward tightening, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller said that 3% is not the inflation number to think about going forward. Wall Street responded by pricing out some of the comfort that had accumulated around a pause.

Gold is a peculiar instrument in this environment. It pays no coupon and no dividend, so its appeal is relative: the higher the return on cash and short-dated government paper, the less attractive a bar of metal looks beside them. With the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.08% in the morning session, down from 5.10% on Tuesday according to the Associated Press, the arithmetic of holding a non-yielding asset has become rather more expensive than it was a year ago.

The same logic works in reverse for the dollar. A central bank that signals more rate rises is also signalling a currency worth owning. The greenback's 19-month high is less a vote of confidence in American policy than a vote of confidence in American policy being tighter than everyone else's.

## The yield curve gets involved

Treasuries did not move in parallel. Reuters reported that the 2-year yield rose 1 basis point while the 30-year fell 5 basis points, flattening the curve. A flattening curve in a hiking cycle is the bond market's way of saying that it expects tighter policy today to mean slower growth later. It is a polite way of saying the Fed may be buying inflation control with a cooler economy, a transaction most governments describe as "soft".

Meanwhile, Yahoo Finance's intraday figures had gold down roughly 2.9% at about $3,751 and bitcoin down 0.7% at about $63,299, which suggests the sell-off in both was well underway before the close. There was no single catalyst for the metal, merely a gradual tightening of the case for owning it.

## Energy, deflated

Oil gave back ground as well. Brent crude fell about 1% on the day and U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate fell 3%, according to Reuters, dragging the energy sector down 3.5%. In morning trading the AP noted Brent was down 1.4% at $91.85 a barrel, with ExxonMobil falling 1.7% and Chevron 2.3%. Lower fuel costs should ease the pressure on inflation at the margin, which in a hiking cycle is the kind of news that is received with measured suspicion.

Abroad, London's FTSE 100 fell 0.7%, while South Korea's Kospi rose 2%, the AP said. A strong dollar is a headwind for companies and governments that borrow in it, and the pattern across Asian and European markets reflected the usual divergence between exporters and importers of dollar funding.

Bitcoin's slide below $63,000 completes the picture. Neither gold nor the leading cryptocurrency offers a yield, and both were reminded on the same afternoon that holding something merely scarce is a poor substitute for holding something that pays.

## The trade, such as it is

The unglamorous conclusion is that Wednesday was a rates day wearing a commodities costume. When a central bank signals that tightening is more likely, assets that depend on its patience tend to fall: gold, bitcoin and long-duration technology. Assets that benefit from its resolve, chiefly the dollar, tend to rise.

Investors who bought gold as a hedge against central bank error may reflect that the error being hedged has so far consisted of the central bank not being tough enough. That is a strange thing to insure against, and Wednesday's price suggests the market has begun to notice.
