# S&P 500 Drops 1.9% to 7,416.59 for Third Straight Loss

The S&P 500 fell 145.08 points, or 1.9%, to 7,416.59 on Monday, its third straight loss, as a technology sell-off dragged on the broader market, according to the Associated Press. The Nasdaq composite lost 626.52 points, or 2.5%, to 24,509.76, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gave up 739.38 points, or 1.4%, to 51,608.17.

Smaller companies offered no refuge. The Russell 2000 slid 47.55 points, or 1.7%, to 2,742.94. For the year to date the S&P 500 remains up 8.3%, the Dow up 7.4%, the Nasdaq up 5.5% and the Russell 2000 up 10.5%. The index with the strongest year is, as usual, the one that nobody opens a broadcast with.

## Nvidia, again

Nvidia fell again after Jensen Huang's resignation the previous week, the AP said, and rival chip companies fell with it. The damage was cumulative. On Friday, according to Zacks, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.4%, AMD lost 8.8% and Broadcom lost 4.8%, with Nvidia down 2% after a 4% drop the day before. Zacks also had the S&P 500 closing Friday at 7,561.67, up 2.5% on the week, and the arithmetic checks out: Monday's loss of 145.08 points takes the index to precisely the figure above. By this desk's calculation, the session handed back roughly three quarters of the previous week's gain.

Nvidia was due to report quarterly results after Tuesday's close, which gave the Monday session the character of a collective decision to hold its breath, except that the breath-holding was carried out at a loss.

## The rest of the tape

L3Harris fell after Trump administration officials suggested that the contractor responsible for a piece of the Federal Aviation Administration's systems may be at fault for a nationwide ground stop on Friday, according to the AP. The AP report did not give a finding. The market, characteristically, did not wait for one.

Treasury yields eased after jumping the previous week on inflation worries, and oil prices fell, the AP said; it gave no levels for either. Both are ordinarily the helpful half of an inflation story. The worries have a source: consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, hotter than expected, according to AP reporting on Tuesday, and Zacks reported that producer prices rose 6.5% in the year to October, the largest annual increase since November 2022. A bond market that eases on that evidence is either optimistic or exhausted, and the equity market's reaction suggested it had not been consulted.

Context helps. According to Saxo Bank, the S&P 500 closed at 7,549.94 on 9 November after a 2.4% rally, which puts Monday's close about 1.8% below that level, and the VIX volatility index closed at 19.92 that day, its lowest since the post-election rally. By Friday, Zacks reported, the VIX had risen 3.21% to 19.30, and the market's internal rotation was visible: energy was the only one of the main sectors to gain, up 1.5%, while materials lost 1.3% and communication services lost 1%. Nasdaq closed Friday at 25,136.27, down 0.9%, and Monday took a further 626.52 points off it.

For the week, Zacks reported, the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 had risen 2.3% and 2.5% respectively, snapping a four-week losing streak, and the Dow had gained 2.1%. Chips have been the market's main source of volatility for two weeks. On 9 November, Saxo reported, the Nasdaq 100 rose 3.4% to 27,907.63 even as Nvidia fell 4.3%, and by Friday the semiconductor index was losing 3.4% in a day. The Nasdaq's year-to-date gain of 5.5% now trails the S&P 500's 8.3% and the Dow's 7.4%, which is the order a technology index is not supposed to finish in. As for the Treasury market's relief, the US ten-year yield was about 4.88% on 10 November, according to Saxo, so "eased" is a relative term.

The tech sell-off was the stated cause of Monday's decline, and it has company in the earlier sessions. Three straight losses in the S&P 500 are not, by themselves, a trend, and this desk makes no claim that they are. They are, however, a sequence, and the market has now drawn down from a week it spent recovering from the one before.

Year to date, the S&P 500 is up 8.3%. At 7,416.59 it is also 1.9% lower than where it stood on Friday, a fact that the year-to-date figure is designed to make you forget.
