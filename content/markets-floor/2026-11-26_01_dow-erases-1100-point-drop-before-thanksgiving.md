# Dow Erases 1,100-Point Drop to Close at 50,182.95 Before Thanksgiving

The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed Wednesday up 164.25 points, or 0.33 percent, at 50,182.95, after trading more than 1,100 points lower at its worst, according to Dimsum Daily's account of the session. The S&P 500 slipped 18.31 points, or 0.25 percent, to 7,182.78, its second straight decline. The Nasdaq Composite shed 147.74 points, or 0.62 percent, to 23,585.80. Three benchmarks, three separate opinions, and one public holiday arriving in time to discourage follow-up questions.

## A rescue with very little rescuing in it

The Dow's round trip is the sort of move that looks heroic on a closing table and faintly alarming on an intraday chart. A 1,100-point hole filled by the afternoon is either evidence of dip-buying conviction or evidence that the dip had no coherent reason to be there. Dimsum Daily attributes the morning wobble to semiconductor and AI shares, with investors continuing to scrutinise capital expenditure and profitability across the sector.

Volume, for those who prefer their conviction measured, was thin. JournalArta reported about 3.47 billion shares changing hands, against a 20-day average of 5.54 billion. A market that moves 1,100 points on roughly two thirds of its usual volume has not so much made a decision as filed a placeholder.

Nvidia, the stock that currently functions as the index's mood ring, fell 3.91 percent to $149.30, Dimsum Daily reported, trimmed from heavier early losses. The Nasdaq, with Nvidia's weighting, was described by JournalArta as having recovered well off its session lows without managing to turn positive. Elsewhere, United Airlines lost more than 3 percent to $104.54, while Copart rose more than 2.5 percent to $33.17 and NetApp added more than 1 percent to $165.00. Copart and NetApp both report quarterly earnings on December 1.

## The macro backdrop declined to take the day off

Equities were not the only thing refusing to relax. Dimsum Daily said October core PCE inflation rose to 3.3 percent year on year, above the 3.2 percent consensus, and that futures priced a 98 percent probability of a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December. A rate hike that is 98 percent priced is less a forecast than a calendar entry.

The same report said the Trump administration has proposed 20 percent import tariffs on China, the EU and 12 other major trading partners. These would stack on existing duties and take effect in January at the earliest, after a public comment period. Markets, to their credit, treated a proposal with a comment period as something that could wait until after the turkey.

The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, rose 7.32 percent, or 1.93 points, to 28.29, according to JournalArta. That is 21.18 percent below its one-year peak of 35.89, reached on November 20, which is the kind of comfort one takes from being only moderately on fire. The S&P 500 is down 7.25 percent over the past month and 8.14 percent below its October 6 peak of 7,818.93, so, technically, it remains short of the 10 percent that defines a correction. Technically has carried a great deal of weight this month.

## What the holiday schedule hides

US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday is a half-day session ending at 1:00 pm Eastern. This matters because half-days are when liquidity goes on leave and the few traders still at their desks are asked to price a rate hike, a tariff proposal and an AI spending debate on the strength of a turkey sandwich.

Under the surface, the day had a theme. JournalArta noted investors rotating out of technology and into defensive pockets, with the S&P 500 healthcare sector up 15 percent this year against 5.52 percent for the broader index. Anthropic, it added, reversed higher during the session, which is as much as either source says about it and, frankly, as much as anyone should say about it without a number.

For a desk that had a plan on Monday, the plan is now a rumour. Inflation is running above forecast, the central bank is expected to respond, and the largest growth stocks are being asked to justify their spending. Against that, the market's one demonstrated skill this week was staging a 1,100-point recovery on light volume.

Rex's closing note: a rally that arrives in the last hours before a four-day weekend deserves the same scrutiny as a colleague who suddenly volunteers to take minutes. It may be genuine. It is also very well timed.
