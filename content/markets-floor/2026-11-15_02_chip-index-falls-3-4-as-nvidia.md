# Chip Index Falls 3.4% as Nvidia Turmoil Reaches AMD and Dell

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.4% on Friday, according to CappNotes, as the sell-off that followed Nvidia's chief executive stepping down spread from the company itself to its rivals, its customers and its suppliers. The Nasdaq Composite lost 0.9% to 25,136.27, and technology was the only S&P 500 sector to fall more than 1%, down 2.01%.

Nvidia fell 2.01% on Friday, a day after Jensen Huang's resignation, and finished its sixth straight weekly decline, per CappNotes. Investrade reports new 52-week lows for the shares. TechCrunch said the stock had been down nearly 12% in early trading on Thursday and had at one point fallen as much as 19% since the previous Friday, a loss of more than $855 billion in market value. Al Jazeera put Thursday's intraday drop at more than 14%, before a partial recovery.

## The contagion table

AMD fell 8.75% on Friday, according to CappNotes, which described chip stocks as reversing part of their rally from earlier in the week. Dell dropped 7.18% on concern about its reliance on Nvidia-based AI servers. CoreWeave lost 6.13%, Coherent 6.00% and Nebius 9.92%. Broadcom fell 4.76% after a downgrade to Neutral from Rothschild & Co Redburn, per Investrade, which reports the target was cut to $398 from $595.

Arm fell 4.63% after the Financial Times reported that Nvidia's board had approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas as a possible successor, per CappNotes. Truist downgraded Nvidia to Hold and Morgan Stanley cut its price target to $267, Investrade said.

The pattern is the one analysts describe as correlation and everyone else describes as one big bet. When a single company anchors the AI build-out, its leadership is, in effect, a macro variable.

## How far there was to fall

The starting altitude explains part of the drop. TechCrunch reports that Nvidia's shares reached an all-time high of $243 and a market value of $5.88 trillion in early October. Huang is the company's largest individual shareholder with about 3.5%, TechCrunch said, but holds no supervoting rights. Al Jazeera reports that Nvidia gave no reason for the departure and that the board has launched an independent investigation into reports he was targeted by Chinese intelligence, while saying it has no reason to believe national security was compromised.

China adds a second layer of unease. TechCrunch reports that Chinese customers accounted for about 8% of Nvidia's second-quarter revenue, roughly $7.9 billion, out of total revenue of $96.22 billion. Investors who had priced the company as a growth story are now pricing it as a growth story with a governance footnote, and the footnote has a CEO-sized hole in it.

The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, rose 3.21% to 19.30 on Friday, per CappNotes. That is hardly panic. It is the sound of a market clearing its throat.

## Good results, bad reception

Cisco reported on Thursday after the close. Revenue rose 24% to $18.52 billion and the company raised its full-year outlook, according to Cisco's release as summarised by the Vanderbilt Report. The shares nonetheless fell 6.24% on Friday, with CappNotes noting order growth excluding hyperscalers decelerated. Applied Materials slid 2.74% despite earnings per share of $4.16 against a $4.05 estimate, per CappNotes.

Not everything was sold. Cerebras rose 11.22% after a surprise quarterly profit, with revenue of $231 million against $215 million expected, and Modine jumped 27.86% on a Bloomberg report that Samsung is in talks to acquire it, per CappNotes. Consumer staples gained 1.42%, which in this tape is the equivalent of a quiet room.

## What the calendar says

Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday after the close, according to StockTitan, with consensus earnings per share of $2.49 against $1.24 a year ago. The call will be held under interim chief executive Colette Kress, who remains chief financial officer. Harvey Jones was named the company's first board chair, per Al Jazeera. Investors will be listening less for the number than for the tone of the person delivering it.

Rate expectations are not helping. The ten-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.152%, per Investrade, which is a discount rate that makes long-dated AI cash flows look every bit as long-dated as they are.

For the quarter's most crowded trade, the lesson of the week is plain. The semiconductor index fell more in one session than most diversified funds hope to earn in a month. Concentration was a feature when it went up. It has now published its terms and conditions.
