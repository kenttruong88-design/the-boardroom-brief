# Ten-Year Yield Hits 5.15% as Stocks Gain 2.5% Anyway

The US ten-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.152%, up about 29 basis points on the week, according to Investrade's daily market report. That was the largest weekly rise since April 11, 2025. The S&P 500, which does not appear to have been consulted, rose 2.5% over the same five days.

On Friday itself the S&P 500 fell 0.4% to 7,561.67, according to AP index data carried by La Nación. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.1% to 52,347.55, the only major index to finish higher, per CappNotes. The Nasdaq Composite lost 0.9% to 25,136.27 and the Russell 2000 slipped 0.3% to 2,790.49.

For the week, the S&P gained 2.5%, the Dow 2.1%, the Nasdaq 2.3% and the Russell 2000 1.3%. Investrade notes that the S&P and the Nasdaq both snapped four-week losing streaks, and that all 11 S&P sectors finished the week higher, led by energy. By the end of Friday the S&P was up 10.5% for the year, according to La Nación.

## The bond market files a dissent

The two-year yield ended at 4.872%, up 35.2 basis points on the week, its biggest weekly jump since September 2024, per Investrade. CappNotes puts the 30-year at 5.47%. The Federal Reserve held its policy rate at 3.75%-4.00% on October 28, with two officials voting for a hike, according to StockTitan. The ten-year therefore sits more than a full percentage point above the overnight rate, which is the bond market's polite way of saying it has read ahead.

The proximate causes are not mysterious. October consumer prices came in hotter than expected on Tuesday. On Friday the producer price index showed final demand up 0.3% on the month against a 0.4% consensus, but up 6.5% on the year, the fastest annual pace since November 2022, according to StockTitan's summary of the Bureau of Labor Statistics release. A monthly miss that still produces a 2022-vintage annual number is the sort of good news that comes with a rider.

Then there were the speeches. Governor Christopher Waller said on November 10 that he supports at least a quarter-point hike in December. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said at least two more quarter-point increases are needed, per Investrade. Boston Fed President Susan Collins said on Friday that further tightening is warranted soon, and CappNotes reports that San Francisco's Mary Daly described her bias as toward more tightening. Investrade puts the probability of a 25 basis point hike in December at about 88%, with roughly 12% on a half-point move. Fed officials are rarely this coordinated outside a group chat.

## Everything else, repriced

The dollar index closed at 103.38, the highest since April 2025, and gained about 1% on the week, its best in two months, per Investrade. The euro fell 0.8% against it to about 1.1022.

Gold, which pays no coupon and therefore resents a 5% risk-free alternative, settled December futures at $3,886.30 an ounce. That is roughly 4% lower on the week and a fresh 52-week low, according to Investrade. Bitcoin, no longer marketed as digital gold to anyone with a pulse, fell 4.35% to about $68,237, its first trade below $70,000 since late August and a sixth straight weekly decline, per CappNotes and Investrade.

Oil was the exception that proved the rule. West Texas Intermediate settled at $88.56 and Brent at $95.84, both roughly flat on the day, though energy shares led the week's sector gains. CappNotes reports that Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers had hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

The pairing is instructive. A rising yield and a rising equity index can coexist for a while, provided earnings keep growing faster than the discount rate. They coexist less comfortably once the discount rate is moving at the pace of the past five sessions.

## Why equities were unbothered

They were not entirely. Technology fell 2.01% on Friday, the only sector down more than 1%, per CappNotes, while 314 S&P 500 members rose against 185 that fell. The equal-weight S&P gained 0.2%. The market's weight sits in a handful of names, and Friday's weight was heavy in the wrong direction.

The coming week offers several chances to settle the argument. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks at noon on Monday, retail sales arrive Tuesday, the minutes of the October FOMC meeting land Wednesday, and Walmart reports Thursday, per Kiplinger and CappNotes.

Equities are pricing growth. Bonds are pricing the bill. One of them will be asked to reconcile its figures first, and Treasury desks have a decent record of being right about who.
