# Nvidia Revenue Doubles to $115.8bn, Guides $130bn After Six-Week Rout

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8bn for the quarter ended 25 October, up 103% on a year earlier and 20% on the previous quarter, in its first results since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on 12 November. The company also guided to revenue of $130.0bn, plus or minus 3%, for the current quarter, according to its earnings release on Tuesday.

Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected revenue of about $109.1bn and adjusted earnings per share of $2.47, according to CNBC. Nvidia delivered non-GAAP earnings of $2.68 a share and GAAP earnings of $2.70. The company's own guidance for the quarter had been $108bn, plus or minus 2%, so the result cleared consensus by roughly $6.7bn, or about 6%. A company that beats its own forecast by that margin has either been conservative or has stopped being able to find the ceiling.

## The rest of the ledger

Data Center revenue was $107.6bn, up 21% on the quarter and 110% on the year, which means the segment alone nearly matched the whole company's guidance. Edge computing contributed $8.2bn. GAAP gross margin was 74.8%, against 75.0% in the previous quarter and 73.4% a year ago, and GAAP net income came to $65.3bn.

Nvidia returned $28.1bn to shareholders in the quarter through buybacks and dividends. The board approved a $150bn increase to the repurchase authorisation in September, and about $227.3bn remained at quarter end. The next dividend is $0.25 a share, payable on 23 December. The release also noted that, from the first quarter of this fiscal year, non-GAAP results no longer exclude stock-based compensation, with prior periods restated. It is unusual for a company to make its adjusted number less flattering and then beat it anyway.

## The setup

Expectations were not generous. CNBC reported that the shares had fallen six weeks in a row, the longest losing streak since 2012, taking about $1.7tn off the company's market value. Analytics Insight put the decline at roughly a third from the October peak of $243 to a low of $148, and said options markets had priced a move of about 8.3% in either direction after the results, against an average of 5.7% over the past two years. This desk does not forecast which way the shares move. The figures the company published are not in dispute; what the market does with them is its own affair.

## What the guidance leaves out

Two lines in the outlook deserve attention. Gross margin is guided to between 71.0% and 72.0%, about three points below this quarter's level, and Analytics Insight had said analysts expected margins to dip as memory costs rise. The company also said it is "not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in its outlook." Culper Research, a short seller, has alleged that 20% of fiscal 2026 compute revenue was driven by China, according to CNBC. Nvidia denies any diversion. The $130bn, then, is a number with its most contested customer already subtracted. The outlook also includes an additional week in the quarter, according to the release, which flatters any sequential comparison by roughly one-twelfth of a quarter. GAAP operating expenses are guided to about $11.0bn, with non-GAAP at about $10.7bn, and the full-year tax rate to between 16.0% and 18.0%.

Colette Kress, who is both chief financial officer and interim chief executive, said in the release: "Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people." She added: "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater." The arrangement has a certain elegance. The person who guided the quarter has now presented it, in her second capacity, to herself.

The governance detail is worth recording. Mr Huang resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. Ms Kress, who joined Nvidia in 2013 after about 25 years in finance at Cisco and Microsoft, remains chief financial officer while serving as interim chief executive until a permanent successor is named, and the board is searching internal and external candidates. Harvey Jones, the company's first chair of the board, said in the 12 November announcement that "the Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team." That release gave no reason for Mr Huang's departure. Tuesday's release gave every figure for the quarter. Of the two disclosure philosophies on offer this month, investors will presumably be asked to choose, and the order book suggests which one they find easier to read.
