# VIX Nearly Doubles in a Week to Above 37 as Gold and Bitcoin Fail to Hedge

The Cboe Volatility Index, Wall Street's preferred fear gauge, closed Friday above 37, up from about 20 on Monday, according to Schwab's end-of-day commentary. Reuters said it was the VIX's highest close since April 7, 2025. A volatility index that rises by roughly 85% in five sessions is not so much measuring anxiety as filing a formal complaint.

The VIX is built from the prices of S&P 500 options and expresses how much movement traders are paying to insure against over the coming month. A reading of 20 is a market comfortable enough to complain about other things. A reading above 37 means the insurance premium has become a significant line item. On Friday, the S&P 500 fell 3.70% and the Nasdaq Composite 5.28%, Reuters reported, which explains why the people selling protection suddenly wished to renegotiate.

## The hedges that went with the tide

The more instructive part of the day was what failed to rise. Gold, the traditional refuge of the nervous, fell 4.4% to near $3,500 an ounce, Schwab said. Bitcoin, whose enthusiasts have long described it as digital gold, slipped toward $58,000, per Schwab; Reuters put it below $60,000. Gold and bitcoin had, for once, agreed on something, and it was down.

Banks, which investors tend to own when they wish to look prudent, were also sold. Schwab reported that JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America lost more than 3% and Citigroup 4%. The Philadelphia semiconductor index, meanwhile, trimmed its year-to-date gain to 18.7%, according to Reuters, which is what a year of gains looks like after a bad week.

The market's only hiding place was consumer staples, the sector of the economy that sells soap and cereal. According to Schwab, it was the only S&P 500 sector to gain, and it gained by less than 1%. If a gain of under 1% is the best a defensive sector can offer on a day like this, "defensive" may be a generous job description.

## Why correlation is the enemy

Portfolio construction is premised on assets that do not all fall together. On Friday they largely did. When forced selling, margin calls or simple de-risking dominate, investors sell what they can rather than what they would like to, and winners get sold alongside losers. Reuters reported that volume reached 25.19 billion shares, against a 20-day average of 19.50 billion, and that on the NYSE decliners outnumbered advancers 3.77 to 1. That is the signature of a market selling by category rather than by merit.

Schwab's Kevin Gordon described the setting as a "rock-and-a-hard-place backdrop for stocks," with the hard place being a Federal Reserve expected to raise rates again. Reuters said Fed Governor Lisa Cook indicated that the policy rate is not high enough, and futures priced a near-certain 25 basis point hike in December, with Schwab citing odds of 92% on the CME's FedWatch tool. The usual instrument for calming a panic, a central bank that signals it will be gentle, is currently being used the other way.

## The fine print on fear

It is worth being careful about what a VIX spike means. It measures expected volatility, not direction, and it has a long record of mean reversion once the immediate scare passes. A reading in the high 30s has historically been associated with stress, not with permanent regime change, though the April 2025 comparison is a reminder that stress can last several sessions. Markets which have closed lower seven times in a row, as the S&P 500 has, do not always rebound on the eighth.

Equally, a doubling in implied volatility makes everything more expensive to protect, including the positions that were supposed to be cheap insurance. Investors who bought protection early look prescient. Those who waited until Friday paid the price of waiting, which is the only price that has ever been stable in this business.

The honest summary is that nothing broke on Friday that was not already fragile. A crowded trade in artificial intelligence, a Fed expected to raise rates and a set of safe havens that turned out to be positively correlated with the thing they were meant to hedge together made a day that looked like a crisis and behaved like arithmetic. The VIX will eventually fall. The question that matters for next week is whether the hedges will be there when it does not.
