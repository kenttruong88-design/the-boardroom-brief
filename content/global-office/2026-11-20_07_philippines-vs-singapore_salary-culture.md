# The Philippines Legislates the Thirteenth Month and Spends It by Christmas; Singapore Leaves It to Your Contract

🇵🇭 Philippines · 🇸🇬 Singapore

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

In the Philippines, the thirteenth month of salary is mandatory under Presidential Decree 851, due by 24 December and, in the observation of one Manila Bulletin article, widely treated as the largest cash payment of the year. In Singapore, the equivalent payment is not required by law at all: the Ministry of Manpower merely encourages it, and whether it appears depends on the contract. One country made the bonus a right and the other made it a negotiating term, and the distinction matters a good deal more to the person receiving the offer than to the person writing it.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In the Philippines

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect the thirteenth-month pay by 24 December, calculated as a year's basic salary divided by 12 | Assume the Christmas bonus is owed; it is not required by law |
| Remember that overtime, holiday pay and night differentials are excluded from the calculation | Add them to your expectations |
| Know that up to PHP 90,000 of thirteenth-month pay and other bonuses is tax-exempt | Forget to check the cap |
| Budget for the fact that many colleagues have already committed the bonus | Plan your own finances around a number you have not received |
| Check whether you are rank-and-file, since that is who the decree covers | Assume it applies to managers in the same way |
| Keep an eye on the minimum wage orders in Metro Manila | Assume the legal minimum is what most employers pay |

### In Singapore

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check whether the "13th month" is a guaranteed Annual Wage Supplement in the contract | Treat an unwritten bonus as salary |
| Compare take-home pay with the 20 percent employee CPF contribution in mind | Compare gross figures across countries |
| Ask whether the bonus depends on company performance or personal rating | Assume it is automatic after a year |
| Consider a higher monthly gross with no 13th month, if the offer allows | Assume more instalments means more money |
| Read the local contract carefully, since expat packages with housing and medical allowances are less common | Expect an allowance package by default |
| Get bonus terms in writing | Rely on a verbal assurance, since the Ministry will not enforce an unwritten bonus |

## Philippines

Philippine pay is structured around a statutory floor and a customary celebration. ASEAN Briefing explains that the thirteenth-month pay under Presidential Decree 851 covers private-sector rank-and-file employees who have worked at least one month in the year, is calculated as total basic salary for the year divided by 12, prorated for part-year workers, and excludes overtime, holiday pay, night differentials and non-regular bonuses. A Christmas bonus is not required by law, though many employers give one. Up to PHP 90,000 of thirteenth-month pay and other bonuses is tax-exempt.

Minimum pay is moving. Philstar reports that the Metro Manila daily minimum wage for non-agricultural workers rises from P695 to P755 on 25 July 2026, with a further P25 taking it to P780 on 20 January 2027, which the Department of Labor and Employment says benefits about 1.1 million workers and calls the largest hike in the region to date. A Khaleej Times headline reports that Filipino workers call the increase inadequate as living costs surge, a view that the grocery bill is not known to have disputed.

The culture around the payment is as informative as the rule. The Manila Bulletin notes that many Filipinos treat the thirteenth month as the largest cash payment of the year and describes an office chant demanding the Christmas bonus. The Cebu Daily News reports that Bicol University professors regard Christmas-bonus jokes as social commentary, that many workers have already committed the bonus to basic needs and debts, and that the jokes build solidarity and form a kind of inversion by the less powerful. A bonus that is spent before it arrives is not a bonus. It is a repayment schedule with decorations.

## Singapore

Singapore has no legal minimum wage and no mandatory thirteenth month. According to the Ministry of Manpower, the Annual Wage Supplement depends on the contract or collective agreement and is merely encouraged, and bonuses generally are not compulsory unless the contract provides for them. The median gross monthly income for full-time resident employees, including employer CPF contributions, was S$5,775 in 2025, up from S$5,500 in 2024, an increase of about 5 percent in nominal terms and 4.3 percent in real terms.

The structure behind that figure is the Central Provident Fund. From 1 January 2026, according to the CPF Board, employees aged 55 and under earning above S$750 a month have contributions of 17 percent from the employer and 20 percent from the employee, a total of 37 percent. The employee's 20 percent comes out of the pay before it reaches the account, so a comparison of gross salaries between the two countries is a comparison of different things.

On the forums, the realities are plain. A British Expats forum thread has a job-seeker offered 13 instalments, the last labelled AWS, asking whether it was on top of base pay; one reply said an AWS is a bonus and not salary, that the employer decides whether to pay it, and that you cannot complain to the Ministry if it is not paid. The newcomer should read an offer of thirteen months as an offer of twelve, plus a possibility.

The practical consequence of these arrangements is that a straight comparison of headline pay can mislead in either direction. The Singapore median of S$5,775 a month includes employer CPF contributions, so it is not what a worker takes home, and the Philippine minimum-wage figures describe a floor in a single region and not a national average. The Blind replier's advice to prefer a higher monthly gross with no thirteenth month, if the choice is offered, reflects an understanding of how bonuses behave: a guaranteed amount is worth more than a promised one. In Manila the same logic runs the other way, because the statute makes the thirteenth month more reliable than most discretionary pay, and the sensible worker treats it as a fixed part of the year's income and not a windfall. That is the lesson of the Christmas-bonus jokes: the money is real, and so is the list of things it is already committed to.

## The Reckoning

Hofstede Insights scores, recalled from the published dataset, put the Philippines at a power distance of about 94 and Singapore at 74, with Singapore scoring much lower on uncertainty avoidance, at about 8 against 44. In practice, the Philippines uses the law to guarantee what the hierarchy might otherwise withhold, and Singapore uses the contract to specify what a low-uncertainty market is content to leave to negotiation.

The head-to-head result is that the Philippines gives employees a statutory certainty at a low base, and Singapore gives a higher base with a contractual uncertainty on top. A worker in Manila knows that December will bring a payment. A worker in Singapore knows that it will bring one only if the contract says so, and that the contract was written by someone else.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Blind — A replier on a Singapore thread said that "up to three months" of bonus is real mainly at small and medium firms, government-linked companies and non-FAANG employers, which often pay a lower base plus a thirteenth month after one year, with the bonus depending on the company's performance rating. The advice was to prefer a higher monthly gross with no thirteenth month, ideally with stock awards.*

> *British Expats forum — A job-seeker offered 13 instalments with the last labelled AWS asked whether it was on top of base pay. A reply said it is a bonus and not salary, the employer decides whether to pay it and the Ministry will not intervene if it is not paid. Another noted that expat packages with housing and medical allowances are now often replaced by local contracts.*

> *Manila Bulletin — An article on common mistakes said many Filipinos treat the thirteenth month as the largest cash payment of the year, described an office chant for the Christmas bonus, and drew its savings advice from the deposit insurer and not from workers.*

> *Cebu Daily News — Bicol University professors described Christmas-bonus jokes as social commentary: many workers have already committed the bonus to needs and debts, and the jokes build solidarity among the less powerful.*

## Conclusion

If you value a legal guarantee and can live with a lower base, the Philippines offers a thirteenth month that is not up to anyone's goodwill. If you value a higher base and a market that rewards negotiation, Singapore offers more and guarantees less, and the difference is yours to write into the contract.

In both countries the practical advice is to ask what is guaranteed and what is hoped for. In Manila, check what the decree covers and whether the Christmas bonus is customary or promised. In Singapore, check whether the extra month is called a supplement, a bonus or a wish, and whether it is written. The number at the top of the offer is a headline. The terms below it are the salary.
