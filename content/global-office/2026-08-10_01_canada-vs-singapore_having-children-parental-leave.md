# Two Ways to Raise a Kid on a Government Subsidy: Canada vs. Singapore

**🇨🇦 Canada · 🇸🇬 Singapore**

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

A Canadian father can now take up to 69 weeks of shared parental leave, paid at a slow drip of 33 percent. A Singaporean father gets four weeks, mandatory, at full pay, and is expected to be back at his desk in time for the next Primary One registration cycle. Neither country is being unreasonable by its own logic — they have simply built two entirely different machines for producing the next generation of taxpayers, and a new arrival who assumes "parental leave" means the same thing in both countries is in for a scheduling surprise.

### Do's & Don'ts

#### 🇨🇦 Canada

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Register for daycare the moment the pregnancy is confirmed — some centres have two-year waitlists | Assume $10-a-day child care is available where you live; it's a patchwork rolling out province by province |
| Compare EI's "standard" (12 months, 55% pay) versus "extended" (18 months, 33% pay) parental benefit options carefully — you can't switch once you start | Expect your employer to top up EI benefits unless it's stated in your contract; many don't |
| Budget for a waitlist deposit at private daycares, even if regulations in your province limit it | Wait until month eight to start touring daycares — spots for infants are the scarcest |
| Ask HR whether your employer offers a "top-up" plan to bring EI's 55% closer to full salary | Assume grandparents can casually help with childcare visas — cross-border family support has its own immigration rules |
| Use the Canada Child Benefit calculator before budgeting — it varies by income and province | Forget that "parental leave" and "maternity leave" are separate, stackable EI benefits in Canada |

#### 🇸🇬 Singapore

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Apply for Government-Paid Paternity Leave the moment the child is born — it's a use-it-or-lose-it four weeks | Expect Shared Parental Leave to be a full 10 weeks yet — that only applies to children born from April 2026 |
| Research the P1 (Primary One) registration phases years in advance if you want a shot at a popular school | Assume enrichment classes are optional if you want your child to feel unremarkable among local peers |
| Check whether your child qualifies for citizen-linked subsidies — permanent residents and foreigners get considerably less | Underestimate the cost of infant care centres; subsidies for non-citizens are thin |
| Budget separately for tuition — private tuition spending nationally topped S$1.8 billion in 2023 | Assume your employer will be sympathetic to taking the full 6 days of paid childcare leave without justification |
| Ask about the CPF (Central Provident Fund) implications for take-home pay when negotiating salary as a new parent | Plan on flexible, ad hoc school pickup — Singapore's system runs on precision timing |

### Canada

Canada's parental leave system runs through Employment Insurance, not the employer, which means generosity is capped by a formula rather than a boss's mood. According to Canada.ca, birth mothers can claim up to 15 weeks of maternity EI, then either parent can add standard parental benefits (40 weeks shared, paid at 55% of average earnings up to roughly $729 a week in 2026) or extended parental benefits (69 weeks shared, paid at 33%). It is generous in duration and stingy in dollar amount — a structure that rewards households that can absorb a temporary pay cut and quietly disadvantages those that can't.

The bigger story for new parents, though, is child care. Ottawa's $10-a-Day Early Learning and Child Care initiative has been the headline policy for several years, but its rollout is uneven: Ontario, for instance, was still averaging $19 a day as of 2025 according to CBC News, with provincial officials citing insufficient federal funding to close the gap. Add to that daycare waitlist fees that in some provinces have run parents into the thousands of dollars before a spot even opens, and Canada's promise of near-free child care looks less like a finished product and more like a construction site with a very optimistic sign out front.

### Singapore

Singapore takes the opposite approach: leave entitlements are shorter but mandated, funded by the government, and reimbursed directly to employers — a system built for compliance rather than flexibility. As of April 2025, the Ministry of Manpower confirmed 16 weeks of paid maternity leave and four weeks of Government-Paid Paternity Leave, both fully mandatory for eligible fathers. A new Shared Parental Leave scheme adds a pool that both parents divide between them — six weeks for children born through March 2026, rising to a full ten weeks for children born after that. Each parent also gets six days of paid childcare leave a year for children under seven.

What the leave calendar doesn't capture is what happens after the leave ends. Singapore's education system is intensely, structurally competitive, and parents who arrive expecting a relaxed Southeast Asian pace discover that "kiasu" culture — the fear of losing out — begins in preschool. National tuition spending crossed S$1.8 billion in 2023, and Primary One registration, which determines school placement years in advance based on proximity and parent alumni status, is treated by many households as a multi-year strategic campaign rather than a form to fill out.

### The Reckoning

The irony is that Canada's system optimizes for the newborn phase and underdelivers on the school-age phase, while Singapore does precisely the reverse: less time bonding with an infant, but an education and childcare infrastructure engineered with government-grade precision, provided your child holds citizenship. A Canadian father gets more weeks off than almost anyone in the OECD; a Singaporean father gets a fraction of that but knows exactly what his co-pay will be and exactly when the government check clears, because Singapore reimburses the employer directly rather than leaving benefit levels to an insurance formula.

The other divergence is who bears the risk. In Canada, EI is a shared national insurance pool — the risk of an inadequate benefit is socialized, but so is the frustration when provincial daycare policy lags federal promises. In Singapore, the state's generosity is explicitly conditional on citizenship status, meaning two colleagues in the same office, one a citizen and one a permanent resident, can have a genuinely different parental leave experience for the same pregnancy.

### The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Blind (teamblind.com) — An engineer who transferred internally from Canada to Singapore said the leave policy was almost beside the point; what caught them off guard was being told, within weeks of arrival, to start thinking about Primary One school zones for a child who was still in diapers.*

> *Quora — Someone who had lived in both countries described Singapore as excellent for raising children under ten — safe, clean, orderly — but said the calculus changes sharply once a child approaches secondary school and the exam-driven system starts shaping every weekend.*

> *Blind (teamblind.com) — A parent asking whether to raise kids in Canada or Singapore was warned by several replies that Canadian schooling is comparatively low-key and non-competitive, which some framed as a relief and others as a worry that their child would be underprepared for a more competitive global job market later.*

> *Quora — One respondent flagged that Singapore's reputation for being "stressful" didn't match their own experience, crediting the country's efficiency and low corruption for actually reducing the daily friction of parenting compared to what they'd dealt with elsewhere.*

> *News-sourced parent account (via CBC/Malay Mail reporting) — Parents in both countries independently voiced the same complaint through different mechanisms: Canadian parents describing daycare waitlist deposits running into the thousands with no guaranteed spot, and Singaporean parents describing tuition bills that begin before a child can read, both amounting to the same lesson — budget for child care as a second rent payment, not a line item.*

---

### Conclusion

If you're optimizing for the first year of a child's life, Canada's leave calendar is hard to beat, even if the $10-a-day promise hasn't caught up to itself in every province. If you're optimizing for a decade-long runway of structured, well-funded education — and you or your child can access citizen-tier benefits — Singapore's precision is its own kind of relief, provided you're prepared to start planning school placement absurdly early. Talk to your HR team about top-ups either way, because neither government's baseline is designed to replace a full salary. The honest version I'd tell a friend: pick Canada if you want time with the baby, pick Singapore if you want a system that runs on time — just don't expect either one to give you both.
