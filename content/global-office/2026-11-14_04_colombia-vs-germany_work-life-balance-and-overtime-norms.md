# Germany Counts Every Working Hour, Colombia Is Still Working Out Who Pays for Them

🇨🇴 Colombia · 🇩🇪 Germany

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

The OECD figures tabulated for 2025 put German workers at 1,332 hours a year, while the ILO-based figure cited in the Colombian press puts the average Colombian at more than 44 hours a week. The two numbers are measured differently and should not be subtracted from one another, but the gap is not subtle. On 15 July 2026 Colombia's legal working week fell to 42 hours. Germany's highest labour court had already told employers to record every hour in 2022. One country is cutting the clock; the other is checking it.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In Colombia

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check whether your contract classes you as dirección, confianza y manejo, since those roles are excluded from the maximum working day and overtime pay | Assume the 42-hour week protects a senior hire |
| Get overtime authorised in advance, in writing; it must be voluntary and capped at 2 hours a day and 12 a week | Accept an unscheduled 6:30pm mandatory training as unpaid goodwill |
| Know that night work starts at 7pm and carries a 35 per cent surcharge | Assume your evening shift is paid at the ordinary rate |
| Check Sunday and holiday pay: the surcharge is 90 per cent from July 2026 and 100 per cent from July 2027 | Quote last year's 80 per cent in a salary negotiation |
| Join the almuerzo, which often runs 60 to 90 minutes and is a social obligation | Publicly challenge a superior in a meeting |
| Check whether a service contract (prestación de servicios) leaves you outside the working-day rules | Treat a service contract as an employment contract |

### In Germany

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Record your hours in whatever system your employer provides, since recording is a legal duty | Assume trust-based working time means nobody is counting |
| Ask in writing whether overtime is paid or banked as time off, because the contract or collective agreement decides | Assume overtime is paid because it would be elsewhere |
| Take the 30-minute break after six hours and 45 after nine; breaks cannot be skipped | Eat lunch at your keyboard as a point of pride |
| Respect the 11-hour rest period between working days | Send the late-evening email and expect it to be admired |
| Use your four weeks of paid leave, at minimum | Work on a Sunday expecting a bonus; none is legally required |
| Raise problems with the works council, then the authorities | Assume enforcement is automatic |

## Colombia

Law 2101 of 2021 has reduced the working week in stages from 48 hours; since 15 July 2026 the statutory ceiling is 42. According to Infobae and GeoVictoria, overtime may not exceed two hours a day or 12 a week, daytime overtime carries a 25 per cent surcharge and night overtime 75 per cent. Night work now begins at 7pm rather than 9pm and attracts a 35 per cent surcharge on the ordinary hour. Work on rest days and holidays pays a 90 per cent surcharge from July 2026, rising to 100 per cent in July 2027, and salaries cannot fall to offset the shorter week. GeoVictoria adds that inspectors may suspend an employer's right to use overtime for up to six months where recording or payment fails, which suggests the state has noticed the problem.

Practice is less tidy. La República reported in 2023 that, according to Deputy Minister Iván Jaramillo, only 10.7 per cent of those working more than 48 hours a week said they had received paid overtime, and that half of workers log at least 48 hours. The Ministry of Labour also holds, as El País Cali reports, that workers in management, trust and handling roles are excluded from the maximum working day, and so from overtime pay however long they work. Job titles do not decide the question; the functions performed do. People on service contracts fall outside the rules as well. For a foreign hire on a senior title, the clause matters more than the statute.

The culture around the hours is formal and relational. ColombiaOne describes formal-sector days in Bogotá and Medellín running from around 8am to 5 or 6pm with a long lunch that is a social obligation, and a hierarchy in which publicly challenging a superior is a serious breach of etiquette. Vanguardia, citing ILO-based figures, placed Colombia at 44.3 hours a week against 36 in Panama. The law is converging on a shorter week. The calendar is converging more slowly.

## Germany

The OECD data as tabulated show 1,332 annual hours per worker in Germany, a per-worker average that necessarily includes part-timers. The law is exact. According to IamExpat, employees generally may not work more than eight hours a day, extendable to ten if the six-month average stays at eight, with 11 hours of rest between days, unpaid breaks of 30 minutes after six hours and 45 after nine, and at least four weeks of paid leave. Sunday and holiday work is generally prohibited outside exempt sectors. A coalition plan to move from a daily to a weekly cap was described as a plan, not law, so check its current status.

Then there is the clock. In September 2022 the Federal Labour Court (case 1 ABR 22/21) held that employers must record working time, as the law firm WFW reported, while observing that trust-based working time remains possible. Colombia's problem is proving that overtime happened and was paid for. Germany has decided, at least in principle, to make the proof routine.

None of this means nobody works late. A survey commissioned by Novotel and reported by The Local in 2022, covering about 5,000 adults in four countries, found Germans averaged almost five hours of overtime a week, the most of any country surveyed, even though they finished work earliest, at 5:36pm. Forty-six per cent emailed outside office hours and 27 per cent had taken sick leave because of stress. The sponsor is a hotel chain, so season accordingly. An older DGB study, cited by The Local in 2016, found about a third of full-time workers regularly worked overtime, while an economist at the IW institute attributed heavy overtime in some sectors to poor staff planning rather than a national habit. The same study reported that the average employee worked 46.7 hours of overtime in 2014, roughly one extra hour a week, and that 57 per cent of those working more than 45 hours a week often lacked time for family and friends. Overtime in Germany is modest on average and unevenly distributed, which is how it usually goes.

## The Reckoning

The central contrast is between a country that has the rule and a country that is acquiring the rule. Germany combines a firm daily limit, mandated recording and a short annual-hours figure with a measurable overtime habit. Colombia has a new 42-hour ceiling, detailed surcharges and a ministry estimate that paid overtime is the exception for long-hours workers. The enforcement is where the countries diverge, and the exclusions in the Colombian statute are the larger loophole.

Hofstede Insights scores Germany at 35 for power distance, 67 for individualism and 65 for uncertainty avoidance, and Colombia at 67, 13 and 80. A rule-minded, individualist culture tends to produce rules people invoke; a hierarchical, collectivist one tends to produce arrangements people observe. This is a generalisation, and national scores are blunt instruments: a German employee who objects cites the Arbeitszeitgesetz, while a Colombian one may weigh what the objection will do to the relationship. The expat forums below are anecdote, not evidence.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Expat.com, Colombia forum — A long-term expat teacher said many Colombians work half-days on Saturdays and do unpaid overtime, that the benefits look favourable on paper, and that most locals earn near minimum wage and are expected to accept whatever is asked of them.*

> *Expat.com, Colombia forum — Another member claimed, without evidence, that many employers try to avoid labour law and that those facing charges can improperly influence judges; this is one user's allegation, not an established fact.*

> *HackerNews — A 2019 commenter said sixty-hour weeks and unpaid overtime are common in tech in places including Germany, and that the career only makes sense if you can save enough to leave within about ten years.*

> *Expat.com, Stuttgart forum — A member whose colleague worked about 50 hours a week with five-minute breaks was told the weekly limit is 48 hours, but other contributors said enforcement is unreliable and leaving a bad employer is often the practical remedy.*

> *Reddit r/germany — A worker whose contract folded four hours of overtime into a 40-hour minimum-wage salary was told by commenters it was illegal, because the hourly rate for the 44 actual hours fell below minimum wage; the worker planned to raise it with HR.*

## Conclusion

Move to Germany for the rules and Colombia for the relationships, but read the contract in either. In Germany, expect your hours to be recorded, your breaks to be mandatory and your overtime to depend on the wording of your agreement. In Colombia, find out whether your role is classified as management, trust and handling or a service contract before you rely on the 42-hour week, and make sure any overtime is authorised in advance. Track your own hours in both countries, because a record is the one asset that travels. Ask also about the commute and the after-hours inbox: The Local's survey found 43 per cent of Germans used commuting time for work, so the statutory limits do not always reach the phone in your pocket.

The forums suggest a tidy symmetry. In Germany you may be asked to stay late and discover that your contract, not your goodwill, decides whether you must. In Colombia the late hours may be generously surcharged, unless your title says you were never owed them.
