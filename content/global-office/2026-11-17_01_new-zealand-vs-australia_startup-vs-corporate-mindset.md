# New Zealand Will Let You Fail Quietly; Australia Will Let You Fail With a Quarterly Review

🇳🇿 New Zealand · 🇦🇺 Australia

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

Two countries share a time zone's worth of ocean, a sporting grudge and an accent that neither will admit the other has. They also share a belief that the right amount of ambition is "some, but not so much that people notice". Where they diverge is in what happens next: Australia built an ecosystem for people who want to scale a company, while New Zealand built one for people who would rather have their evenings. If you are deciding where to take a startup job or a corporate one, the Tasman Sea turns out to be wider than the map suggests.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In New Zealand

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Be prepared to wear several hats; flat organisations mean fewer senior titles and broader jobs | Expect a rigid ladder with a title for every rung |
| Look for generalist startup roles where you can own a whole problem | Assume the startup scene is as deep as Sydney's |
| Understate your results and let colleagues do the praising | Boast about your last employer or your valuation |
| Finish the workday and actually leave; the culture respects it | Treat a 6 p.m. email as a loyalty test |
| Check consulting firms as well as startups; they employ much of the local tech talent | Dismiss the big consultancies as the boring option |
| Ask about the pay band openly, but gently | Compare New Zealand to Australia in front of Kiwis |

### In Australia

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask a corporate employer about flexibility and leadership transparency, not only salary | Judge a job on its salary alone |
| Be direct about what you want from a role; the business style is blunter | Wrap every request in three layers of politeness |
| Use Sydney or Melbourne for venture-backed startup ambitions | Expect Auckland-level quiet in a funded scale-up |
| Budget for Sydney rent, roughly AUD 2,500 a month for a one-bedroom | Assume the higher salary nets out higher |
| Ask whether overtime is paid at large corporates | Assume an unpaid late night is a personal quirk rather than policy |
| Support colleagues visibly; helpfulness is noticed | Boast; tall poppies are cut down here too |

## New Zealand

According to a Great Place To Work survey of more than 156,000 employees across the two countries, reported in April 2025, New Zealand workplaces scored better on 48 of 60 questions, and Kiwi staff were five percent more likely to describe their management as honest and ethical. Remote's Global Life-Work Balance Index for 2025 ranked New Zealand first in the world, with a score of 86.87, which is the sort of result a country achieves by quietly shutting the laptop. OECD figures put the average Kiwi worker at about 1,723 hours a year, so the quiet laptop-shutting is relative, but the mood is plain.

For a startup hire, the implications are mixed. Startup Genome's 2025 report lists Auckland among the top five ecosystems in Oceania but gives it no global rank, while Sydney sits at 25th in the world and Melbourne at 32nd. A Wellington engineer who wrote about his culture shock in 2018 described a scene dominated by consulting firms that absorb the talent, with startups present but few. That picture will have shifted since, though not, one suspects, dramatically. The tradeoff is a flatter structure in which a junior person is handed real responsibility early, which a migration-advice site that has lived in both countries describes as fewer senior roles and more generalist work.

Hofstede's scores, as reproduced by Cyborlink, put New Zealand's power distance at 22 against Australia's 36, which is about as formal as saying you sometimes call the boss "mate" and sometimes "boss". In practice this means that the founder will take your call and the corporate vice president will, too, which is lovely until you want to be promoted over somebody's head. Founders also face tidy numbers: a goods and services tax registration threshold of NZD 60,000, a 28 percent corporate rate, and an entrepreneur work visa that requires NZD 100,000, according to TechRound.

## Australia

Australia is the louder, larger and richer neighbour, and its corporate sector behaves accordingly. The same Great Place To Work data found that in Australian workplaces only 55 percent of employees think pay is fair and 51 percent consider their workplace psychologically and emotionally healthy, while 58 percent say redundancies are a last resort. These are not catastrophic figures. They do suggest that the Australian big-company experience is more transactional than the Kiwi one, and the rebuttal, which is that you are paid more, has some truth in it. The migration site mentioned above estimates Australian pay at roughly 10 to 30 percent higher in IT, health and engineering, and notes that big-corporate roles more often carry unpaid overtime. OECD figures show Australians working about 1,633 hours a year, fewer than Kiwis, which is a reminder that hours and pay are not the same thing.

For founders, Australia offers scale. Sydney tops the Oceania ranking on funding and talent in Startup Genome's 2025 report, and Melbourne's venture funding rose 14 percent, according to the same source. A Singapore-born engineer who spent a year at an Australian company found lean teams with wide process freedom, separate engineering and management career tracks, and colleagues who help even when it costs them their own time. The price is that decisions take longer, because more people weigh in, and that you will be asked to have an opinion. TechRound lists Australia's GST registration threshold at AUD 75,000 and corporate tax at 25 to 30 percent, which is roughly what you would expect a larger market to charge for admission.

The mindset is individualist in a way that New Zealand's is not. The same scores give Australia 90 on individualism against New Zealand's 79, and the practical effect is that Australians will tell you what they want, tell you what they think of your plan and expect you to do the same. A founder in Sydney commented that business there is blunter and faster, while Kiwi business is flat but slower and more indirect.

## The Reckoning

Put the two side by side and the trade is clear enough to fit on a napkin. New Zealand offers the flatter organisation, the better work-life balance and the more honest-sounding management, in exchange for a smaller ecosystem, a thinner senior ladder and a salary that will not impress anyone in Sydney. Australia offers scale, funding and pay, in exchange for a more transactional corporate culture, blunter manners and, at the large end, more unpaid overtime. The corporate reader should look at the Great Place To Work figures and ask what an extra ten or twenty percent in salary is worth when only half of colleagues think pay is fair. The startup reader should look at Startup Genome and ask whether they want a very good lifestyle with a small cluster, or a larger cluster with a more demanding one.

Both countries share one inconvenient trait, which is the tall poppy. A 2016 founder opinion piece in SmartCompany called it "by far the largest impediment" to venture-backed startup success in both countries, and no relocation package has yet solved it. Boast in Auckland and you will be quietly corrected; boast in Sydney and you will be loudly corrected, and then asked to buy a round. In both places, the winning move is to deliver results, keep your announcements short and let someone else say how well it went.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Reddit (via an NZ Herald roundup) — a UK-born commenter in New Zealand says jobs there pay well, hand out real responsibility and run sane hours, with no expectation of overtime and a few extra public holidays. They were comparing New Zealand with the UK, not Australia.*

> *Expat.com forum — members discussing New Zealand workplace conduct say frustration at work usually comes from poor pay and tolerated bullying, and one advises that bystanders should report harassment so that silence does not become the culture. The thread says little about hours.*

> *DEV Community — a Singapore-born engineer, a year into an Australian job, finds lean teams, plenty of freedom over process, and colleagues who will help even at a cost to their own time. Decisions take longer, because more people have a say.*

> *Personal blog — a software engineer who moved from the United States says Wellington tech is dominated by consulting firms that soak up talent, with few startups, while Melbourne made contract work easy to find and let people try a startup without worrying about the rent.*

> *Startup Daily — a founder advises that business in New Zealand is flat but slower and more indirect than Australia's blunter style, and notes that Kiwis are productive in the day and then stop working.*

## Conclusion

If you want to build something that might become large, go to Sydney or Melbourne, and accept that you are buying funding and scale with hours, directness and a corporate culture that rates itself a little lower on fairness. If you want a startup that stays small and a life that stays yours, Auckland or Wellington will give you broad responsibility, honest managers and an evening. Check the specific employer in either country, because a good Australian company is better than a mediocre Kiwi one and the surveys describe averages, not offers.

Whichever you choose, do not tell a Kiwi that Australia has better weather, a Sydneysider that Auckland is the nicer city or anyone that you are a visionary. All three statements are accepted as true only when somebody else makes them.
