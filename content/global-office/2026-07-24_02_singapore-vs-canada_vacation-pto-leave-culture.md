# Fourteen Days, Barely Used: What Vacation Actually Looks Like in Singapore and Canada

**🇸🇬 Singapore · 🇨🇦 Canada**

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

Canadians get fewer statutory vacation days than almost anyone in the OECD, and use nearly all of them. Singaporeans often get twice as many contractual days as their statutory minimum, and routinely leave a third of them on the table. The lesson for anyone relocating: the number printed in your offer letter tells you almost nothing about how much time off you will actually take.

### Do's & Don'ts

#### 🇸🇬 Singapore

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check your contract, not just the Employment Act — statutory minimum is only 7–14 days, but most MNCs offer 14–21 | Assume the statutory 7-day minimum reflects market practice — it's a floor, not a norm |
| Book leave during the June and September school holiday windows early — everyone else is trying to as well | Expect colleagues to be fully offline during their leave — many stay reachable by habit |
| Ask HR specifically whether unused statutory leave carries over (it must, by law, for 12 months) | Assume all contractual leave (above the statutory minimum) carries over the same way |
| Use annual leave for actual travel — Singapore's flight connectivity makes short regional trips easy | Feel obligated to justify why you're taking leave — it's a legal entitlement, not a favour |
| Expect long hours to be culturally normalised, even where leave policy is generous on paper | Mistake a generous leave allowance for the company's expectation to actually use it |

#### 🇨🇦 Canada

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect only 2 weeks statutory after 1 year federally — confirm your province's minimum, since they vary | Assume Canada matches European vacation norms — it doesn't; it's among the lowest in the OECD |
| Actually take your allotted days — Canadians use nearly all of theirs (Expedia data shows just 1 day left unused out of 19 offered) | Feel you need to apologise for using vacation — it's culturally normal to fully take it |
| Plan ahead for stat holidays, which vary by province and add meaningfully to total time off | Assume the same leave policy applies coast to coast — Saskatchewan and Quebec differ from Ontario |
| Negotiate for 3 weeks from day one if you're coming from a market with a stronger baseline | Expect employers to offer more than the statutory minimum by default — many don't |
| Budget for the fact that paid sick leave is patchy outside Quebec and federally regulated sectors | Assume sick days and vacation days are pooled the same way they might be at home |

### Singapore: Generous on Paper, Unused in Practice

Singapore's Employment Act sets a statutory floor of 7 days of paid annual leave in year one, rising by a day each year to a maximum of 14 from year eight. In practice, most white-collar employers land well above that: 14 to 21 days is standard, with 18 to 21 common in finance, tech, and professional services, according to multiple Singapore employment law guides including Leave Balance and Asanify's 2026 policy briefings.

What the entitlement number hides is usage. Singapore has ranked among the most "vacation deprived" workforces globally for five consecutive years in Expedia's annual survey, with 57% of Singaporean workers failing to use all their allotted leave in 2023, and 77% saying they'd accept a pay cut for an extra day off. The InterNations Expat Insider survey placed Singapore 49th of 65 countries ranked for work-life balance among expats — behind its regional reputation would suggest — even though the average full-time expat surveyed worked 44.3 hours a week, not dramatically more than global norms. The gap isn't hours worked; it's a culture where taking the leave you're owed still reads, to many employees, as a minor risk.

### Canada: Fewer Days, Fully Spent

Canada sits at the opposite end of the OECD leave-generosity table. Under the federal Canada Labour Code, employees get two weeks of vacation after one year, rising to three weeks after five years and four after ten — provinces set their own minimums, and most track closely to the federal standard, with a handful, like Saskatchewan, doing modestly better. At 10 mandatory annual leave days federally, Canada offers one of the lowest statutory minimums among wealthy economies, well below the European norm and below even the more conservative end of Asia-Pacific practice.

The compensating factor is usage. Expedia's data shows Canadian workers leaving behind, on average, just one day out of 19 offered — near-total utilisation. Vacation guilt exists everywhere, and Canadian commentary acknowledges it, but the behavioural pattern doesn't match Singapore's: Canadians tend to treat the days they do get as non-negotiable personal time, not a discretionary extra.

### The Reckoning

The paradox here is direct: more contractual days does not predict more actual rest. Singapore's employers frequently offer double Canada's statutory minimum, and Singaporean employees still take less of what's available, while Canadians, working with roughly half the entitlement, burn through almost all of it. Hofstede Insights data is instructive here — Singapore scores strongly collectivist (around 20 on Individualism) against Canada's much higher individualism score, and the leave gap tracks that divide reasonably well. In a collectivist, high-context workplace, being visibly present can carry more social weight than a written leave policy suggests it should; in a more individualist one, using your entitled time off is simply understood as your own business.

The other divergence worth flagging: Canada's leave culture is genuinely fragmented by province, in a way Singapore's single national Employment Act is not. Moving from Ontario to Quebec is not the same leave regime, whereas moving within Singapore obviously involves no such variation.

### The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *r/PersonalFinanceCanada — A poster who'd moved from a US employer to a Canadian one said the two-week statutory minimum felt like a downgrade on paper, but the surprise was cultural: managers actively encouraged using all of it, and colleagues openly discussed vacation plans without the performative reluctance they'd seen at their old US job.*

> *r/singapore — In a thread reacting to the annual vacation deprivation survey results, several commenters said the issue wasn't leave approval — managers rarely refused a request — but an unwritten expectation to stay reachable on Slack or email even while officially on leave, which made the time off feel incomplete.*

> *Quora — A respondent describing Singaporean work culture said long hours are normalised to the point that the office functions as a "second home" for many employees, and that family time and health often lose out even when formal leave policy looks competitive on paper.*

> *teamblind.com — An employee discussing culture at a Singapore tech office noted that leave requests were never denied outright, but that a associate who took two consecutive weeks off drew visible surprise from colleagues, since the norm was closer to single days or long weekends.*

> *InterNations — Expat Insider survey respondents based in Singapore reported comparatively low satisfaction with work-life balance relative to other expat destinations, despite average working hours (44.3 a week) that were not dramatically higher than the global expat average — suggesting the dissatisfaction tracks culture more than raw hours.*

### Conclusion

If you're negotiating a move to Singapore, treat the leave number in your contract as a ceiling you'll need active discipline to actually use, not a benefit that delivers itself. If you're moving to Canada, expect a smaller number that functions, culturally, as a floor everyone is expected to fully claim — and don't assume the provinces are interchangeable when you're comparing offers. The honest version I'd give a friend: in Canada, taking your vacation is unremarkable; in Singapore, it can quietly become the most political two weeks of your year.
