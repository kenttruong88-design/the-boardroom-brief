# Five Days of Leave, Fully Paid, Eventually: The Philippines and the United Kingdom Meet Their Calendars

🇵🇭 Philippines · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

A British employee is legally owed up to 28 paid days off a year from the first day of the job. A Filipino employee is legally owed five, after a year of service has been completed, and only if the employer has not already been more generous. Between these two arrangements lies the entire question of what "leave" means, and it is wider than the numbers suggest. The first country treats time off as a statutory baseline that the employer may decorate; the second treats it as a benefit that the employer may, with some prompting, decide to provide. Anyone weighing a move between them should understand that the arithmetic is the easy part.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In the Philippines

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask for the company leave policy in writing; many employers offer more than the legal five days | Assume the statutory minimum applies from day one, because it does not |
| Check whether your sick days draw from the same leave pool as your vacation | Count on separate paid sick leave being guaranteed by law |
| Plan around the 12 regular holidays and 6 special days in the national calendar | Schedule major travel without checking which special days are non-working |
| Find out how unused leave is treated at year-end and on resignation | Let leave lapse without asking whether it converts to cash |
| Negotiate leave days at the offer stage, when you have the most leverage | Wait until after the probation period to discover the policy |
| Be clear about whether you will be reachable while away | Assume a manager will treat your out-of-office reply as final |

### In the United Kingdom

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check whether your contract counts bank holidays inside the 28-day statutory allowance | Assume bank holidays are always on top of your annual leave |
| Book leave early, since popular summer weeks fill quickly | Present a holiday request as a favour; it is an entitlement |
| Ask how part-time entitlement is calculated, which is pro rata in weeks | Expect 28 days if you work three days a week; the figure is 16.8 days |
| Take your leave in blocks, because colleagues do | Treat a full fortnight away as a sign of disloyalty |
| Confirm the carry-over and payout rules in your contract | Leave a large unused balance to the final month of the leave year |
| Ask whether your employer offers more than the statutory minimum | Treat the statutory figure as the ceiling |

## The Philippines

The Labor Code is stingy by design. According to WageIndicator's summary of Philippine labour law, workers become entitled to five fully paid days of Service Incentive Leave only after one year of service, the law adds no days for tenure, and unused days can be converted to cash at year-end. Employers with fewer than ten employees, along with managerial and field staff, are among those excluded. The calendar adds 12 regular holidays and six special days, though the source confirms regular wages only for the regular holidays.

Comparative studies are unkind. A Moorepay study of 187 countries, reported by When In Manila in August 2025, put the Philippines near the bottom for statutory paid annual leave at five days, and counted 16 days of total paid leave once public holidays were included. The same article cited Remote.com's Global Life-Work Balance Index, which ranked the Philippines 59th of 60 countries, with a 40.63-hour average working week. A country can post such results and still be a pleasant place to work, but the figures do explain why a prospective hire is well advised to read the leave line of an offer before the salary line.

The legal protections that do exist are narrower than they look. Commentary from the Manila firm NDV Law notes that the five days apply only to employees with at least a year of service, that employees who already receive five or more paid vacation days from the employer cannot claim them twice, and that establishments with fewer than ten workers are exempt altogether. On the useful side, unused Service Incentive Leave is generally convertible to cash on separation, and the employer, who holds the records, carries the burden of proving that leave was granted, used or paid. In other words, the system is lenient to the keen employee who keeps receipts.

The practice is more forgiving than the statute. A discussion on Forum Expat Management notes that many employers offer 12 to 15 days by contract or policy, and that this is so common that people assume the legal minimum is higher than it is. The same discussion points out that there is no mandated paid sick leave, so employees frequently spend vacation days on being ill. Taking the days is another matter. A Regus survey of more than 26,000 business people in 96 countries, reported by the Inquirer without a date, found that about 72 percent of Filipino respondents expected to work during holidays, 52 percent for one to three hours a day and 20 percent for more than three. Hofstede's published scores explain some of this: the Philippines scores 94 on power distance and 32 on individualism, which describes a workplace where the manager's convenience is rarely ranked below the employee's holiday.

## The United Kingdom

The British position is simple and, by global standards, comfortable. According to GOV.UK, almost all workers are entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid holiday a year, which is 28 days for a five-day week, and the statutory figure is capped at 28 days. Part-time workers receive the same 5.6 weeks pro rata, so someone working three days a week gets 16.8 days. Bank holidays need not be paid as extra leave, and employers may count them inside the allowance. Many firms offer more, and the contractual figure is usually the one that matters.

The culture matches the law more closely than might be expected. A YouGov survey of 22 countries, reported by HRreview in 2015 and therefore not new, found a median of 27 holiday days for British workers against a 21-day average across the countries surveyed, and 75 percent of Britons said they intended to take all of it. The age of the data is worth noting; the pattern it describes has not, as far as this desk can tell, been repealed. Hofstede's scores put the United Kingdom at 35 on power distance and 89 on individualism, which is another way of saying that a request for time off is treated as a request from an adult and not a petition to a superior.

None of this makes the system painless. Part-time arithmetic surprises newcomers, the bank holiday question depends entirely on the contract, and the leave year has its own deadlines. Mumsnet posters comparing their lot with American employers describe 25 days, sometimes with extra days purchased, as ordinary. For the Filipino professional arriving to work in Britain, the adjustment is less about the number than about the permission: the days are simply there, and asking for them is administrative, not political. The GOV.UK guidance also notes that employers may offer more than the statutory minimum, and that different rules can apply to the extra days, so the contract deserves a careful read even in the more generous country.

## The Reckoning

On paper, the United Kingdom wins by a factor of more than five on statutory leave, and the gap on the first day of work is infinite, since Filipino employees have no legal entitlement until the first year is complete. The Philippines narrows the distance through employer generosity of 12 to 15 days, but it remains a market where the contract matters more than the law, where sick leave and vacation share a pool, and where a survey finds most respondents planning to work on holiday. The United Kingdom has the opposite problem. The law is generous enough that the contract is usually a refinement.

The gap in culture is at least as large. In Britain, leave is something one uses; in the Philippines, it is something one negotiates, protects, and occasionally converts to cash. Anyone moving from Manila to Manchester will gain, depending on the old employer, somewhere between 13 and 23 additional days of annual leave, and will lose the instinct to defend each day as if it were under audit. The reverse move requires a more serious recalculation. A person leaving the United Kingdom for the Philippines should treat the offer letter as the primary legal document and read the leave clause twice.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Mumsnet — UK posters comparing notes on American leave describe 25 days a year as a normal baseline, and one regularly buys five more. They are openly baffled that anyone manages a long trip abroad on 14 days.*

> *BritishExpats forum — Britons in the US report that starting allowances are often two weeks, that sick time is frequently folded into the same pool, and that a low offer can sometimes be negotiated up. One poster who had lived in the UK turned down a 10-day offer and got 15.*

> *Tinig UK — Filipino nurses working in the NHS say they receive more annual leave and more days off than they did at home, which they use to spend time with relatives, although some take extra bank shifts to send money back to the Philippines.*

> *Forum Expat Management — Contributors say many Philippine employers give 12 to 15 days despite the five-day statutory floor, that new hires are owed none in their first year, and that illness often comes out of vacation days.*

> *Expat.com magazine — Expats from generous-leave countries often hesitate to use their full allowance in an always-on culture, worrying that colleagues will see it as laziness even where the law is on their side.*

## Conclusion

For a Filipino worker, the United Kingdom is a straightforward upgrade in leave, provided the contract is read for bank holiday treatment and part-time arithmetic. For a Briton heading the other way, the legal minimum is a floor under a floor, and the real number is whatever the employer has agreed to in writing. Negotiate it before accepting, not after.

Before moving, ask for the leave policy, the sick leave policy, and the year-end rules, in that order. In both countries the days exist; in only one of them does anyone expect you to be unreachable while using them, and it is not the one with the better weather.
