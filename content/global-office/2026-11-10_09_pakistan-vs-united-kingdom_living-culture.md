# Pakistan Rents a Flat for £112 a Month; Britain Charges £1,021, and Somehow Wins

🇵🇰 Pakistan · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

According to Numbeo, a one-bedroom flat in a Pakistani city centre costs about £112 a month on the national average, against roughly £1,021 in Britain, and a month of private preschool costs £40 against £1,382. On paper, the case for staying home is overwhelming. The same Numbeo comparison, however, puts local purchasing power in Britain 414% higher, which is the sort of footnote that explains why people keep paying ten times the rent. Cheap is a property of prices. Affordable is a property of prices divided by income, and the two rarely agree on which country is the bargain.

## Pakistan: Do's and Don'ts
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Budget in rupees and track the monthly inflation print; it was 9.61% in October 2026 | Convert every price into pounds and conclude that life is nearly free |
| Check whether your employer offers social security or private health cover before you choose a neighbourhood | Assume a government insurance card covers outpatient visits; it mostly covers hospital stays |
| Ask any landlord what the electricity bill looks like in July, and whether there is solar or backup power | Sign a lease on the strength of a rent figure alone |
| Test the internet connection before committing, if your job runs on UK hours | Rely on a single connection for client calls |
| Hire a lawyer and a competent builder before buying land or constructing a house | Treat property purchase as a form of tourism |
| Compare areas by rent: central Lahore and DHA are different markets entirely | Quote one "Lahore rent" to anyone, including yourself |

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |

## Pakistan: A Bargain With Terms and Conditions

The headline prices are real. Numbeo records a city-centre one-bedroom at about Rs41,800 a month and an inexpensive restaurant meal at Rs600. The exceptions are expensive in their own right: Profit, a Pakistan Today publication, reported in July that average monthly rents in Lahore reached about Rs281,000 in DHA and above Rs600,000 in Gulberg, while a two-marla house in older central neighbourhoods rents for Rs25,000 to Rs35,000. Which Lahore you rent decides which country you are living in. Inflation, meanwhile, fell to 9.61% in October according to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, as reported by Minute Mirror, after two months in double digits. A falling rate is still a rate.

Welfare and insurance are where the bargain acquires terms and conditions. The World Bank says the Benazir Income Support Programme has delivered cash transfers to 4.6 million women heads of household, reaching about 23 million family members. That is a serious safety net for the poor, and it is not designed with a relocating professional in mind. The same brief records rural poverty at 30.7% against 12.5% in cities, which is a reminder that the average Pakistani household and the household you are likely to join live in different statistical countries. Health is the sharper edge. The Commonwealth Fund reports that out-of-pocket payments make up about 47.4% of total health spending, against 35.5% for other lower-middle-income countries, and that only about 1.3% of health spending is prepaid private insurance. Employment-linked cover exists in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, funded by employer contributions of 6% and 7% of wages, which is why the contract matters more than the clinic. The government-subsidised Sehat Sahulat programme covers most eligible families for up to PKR 460,000, but mainly for inpatient care, and Punjab suspended it in public hospitals in July 2025. In a study of 400 households cited by the same profile, only 11.8% had used the card, with low awareness and too few empanelled hospitals blamed. An insurance scheme that most people cannot find a hospital to use is, strictly, a pamphlet.

Childcare is the happy exception. Numbeo puts a month of private full-day preschool at Rs14,999, about £40. The practical consequence is that a household with a foreign-currency salary can buy private schooling, private hospitals and private everything, and treat the public system as a spectator sport. The practical consequence for a household on a rupee salary is that private providers receive most of the money, and the Commonwealth Fund notes they absorb roughly 83% of out-of-pocket spending.

## United Kingdom: The Cost of Having a Safety Net

Britain sells security at a London price. The Office for National Statistics reported an average private rent of £1,406 in the twelve months to September 2026, rising 3.8% a year, with London at £2,338. Add council tax: the Government's figures put the average Band D bill in England at £2,392 for 2026-27, up 4.9%. Motor insurance, at least, has stopped climbing; the Association of British Insurers recorded an average premium of £560 in the first quarter of 2026, £20 lower than a year earlier. Repair costs, it noted, are rising anyway. Energy is the quieter bill: Expatica puts the average household at about £748 a year for electricity and £682 for gas, before standing charges, and expects both to rise again.

Childcare is where the sums turn personal. Coram's 2026 survey says eligible working parents of children from nine months to school age can claim 30 hours a week for 38 weeks a year, and that a part-time under-two place is now theoretically free for eligible families in term time. Families who do not qualify pay an average of £189 a week for a part-time place for an under-two in England. Numbeo's £1,382 monthly preschool figure is therefore best read as the price of being the wrong sort of eligible. The welfare state, meanwhile, has a door and a doorbell. According to Home Office guidance on GOV.UK, a migrant under the no-recourse condition cannot claim Universal Credit, Housing Benefit or Child Benefit. The NHS remains the redeeming exception. Care is free at the point of use, though Expatica notes that most prescriptions carry a charge, and an immigration law firm lists the health surcharge at £1,035 per adult for each year of leave, payable upfront with the visa. The surcharge is, in effect, an insurance premium that the brochure calls a fee, and which it collects before you have been ill.

## The Reckoning

The honest comparison is between two different bargains. Pakistan offers low prices, private services and thin public protection, so the household pays for its own safety net by the hour, out of pocket. Britain offers high prices, a funded health system and a contingent welfare state, so the household pays in advance, through rent, tax and surcharges. Hofstede Insights scores, as published, put Pakistan at 14 for individualism against 89 for the United Kingdom, and 55 for power distance against 35. In practice this means Pakistani families substitute for the state, while British ones outsource to it, or try to.

Which is cheaper depends on the currency of your salary and the age of your children. Earn in pounds or dollars and Pakistan's arithmetic is hard to argue with, provided you accept the inflation, the electricity bills and the self-funded hospital. Earn in rupees and Britain's wage premium, 414% on Numbeo's measure of local purchasing power, buys a steadier floor at the cost of a higher ceiling on everything else.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Reddit (r/pakistan) — paraphrased experience: A woman who spent three years in England working at an airport shared rented rooms with other women, then went home when a visa renewal became unaffordable. Commenters from the UK described a punishing cycle of work, sleep and rent, with a few saying a salary well above £35,000 is needed to live decently.*

> *Expat.com — paraphrased experience: A poster in Pakistan estimated a three-bedroom rental at around 100 US dollars a month in cheaper areas and 300 to 500 dollars in places like Defence. Another warned that electricity, gas and petrol prices move unpredictably and that unreliable internet would sit badly with UK working hours.*

> *Marshmallow — paraphrased experience: A Pakistani teacher arriving in Britain found that letting agents wanted proof of rental history he did not have, and eventually paid six months of rent upfront. He was quoted about £1,400 a year for car insurance and collected several parking fines in his first six months.*

> *Hacker News — paraphrased experience: One commenter said London tech salaries mostly flow to landlords, while another moved to Leeds, kept a London job remotely, and swapped a cramped £2,000 to £3,000 rental for a large house on an £1,800 monthly mortgage.*

> *Reddit (r/Scotland) — paraphrased experience: Parents said nursery fees were the reason they stopped at two children. One estimated more than £2,500 a month for two children at three and a half days each, and noted that funded hours cover barely two days at a private nursery.*

## Conclusion

If you are weighing the two on living costs alone, run both calculations: one in the currency you will be paid in, and one with a realistic bill for health care and childcare in the country where your children will actually be. Pakistan rewards foreign-currency income and punishes a household that falls ill without a plan. Britain rewards patience with paperwork and punishes anyone who confuses "free at the point of use" with "free."

Take the cheaper rent if you can also afford the private hospital, and the dearer one if you value the idea that the hospital is not a separate line item. Either way, read the lease, the insurance quote and the eligibility form before the removal van arrives. The removal van, unlike the paperwork, will not be sorry.
