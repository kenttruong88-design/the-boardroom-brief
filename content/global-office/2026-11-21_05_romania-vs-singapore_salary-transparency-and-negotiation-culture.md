# Romania Is Preparing to Print the Salary in the Advert; Singapore Prefers You Guess, Politely

🇷🇴 Romania · 🇸🇬 Singapore

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

Romania has drafted, and its Senate has begun debating, a law that would require employers to state pay in the job advertisement or before the first interview, and forbid asking candidates what they earn now. Singapore has no such law, a median wage that preliminary Ministry of Manpower figures put at S$5,775 in 2025, and a recruiting culture in which, Robert Half observes, talent managers often open at the lower end of the range and expect to be countered. One country is learning to publish. The other learned to haggle long ago and sees no reason to stop.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In Romania

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Quote expectations as a net monthly figure, which an Expat.com poster says is the local custom | Quote gross annual pay in the UK style and wait for the translation |
| Expect the contract to state pay in lei even if the negotiation happened in euros | Assume the euro figure discussed is the one on the contract |
| Ask for the pay range before the interview; the draft law would require it | Volunteer your current salary; the draft would bar employers from asking |
| Check sector benchmarks before naming a number; the 2026 barometer's median net for IT is 11,750 lei | Ask far above market, which the same poster says can get an application filtered out early |
| Expect an offer below your ask; candidates averaged 7,000 lei net against offers of 5,400 | Treat the first offer as final |
| Ask what bonuses and benefits sit outside base pay; the draft requires a summary of them | Expect finance, IT, marketing or consultancy employers to publish pay without being asked |

### In Singapore

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Name a specific figure; Robert Half cites a study finding that precise asks beat rounded ones | Offer a round number and a smile |
| Expect an opening offer at the lower end of the range and counter it | Accept the first figure to appear grateful |
| Ask in writing whether the 13th-month payment is contractual | Assume it exists; 62 percent of respondents in one poll said they do not receive one |
| Calculate take-home pay after CPF and tax, as JobStreet advises | Compare gross figures across offers |
| Negotiate leave and benefits when base pay is fixed; statutory leave starts at 7 days | Assume leave is generous by default |
| Read the confidentiality clause before discussing pay with colleagues | Assume the topic is welcome; nearly half of respondents in a 2019 survey, per NodeFlair, called it inappropriate |

## Romania

The European Union's Pay Transparency Directive had a transposition deadline of 7 June 2026. WTW's summary of the Romanian draft says employers would have to disclose the initial pay level or range in the posting or in writing before interviews, and could not ask about current salary or salary history. Employees could request their individual pay level within 30 working days. Companies with 250 or more staff would report gender pay gaps annually from 7 June 2027, and those with fewer than 100 would report voluntarily. Forvis Mazars adds that fines run from three to five gross national minimum wages. DLA Piper reported that a bill was registered in the Senate on 17 June 2026 and sent through the emergency procedure; whether it has since been adopted could not be confirmed.

Practice trails the paperwork. An eJobs survey of 198 employers, reported by Romania-Insider in October 2025, found that 62 percent were not yet prepared to publish pay in advertisements. By late June 2026, eJobs data reported by Stiripesurse showed about 40 percent of vacancies carrying salary information, an increase of no more than 5 percent since the deadline. Financial services, IT, marketing and consultancy were the reluctant sectors. Advertisements with pay reportedly draw about 40 percent more applications, which is the sort of finding that persuades an employer faster than a directive.

The draft also reaches inside the pay packet. DLA Piper notes that candidates must receive a summary of complementary or variable pay, such as bonuses and benefits, separate from core salary, and that fines are now tied to the national minimum gross salary so they rise with it. Unjustified gender gaps must be remedied within 90 working days, and gaps of 5 percent or more in a worker category that are not corrected trigger a joint pay assessment. Meanwhile the figures eJobs collected for the first weeks of disclosure, drawn from salary data by Salario, show how wide the range is: net averages of about 4,500 lei in services and tourism, 5,000 in transport, 5,900 in consultancy and 8,000 in IT.

The gap that matters to a newcomer is between asking and offering. The Romania Salary Barometer 2026, reported by Undelucram, found candidates asking about 30 percent more than employers offer: 7,000 lei net against 5,400. Bucharest's average net wage was 46 percent above the national figure. Hourly labour cost, at 13.6 euros, is about 39 percent of the EU average, and only Bulgaria's is lower. Eurostat estimates the gender pay gap at 3.8 percent, one of the EU's lowest, though Forvis Mazars notes that individual private-sector gaps can be larger.

## Singapore

Singapore has no pay transparency statute. TAFEP, the body that administers fair employment guidance, says the Workplace Fairness Act is expected to take effect by the end of 2027, and its recruitment guidance asks employers to base offers on clear and consistent frameworks. It says nothing, in the pages consulted, about publishing pay. The market therefore settles the matter, through negotiation that Robert Half describes as expected, courteous and specific. JobStreet adds that the package, not the base, is the unit of account: leave, medical benefits, bonuses and flexible hours are all open.

The package varies more than the base does. A Ministry of Manpower survey from 2023, cited by MustShareNews, found about 65 percent of firms include an annual variable component, yet 62 percent of respondents in an Answers.sg poll said they get no 13th-month payment. Mercer's 2023 survey projected a median 2024 increment of 4.2 percent, below an Asian average of 5.2 percent. Statutory annual leave, JobStreet notes, begins at seven days and rises to a maximum of 14. A salary that looks high should be read alongside a leave policy that does not.

The macro numbers are comfortable. The Star, reporting preliminary Ministry of Manpower data, said the median wage rose 5 percent in nominal terms, from S$5,500 in 2024 to S$5,775 in 2025, and that real median income rose 4.3 percent, with permanent employment at a record share of over 90 percent of workers. Mercer drew its projections from 1,106 organisations across 18 industries. Robert Half's advice for the individual is to justify a counteroffer with past examples of value added, and JobStreet's is to ask for a total compensation statement, request time to review the offer, and get every agreed term in the written letter. The system is transparent about its etiquette, if not about its numbers.

Talking about pay is another matter. NodeFlair, a salary platform, states that employers cannot legally forbid employees from discussing pay, though some contracts include clauses that try, and that nearly half of Singaporeans surveyed in 2019 considered it inappropriate. Treat that as the publisher's claim rather than legal advice. The practical point stands: the information that Romania will soon supply by statute, Singapore supplies by recruiter, rumour and a carefully worded question.

## The Reckoning

The contrast is between a country that will mandate disclosure and one that has made bargaining a professional skill. Romania offers its candidate a legal floor of information, eventually, and a market in which the employer still offers about 23 percent less than the candidate asks. Singapore offers none of the information and a culture in which asking is expected. The candidate who does the research does well in both; the one who relies on the system does well in neither.

There is a wage-level story underneath. Romania's cost advantage is narrowing: the barometer reports hourly labour cost rising 10.6 percent in a year, and that in 2025 nominal wages rose 4.8 percent while inflation of 9.7 percent cut real wages by about 4.5 percent. Singapore's real median income, by contrast, rose. A Romanian candidate has reason to negotiate hard because the ground is moving; a Singaporean one has reason to negotiate precisely because it is not.

Hofstede Insights, as reproduced in university course materials, scores Romania at 90 for power distance and 90 for uncertainty avoidance, and Singapore at 74 and 8. Both defer to hierarchy; they differ in their appetite for rules. That is a fair summary of the situation: Romania legislates transparency because Brussels has told it to and the bureaucracy likes a form, while Singapore tolerates ambiguity and expects the individual to resolve it with a number.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Expat.com (Romania) — An established poster advised an IT architect applicant that Romanian salaries are quoted net per month, that contracts are written in lei although deals are often struck in euros, that employers usually offer less than requested, and that asking far above market can get an application filtered out early.*

> *Expat.com (Bucharest) — A US senior developer with more than twenty years of experience deliberately set his pay requirements to local rates and still got one interview in several weeks; a resident of Brasov explained that employers must first show they could not find a Romanian, which lengthens hiring (the thread is several years old).*

> *ExpatExchange — A German applicant for a Bucharest IT support job reported that the company phoned within two days to ask his salary expectations, that he was unprepared, and that he wanted to know how much he could ask for without being ripped off.*

> *Reddit r/askSingapore (via MustShareNews) — A job seeker with an offer in hand asked how to ask for slightly more. Replies ranged from stating a number and declining to justify it, to asking directly because the worst outcome is a no, with one adding that a very fast offer suggests the employer either likes you or is desperate.*

> *Reddit (via The Independent Singapore) — An employee asked whether colleagues share increments and bonuses. Many said they do, despite HR discouraging it, to learn whether they are underpaid; others share only percentages, and one regretted sharing after lower-paid colleagues grew resentful.*

## Conclusion

In Romania, learn the sector benchmark, quote a net monthly figure, expect a counteroffer below your ask, and ask for the pay range before the interview, because the law may soon require it. Verify the status of the legislation at the time you apply; the sources available to this column stopped in June. In Singapore, name a precise number, expect to be opened low, and negotiate the package and the 13th month in writing before you sign.

Romania is moving from not telling you what the job pays to being required to. Singapore will not be required to for some time, and will meanwhile accept your counteroffer with perfect courtesy and a small, unreadable pause.
