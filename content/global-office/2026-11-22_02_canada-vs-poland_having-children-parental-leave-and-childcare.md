# Poland Gives Mothers 61 Weeks, Canada 50, and Both Keep a Waiting List

🇨🇦 Canada · 🇵🇱 Poland

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

On paper, Poland is the more generous host: 20 weeks of maternity leave followed by 41 weeks of parental leave, according to the Polish government, against a Canadian standard of 15 weeks of maternity and up to 35 of parental leave, paid at 55 percent of insured earnings and capped at 729 Canadian dollars a week. On paper, Canada is also the country that has promised parents child care at ten dollars a day. In practice, the leave ends, the nursery place does not exist, and the family discovers that the two countries have been solving the wrong half of the same problem.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In Canada

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check early whether you are in Quebec, where a separate parental insurance plan pays up to 70 or 75 percent for most weeks | Assume the federal Employment Insurance figures apply in every province |
| Confirm you have 600 insurable hours in the last 52 weeks before you plan a pregnancy around a new job | Assume a recent arrival or a new contract qualifies automatically |
| Choose between standard leave (55 percent) and extended leave (33 percent) with a spreadsheet | Elect extended leave without checking that your household can live on a third of your earnings |
| Ask your employer in writing whether it tops up the federal benefit | Assume the benefit is the whole of what you will receive |
| Join daycare waitlists while pregnant, as one Toronto family joined about forty | Wait until the leave is nearly over to begin the search |
| Keep a financial buffer, because a layoff during leave can interrupt benefits | Assume severance will leave your EI claim untouched |

### In Poland

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Apply within 21 days of the birth if you want the 81.5 percent averaged rate on parental leave | Miss the window, after which the rate defaults to 70 percent |
| Plan around the nine weeks of parental leave reserved for each parent | Count on transferring the reserved weeks to the mother |
| Check your contract type, since those on a civil-law contract get an allowance only with voluntary sickness insurance and no leave right | Assume every form of work in Poland carries the same protections |
| Apply early for a nursery place and for the monthly fee subsidy | Assume the local commune must provide a place, because the law does not guarantee one |
| Choose a midwife who speaks English and keep your own pregnancy records | Expect a fixed schedule of prenatal appointments |
| Remember that you are not obliged to tell an employer about a pregnancy at interview | Be surprised if you are asked about children anyway |

## Canada

Canada's leave system is long, flexible and thin. According to the federal government, maternity benefit is paid for 15 weeks at 55 percent of insurable earnings, and standard parental benefit runs for up to 40 weeks at the same rate, with one parent limited to 35. The extended option stretches to 69 weeks, but at 33 percent and a weekly maximum of 437 dollars. Quebec runs its own plan, which, according to a Childcare Resource and Research Unit summary, pays 70 percent for most of the maternity and paternity weeks and reserves five weeks for fathers. Statistics Canada reported in July 2026 that about 91 percent of mothers in EI coverage regions received parental benefits, roughly 70 percent on the standard option and 21 percent on the extended one, while only about 30 percent of the spouses of standard-option mothers claimed or expected to claim, three quarters of them for five weeks or less.

The career consequences are real, and mostly legal. CBC reported that parents can lose their jobs during parental leave if the dismissal is unrelated to the leave, which an HR expert called common but not illegal, and that a laid-off parent cannot stack unemployment and parental benefits. One employment lawyer said leave uses up the parent's EI allocation and falls disproportionately on women. The benefit is generous in duration and austere in rate, which suits a country that regards both austerity and generosity as forms of politeness.

Then there is child care. The Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives found in August 2026 that median infant fees were 9.65 dollars a day in Quebec's major cities, 10 dollars in cities such as Winnipeg and Saskatoon, 22 dollars in every Ontario city studied and between 28 and 52 dollars in British Columbia. Only 11 of the 35 cities studied were at or below ten dollars. The Canadian Press reported in September that Ontario had created just under 60,000 of the 86,000 spaces promised under the federal agreement, while the province's financial accountability officer put the need nearer 220,000. Cheap care that is not available is, in the economist's phrase, a rumour.

## Poland

Poland's leave system is shorter in its rate of generosity and longer in its duration of payment. The government lists 20 weeks of maternity leave, 41 weeks of parental leave for one child and two weeks of paternity leave. According to Boundless, maternity leave is paid at 100 percent of the benefit base through the social insurance institution ZUS, parental leave at 70 percent by default, or 81.5 percent for parents who apply within 21 days of the birth, and paternity leave at 100 percent. Dismissal of a pregnant employee or one on maternity leave is generally prohibited. A King's College London review notes that only about 1 percent of fathers used the reserved parental leave before the 2023 reform, rising to about 24 percent in 2025, and that 67 percent used paternity leave in 2023.

The state does not, however, guarantee anywhere to put the child afterwards. Eurydice, the European Commission's education network, reports that municipalities have a statutory task to care for under-threes but no obligation to guarantee a place. Participation was 25.4 percent in 2023, with 226,405 places, and about 31 percent of communes reported a need for roughly 56,000 more. Monthly attendance fees mostly ranged from 350 to 1,500 zlotys, with meals adding at least about 210, against a 400-zloty subsidy. The result is that Polish families bridge the gap with the 36-month childcare leave the government also lists, which protects the job and does not pay for the nursery.

The workplace remains an uneven place for mothers. An SWPS University article notes that questions about family situation and plans to have children are common in Polish interviews, usually directed at women, and that candidates are not obliged to answer them. Notes from Poland reported in April 2026 that four rights organisations had raised concerns over the dismissal of a senior official who had given birth and was on parental leave, a case the government denies is a firing. The law is firm, the practice is variable, and the employee is advised to keep her paperwork.

## The Reckoning

Compared directly, Poland pays more of your salary for more weeks, and Canada pays less of it for a choice of how long to stay home. The Polish mother on a 70 or 81.5 percent rate for 41 weeks, after 20 weeks at 100 percent, is better compensated than the Canadian mother on 55 percent of earnings capped at 729 dollars. The Canadian system offers flexibility and, in Quebec, something close to a Nordic outcome; the Polish system offers a stronger rate and a childcare leave of up to 36 months, but only for those with a contract the leave actually covers. Hofstede Insights scores Canada at 39 for power distance and 48 for uncertainty avoidance against Poland's 68 and 93, which is a tidy statistical way of saying that Canadian employers will let you improvise and Polish employers will want to see the form.

Neither country has solved the nursery. Canada has made care affordable and then discovered it was scarce; Poland has made leave generous and left the supply of places to municipalities. The decisive variable for an expatriate is therefore not the leave but the contract, the province or commune, and the date on which you joined the waitlist. A Quebec parent on a stable contract and a Warsaw parent on a full-time employment contract are the two people on this page with good news.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Reddit r/poland — a man relocating to Warsaw with a wife expecting twins was told that public pregnancy and birth care covers foreigners, that a private delivery at a major hospital can cost around four thousand dollars, that postnatal support is limited to a few midwife visits, and that families can claim a monthly child benefit of about 800 zlotys*

> *Insider — an American who gave birth in Warsaw said the freedom to choose a birth package, hospital stay and dedicated midwife was useful but sometimes overwhelming, and that she would choose the United States for another pregnancy because of language and more attentive prenatal care*

> *CBC News — a mother in Ontario, about ten months into an eighteen-month maternity leave, was laid off for restructuring, lost her EI because severance counted as income, and had to repay payments she had already received*

> *The Canadian Press — a Toronto mother joined about forty daycare waitlists while pregnant and received a single offer a year later, at a centre half an hour away that started after her return-to-work date*

> *Hacker News — commenters arguing about Canadian parental leave found that a claim of five years at Canada Post did not survive a check against a union document, which showed 17 weeks of maternity plus up to 37 weeks of shared parental leave, though they noted the document might be out of date*

## Conclusion

If you are choosing between the two for a family, choose by contract and location before country. In Canada, confirm your EI eligibility and, if possible, Quebec; model the 55 and 33 percent rates against your actual rent; and put your name on waitlists as soon as there is a pregnancy to report. In Poland, make sure your contract carries leave rights, apply within 21 days of the birth, and register for a nursery long before you need one.

Both countries will tell you the system is supportive. Both are correct, in the sense that the support is available the moment you have found it.
