# Britain Is Legislating the Home Office, and South Africa Is Exporting It

🇿🇦 South Africa · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

In Britain, remote work is a right to request, available from the first day of employment and soon to be defended by a statutory reasonableness test. In South Africa, where 33.6 percent of the labour force was officially unemployed in the second quarter of 2026, remote work is more often something requested from a foreign employer, who is delighted to oblige. The two countries have reached the same conclusion about the office from opposite directions: it is not going away, and neither is the argument about it. This guide compares what each offers the professional who prefers to work in pyjamas.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In South Africa

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Get any remote or hybrid arrangement in the written terms of employment | Rely on a verbal promise from a manager who may not be there next year |
| Settle who pays for internet, electricity and equipment before you start | Assume the employer has thought about it |
| Ask how many office days are required, since two-thirds of hybrid firms now want three or more | Read the word hybrid as a promise of flexibility |
| If joining a foreign employer, ask whether it hires through an employer of record | Assume a contractor label protects either side from employment rules |
| Ask about a clause letting the employer end remote work and recall you to the office | Be surprised when it appears in the contract |
| Convert foreign-currency pay through a service with sensible rates | Accept the first bank exchange rate offered |

### In the United Kingdom

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use the statutory right to request flexible working, available from day one and twice a year | Assume the employer will refuse or accept without consulting you |
| Define hybrid in writing, including which days and who decides | Treat three office days and two as the same arrangement |
| Check the contract's stated place of work, because office-based wording weakens later pushback | Accept a vague remote label without asking about travel and office days |
| Ask what the team's anchor days are before agreeing a pattern | Expect the office to be full simply because attendance rules say so |
| Confirm your right to work in the UK before moving, even if your employer is abroad | Move on a visitor's status and keep working for an overseas firm |
| Keep a record of each request and the reason given for any refusal | Raise a refusal informally and forget it |

## South Africa

The labour market sets the mood. Statistics South Africa reported in August 2026 that 8.5 million people were unemployed, an official rate of 33.6 percent. Against that background, remote work is a niche but a growing one. Pnet, reported by Business Day in July 2026, found remote employment up 28 percent in the first half of the year on the same period of 2025 and remote job opportunities up 41 percent over two years, with information technology up 44 percent and finance more than quadrupling. Pnet also noted that remote work remains a relatively small share of the market, though it called the trend permanent. CareerJunction data cited by BusinessTech put remote or hybrid listings at 3.7 percent in 2024, down from 4.3 percent in 2023.

Domestic employers are, meanwhile, summoning staff back. In the same CareerJunction material, nearly 60 percent of employers said they required more in-office presence, and vacancy in Sandton offices, which peaked near 29 percent, had fallen below 20 percent by June 2024. The buildings, it appears, are filling. Remchannel's 2025/2026 benefits guide, reported by the George Herald in February 2026, found that 67.4 percent of hybrid organisations require at least three office days a week, up from 40.7 percent in 2023, and that 41.3 percent had raised their minimum in the past two years. The law is not much help. According to Daily Maverick, South African legislation does not specifically address remote work. A return to the office must be fair, lawful and consistent with the contract, and a change in work location generally needs the employee's consent. If remote work is written into the contract as a benefit, the employer generally cannot withdraw it unilaterally, and lawyers recommend clauses that say otherwise.

The remarkable trend is outbound. Sasha Knott, chief executive of the recruitment firm Job Crystal, told EWN in July 2026 that UK businesses in particular are hiring South Africans remotely in finance, sales and marketing as well as technology, paying more than local employers while the worker remains in rands and in South Africa. BusinessTech reported her estimate that employers can save up to 60 percent, a figure from one interviewee whose firm sells such services. The hiring often runs through an employer of record, which keeps staff under South African labour law. Ms Knott calls the arrangement a win-win rather than a skills drain, since the workers stay put, though a headline over the same story managed to describe the saving as a pay cut, which it was not. Demand, she expects, will continue to grow.

## United Kingdom

Britain's debate is about degree, not existence. The Office for National Statistics found that 28 percent of working adults in Great Britain were hybrid workers in early 2025, a share that has risen gradually since 2022. The access is unequal. Forty-one percent of those with a degree worked hybrid, against 21 percent below degree level, and 45 percent of those earning £50,000 or more, against 8 percent of those earning under £20,000. Age and hours matter as well: 36 percent of those aged 30 to 49 worked hybrid, against 19 percent of those aged 16 to 29, and 34 percent of full-time workers, against 18 percent of part-timers. The arrangement favours the established. Remit Consulting's survey, reported by The Guardian in March 2026, put average office attendance at 44 percent in early February, the highest since March 2020 and still below the roughly 60 to 80 percent said to be typical before the pandemic.

The law favours the asker, within limits. Employees have been able to request flexible working from their first day since April 2024, may make two requests a year, and must receive a decision within two months after consultation, according to VWV. From autumn 2027 an employer may refuse a request only if refusal is reasonable, and must first hold a consultation meeting with a view to reaching agreement. Nothing creates a right to work from home. It creates a right to be taken seriously, which is a more British entitlement. Trial periods and alternative start dates, which a cautious employer might use to say no politely, will be encouraged in guidance rather than required. The ONS, for its part, found the share of people working solely from home broadly constant since March 2022, so the debate concerns the hybrid middle rather than the fully remote edge.

The leverage comes from staff, not statute. A King's College London study found that the share of workers who would comply with a five-day office mandate fell from 54 percent in early 2022 to 42 percent in mid-2024, and that 58 percent would resign or look for another job if ordered back full time, with 9 percent resigning outright. Employer-permitted homeworking had risen from under one day a week to about 1.3. Employers can ask for more, in other words, and employees can leave.

## The Reckoning

Hofstede Insights supplies the cultural gloss. The United Kingdom scores 89 on individualism against South Africa's 65, 35 on power distance against 49, and 35 on uncertainty avoidance against 49. A low-power-distance, low-uncertainty culture treats flexibility as a negotiation among adults, which is roughly how British hybrid arrangements are discussed. South Africa's moderate hierarchy produces employers who set the policy and employees who accept it, unless a better offer arrives from abroad, which, in the skilled professions, increasingly it does.

Head to head, the United Kingdom offers procedure and South Africa offers arbitrage. A British employee has a statutory route to ask, a consultation before refusal and, soon, a reasonableness test, but the best arrangements still go to graduates and higher earners. A South African employee has fewer rights specific to remote work and a tightening office, but access to a global employer market that pays in stronger currencies. For someone considering moving between the two, the sensible question is which of these advantages the move would cost. Relocating to Britain may trade a foreign contract for a domestic one; staying in South Africa may preserve the better-paid remote job, provided the employer, the tax position and the power supply behave.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Hacker News — in a discussion of why remote listings are often limited to the US, Canada, the UK and Germany, a senior executive at a global financial firm said legal and tax complexity alone limits where they hire, and several commenters noted that calling someone a contractor does not stop tax authorities from treating the arrangement as employment. The thread never mentioned South Africa, which may be the point.*

> *MyBroadband forum — South African developers swapped advice on landing remote roles abroad. One said LinkedIn and open-source work produced steady recruiter interest and that local employers cannot match foreign-currency salaries; another suggested working overseas first and then returning while keeping the foreign employer; a third lamented the brain drain. A commenter who preferred the office cited load shedding, and was told home setups can be better.*

> *Overclockers UK forum — posters could not agree what hybrid means. One wanted to work fully remotely for the rest of their career and resented adverts that call a role remote and then require travel; another argued that hybrid with only one or two home days is an office job with better marketing.*

> *Singletrack World forum — a poster with a chronic illness said a remote work request was refused partly because the arrangement might become permanent. Another, who had accepted lower expenses in lieu of a rise, said a full return would feel like a pay cut. A finance director replied that in-person time helps mentoring and spotting colleagues who are struggling.*

> *Expat.com forum — a French applicant asked whether she could follow a partner to London and keep working for her French employer for under six months. A forum expert replied that she would need a work visa, warned that she would not be covered by the NHS, and described the process as expensive for a short stay.*

## Conclusion

Before accepting a post in either country, ask four questions in writing: how many office days, who decides, who pays for the connection, and what happens if the policy changes. In Britain, add a fifth, about whether the contract names the office as your place of work, because that is the sentence that will be quoted back to you. In South Africa, check whether a foreign remote offer runs through an employer of record, and what that means for your tax position and your leave. For anyone relocating from one to the other, confirm the right to work before the flight, since a laptop is not a visa.

Britain, in short, gives you the right to ask. South Africa gives you the chance of being asked, from abroad, in a better currency, which is a different kind of right and one that does not appear in any statute.
