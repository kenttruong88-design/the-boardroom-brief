# South Africa Offers Four Months Off and a Lottery Ticket; New Zealand Offers 26 Weeks and a Childcare Invoice

🇿🇦 South Africa · 🇳🇿 New Zealand

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

The Constitutional Court of South Africa ruled in October 2025 that parents may share four months and ten days of leave, which is a generous-sounding sentence until one notices that the leave is, by statute, unpaid. New Zealand, by contrast, pays up to 26 weeks of parental leave from the state, then presents parents with a childcare bill that some describe as a second mortgage. Both countries therefore have a policy on babies. Neither has quite managed to make the baby affordable.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In South Africa

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Confirm in writing that your employer has registered you with the Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) before you conceive, not after. | Assume the four months of leave come with a salary. The Basic Conditions of Employment Act makes them protected, not paid. |
| Ask HR exactly what top-up, if any, the company pays, and for how long. | Treat a verbal "we look after our mothers" as a policy. |
| Give at least four weeks' written notice of your leave and return dates. | Announce the dates in a corridor and hope the paperwork finds them. |
| File the UIF application as early as the rules allow, and keep copies of every form. | Count on the department's stated three to six weeks for a first payment. |
| If you are a father, agree the split of the shared leave with your partner before you tell the employer. | Assume fathers can claim UIF benefits yet. The UIF Act has not been amended. |
| Budget for a nanny or crèche from the first month back. | Rely on a public childcare system, which is thin. |

### In New Zealand

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Apply for government paid parental leave early, and check your employer's top-up policy separately. | Assume the state payment equals your salary. It is capped at $811.05 a week from July 2026. |
| Put your child on a waiting list at several early childhood centres as soon as you are pregnant. | Wait until your leave ends to look for a place. |
| Use the 20 funded hours of early childhood education once your child is eligible. | Assume 20 funded hours means 20 free hours with nothing else to pay. |
| Ask whether your employer pays partner leave, because the law does not. | Expect a partner to receive paid leave by default. |
| Register with a midwife, who acts as lead maternity carer through about six weeks after the birth. | Panic about maternity care costs if you are a resident or on a qualifying permit. |
| Check the income ceiling on the childcare rebate against your household earnings. | Assume a household of two good salaries qualifies for help. |

## South Africa

The statute is, in the strict sense, generous. According to BDO South Africa's summary of the Constitutional Court judgment in Van Wyk and Others v Minister of Employment and Labour, delivered on 3 October 2025, parents may now share four months and ten consecutive days of leave, and a single or sole-working parent takes the lot. Parliament has 36 months, until October 2028, to rewrite the Basic Conditions of Employment Act accordingly. The same summary notes that the benefit comes from the UIF at 66 per cent of earnings, up to a ceiling, for 17.32 weeks. The employer is not obliged to pay anything.

Whether employers do so is a matter of goodwill, and goodwill is in decline. TimesLIVE reported in May 2026, citing Remchannel data, that the share of employers offering four months of fully paid maternity leave fell from 58.5 per cent in 2023 to 41.7 per cent in 2025. The same report noted that fathers may not yet be able to claim UIF benefits, since the UIF Act itself has not been amended and the court declined to touch it. A shared right to leave, in other words, sits on top of a fund that has not been told about the sharing.

The fund has a reputation to manage. A Melanie Verwoerd column describes the department's own promise of first payments within three to six weeks, a promise the author says is not met, leaving some families without income for one to two months. A feature on allAfrica cites researchers who estimate that only about 20 per cent of the people who should be registered with the UIF are in fact registered. For a domestic worker whose employer never registers her, the four months are protected in theory and uncompensated in practice. For a professional on a corporate payroll, the experience is considerably gentler. Childcare, meanwhile, remains a private arrangement, financed by whoever has a salary and a relative. The structure of the leave itself is sensible enough. BDO notes that a birth mother must not work for six weeks after the birth unless certified fit, that the leave may begin four weeks before the due date if medically necessary, and that the old cap limiting adoptive leave to children under two has been removed. Verwoerd adds that some mothers return at around six weeks, the legal minimum, because the alternative is a household running on one reduced income. The law offers a recovery period. The economy, in many cases, declines to subsidise it.

## New Zealand

New Zealand's state does something South Africa's does not: it pays, directly, and on time. According to Inside Government, the maximum weekly rate rises from $788.66 to $811.05 on 1 July 2026, for up to 26 weeks. The Crayon-run New Zealand Parental Leave Register, reported by HRD New Zealand in September 2026, found that among 370 employers, 92 per cent offer paid primary carer leave, with 51 per cent topping up the government payment and 38 per cent paying full salary. The same report observes that New Zealand's statutory primary carer leave sits in the bottom quartile of developed countries, and that there is no statutory paid partner leave, against an OECD average of 13.4 weeks.

The pinch arrives after the leave ends. RNZ reported in February 2026 that some returning parents face childcare bills of between $15,000 and $20,000 a year, and that the Early Childhood Council counts 443 centres closed between March 2022 and July 2025. The Council also says subsidy adjustments since 2019 have trailed inflation by 11.5 per cent. The 20 funded hours cover six hours a day, which is a generous allocation for a working day only if the working day is six hours long. Change is slow and modestly generous. The Crayon register found that 85 per cent of employers kept their policies unchanged over the past year, 12 added two to four weeks of paid partner leave, and the National Party has pledged to extend government-paid leave to 30 weeks by 2029 if re-elected. Pledges, like toddlers, tend to arrive later than scheduled. A separate RNZ survey of 1,238 parents found that 70 per cent considered raising a family harder financially, that about a third had held off applying for a promotion after becoming parents, and that more than half felt parenthood hurts women's careers more than men's.

## The Reckoning

Set side by side, the countries have inverted problems. South Africa has a right to leave without a reliable income to fund it; New Zealand has a funded income without enough leave, a partner entitlement or an affordable place to put the baby afterwards. A South African mother is more likely to be underpaid, and to wait for it. A New Zealand mother is more likely to be paid on time and then billed. One country has an administrative problem. The other has an arithmetic problem.

Hofstede's published scores add a faint cultural echo. South Africa scores 49 on power distance and 65 on individualism; New Zealand scores 22 and 79. The lower the power distance, the more likely an employee is to ask a manager for a flexible return, and the more likely the manager is to say yes. This is a tendency, not a guarantee, and Hofstede's scores describe national averages gathered decades ago. It is also worth saying that national averages tell a newly arrived accountant very little about what the Johannesburg accountancy firm or Auckland council office will actually do.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Expat Exchange — A Christchurch expatriate says maternity care is free for residents and certain permit holders, midwives lead the process, and the culture leans toward natural birth and breastfeeding. Childcare, in the same breath, means daycare centres or home-based care, so plan early.*

> *allAfrica feature — A live-in domestic worker received half her salary for eight weeks of leave, was told to employ a nanny to keep the baby upstairs, and by her second child could see the baby only at weekends. Her employers had warned her against a second pregnancy, and she was later dismissed.*

> *Melanie Verwoerd column — A maternity-ward nurse whose employer paid a third of her salary waited months for UIF money, was asked to resubmit forms already on file, and received the first payment around her baby's first birthday. An ICU nurse was still waiting more than a year after birth.*

> *HuffPost UK South Africa — One mother paid a company to file her UIF application, only to find it was never submitted, then sent the documents herself and was rejected over signatures she could prove existed. Two years later she was still unpaid.*

> *RNZ parent interviews — A Wellington mother chose to stop at one child because childcare would cost about $400 a week, and a teacher on six months of paid leave decided she could not afford another, her household earning just above the childcare rebate ceiling.*

## Conclusion

For someone weighing a move, the question is less which country likes children more than which kind of friction one can tolerate. In South Africa, get your UIF registration confirmed in writing, ask what the employer tops up, and assume the first payment will be late; keep a reserve equal to two months of income. In New Zealand, join childcare waiting lists in the first trimester, ask about partner leave before accepting an offer, and price childcare against the second salary before deciding who works full time.

In both places the single best protection is a generous private employer, which is to say a decision made at the job offer, not at the maternity ward. Neither state, it turns out, will do the thinking for you, though New Zealand at least sends the cheque.
