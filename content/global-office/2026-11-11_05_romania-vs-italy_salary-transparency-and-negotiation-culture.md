# Italy Must Now Name the Salary, While Romania Is Still Whispering It

🇷🇴 Romania · 🇮🇹 Italy

*By Priya Mehta, The Global Office*

On 7 June 2026 every European Union member state was due to write pay transparency into national law. Italy did, in time, and now requires employers to state the pay or pay range in a job advertisement. Romania did not, and a Romanian job advertisement may still decline to mention money at all. In Italy, according to a survey by Sesame HR, only 8 percent of employees share their pay with colleagues, so the law has arrived in a country that has not yet agreed to discuss it. This guide compares the two for the professional who wants to know what to ask, when and of whom.

## Do's & Don'ts

### In Romania

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask for the salary range before the first interview, since the advertisement may not say | Assume a missing figure means the employer has none in mind |
| Quote and negotiate in net monthly terms, as local convention does | Mix gross and net figures in the same sentence |
| Name a number at the upper end of your range, since candidates are usually asked first | Wait for the employer to be generous unprompted |
| Ask about the full package, including a 13th-month payment and extra leave | Compare the offer with your Western salary as grounds for a premium |
| Benchmark against current data, because candidates ask about 30 percent more than employers offer | Be surprised when the opening gap is wide |
| Ask whether peers in the same role are paid the same, as most employers admit they are not | Discuss your own pay casually with colleagues |

### In Italy

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check that the advertisement states the pay or range and the applicable collective agreement | Answer questions about your current or past pay, which employers may no longer ask |
| Identify the national sector contract and the level you are placed at | Assume the contractual minimum is negotiable |
| Calculate net pay after tax, social security (INPS) and severance (TFR) | Trust an online net salary calculator without checking it |
| Ask how many monthly payments the contract provides each year | Compare annual gross figures without asking |
| Request the average pay by sex for your category in writing, once a year if you wish | Expect an answer faster than two months |
| Look for room above the floor in company-level agreements | Ask colleagues what they earn at the coffee machine |

## Romania

Romania missed the deadline. According to Economedia on 22 June 2026, a draft law went to public consultation in April but was never adopted by the government and appears to have fallen with it. A bill, numbered L445/2026, entered the Senate on 29 June. A report on 1 October said that it was still in committee, that the Senate had extended its tacit adoption deadline to 17 October, and that it still needed a vote in the Chamber of Deputies. Until it passes, a Romanian advertisement can omit the pay range and an interviewer can ask what you earned last year. Eurostat puts Romania's unadjusted gender pay gap at 3.6 percent, against about 12 percent for the European Union, a figure that a women entrepreneurs' confederation warned was reassuring only until one looked inside individual firms.

The culture does the rest. A June 2026 study by Up Romania and Reveal Marketing Research found that about 76 percent of employers acknowledge paying different salaries for comparable roles, attributing the differences to performance, experience or individual negotiation. A separate study for XTB Romania found that 30 percent of respondents named income levels as the most delicate financial subject, second only to debt at 36 percent, and nearly a third fear envy or pressure to lend money if their earnings are known. The result is a market where the individual figure is a private deal. Even so, bargaining is a habit: an eJobs.ro survey in 2023 found that 35.3 percent of employees received raises only after asking, and the 2026 Romania Salary Barometer found candidates requesting about 7,000 lei net a month where employers offered about 5,400, a gap near 30 percent.

The opening gap is large because the average is small. Eurostat puts Romania's hourly labour cost at 13.6 euros, against 34.9 euros across the European Union, and the National Institute of Statistics reported an average net wage of 5,820 lei in July 2026, up 5.5 percent in nominal terms but down 2.5 percent in real terms. Sector medians vary sharply: 11,750 lei net in information technology, 4,700 in retail. Where the average disappoints, the negotiation is the opportunity. Geography counts too: the same barometer lists average net pay of 7,073 lei in Bucharest and 6,633 in Cluj, against 4,108 in Hunedoara county, and the opening gap between ask and offer reaches about 42 percent in energy engineering and 40 percent in banking and real estate. A candidate who knows the sector and the city has a considerably better idea of what to say than one who knows only the country.

## Italy

Italy has done the paperwork. Legislative Decree 96/2026, in force since 7 June 2026, was among the few transpositions completed on time, according to Deloitte. As summarised by A&O Shearman, advertisements must state the initial pay or range and the applicable collective agreement, and employers may not ask candidates about current or past pay. Workers may request, in writing, the average pay by sex for their category, with a reply due within two months. Employers with 100 or more staff must report gender pay gaps, from June 2027 for the largest, and violations carry fines of 5,000 to 10,000 euros. Ministerial decrees to fill in the details are expected by the end of the year, and the definition of pay level remains narrower than the directive's. Where an unexplained gap of at least 5 percent in average pay within a category survives six months, the employer must conduct a joint assessment with worker representatives, and for employers with 49 or fewer staff the procedure for individual requests is still to be set by decree. In practice, the smaller the employer, the longer the wait for the right to arrive.

The decree meets a habit of discretion. Sesame HR, reported by 9Colonne in April 2026, found that 51 percent of Italian employees discuss pay only with a partner and 28 percent with family or friends, while small and medium-sized enterprises, which make up over 90 percent of firms, lack clearly defined pay structures. Underneath sits the national sector contract, which sets pay floors and structures by industry. Forum contributors who know the system describe negotiation as common but usually conducted upward from those minimums, with company agreements as the route to more. Eurostat reported a gender pay gap of about 2 percent in 2023, among the smallest in Europe.

The harder number is the OECD's. Its Employment Outlook 2026, as reported by Euronews, found Italian real wages down 6.1 percent between the first quarter of 2021 and the first quarter of 2026, the largest decline among the countries analysed. A recruitment director told The Local in 2013 that Italian salary packages were not competitive and that experience and the Italian lifestyle tended to compensate. The sentiment has since acquired a statistical footnote.

## The Reckoning

Hofstede Insights explains the temperament. Romania scores 90 on power distance against Italy's 50, and 90 on uncertainty avoidance against 75, with individualism of 30 against 76. A high-power-distance, rule-wary workplace tends to settle pay at the top and explain it to no one, which fits the Romanian employers who admit to unequal pay for equal roles. Italy's collective structure supplies an explanation of sorts: the contract says what the floor is, even if it does not say what your neighbour earns.

Put plainly, Italy now offers a visible starting number and a narrow margin, while Romania offers a hidden starting number and a wide one. In Italy the process is becoming transparent and the colleagues remain opaque. In Romania, for now, both are opaque, and the candidate who does not ask will be paid by default. Both are negotiating against the same headwind, since real wages have fallen in each, by 6.1 percent over five years in Italy and by 2.5 percent over the past year in Romania, so the employer's first offer is unlikely to have been written with your rent in mind. Neither is a reason to avoid either country, but they are reasons to prepare differently: in Italy, with a copy of the collective agreement; in Romania, with a number and the nerve to say it first.

## The Part the Brochure Left Out

> *Expat.com, Italy — contributors agreed that negotiation exists but starts from the national sector minimums, that company agreements can improve terms, and that people in urgent need of work tend to accept less. One urged newcomers to understand net pay after tax, INPS and severance, and warned that most online calculators are inaccurate.*

> *Expat.com, Bucharest — in a thread many years old, an applicant offered 1,000 to 1,500 euros for a multilingual IT support job was told salaries are quoted monthly and net, to start toward the top of the range, and that leaving family behind does not by itself justify a premium. A second reader thought 1,500 net was not a lot but not little; a third argued it would not stretch to a car, a phone, a laptop and a 13th-month salary.*

> *ExpatExchange — a German applicant earning roughly 2,500 euros a month at home reported that a Bucharest employer rang two days after the application to ask about salary expectations. He had no idea what to say, feared being overcharged or underpaid, and received no reply on the thread.*

> *The Local Italy, 2013 — a recruitment director said Italian pay was not competitive, that foreigners came for experience or personal reasons rather than money, and that the lifestyle made up the difference. It was said to a journalist in 2013 and has the air of a standing policy.*

> *Reddit r/cscareerquestionsEU — in a 2021 salary-sharing thread, a commenter asked about tax for developers in Romania and was given a take-home figure of about 43k net; another asked about life in Bucharest and was told that renting a well-located flat there is very expensive.*

## Conclusion

Treat the number as a negotiation in Romania and as a research question in Italy. In Romania, ask for the range early, work in net monthly terms, and name your figure first, accepting that the first offer is probably an opening. In Italy, read the advertisement for the pay range and the collective agreement it cites, establish your contractual level, and use your statutory right to ask how your category is paid before concluding that the offer is final. Where possible, get everything in writing: both countries have rules, and the rules are best invoked by those who hold the paperwork.

One honest caveat. Neither country has settled the question of what a colleague is entitled to know about your salary, and in both the answer, for the time being, is nothing at all.
