# Cisco Grows 24%, Raises Outlook and Still Loses 6.2% of Value

Performance review: Cisco Systems, Q1 fiscal 2027. Overall rating: exceeds expectations on every metric except the one the reviewers brought up in the meeting.

![Network switches and cables in a data centre](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/c-suite-circus/2026-11-15-02-cisco-record-growth-shares-fall-hero)
*Photo: Brett Sayles via Pexels*

Cisco reported on Thursday, November 12, after the close, according to its results as summarised by the Vanderbilt Report. Revenue for the quarter ended October 24 rose 24% to $18.52 billion, from $14.88 billion a year earlier, which Yahoo Finance describes as the fastest growth in more than 15 years. Non-GAAP earnings per share rose 40% to $1.40, from $1.00. The Vanderbilt Report lists consensus at about $18.1 billion in revenue and $1.29 in earnings per share, though third-party estimates vary.

On Friday the shares fell 6.24%, according to CappNotes.

## Objectives achieved

Product revenue rose 32% to $14.67 billion, and networking product revenue rose 43% to $11.13 billion, per the Vanderbilt Report. Services revenue, by contrast, rose 1% to $3.85 billion, which is the professional equivalent of the colleague who attends every meeting. Non-GAAP operating margin reached 37.8%, described as a record, and non-GAAP operating expenses rose just 2%.

Product orders rose 21%, or 14% excluding the hyperscalers. Remaining performance obligations were $46.2 billion, up 8%. Operating cash flow was $4.4 billion, up 36%. The company returned $3.7 billion to shareholders, including $2.0 billion in buybacks at an average price of $112.38, and declared a quarterly dividend of $0.42.

Other segments were less dramatic. Security revenue rose 7%, collaboration 8% and observability 2%, per the Vanderbilt Report. Networking orders grew 28%, the ninth straight quarter of double-digit growth, according to MarketBeat, and the company reported three new AI design wins in the quarter and 18 over the past five. The company is, in short, doing one thing extraordinarily well and several other things reliably.

Guidance moved in the right direction. For fiscal 2027, Cisco now expects revenue of $74.8 billion to $75.8 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share of $5.47 to $5.53. Its target for AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers was raised to $9 billion, which excludes neocloud customers, according to chief executive Chuck Robbins as reported by MarketBeat.

## Areas for development

The review's difficulty is a single line. Non-GAAP gross margin fell to 65.6% from 68.1%, a drop of 250 basis points. Product gross margin fell 350 basis points to 63.7%. The Vanderbilt Report attributes the decline to a higher mix of hardware and to memory costs. Guidance for the second quarter calls for gross margin of 65% to 66%, and the full-year outlook is "just below 65%", per MarketBeat's account of the earnings call.

In plain terms, the company sold more of the product that is expensive to make. That is a good problem, but investors have a long history of refusing to take it as one. Yahoo Finance noted that the shares had risen about 35% for the year before the results, and that Morgan Stanley analyst Meta Marshall had downgraded the stock to Equal-Weight from Overweight on November 5, cutting her price target to $120 from $135.

CappNotes adds that order growth excluding the hyperscalers slowed, a detail that sits uncomfortably next to an AI target that is entirely about hyperscalers.

## Manager's comments

The executive quotations were suitably confident. Robbins described the company as being in the early stages of a multiyear networking super cycle, per Yahoo Finance. Chief financial officer Mark Patterson said in the release that the company is "unlocking significant operating leverage which is enabling us to grow earnings much faster than revenue", according to the Vanderbilt Report.

It is a useful sentence to hold up against the gross margin line. Earnings can grow faster than revenue when operating costs rise 2% and sales rise 24%, which is what happened here. Operating leverage, in other words, is doing the work that gross margin is no longer able to.

## Final assessment

None of this makes the quarter a poor one. By any conventional measure it is among the strongest Cisco has produced in years. The market's response is a reminder that the reviewers in this process are not the executives who write the narrative but the shareholders who read the tables, and they tend to start at the bottom.

Recommended action: continue to grow. Please also grow the thing that pays for the growth. The next review is scheduled for February 2027.
