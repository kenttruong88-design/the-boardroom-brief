# Nvidia Faces an SEC Probe, a Treasury Deadline and a 14% One-Day Fall

The United States Securities and Exchange Commission is probing Nvidia after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, the Wall Street Journal reported on November 17, citing people familiar with the matter. By Friday the company's shares had fallen 13.8% in a single session, according to Reuters, and Axios reported that the chipmaker has lost more than $2.5 trillion in market value since its all-time high on October 6.

## Performance review, Q4

Employee: Nvidia Corporation. Tenure of previous chief executive: 33 years. Reason for departure: Huang's own statement, reported by InvestmentNews, said that he "did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia." Review period: this week.

Strengths. The company delivered, by any conventional measure, a good quarter. Interim chief executive Colette Kress told analysts, according to InvestmentNews, that revenue for the fiscal year ending 2028 is expected to grow about 75% year over year, up from a 70% forecast in August. Axios noted that Nvidia raised its outlook despite strong earnings and still saw its shares fall. On the call, said InvestmentNews, Kress told analysts that "our ambition will not change," which is the sentence that every interim chief executive is issued with the keys.

Areas for development. The SEC probe is a civil matter, per InvestmentNews's account of the WSJ report, and examines in part whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. California Attorney General Rob Bonta told Bloomberg TV that "red flags have been raised" about the possible diversion of chips to China, InvestmentNews reported. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, the same article said, wants answers from the board and outside counsel within ten days, and questioned whether Nvidia knowingly sold advanced chips to China.

Axios reported that Reuters said the SEC is examining the company's accounting practices, and Schwab placed that report late on Friday. A reviewer who starts by examining disclosures and moves to accounting is not a reviewer who is planning to stop at the cover sheet.

## The institutional memory problem

Nvidia has been here before, in a smaller way. InvestmentNews recalled that the company settled with the SEC in May 2022 for $5.5 million over the failure to disclose cryptomining's role in its gaming revenue. A $5.5 million settlement for a company that today is measured in trillions is the corporate equivalent of a parking fine, and boards tend to treat parking fines as one-offs. The question for this review cycle is whether anyone filed the 2022 episode under "pattern."

That is a question for the board, which has had a difficult month. In a decision reported earlier this week, directors ruled out a return for Huang, so the company is run by an interim chief executive while regulators, a state attorney general and a cabinet secretary all ask for documents. In fairness to Kress, interim chief executives are not issued with a long runway, and the numerical machine she inherited is still working.

## The market's feedback

Investors have not been patient. The shares fell more than 10% after Huang's exit, InvestmentNews said, then recovered to $160 after the earnings call. On Friday, though, Reuters reported that the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28%, and Nvidia led the descent. Chuck Carlson, chief executive of Horizon Investment Services, said, per Reuters, that Nvidia "did everything right" on its quarterly report. Few reviews begin with the observation that the employee did everything right and end with a 14% drop.

The ETF angle makes it a larger story than one company. InvestmentNews said that Nvidia sits in 1,068 US-listed exchange-traded funds, nearly 20% of the domestic ETF universe, and is among the top 15 holdings of 628 of them. Retirement savers who thought they were buying a diversified basket of equities discovered they were also buying a governance situation. That is not a criticism of the savers. It is an observation about what index funds do.

## What the board should be asked

There are three questions that deserve a plain answer. What exactly did the board know about the matters now under review, and when? What does the ten-day request from Treasury, and the attention from California, mean for the company's ability to sell in China, which is a question of revenue and not of tone? And who, specifically, is accountable for the answers?

Boards like to say that governance is a process rather than an event. This week has presented them with the event. The process, if it exists, now has about ten days to demonstrate that it can produce a document that would satisfy a Treasury secretary, a state attorney general and a federal regulator at the same time.

Overall rating: strong numbers, weaker oversight, urgent need for a plan. The review notes that the employee has taken accountability in a statement. It also notes that a statement is not a control.
