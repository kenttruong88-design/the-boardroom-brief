# SAP Weighs Cutting at Least 10,000 Jobs, 9% of Staff, in AI Overhaul

SAP, the German enterprise-software group that sells companies tools to make work more efficient, is considering cutting at least 10,000 positions, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, November 26. The figure represents at least 9% of its global workforce. The report cited unnamed sources and said the plan is not final and could change after talks with labour representatives.

SAP did not confirm the report. In a statement relayed by Reuters and Dealroom, it said: "SAP continuously reviews its organization to ensure it remains aligned with its strategy and customer needs." The company declined further comment. As statements go, it is a model of the form: true of every company, informative about none.

## Performance review: SAP SE, fiscal year 2026

Financial performance is hard to fault. Third-quarter revenue passed €10 billion for the first time, up 13%, and cloud revenue rose 26% to €6.7 billion, according to figures reported by Dealroom. By the standards of the annual review, this is an "exceeds expectations" employee who has just been told that its team will be reduced.

The share price tells a more mixed story. SAP's German-listed shares are down 6.8% this year and its US-listed shares are down 13.8%, according to Investing.com. That is the gap between delivering growth and being believed about what follows it. In the language of the appraisal, the employee is meeting targets and failing to inspire confidence in the reporting line.

Headcount stood at 113,076 as of October 30, per Investing.com. Annual-report figures cited by Dealroom put it at 111,397 at the end of 2025 and 109,973 a year earlier. The company has been adding people. According to Business Today, Chief Financial Officer Dominik Asam told a Morgan Stanley conference last week that headcount had risen slightly and that he would be surprised if the trend did not reverse. Reversals, as a rule, are like that: much less surprising once someone has said the word.

## Déjà vu, with a budget line

The 10,000 figure will be familiar. In January 2024, SAP launched its "Next Level Transformation" programme, which also targeted around 10,000 roles. It cost about €3 billion and took more than a year, mostly through voluntary exits and retraining, according to Dealroom and Business Today. Its initial target had been around 8,000 roles.

Two programmes of equal size in under three years carry an implication that the first one did not achieve what the second one now intends to. That is not necessarily a failing. Restructurings are typically described as one-off events, and then repeated, in the manner of a diet that is reported as a lifestyle. This correspondent notes only that when a company describes a transformation as "next level", the possibility of a next-next level should be factored into the model.

The stated logic, per the report, is artificial intelligence. SAP's customers are told that AI will take the repetitive work out of finance, procurement and supply chains. SAP, it seems, is a customer of its own pitch. The company has an advantage that most of its clients lack: it can claim to have tested the product on itself. It also has the disadvantage that employees can read the same slides.

## Governance and timing

The timing is awkward. Two of the six members of SAP's executive board are leaving in March, according to Dealroom. Product chief Muhammad Alam did not renew his contract, and CEO Christian Klein takes on most of his duties. Sales chief Thomas Saueressig is becoming chief executive of Ionos. A cost programme announced alongside a smaller board is the corporate equivalent of cutting the guest list and the venue at once.

The geography also matters. Germany has an employment guarantee in place through 2028, which limits the scope for compulsory reductions at home. That leaves voluntary exits, retraining and redeployment as the main tools in Germany, with the rest of the world carrying more of the burden than the headline implies. The prior programme leaned heavily on voluntary departures, and the new one will presumably do the same, because it is the only method available in the home market.

## The forensic reading

Treat the unconfirmed numbers with care. A figure from anonymous sources can end up higher, lower or restated as a "reallocation". The company has not said that 10,000 jobs will go. It has said that it reviews its organisation continuously.

Still, the pattern is useful to anyone who has sat through a restructuring briefing. Revenue is up. Cloud is up. Profit allegedly matters. And the headcount line, the one the board can control, is the one that moves. A company that grows 13% and weighs cutting 9% of its people is not in distress. It has simply concluded that the way to impress investors is to prove that growth no longer needs as many colleagues. In the review meeting, that is called a development opportunity. For the colleagues, it has a different name.
