# Nvidia Reinstates Huang as CEO, Eleven Days After His Resignation

Nvidia's board has reversed itself. In a press release filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission and dated November 23, the company said it had reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive and as a director, effective immediately. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, who had been running the company since Huang resigned on November 12, remains chief financial officer.

## The reversal

The timeline is brisk even by the standards of corporate governance. According to the company's earlier filing, Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director on November 12, and Kress was named interim chief executive. On November 18, the day after Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, the board said it did not intend to reinstate him, Al Jazeera reported at the time. Five days later, it did.

The board's explanation is the unusual part. Chair Harvey Jones said in the release: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA." The company added that the board had "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and concluded that reinstating Huang "poses no national security risk." Huang's own statement was brief: "I can't wait to get back to work."

The release did not say that the underlying questions had been resolved. A separate investigation by independent directors, into reports about foreign intelligence targeting of the chief executive, continues. Kiplinger reported on November 9 that the board had opened that investigation after Huang acknowledged an extramarital affair with a woman whom media reports said US officials had classified as a Chinese spy. The same Kiplinger report quoted Huang telling employees that leading Nvidia was "the privilege of my life" and that he had "fallen short of that responsibility."

## Performance review: the board

Employee reviews usually cover a year. This one covered eleven days, and the outcome moved the market. According to Trading Economics, Nvidia's shares rose 31.5 percent on Monday, closing the session at about $165, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 6.51 percent. A week earlier, on November 16, the stock had fallen 14.01 percent in a session, according to Schaeffer's Research.

The board's position, then, can be summarised as follows. It accepted a resignation, praised the company's results, declined to reinstate the founder, hired a search consultant (according to Kiplinger's earlier report), consulted the government and reinstated the founder. In a corporate setting, each of these steps is defensible. Taken together they describe a body that formed a view, sought advice and changed the view after the advice arrived.

It is hard not to notice that the sequence leaves the board's original judgment in an awkward place. If Huang's departure was warranted by his conduct, the reinstatement needs an explanation that goes beyond national security. If the departure was not warranted, it needed no investigation. The release addresses neither point. It says what the board concluded, and not what it considered.

## What the company said, and what it did not

Corporate communications have a conventional structure: the decision, the reason, and a reassurance. This release has the first and third elements and a version of the second. "Input from senior levels" of the government is an assurance of a kind. It is not an identification of a person, a document or a date, and the filing does not provide any of them.

Governance specialists also tend to ask about the relationship between a chair and a chief executive. Jones was appointed the company's first board chair on November 12, according to the earlier filing, the same day Huang left. He has therefore held the title for eleven days, the entire tenure of the interim period, and has now presided over both the removal and the return. As career trajectories go, it is compact.

## Forensic note

Boards exist to exercise judgment that the chief executive cannot exercise about himself. The test of that judgment is not whether it is popular, because it plainly is, with investors who bought on Monday. It is whether the process that produced it would survive an audit.

On the evidence in the filing, the board has a conclusion, a chair, a continuing investigation and a very large market capitalisation that agrees with it. That is a respectable position. It is also the position of every board that has ever reversed a decision after seeing the share price.
