# Nvidia's Board Accepts CEO Resignation Four Days After Expressing Confidence

**Annual performance review: Nvidia Corporation, Board of Directors. Reviewer: Miles Bancroft. Rating: Needs Improvement, with a note on timing.**

On Sunday, November 8, Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation and said, according to StockTitan, that "The Board has confidence in Jensen and his leadership team." On Thursday, November 12, the company filed an 8-K reporting that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. The distance between those two statements is four days, which in corporate governance is about the time it takes a statement to go from conviction to archive.

## Objective one: communicate clearly

The 8-K gave no reason for the resignation. Fortune supplied one, reporting that Huang had acknowledged an extramarital relationship with a woman whom US intelligence officials considered a suspected Chinese spy. Fortune said the board stated it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised." Nvidia has not made that account part of its filing, and this review confines itself to what the board said in writing. On that, the rating is "meets expectations": the filing is short, accurate and entirely silent about the thing the market was asking.

The press release did offer quotations. Harvey C. Jones, named the company's first chair of the board, said: "The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team." Colette Kress, the chief financial officer who becomes interim CEO, said: "NVIDIA's strength has always come from its people." Anyone who has read a corporate transition release will recognise the genre. The sentence about people is the one companies write when they want the reader to focus on anything other than the one person who left.

## Objective two: plan for succession

Here the review becomes less generous. According to Fortune, Nvidia had no chair, no chief operating officer and no clear succession plan before Thursday, and Huang had about 60 direct reports. He had led the company for 33 years and owned a 3.6% stake that Fortune valued at about $150 billion. The first-half fiscal 2027 revenue was $96 billion, up 85% from the year before, according to the same report.

Michael Useem of the Wharton School told Fortune: "Rarely has a board faced this much pressure. This is the board's moment to shine or fade." He added that if the board had been doing its job it would already have had successor candidates. Jeffrey Sonnenfeld of Yale compared the case with McDonald's removal of Steve Easterbrook in 2019, which was later followed by SEC charges. A governance expert comparing your week with a restaurant chain's worst one is not a promotion indicator.

The chair role itself is new. Jones, 73, has been a director since 1993 and was appointed chair on November 11, a day before the announcement, per the 8-K. The role did not exist until the moment it was needed, which is a form of just-in-time inventory management rarely applied to boards.

## Objective three: protect shareholder value

Nvidia closed at $166.19 on Thursday, down 4.34%, after falling as much as 13% during the session, per Yahoo Finance. Truist cut its rating to Hold from Buy and its target price to $174 from $346. Craig-Hallum made a similar move, with a $180 target, down from $275, and said: "Huang's departure leaves a major leadership gap that will be extremely difficult to fill." Bret Greenstein of West Monroe described the moment to Fortune as a "reset," with competitors given an opening.

For a board, the reasonable standard is that value should survive a transition. Nvidia's value fell by about 4% in a day and the intraday drop was three times that. It is a fair result, not a catastrophe. The fair reading is that investors see a management gap, not a collapse in the business.

## Objective four: manage the interim

Kress will serve as interim CEO and will remain CFO until a permanent successor is named, the company said. Results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 are due on Tuesday, November 17, with a call at 2 p.m. Pacific. The board said it will consider internal and external candidates.

The arrangement has an elegant simplicity. On the call, the person presenting the numbers and the person responsible for the strategy will be the same person. It also has an obvious risk: a single executive carrying two of the three most demanding jobs in the company, during an investigation, three trading days after the announcement.

## Overall rating

A board's first duty is to have a plan for the day the chief executive is no longer there. Nvidia's board had, by Fortune's account, a leader, a long record of growth and no spare parts. The filing is competent. The timeline is awkward. The decision to name an interim from within the finance department is defensible and, in the short run, probably wise.

Recommendation: continue in role, subject to a documented succession plan to be delivered before the next confidence statement.
