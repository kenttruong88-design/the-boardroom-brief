# Anthropic Markets 825 Million Shares at $70-$78; ANT Debut Expected Thursday

A disclosure first, in the interest of the governance standards this column normally demands of others: The Alignment Times is written with Claude, a product of Anthropic, the issuer of the shares discussed below. Readers should weigh this column accordingly, as they would any analysis from a party with an interest in the subject. The facts that follow come from the company's own announcement and from independent coverage.

Anthropic, PBC launched the roadshow for its proposed initial public offering on November 10, marketing 825,000,000 shares of Class A common stock at an expected price of $70 to $78 each, the company said. Of that total, 801,351,354 shares are being offered by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by certain existing stockholders. Anthropic has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT."

## The numbers, as disclosed

The underwriters are expected to receive a 30-day option to buy up to 123,750,000 additional Class A shares, according to the announcement. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are named as contacts for the preliminary prospectus. Anthropic will receive no proceeds from the shares sold by existing stockholders.

On our own arithmetic, which is not a figure the company has published, the shares Anthropic itself is selling would raise roughly $56 billion at the bottom of the range and $62.5 billion at the top, before underwriting discounts and fees. That is a very large sum to request from public investors in a single week, particularly one in which long-term Treasury yields rose to their highest weekly gain in nineteen months.

The company also stated that the registration statement has been filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission but has not yet become effective, and that the securities may not be sold until it does. The announcement links to an S-1 filing dated October 26. FXEmpire reports that Anthropic is expected to begin trading on Thursday, and an Investrade market recap says it is expected to price on Wednesday, November 18, with shares trading from Thursday, November 19. Both are expectations, not guarantees.

## The performance review

**Structure.** Anthropic describes itself as a public benefit corporation. For investors, this means the company is legally permitted to consider interests other than shareholder returns, which is an unusual feature in a market that has historically viewed such consideration as a rounding error. Rating: distinctive. The announcement does not discuss voting rights or lock-up terms, and this column has not seen the parts of the prospectus that do.

**Timing.** The offering arrives after a week dominated by a chief executive resignation at Nvidia, a hotter-than-expected inflation report, and Federal Reserve officials discussing a half-point rate increase. Roadshow timing is rarely controlled by the issuer alone, but the calendar could be described as ambitious. Rating: confident.

**Communications.** The announcement is a model of the genre. It states the share count, price range, exchange, ticker, the greenshoe and the legal limitation that the securities cannot yet be sold. It does not state a valuation. Valuation, in IPO communications, is traditionally left to the market, which is expected to arrive at it independently and with enthusiasm.

## What the market is being asked to do

The week's earnings calendar has been described as a test of the AI trade. Nvidia reports on Tuesday, and FXEmpire called the combination of Nvidia's results and the Anthropic debut a test of investor appetite for artificial intelligence. That seems a fair description. A weak Nvidia quarter would colour the mood in which ANT's book is built. A strong one might do the reverse.

There is also a more routine question that any new issuer faces: what happens in the first few days of trading. Shares priced inside a range can move in either direction once they are free to trade, and an offering of this size means more shares to be absorbed than most markets see in a month.

None of that is a prediction. This column does not make them, and in this case it has the additional reason for restraint noted at the top.

What can be said is that a company that sells software designed to produce language at scale has chosen a week of unusual market volatility to ask the public to buy shares. If it succeeds, it will have done what few companies manage: selling investors confidence in the same weeks the Federal Reserve is openly discussing a larger rate hike. If it does not, the announcement already contains the relevant sentence, which is that the securities may not be sold until the registration statement is effective, and that nobody is obliged to be early.
