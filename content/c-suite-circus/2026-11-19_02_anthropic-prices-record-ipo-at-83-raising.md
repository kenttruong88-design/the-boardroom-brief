# Anthropic Prices Record IPO at $83, Raising $81 Billion

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed, raising about $81 billion and valuing the company at $1.5 trillion, according to a wire report carried by Click On Detroit. Shares begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT, shortly after the 9:30 a.m. Eastern open. The report described it as the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX's roughly $75 billion raise in June.

## Performance review: capital formation

The mechanics are straightforward. The offering comprises 980 million Class A shares, of which 956,351,354 are being sold by the company and 23,648,646 by existing stockholders, according to the report. CBS News noted that Anthropic receives no proceeds from the shares sold by stockholders. A separate $26 billion private placement accompanies the listing, with participants including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, CBS said.

Pricing above the range is the standard marker of demand in a bookbuild. Reuters reported that the offering size was increased and that the valuation was expected to exceed earlier market expectations, while Vanda Research said its estimate of $250 million in first-day retail buying looked too conservative. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. said the pricing shows strong demand among investors.

On the evidence of the order book alone, management would be rated "exceeds expectations".

## The footnotes

An accurate performance review also reads the footnotes. According to the wire report, Anthropic carries about $6.2 billion in debt, which it describes as substantial, and is not profitable under standard accounting. It has roughly $900 billion in compute and infrastructure commitments, including about $70 billion over the next 12 months. Those are the obligations the proceeds are expected to help meet.

TradingKey, in a pre-pricing analysis, cited a company run-rate revenue figure of about $81 billion as of October 31, a non-GAAP measure, which makes the $81 billion raised and the annualised revenue the same number by coincidence. A run rate is an annualised extrapolation, not a statement of results. The investment bank's term for the gap between the two is "growth".

The shareholder register is also worth a glance. The same TradingKey analysis estimated that Amazon would hold about 14.7% and Google about 13.7% after the offering, and that retail investors could receive up to 20% of the allocation. Customers, suppliers and competitors on the same cap table are efficient, in that everyone with an interest in the outcome is in the room. They are also a governance exercise waiting to happen.

## The sector context

The listing arrives at an awkward time for the sector it is meant to anchor. Jay Ritter of the University of Florida told CBS that the whole AI sector has sold off from its July peak and that the market is pricing in very lofty expectations. Wednesday was a case in point. Nvidia, which reported record quarterly revenue, closed down 2.89%, and the Nasdaq Composite has fallen 4.07% over five days, according to Reuters.

Matthew Kennedy of Renaissance Capital called the deal central to the AI thesis, saying it could be a turning point for the sector. He told CBS that a first-day close around $100 would be solid and one near $140 would signal renewed enthusiasm for AI. Rich Lesser, global chair of BCG, predicted AI valuations would be up and down for many years but more up than down.

This publication notes, in the interest of full disclosure, that it does not have the first-day result at the time of writing, and that the shares were still to begin trading.

## Forensic note

The valuation of $1.5 trillion is below the $2 trillion that some experts projected earlier in the year, according to CBS. That detail rarely appears in celebratory coverage. A company can price a record IPO and still fall short of a number once whispered over lunch.

The relevant question for investors is not whether the first trade goes well. It is whether a company with around $900 billion of commitments and no standard-accounting profit can grow into a valuation set by people who expect it to. In consulting, we called that a "stretch target". In the public markets, it is a share price.
