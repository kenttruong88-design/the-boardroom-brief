# Nvidia's Jensen Huang Resigns; the CFO Becomes Interim CEO

Jensen Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director of Nvidia on Thursday, effective immediately, after 33 years at the company he co-founded, according to a press release the company issued on November 12. Colette Kress, the executive vice president and chief financial officer, was named interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones, a former CEO and executive chairman of Synopsys, was appointed the company's first-ever chair of the board.

Annual reviews rarely cover a departure of this speed, so this one has been adapted. Objective: run the company, indefinitely. Tenure: 33 years. Notice period: none. Handover: a three-paragraph press release and a chair who did not previously exist.

## What the release says

The company's statement is a study in controlled language. Mr Jones said the board "has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team", and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue "without interruption", according to the release. Ms Kress said she was "committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years." The board will search for a permanent successor and "will consider internal and external candidates."

The word "continuity" does a lot of work in a release like this. It tells customers that no one is panicking, tells staff that no one is being fired, and tells investors that the interim arrangement is an interim arrangement. The more often it appears, the more one suspects that the first draft did not contain it.

The release also confirmed that Nvidia will report its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, "as previously scheduled". The call is at 2 p.m. Pacific. Ms Kress will therefore present the quarter as interim chief executive while continuing as chief financial officer, until a permanent successor is named. She will be, on Tuesday, both the person making the promises and the person certifying the numbers behind them. In most governance textbooks this is described as a control weakness. In this case it is described as a plan.

## What the reports say

The release does not explain why Mr Huang left. TheStreet, citing CNBC, reported that his departure followed reports about a relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset, and about company subordinates. TheStreet also reported that Mr Huang acknowledged an affair with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore company that bought more than $4.8 billion of advanced Nvidia AI chips over two years, according to CNBC. A 24/7 Wall St. article published on November 9, before the resignation, said the New York Times and the Financial Times had reported on a board investigation into those ties, and that some of the Times's findings had been denied.

These are reports, not findings, and Mr Huang has not been charged with anything in the coverage reviewed here. The political attention, however, is concrete. According to TheStreet, Senate Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott asked Mr Huang to testify on December 3, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on December 10. Mr Lutnick has agreed to testify, per CNBC as cited by TheStreet. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Treasury backed restrictions on Megaspeed but that Commerce "controls the process".

## The market's review

Nvidia shares closed Friday at $162.85, down 2.01%, according to the Motley Fool, and Eurasia Business News put the week's decline at 12.75%. That is a considerable discount for a company that had, a week earlier, a founder.

Analysts had tried to reassure. In a 24/7 Wall St. piece, Dan Ives of Wedbush said Ms Kress's CFO leadership had been "instrumental in Nvidia's success with Wall Street". It is a flattering reference, and a revealing one: the interim chief executive is valued for her relationship with investors, which is precisely the relationship that will be tested on Tuesday afternoon.

Corporate boards describe succession as a process right up until the moment it becomes an event. The distinction matters because processes have timetables and events have deadlines. Nvidia's board has about three days, one earnings call and a Senate calendar. It also has a chair, a role that did not exist until Thursday, who by definition has no track record in it.

Whether Ms Kress becomes permanent, whether an outsider is recruited, or whether the board turns to someone who has not yet been mentioned, the first review of the new arrangement will come from the market on Wednesday morning. It will be conducted quickly, without a template, and with no one in the room to say that the results were above expectations.
