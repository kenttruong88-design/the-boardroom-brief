# Disney Reports Before the Bell, With an Extra Week Doing the Heavy Lifting

The Walt Disney Company will report fiscal fourth-quarter results before the market opens on Tuesday, with a webcast at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, and the headline numbers look excellent. Analysts expect adjusted earnings of $1.69 per share against $1.11 a year earlier, on revenue of about $25.19 billion, up 12.2%, according to a preview published by Pomegra. The performance review, as is customary, should be read with the footnotes.

## Self-assessment: the extra week

The first footnote is the calendar. Disney's fiscal 2026 has 53 weeks, which makes the fourth quarter a 14-week period. According to a Morgan Stanley note reported by TheStreet, the extra week adds about $600 million to total segment operating income, and Disney's August shareholder letter said it would lift fourth-quarter revenue by roughly 1.5% to 2%. Total segment operating income is expected to be about $4.9 billion.

In performance-review terms, this is the colleague who reports a 12% improvement in output after working an additional shift. It is not dishonest, and Disney has disclosed it. It is, however, a number that will not repeat next year, and the next year's review will have to explain why.

For context, the third quarter delivered adjusted earnings of $2.06 per share against $1.61 a year earlier, with revenue up 7% to about $25.25 billion, according to TheStreet. The fourth-quarter consensus is lower in dollars and higher in percentage terms, which is what an extra week and a flattering comparison will do to a ratio. Disney shares, TheStreet noted, were up only 4.7% for the year at the time of writing, so investors have not been generous in advance.

## Direct reports: parks, streaming and the rest

The theme parks, as usual, carry the team. Analysts expect Experiences revenue of about $10.01 billion, up 14.2%, according to Pomegra, with the extra week contributing about eight percentage points of that growth and underlying growth closer to 6%. In the third quarter, Disney reported Experiences revenue of just under $10 billion, up 10%, with domestic attendance up 3% and per-capita spending up 4%, per TheStreet's account of Morgan Stanley's note. International was the weaker department: operating income there fell 13% in the third quarter, and Pomegra said the softness is expected to continue into the fourth.

Streaming, once the problem employee, has been promoted. Disney+ and Hulu subscription video operating income rose to $712 million in the third quarter from $329 million a year earlier, according to TheStreet. Disney raised prices in September, with the Disney+ Premium and Hulu ad-free plans moving to $21.49 a month from $18.99. Raising the price of a service by more than 13% in one step is the kind of decision that tests whether customers regard a product as improved, and no appraisal system is designed to measure that.

Not every assignment went well. TheStreet reported that CEO Josh D'Amaro has acknowledged that the live-action Moana and Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu fell short of box-office expectations. Admitting this is a good habit, and a rare one. Most reviews describe such outcomes as "learning opportunities."

## Development plan: the 20% question

The line that matters more than the quarter is what comes next. D'Amaro took over as chief executive on March 18, and this is his third quarterly report, according to Pomegra. Disney's guidance calls for about 12% adjusted EPS growth in fiscal 2026 and double-digit growth in fiscal 2027.

TheStreet also reported that managers have been asked to find annual savings of up to 20%, a claim that Disney has not confirmed. Morgan Stanley, in a hypothetical scenario and not a forecast, calculated that trimming 2% to 5% from Disney's roughly $83 billion annual expense base would produce gross savings of $1.7 billion to $4.2 billion. A 20% request is therefore either a rumour, a negotiating position, or the most ambitious stretch goal in the history of stretch goals. Employees will find it difficult to tell which, which is the entire purpose of the format.

Morgan Stanley raised its price target to $133 from $125 and kept its overweight rating, while forecasting fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS of $6.85, slightly below the $6.94 consensus quoted by Refinitiv, according to TheStreet. Options markets, per Pomegra, imply a move of about 6.3%, or $7.54 a share, with the stock near $120.

## Overall rating

The preview points to a strong quarter that will be described, correctly, as strong. The question is whether D'Amaro explains the part of it attributable to the calendar with the same enthusiasm as the part attributable to him. Executives who do that earn credit. Those who do not tend to find that analysts, like all good reviewers, read the appendix.
