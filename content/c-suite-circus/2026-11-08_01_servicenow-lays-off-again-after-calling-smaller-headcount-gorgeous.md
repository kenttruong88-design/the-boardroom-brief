# ServiceNow Lays Off Staff Again After 27% Stock Drop

ServiceNow has begun another round of layoffs across the UK, the US and India, its third of 2026, according to NowBen and, citing CTech, People Matters. The company has not said how many roles are affected. It has said that it is making necessary changes to how it is structured, and that it still intends to finish the year with the headcount it began with.

## Performance review: Q3, with comments

The context is a quarter that, by the standards of enterprise software, went badly in a specific way. ServiceNow's shares fell about 27% the day after its third-quarter results, which NowBen described as the worst day in the company's history. The stock fell 35% between October 23 and November 5, according to the CTech report, and is down more than 30% since the start of the year. The proximate cause was guidance: the company forecast fourth-quarter current remaining performance obligations growth of 18.5% in constant currency, against the 19.8% Wall Street had expected.

A growth forecast 1.3 percentage points short of consensus produced a loss of more than a quarter of the stock's value in a day. This is what is meant by "priced for perfection," and also by "priced for continued headcount reduction", which investors apparently find easier to model.

The more instructive material comes from the earnings call, as reported by People Matters. Chief executive Bill McDermott described a smaller workforce as a "gorgeous situation" and said headcount had slightly declined. Chief financial officer Gina Mastantuono said sales operations headcount was down significantly. The company says its AI efficiencies are generating more than $1 billion in annualised gains.

Forensically, the sentence deserves attention. A reduction in headcount is, in this account, gorgeous. A reduction in sales operations headcount is significant. The annualised savings from artificial intelligence exceed a billion dollars, which, assuming they are real and recurring, would make the company's stated objective of ending the year with the headcount it started with the equivalent of a mission statement for the next round.

## The severance schedule

The numbers in the quarterly filing make the narrative concrete. ServiceNow recorded $87 million in severance and related costs in the third quarter, compared with $24 million a year earlier, according to People Matters. For the first nine months of 2026 the figure is $167 million against $53 million, of which $153 million was paid in cash. Severance reserves stood at $34 million on September 30.

The company recognises termination costs when employees are notified, per the same report, so this week's round would be expected to appear in fourth-quarter results. In plain terms, investors have so far paid for the layoffs that have been announced and will shortly be invoiced for the ones that have not.

NowBen understands that ServiceNow employed about 29,000 people at the start of 2026 and about 30,000 now. If both figures are right, the target of finishing the year where it began requires a reduction of around a thousand, which would be consistent with a third round. It would also be consistent with having hired, earlier in the year, the people it now has to let go, a pattern HR departments know as "growth."

The company did say, according to NowBen, that it is hiring for AI-focused skills, so the net effect is a reshuffle in which some employees are replaced by the technology and others are recruited to supervise it. The affected teams, reportedly including CSM, marketing, media and the Customer Excellence Group, have presumably found the distinction less interesting than the arithmetic.

## The wider picture

ServiceNow is not unusual. Layoffs.fyi, cited by NowBen, counts 142,004 technology employees laid off across 346 companies so far in 2026. What distinguishes the ServiceNow case is the sequence: guidance, a stock collapse, a call in which the executive team praised a smaller company, and then a smaller company. Each step follows logically from the last, which is not the same thing as each step having been planned in advance, though in the second case the press release is unlikely to say so.

The most generous reading is that management is right and a leaner ServiceNow, supported by software that does the work of the departed, will deliver the growth that investors wanted in October. The less generous reading is that a company whose shares have lost a third of their value this year has discovered that cutting costs is the only guidance it fully controls.

On balance, a manager would give this quarter a rating of "meets expectations, on the cost line." The growth line has been referred to the committee.
