# Intel Lays Off More Workers and Declines to Say How Many

Intel is cutting more jobs, this time in its global IT organisation, and has not said how many, Inc. reported on November 19, citing Business Insider and posts from affected employees. A company spokesperson told Business Insider that, as part of a broader strategy to become more focused and efficient, Intel continually assesses its workforce.

## The number the company did not give

The number that Intel did not disclose can be partly reconstructed from the number it did. Company filings show that Intel had 81,100 employees at the end of September 2026, against 85,100 at the end of 2025, Inc. reported. That is a reduction of 4,000 in nine months. It does not tell us what the current round adds, since filings lag the events they describe, but it tells us that this is not a company at the start of a downsizing. It is a company in the middle of one.

The history is a long one. In 2024, under then chief executive Pat Gelsinger, Intel planned to eliminate more than 15,000 jobs, about 15% of its workforce, Inc. recalled. In July 2025, under his successor Lip-Bu Tan, the stated goal became a workforce of 75,000, which the article says implies roughly 24,000 jobs eliminated. In July 2026, Intel confirmed layoffs in its Data Center Group, again without a headcount. If 75,000 is still the target, the company has about 6,100 positions to go from the September figure, though that is my arithmetic, not a company statement, and Intel has not said that this is the plan.

## Reading the statement

The spokesperson's quote is a useful specimen. "As part of our broader strategy to become a more focused and efficient company," said the statement, as paraphrased by Inc., "we continually assess our workforce." Corporate language of this kind is built to be both accurate and uninformative. To "continually assess" is a state, not an event, and a state does not have a start date, an end date or a headcount.

The Data Center Group, per the statement, "is aligning its organization to ensure it has the right roles and skills in place." In a previous era this was called a reorganisation. Today it has the additional advantage of sounding like a vitamin.

What the statement does not say is also instructive. It does not say how many people are affected. It does not say which functions beyond IT are involved. It does not say how the company will measure success. Reporting on the current round relied partly on posts from affected employees, according to Inc. That is a communications choice with a cost: staff who learn about a layoff from a colleague's post tend to assume the worst number.

## Why silence is a strategy

There are rational reasons to avoid a figure. A number invites comparison with earlier numbers, creates a benchmark against which later rounds look like failures, and, for a public company, can become a disclosure obligation of its own. Round after round of unquantified cuts allows a company to claim, on every occasion, that the latest change is routine.

The cost is cumulative. Employees who have seen repeated rounds since 2024, as Intel's have, tend to discount any message that includes the word "focused." Managers who cannot say how large a round is cannot reassure the people who remain. Recruiters who need to hire in the areas that Intel is not cutting face candidates who have read the same posts.

## The performance review

Strengths: Intel has kept its message disciplined, and the headcount, by its own filings, is moving in the direction that management said it would. Areas for development: the company's habit of announcing a destination (75,000) without announcing the route has left its workforce to work out the itinerary from LinkedIn.

Rating: meets expectations on cost, below expectations on candour. The company has described its approach as continual. It might consider that the opposite of an announced plan is not a flexible plan but an anxious workforce, and that an anxious workforce is a cost that does not appear in a quarterly filing until it appears in the attrition numbers. Intel will tell us how many jobs went when it is ready. Employees, who have already been told by other means, will find that they have been counting for some time.
