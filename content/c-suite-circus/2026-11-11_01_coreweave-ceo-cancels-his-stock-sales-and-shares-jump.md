# CoreWeave CEO Cancels His Stock-Sale Plan and Shares Jump Over 20%

Performance review: CoreWeave, third quarter, fiscal 2026. Revenue growth: exceptional. Net income: absent. Debt: considerable. Primary achievement: the chief executive announced on television that he had stopped selling his own shares, and the market rewarded him by buying the company's.

![Rows of server racks inside a data centre](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/c-suite-circus/2026-11-11-01-coreweave-ceo-cancels-share-sales-stock-jumps-hero)
*Photo: Brett Sayles via Pexels*

The facts first. CoreWeave reported on November 10 that third-quarter revenue was $3.66 billion, up 168% from $1.37 billion a year earlier, according to the Motley Fool and Stocktwits. Net loss was $709 million. Adjusted operating income was $379 million, roughly 10% of revenue. The company raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion and reported a contracted backlog of $132.5 billion at September 30, the Fool said.

The stock had been down more than 40% over the prior month, according to the Fool. On Wednesday it was trading around $61.62 midday, up 26.45% as of the Fool's 1:13 p.m. Eastern quote, though Stocktwits described it as up more than 22% earlier in the session. The exact figure depends on the timestamp, which is a tidy metaphor for the quarter.

## Management objectives

Objective one was to explain why a company with $3.66 billion in revenue also has a loss of $709 million. The explanation is interest. The Fool reported that CoreWeave paid $846 million in interest during the quarter on roughly $47 billion of debt. It expects Q4 interest expense of $1.05 billion to $1.15 billion and capital spending of $37 billion to $39 billion for 2026. It does not expect to be profitable on an adjusted basis until the first half of 2028.

Objective two was to reassure the credit market. According to the Fool's account of the earnings call, the chief financial officer said the company does not plan to issue new unsecured parent-level debt at current yields through 2027. Asset-backed financing is planned "in the next week or two" at rates of 7% to 7.5%. Stocktwits reported that the company had also raised $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits. A company that prefers not to borrow unsecured at today's yields is announcing, politely, what today's yields are.

Objective three, and the one that moved the stock, belonged to chief executive Intrator. In an interview with CNBC on Wednesday, quoted by Stocktwits, he said: "This is going to be the first time I've said this. I've canceled my 10b-5." Stocktwits noted he was almost certainly referring to a 10b5-1 trading plan, the pre-arranged mechanism that lets executives sell shares on a schedule. The wording is secondhand, since we could not load CNBC's own transcript.

## Forensic reading

Intrator also said that any future selling by executives would be "RSUs, kind of normal tax stuff", meaning shares sold to cover taxes on restricted stock grants. This is a careful distinction. It says he will not sell for the pleasure of selling, only for the pleasure of paying the tax authority. On capital raising, he said "the ATM sits on the shelf. We will use it opportunistically if we need to raise capital." An at-the-market programme sitting on a shelf is a promise that can be kept right up to the moment it is not.

On the data centres he said: "Our build plans are not being delayed or affected or in any way transformed." Note the three verbs. Executives rarely deny that something has been delayed, affected or transformed in a single clause unless someone has asked whether it has been delayed, affected or transformed.

Asked about scrutiny of Nvidia, Intrator said: "It is not one person. It is an extraordinary company," and "They've begun a process. Let the process run." Nvidia's board announced an investigation over the weekend into reports about its own chief executive, and CoreWeave's chief executive, whose industry leans heavily on that company, endorsed the process with admirable brevity.

## Rating

Does the stock's reaction prove that Wall Street "didn't understand" CoreWeave? That phrase comes from the Fool's headline, not from a quotation, and the analysis it summarises is a fair one. Investors worried about leverage heard a chief executive say he is not selling, a finance chief say there will be no expensive unsecured debt, and a backlog of $132.5 billion that is, at least, a number with a comma in it.

Whether that is a durable thesis or a good afternoon is for the next two quarters to determine. A company that needs $37 billion to $39 billion of capital spending in one year and does not expect adjusted profit for roughly two more has made a bet that revenue will outrun interest. The CEO has now said, on camera, that he agrees with the bet.

Rating: meets expectations, exceeds in communications. Development area: finding a way to reassure investors that does not also require confirming the original worry. Recommendation: keep the sentence handy. It worked once.
