# Nvidia Names CFO Kress Interim CEO After Jensen Huang Resigns

Annual performance review: Jensen Huang, Chief Executive Officer, Nvidia. Tenure: 33 years. Review outcome: resignation, accepted by the board on Wednesday, November 11 according to a regulatory filing cited by TechCrunch, and announced on Thursday.

*Photo: Pixabay via Pexels*

The company's own statement was brief by the standards of a firm that is worth, on a good day, several trillion dollars. "Jensen's contributions to Nvidia are profound," the board said, according to Al Jazeera, and thanked him for his "immeasurable contributions to the company and the industry." Nvidia did not give a reason for the departure, Al Jazeera reports.

## The context the statement omitted

The reasons are not in the filing, but the chronology is in the press. TechCrunch and Al Jazeera both report that Huang had, days earlier, apologised to family and employees over his personal conduct, and that Reuters had reported the board was meeting on Wednesday to consider removing him. Al Jazeera adds that the board has launched an independent investigation into reports that he was targeted by Chinese intelligence, while Nvidia says it has no reason to believe national security was compromised.

From a governance perspective the uncomfortable detail is arithmetic. Huang is the company's largest individual shareholder, but with about 3.5% of the shares and no supervoting rights, TechCrunch notes, the board could have voted him out at any time. A founder who cannot be removed is a governance problem. A founder who can be removed in an afternoon is a board that was always paying attention, or a board that has just started.

## The handover

Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, becomes interim chief executive until a permanent successor is found, and remains CFO, according to TechCrunch. Her first notable accomplishment in the job is that she will now report to herself on the budget. Al Jazeera describes her as CFO since September 2013, during which annual revenue grew from $4.13 billion in fiscal 2014 to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, with earlier roles at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco.

Harvey C. Jones, a managing partner at Square Wave Ventures and former chief executive of Synopsys, was named Nvidia's first-ever board chair. He has sat on the board since 1993, Al Jazeera reports, and also serves on the audit and nominating and governance committees. A first-ever chair appointed in the same announcement as a first-ever interim chief executive is the corporate equivalent of a fire exit that was painted on the wall the week before.

The board is open to internal and external candidates, per Al Jazeera, which lists names discussed in Reuters reporting: Kress, Ian Buck, Rene Haas, Jones, Lisa Su, Sanjay Mehrotra and Tim Cook. President Trump posted on Truth Social that Cook should take the job, writing "A Legend replaces a Legend", though Al Jazeera notes there is no indication Cook is interested. The Financial Times separately reported that Nvidia's board approached Arm's Rene Haas, a report that sent Arm shares down 4.63% on Friday, per CappNotes.

## The market's review

TechCrunch reports that Nvidia's shares fell nearly 12% in early trading on Thursday and by as much as 19% from the previous Friday, erasing more than $855 billion in market value, from a peak of $5.88 trillion in early October. Al Jazeera put Thursday's intraday low at more than 14% down before a partial recovery. On Friday the shares fell a further 2.01%, per CappNotes, the sixth consecutive weekly decline.

The unsentimental conclusion is that investors did not price the loss of a person so much as the loss of a plan. Nobody questions the product. They are questioning who explains it on a Tuesday earnings call.

## Next quarter's objectives

That call is on Tuesday, November 17 after the close, per StockTitan, with consensus earnings per share of $2.49 against $1.24 a year earlier. Interim chief executive Kress will present the results of a business that, by her own tenure's arithmetic, has grown about fifty-fold in a decade.

Strong numbers will be welcome. They will also be beside the point, because the question analysts will put to her is the one every company in the world prefers to answer in a calm quarter: what happens now. Nvidia has the answer for the next few months. It does not yet have the answer for the next few years, and the board has said openly that it is still looking.

A fair assessment: exceeds expectations in product, requires development in succession planning. Recommended action: refresh the bench.
