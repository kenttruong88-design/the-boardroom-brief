# Nvidia CEO Huang Resigns Effective Immediately; Earnings Still Tuesday

Nvidia said on November 12 that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive officer and member of the board, effective immediately. The company's announcement, issued from Santa Clara, did not state a reason. Chief financial officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while continuing as CFO, and the board has begun a search for a permanent successor that will consider internal and external candidates, the company said.

The company also confirmed that it will report third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific time, with a call at 2 p.m., "as previously scheduled." In corporate communications, this is known as demonstrating continuity. In a performance review, it would be noted as an unusually punctual response to the departure of the chief executive.

## The performance review

**Objective: orderly succession.** The release names an interim CEO and a new board chair, Harvey C. Jones, described by Nvidia as its first-ever chair and a former CEO and executive chairman of Synopsys. It promises a search. It offers no timeline. Rating: meets expectations, with the caveat that the search is starting only now that the chief executive has gone.

**Objective: explain the departure.** The release thanks Huang for his "immeasurable contributions" and does not say why he left. Kress said she is committed to building on the foundation he created, and Jones said the board has full confidence in her and the leadership team. A resignation effective immediately, with no stated reason, and no notice period, is a combination boards usually reserve for circumstances they would prefer to discuss in private. Rating: incomplete. Please see me.

**Objective: reassure the market.** Investors were not reassured. According to a closing-market recap published by Investrade, Nvidia shares hit new 52-week lows after the news, and technology stocks fell for a second straight session on Friday. CMC Markets described the shares as trading near $160, having broken a support level that had held around $165 since July 2025. Rating: needs improvement.

## What the earnings call now has to do

Kress, a company veteran who joined in September 2013 after about 25 years in finance at Cisco and Microsoft, will present the numbers on Tuesday in her new capacity as interim chief executive as well as CFO. A dual role is efficient, though it means that the executive reporting the quarter is also the executive approving the narrative around it, which auditors tend to regard as a compact arrangement.

Analysts expect earnings of $2.47 per share on revenue of about $108.8 billion, both FXEmpire and CMC Markets report, with gross margin expected at 74.1%, below 75% in the previous quarter, per CMC. Guidance for the fourth quarter is expected to be around $125 billion in revenue, CMC said, and options markets imply a post-earnings move of roughly 8% to 9%.

A stock that is expected to move that much on routine numbers has now acquired a second variable. The market will be listening not only for the guidance but for any hint about how long the interim arrangement will last, whether Huang remains involved in any capacity, and whether the board's search is as open to outsiders as the release suggests.

## Forensic note on the language

The phrase "effective immediately" appears in the release alongside "interim" and "search." Read together, these suggest a board that had not completed the process it is now describing. Boards that plan successions usually announce them with a successor. Boards that do not usually announce them with a statement of gratitude, which is what this is. Gratitude, in executive transitions, is the polite term for the space where a reason would normally go.

None of this is evidence of anything. The release does not allege misconduct, and nothing in it should be read as doing so. It merely observes, with the dryness of a document drafted by lawyers, that the chief executive of one of the market's most closely watched companies has gone, and that the company would like everyone to attend a meeting on Tuesday as planned.

Investors will also be counting the days. Huang resigned on a Thursday, and the company has had one trading session and one weekend to settle on a message before the numbers arrive. Four calendar days is not a long time to rehearse a first appearance as interim chief executive.

The call begins at 2 p.m. Pacific. Attendance, one expects, will be strong.
