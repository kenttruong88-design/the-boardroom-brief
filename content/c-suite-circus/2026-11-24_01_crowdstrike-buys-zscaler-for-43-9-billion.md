# CrowdStrike Buys Zscaler for $43.9 Billion in Stock, Cites Safety

CrowdStrike said on Monday, November 23, that it will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction that values the cloud-security company at about $43.9 billion, including debt, according to the companies' joint press release filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share, an implied $260.41 a share based on CrowdStrike's closing price on November 20 and a premium of about 34 percent to Zscaler's close that day.

## Performance review: the acquirer

In annual-review terms, CrowdStrike has volunteered for a stretch assignment. The deal is paid for in CrowdStrike paper, which means Zscaler's owners will end up with about 15.9 percent of the combined company and CrowdStrike's existing shareholders with about 84.1 percent, according to the release. The combined group would start with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue, of which Zscaler contributes $3.9 billion. Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, a timetable that gives regulators, shareholders and the integration team roughly the length of a long project plan to disagree with one another.

The market did not applaud the plan. CrowdStrike's shares fell 8.9 percent on Monday, according to Trading Economics, on a day when the Dow rose more than 3 percent. A company that announces a $43.9 billion purchase and loses nearly a tenth of its own value in a single session has received what human resources would call "constructive feedback."

## The synergy slide

Every acquisition has a slide that explains why one plus one equals three. This one is unusually specific. The companies expect revenue synergies of about $1 billion in annualised run-rate revenue over 30 months, and cost synergies of $500 million within 18 to 24 months. They also say the deal expands the addressable market by more than $185 billion by calendar 2029, and note that about 8,000 Zscaler customers are not yet CrowdStrike customers.

Forensic accountants will note the asymmetry. The revenue figure is a hope, expressed as a run-rate, to be reached over two and a half years. The cost figure is a commitment, expressed as a lump sum, to be reached in half that time. Cost synergies are the part of an acquisition plan that tends to arrive on schedule, and in this industry the word has a habit of meaning people. The release does not say which costs, which is customary. Readers may form their own view on how $500 million is usually found in a software company.

The strategic case, as the executives frame it, is that artificial intelligence changes what a security product must do. CrowdStrike chief executive and founder George Kurtz said: "Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in." Jay Chaudhry, Zscaler's chief executive, chairman and founder, said that "In the AI era, Zero Trust security has become non-negotiable." Both statements are likely true. They are also the kind of sentence that survives any amount of due diligence, because it cannot be audited.

## Performance review: the target

Zscaler's side of the ledger reads well. The company says it protects 50 percent of the Fortune 500, operates more than 200 service locations and processes more than 750 billion transactions a day. Those are strong credentials for a business that is about to become a division.

The structure also contains a small act of corporate diplomacy. Chaudhry and his affiliates hold about 34 percent of Zscaler's shares and have signed voting agreements in support of the transaction, according to the release. A founder who controls a third of the vote and has already committed it removes most of the suspense from a shareholder meeting. It is a reliable way to improve one's chances of a unanimous endorsement.

## Forensic note

The most interesting number is not in the table. It is the premium: 34 percent, paid with shares the acquirer's own investors valued less on the day of the announcement. Stock deals have a feature that cash deals lack. The buyer's currency is marked to market every day until closing, so the price of Zscaler will, in effect, float with CrowdStrike's share price for most of the period to closing, which the release puts in the second half of fiscal 2028. If CrowdStrike recovers, the deal will have been cheap. If it does not, Zscaler's shareholders will have agreed to sell a company for a number that depends on a stock which has just fallen 8.9 percent.

In McKinsey terms, which are the only terms in which the author is qualified to speak, this is a "transformational" transaction. It transforms two security vendors into one larger vendor, a balance sheet into a currency, and a quiet Monday into a discussion about integration risk. The rating, subject to the usual twelve to eighteen months of review: meets expectations, pending synergies.
