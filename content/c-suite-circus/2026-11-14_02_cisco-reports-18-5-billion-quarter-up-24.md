# Cisco Reports $18.5 Billion Quarter, Up 24%, and Raises Outlook

Cisco reported first-quarter revenue of $18.5 billion on November 12, up 24% from $14.9 billion a year earlier, and raised its full-year revenue forecast to a range of $74.8 billion to $75.8 billion, according to the company's earnings release. Shares fell on Friday anyway. The Motley Fool's midday report had them down about 6%.

Cisco's quarter ended on October 24. What follows is the performance review.

## Performance review: Cisco Systems, Q1 FY2027

Revenue. Product revenue rose 32% to $14.67 billion. Networking, the division in which Cisco is currently most fashionable, grew 43% to $11.13 billion. Security rose 7% to $2.12 billion, collaboration 8% to $1.14 billion, and observability 2% to $279 million. Services revenue grew 1%, to $3.85 billion. The reviewer notes that one division is carrying the others, and that the others are fine.

Profit. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $1.40, up 40% from $1.00, and GAAP earnings per share were $1.14, up 58%. GAAP net income was $4.55 billion. Operating cash flow was $4.4 billion, up 36%. The non-GAAP operating margin was 37.8%. The company said it returned $3.7 billion to shareholders in the quarter, including the repurchase of about 18 million shares at an average price of $112.38. The quarterly dividend stays at $0.42 a share.

Cost discipline. GAAP operating expenses fell 3% while revenue rose 24%, a ratio that is difficult to achieve and easy to describe. Restructuring and other charges were $28 million, against $147 million a year earlier. The company did not discuss headcount in the portions of its release reviewed here, which, in a document that otherwise covers everything, is its own kind of communication.

## What management said

Chuck Robbins, Cisco's chair and chief executive, said the company delivered "the strongest revenue and non-GAAP earnings per share growth rates for Cisco in over 15 years", and called the period "the very early stages of a multi-year networking supercycle". Chief financial officer Mark Patterson said the company exceeded the high end of its guidance ranges for revenue, non-GAAP operating margin and non-GAAP earnings per share.

A forensic reader will note the phrase "very early stages". It is the standard way to attach a long horizon to a short quarter. It says that the best is yet to come, which is a statement about the future, and it implies that the present is merely excellent.

The company also raised its expectation for revenue from hyperscaler AI infrastructure to $9 billion in fiscal 2027, which it said is more than double the total for fiscal 2026. According to an InvestorsHub report in August, the company had guided for $7.5 billion of AI infrastructure revenue for the year and for full-year total revenue of $72.2 billion to $73.4 billion. The new outlook is higher on both counts. In employee terms, the reviewee set a target in August, beat it in November, and has been asked to set a higher one.

## The market's response

Which brings us to the shares. The Fool's midday report listed Cisco among Friday's decliners, down about 6%, while the broader tech sector was under pressure from the Nvidia leadership news and a rise in Treasury yields. The reports reviewed here do not isolate a cause, and a one-day move is not a verdict. But the shape of the reaction is familiar. A stock that had risen more than 60% this year as of August, according to that same InvestorsHub report, has already priced in a good outcome, and a good outcome therefore arrives as a rounding error.

The same dynamic appeared in August. After the previous quarter's results, the InvestorsHub report noted that the shares fell more than 5% in premarket trading despite stronger-than-expected earnings and guidance, with a Barclays note pointing to a possible dilution of product gross margin from AI-related business. Two strong reports and two falling share prices is not a coincidence. It is a feature of any company whose share price has become a forecast.

## Overall assessment

Exceeds expectations. The employee has outperformed the plan, improved profitability, and returned cash to owners. The employee's one area for development is that the owners now expect this.

Management will, in due course, explain that the supercycle is in the early stages. Investors will check the second stage against the first. As any manager knows, the reward for a record quarter is a larger number in next quarter's objectives, and the reward for that is an even larger one. It is how supercycles are described by those who do not have to deliver them.
