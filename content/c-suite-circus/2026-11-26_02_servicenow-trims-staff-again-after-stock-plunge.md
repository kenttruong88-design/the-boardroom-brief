# ServiceNow Lays Off Staff a Third Time After Record Share Plunge

ServiceNow has made a third round of layoffs this year, with reported cuts in the United Kingdom, the United States and India, according to Salesforce Ben. The number of people affected has not been confirmed, and employees who posted about it on Reddit have so far been the primary source. The cuts follow third-quarter 2026 earnings that the publication describes as strong overall but not strong enough for Wall Street.

## Performance review: ServiceNow, Q3 2026

**Revenue and results.** Salesforce Ben reports that the quarter was strong. The problem was the forward guidance. ServiceNow guided to fourth-quarter current remaining performance obligation, or cRPO, growth of 18.5 percent in constant currency. Wall Street had expected 19.8 percent. The gap is 1.3 percentage points, which in ordinary life would be called a rounding preference and, in enterprise software, is called a catastrophe.

**Market response.** The share price fell sharply. Salesforce Ben calls it the worst day for the stock in the company's history, though the article is inconsistent about the size, giving "over 20 percent" in one place and 27 percent in another. Either figure is the kind of number that ends careers or, more often, begins reorganisations. The article does not say which, so we can assume the latter.

**Headcount.** Here the file becomes interesting. Headcount reportedly started the year at around 29,000 and now sits at around 30,000, according to Salesforce Ben. A company that has grown by about a thousand people while conducting three rounds of layoffs is either extremely good at hiring or extremely thorough at firing. Possibly both. A spokesperson told the publication that the company was making "necessary changes to how [it is] structured" and was "managing headcount with discipline to end 2026 where we started."

## Reading the spokesperson statement

Forensic accountants enjoy a sentence like that last one. To end 2026 "where we started" is to end it at about 29,000, from a current figure of about 30,000. The statement therefore implies a further reduction of roughly a thousand roles, though the company has not said so, and Salesforce Ben has not confirmed the arithmetic. It is the type of target that is always described as "discipline" in the final month of a fiscal year and as "restructuring" in the first month of the next.

The phrase "driving efficiencies across the business," also attributed to the company, belongs to the same genre. Efficiency is the only word in management that is simultaneously a goal, a method and an apology.

## The previous week's testimony

The most useful exhibit in the file is the chief executive's own record. Salesforce Ben reports that Bill McDermott, a week before the layoffs, described a smaller workforce as "a gorgeous situation." He had been asked about declining headcount, particularly in sales and marketing and in research and development. Executives speak in the context of what they know. The remark may have been entirely sincere and entirely sound as strategy. It may have been less well-timed than he intended.

None of this is to suggest the company misled anyone. A chief executive may reasonably describe a leaner organisation in positive terms, and a company may reasonably reduce staff after a weak guide. It is the sequence that does the damage. The audience hears the optimism on Monday and the redundancy notices on the following Monday, and does not need a rolling forecast to see the connection.

## Context for the sector

The layoffs fit a wider pattern. Salesforce Ben cites Layoffs.fyi as counting 142,004 tech employees across 346 companies laid off so far in 2026, a figure that moves with every announcement and so should be read as a floor. Enterprise software, in particular, is being asked to justify its growth rate, its AI spending and its headcount at once. Investors have decided that all three are connected.

**Rating: Needs Improvement.** The business is not failing. By the company's own account, the quarter was strong. The shortfall is one of expectation management, and the cure the market is demanding is a smaller payroll and a larger guide. Miles Bancroft recommends a different development goal for next year: describe headcount in neutral terms until the Reddit threads have been read.

The closing note is a modest one. A company that ends the year where it started has, strictly speaking, achieved nothing, which in this market may be the point.
