# Home Depot Sales Rise 4.9% as Fewer Customers Pay More

The Home Depot reported third-quarter net sales of $43.4bn on Tuesday, up 4.9% from a year earlier, and adjusted diluted earnings per share of $3.86, up 3.2%, in a release that quoted its incoming chief executive as saying that results "exceeded our expectations." This column has reviewed the filing and prepared the quarter's performance appraisal.

## Results delivered

Net sales were $43,398m against $41,352m in the same quarter last year. Comparable sales rose 2.2%, up from 0.2% a year earlier, and the same figure applied in the United States. Diluted earnings per share on a GAAP basis were $3.72, up from $3.62, and adjusted earnings per share were $3.86, up from $3.74. A preview of the week's earnings calendar by Newsquawk had expected adjusted earnings per share of $3.84 and revenue of $42.81bn. Against those figures the company beat on revenue by roughly $590m and on earnings by two cents.

Richard McPhail, whom the release describes as the incoming chief executive officer, said: "Results in the third quarter exceeded our expectations." The assessment is accurate at the level of the second decimal place, and it is worth noting that expectations are the only metric a new chief executive controls entirely.

Over the first nine months of the fiscal year, net sales were $133,024m, up 5.2%, comparable sales rose 1.5%, diluted earnings per share were $11.82, up 1.5%, and adjusted diluted earnings per share were $12.22, up 1.9% from $11.99. GAAP operating income for the quarter was $5,479m, up 2.4%, and net earnings were $3,713m against $3,601m a year earlier. In the language of the appraisal: dependable, incremental, and never in need of a second look.

## Areas for development

The reviewer notes that the quarter's growth came from the till rather than the door. Comparable average ticket rose 3.0%, while comparable customer transactions fell 0.5%. Put differently, the company earned more from each visit and had fewer visits to earn it from. Mr McPhail's further statement that the company's execution "enabled us to grow market share in a dynamic environment" is a claim about relative performance, and the text this column read offers no figure for it.

Profitability, too, deserves a gentle comment. Adjusted operating income rose 2.7% to $5,661m against sales growth of 4.9%, so the adjusted operating margin fell to 13.0% from 13.3%. Sales grew almost twice as fast as the profit made on them, which is the arithmetic equivalent of a promotion that comes with a pay cut.

## Goals for next year

The release says that the company "updates its fiscal 2026 guidance." What it does not say is what has changed. The company also expects a gross margin of about 33.0%, net interest expense of about $2.2bn, capital expenditure of about 2.5% of sales, and around 15 new stores. The new figures are single approximations: total sales growth of about 4.5%, comparable sales growth of about 1.4%, an adjusted operating margin of about 12.8%, and adjusted diluted earnings per share growth of about 2.0% from $14.69. GAAP diluted earnings per share are expected to grow about 1.5% from $14.23.

A pre-earnings preview published on 10 November by news.ifyouspent.com recorded the company's guidance from August as sales growth of 2.5% to 4.5%, comparable sales of flat to up 2.0%, and adjusted earnings per share growth of flat to 4.0%. Set side by side, each new number sits inside the old range. Sales are at the top end, comparable sales are slightly above the middle and earnings growth is in the middle. The guidance has therefore been updated in the sense that a range has been replaced by a number within it, which is the corporate equivalent of announcing that the weather will be somewhere between cold and warm, and that it is now 12 degrees.

## Personnel

The release also quoted Ann-Marie Campbell, described as the incoming president and chief operating officer: "Our team's passion and commitment to our customers makes the Home Depot unique and drives our success." Both executives are described as incoming. In the text this column read, the release does not explain the transition, and does not mention whom the two are succeeding. A leadership change communicated through the quote attribution rather than the headline is, at least, economical.

Overall rating: meets expectations, as defined by the person being rated. The reviewer recommends that next quarter's appraisal include a figure for market share, and a reason for the fewer customers.
