# Canva, Squarespace and People Inc. Cut Staff in Same Week

Three companies told employees this week that jobs were going. Canva is changing roles for 1,271 people, 22% of its organisation. Squarespace is laying off nearly 300, or 19%. People Inc. is cutting 195. Each chief executive sent a memo, and each memo is, for the purposes of this publication, a document that rewards a careful read. The details come from Ragan Communications' "The Week in Comms," published on 6 November.

## Performance review: Canva

Melanie Perkins, Canva's chief executive, published a memo on the company's website. According to Ragan, some roles will change or move to centralised hubs, while others will end. Departing employees receive at least six months' salary and may keep their company MacBooks, Ragan reported. Perkins also told staff that leavers keep access to Slack and Gmail until the end of the week.

Note, too, that the memo was published on Canva's own website rather than left to reach the press by other means. Publishing the bad news yourself is either a sign of confidence or evidence of a very good communications team, and the two are not mutually exclusive. Rating: *exceeds expectations of severance; meets expectations of timing.* A rare example of an organisation that has thought carefully about the last five days of an employee's tenure, which is more than can be said for most.

## Performance review: Squarespace

Anthony Casalena wrote in a memo, obtained by Business Insider, that nearly 300 employees, 19% of the workforce, will go. He cited "smaller teams, more player coaches, and a simplified organizational structure." He also wrote that "this is not an exercise designed to reach a financial target."

A forensic reading of the second sentence is instructive. It does not say there is no financial consequence, only that the consequence was not the design. The design, it appears, was "player coaches," a term that in the corporate dialect means a manager who also has to do work. A company that sheds 19% of its staff and a layer of management is, whatever the intent, going to reach some kind of financial figure.

Rating: *meets expectations of candour; requires development in the use of the passive voice.*

## Performance review: People Inc.

Neil Vogel told staff, in a memo Variety obtained, that the company is laying off 195 people, mainly in print and legacy media. He cited "rapid changes" in media and said People Inc. has added 320 new employees so far in 2026. He also said the company will continue to make meaningful investments in 2027.

The arithmetic is tidy: 320 hired and 195 cut leaves a net gain of 125 people, which is the sort of figure that turns a layoff into a "reallocation." The people leaving are in print. The people arriving, by implication, are not.

Rating: *meets expectations; exceeds expectations of arithmetic.*

## A footnote on Qualcomm

A fourth case, with fewer details, belongs in the file, although it is of a different kind. Qualcomm's chief executive, Cristiano Amon, told staff in an internal memo that the company will begin notifying laid-off employees worldwide in November, following its annual employee reviews, according to a report by Analytics Insight citing Reuters. Qualcomm has not said how many jobs will go or which teams are affected. In September 2025 the company employed about 52,000 people.

Reuters, per the same report, noted that Qualcomm's shares were up about 1.5% this year, against more than 12% for the S&P 500 and nearly 60% for the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index. Amon's reasoning was that the workforce must match changing business needs. A company that has underperformed its sector by that distance has a business need that is not hard to identify.

## Overall assessment

What the three memos share is the register. Squarespace's explicitly describes the cuts as something other than a financial exercise, People Inc.'s points to a changing industry, and Canva's frames the change as one of structure and location. All three place the decision in the company's strategy rather than its spreadsheet. What they do not share is the scale: 22%, 19% and, for People Inc., a number of employees that Ragan's summary does not express as a share of the workforce.

The reader's job, as always, is to hold the language and the arithmetic side by side. The language says the company is simplifying. The arithmetic says that 1,271 people at Canva, nearly 300 at Squarespace and 195 at People Inc. are now looking for something to do on Monday.
