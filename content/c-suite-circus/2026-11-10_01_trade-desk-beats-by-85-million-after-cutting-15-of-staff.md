# Trade Desk Beats by $85 Million After Cutting 15% of Staff

The Trade Desk reported third-quarter revenue of $736 million on Monday, a decline of 0.5% from a year earlier but comfortably ahead of the $650.89 million analysts had expected, according to the transcript of its earnings call published by Investing.com. Adjusted earnings came in at $0.33 per share against a forecast of $0.25. Shares closed up 15.47% at $14.93. This review covers a company that, in September, let go of about 15% of its workforce, and in November was congratulated for it.

## Performance review: Q3 2026

**Revenue:** Exceeds expectations. The $85 million beat, about 13%, is the sort of margin analysts normally describe as "decisive" and finance directors describe as "worth checking". Chief executive Jeff Green opened by noting that third-quarter revenue was "meaningfully ahead of the outlook we provided in August", a sentence that does a respectable job of managing to be both accurate and a little smug. A footnote deserves attention: about three points of revenue came from a strategic partner, and the company does not expect that to recur. The beat is therefore real, but a slice of it is rented.

The composition matters. Connected TV accounts for something in the low 50s as a percentage of the business, according to the call, and political advertising contributed a mid-single-digit share of third-quarter revenue. US revenue was flat, helped by political spending, which suggests that an election year did some of the quarter's work. Analysts from Susquehanna, KeyBanc, Truist and Evercore were among those who joined the question-and-answer session, and the call's participants appear to have been polite to the finance team.

**Profitability:** Strong, with an asterisk. Adjusted EBITDA was $266 million, a 36% margin. Net income was $38 million, or $0.08 per diluted share, against adjusted net income of $155 million. The gap between the two is, in part, the restructuring that preceded the beat. Operating expenses were $643 million, including $44 million in restructuring charges. Platform operations expense still rose 23% on the year, so the efficiency story is, for now, one of headcount rather than infrastructure.

**People management:** Decisive. The September workforce reduction is expected to deliver more than $100 million in gross savings in 2027, the company said. Chief financial officer Nate Olmstead described the aim as "building a more efficient operating model", a phrase that works equally well on a slide and on a leaving card. Co-founder Dave Pickles is returning to an expanded role, which is how one signals continuity when one has just reduced the number of people available to continue.

**Guidance:** Confident. The Trade Desk expects fourth-quarter revenue of at least $755 million and adjusted EBITDA of at least $280 million. Both are lower bounds, a convention that rewards the cautious and bores the sceptical.

**Cash:** Healthy. Operating cash flow was $258 million and free cash flow $153 million, with $1.6 billion in cash at the quarter's end. Management also talked up an agentic AI product called Zuma, an open measurement tool, and Audience Unlimited, which the transcript says more than 100 advertisers are testing. When a company reduces its human workforce by a sixth and then promotes its automated one, the reader is entitled to notice the order of operations.

## The wider context

None of this makes the share price a triumph. The stock closed Monday at $14.93, and the same transcript notes it is down about 65% over the past year and about 61% since January, against a 52-week high of $45.50. A 15% one-day bounce is welcome. It is also a recovery from a very low base, and the company's own revenue is shrinking, if only barely. The adjusted-versus-reported distinction will not escape the next set of analysts who read the footnotes.

Still, the market's verdict was unambiguous. A company that cuts costs sharply and then reports a revenue beat is, in the current environment, a company that has done its homework. Shareholders will see better margins. Customers will see a vendor that is, in its own words, becoming more efficient. Those who remained on the payroll will see a quarterly bonus pool and a calendar invitation from HR titled "Next steps".

Overall rating: exceeds expectations, with development areas in the quality of revenue, the sustainability of the savings and the optics of calling a headcount reduction an operating model. The employees affected were not available to comment on their own appraisals.
