# Marvell Bids $115 Billion for Lumentum; Its Own Shareholders Flinch

Marvell Technology and Lumentum Holdings agreed on a two-step merger on Sunday, 1 November, and told the world on Monday. According to HDFC Sky's market roundup, the all-stock deal is worth about $115 billion. Marvell's shares fell 7.25% the same day. This is the corporate equivalent of announcing a promotion and watching the audience check its phones.

*Photo: Jeremy Waterhouse via Pexels*

The terms, as reported by HDFC Sky: each Lumentum share converts into 4.5 Marvell shares, a 23.9% premium to Lumentum's 28 October close. Marvell expects more than $1 billion of annual run-rate cost synergies, targeted for the first half of 2028. Marvell's filing coverage by Kalkine Media confirms the structure and date but, in the version reviewed here, did not state a price, so the $115 billion figure rests on HDFC Sky's account until the proxy arrives.

## Performance review: Marvell

Marvell's management has done something ambitious and explained it at 8:00 a.m. Eastern on a joint investor call, an hour at which most people have not yet decided what they believe. According to its current report, the deal is a two-step merger in which a Marvell-owned subsidiary merges into Lumentum, which then merges into a second subsidiary, so that Marvell ends up owning the business through a Delaware corporation named after a coffee order. A Form S-4 with a joint proxy statement and prospectus is to follow. The same filing, Kalkine reported, included preliminary third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue for Marvell in a joint press release, though the report did not specify the figure; it is the kind of detail an acquirer attaches to a deal announcement so that the announcement has company.

The market's feedback was prompt. Marvell fell 7.25% to $182.13 from $196.36. Rating: *meets expectations of caution; exceeds expectations of expense.*

## Performance review: Lumentum

Lumentum's side of the ledger is more interesting. The company reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $1.263 billion, up 136.5%, the same day, according to HDFC Sky. Growth of that order is usually the sort of result a company wants to hold on to for a little while before selling it.

Yet its shares fell 12.81% to $732.94 from $840.70. The arithmetic here is worth a pause. Four and a half Marvell shares at Monday's $182.13 close come to about $819.59, which is below Lumentum's previous close of $840.70 and about 10.6% more than the $732.94 at which Lumentum actually closed. That gap is the market saying that a fixed exchange ratio is only as good as the acquirer's share price, and the acquirer's share price had just gone down.

There are only a few readings of a target that trades well below the headline value of the offer. Investors may doubt that the deal will close, may expect the acquirer's shares to fall further, or may simply have been unusually keen to sell a stock that had already risen enough. Nothing in the coverage reviewed here says which. A forensic accountant would write "see footnote" and move on.

## The forward-looking statements

Every transaction of this type arrives with a promise that the combined business will be more than the sum of two. In this case the promise has a number attached: more than $1 billion in annual run-rate cost synergies, reached by the first half of 2028. It is a precise target with a vague mechanism. "Cost synergies" is a phrase that rewards careful reading, because it means that the benefits of the deal are expected to come from fewer of something. It is rarely the executives.

The timing deserves attention too. A target date in the first half of 2028 is more than a year away from a deal that has not yet had a proxy statement. Between now and then lie shareholder votes, regulatory reviews and at least two earnings seasons in which both management teams will be asked, politely, to define "on track."

## The overall rating

Management's strategic logic has not been fully published, and one should resist the temptation to infer it from a share price. But it is fair to say the market has issued a split rating: the acquirer's shares are down 7.25%, the target's are down 12.81%, and nobody who owns either is yet in a position to describe the transaction as a win.

The review's conclusion is the standard one for large deals announced on a Monday: the strategy is bold, the integration plan is forthcoming, and the premium is a number that looked generous on Monday morning and rather less so by the close.
