# HP Beats Its Guidance With Help From a Tariff Refund

HP Inc. reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $16.0 billion on Tuesday, November 24, up 9.3 percent from a year earlier, and full-year revenue of $60.5 billion, up 9.5 percent, according to the company's earnings release filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Profit, as a matter of employee feedback, was less cooperative. Non-GAAP earnings per share for the quarter were $0.83, down 10.8 percent, even though the figure included a $0.15 benefit from tariff refunds.

## Performance review: HP Inc., fiscal fourth quarter

The employee is hard-working and grows sales every year. The employee is also, in the fourth quarter, down on earnings and in receipt of a government refund that makes the quarter look better than it was. Taken at face value, the numbers are respectable. Taken without the refund, they are a conversation.

According to the release, HP's quarter ended October 31. GAAP earnings per share were $0.84, flat on the year, and non-GAAP earnings of $0.83 were down 10.8 percent. For the full year, non-GAAP earnings per share were $3.33, up 6.7 percent, and GAAP earnings per share were $2.62, down 1.1 percent. Tariff refund benefits were $0.15 per share in the quarter and $0.26 for the year, the company said. By my arithmetic, which is mine and not HP's, the fourth-quarter non-GAAP figure without the refund would be about $0.68. Against the prior-year figure implied by the reported 10.8 percent drop, that would be a decline of roughly a quarter.

## What sold

Personal Systems, the division that sells computers, had fourth-quarter revenue of $11.8 billion, up 14 percent, according to the release. Commercial revenue in that segment rose 23 percent and consumer revenue fell 7 percent. For the year, Personal Systems revenue was $44.0 billion, also up 14 percent. Interim chief executive Bruce Broussard said in the release: "In the fourth quarter, we continued to grow our higher-value businesses, with AI PCs reaching nearly 60% of shipments."

Printing, which has been described for some years as a cash machine and in better moments as a harvest, brought in $4.3 billion, down 1 percent. The picture, in other words, is a computer division growing quickly on corporate purchases, a consumer line going the other way, and a printing business shrinking slowly in the background.

Broussard added that "we believe these innovations will drive multiple waves of device refresh and create sustained growth opportunities." A wave of refresh is, in this telling, a future in which companies replace their laptops for the artificial intelligence features, which is a strategy with a great deal of faith and one or two product cycles in it.

## The cash flow and the charges

Free cash flow for the full year was $3.3 billion, up 13.5 percent. Chief Financial Officer Karen Parkhill said the company had "grown FY26 revenue 9.5%, delivered on our guidance for the year, exceeded our free cash flow target," and added "we have a strong track record of addressing challenges and are confident in our ability to drive improvement." That second sentence is the type that is written for investors who may be asking about the profit line.

Restructuring and other charges were $611 million for the fiscal year against $405 million a year earlier, with $72 million in the fourth quarter. The release gives no headcount figure. HP has previously said it plans to cut between 4,000 and 6,000 jobs by fiscal 2028, according to The Register, which I read for the background. I did not find that figure restated in the November 24 release.

## The outlook

Guidance is where the review turns. HP expects non-GAAP earnings per share of $3.15 to $3.45 for fiscal 2027, a range whose midpoint of $3.30 sits below the $3.33 it just reported. First-quarter earnings per share are guided to between $0.66 and $0.76. Free cash flow is expected to be $2.9 billion to $3.1 billion, below this year's $3.3 billion.

A company that has just grown revenue by nearly 10 percent and is guiding to lower earnings and lower cash flow is making a statement about costs, whether it says so or not. The release did not elaborate, and I have not seen the earnings call, so I will not supply the explanation that I am sure someone will.

## The empty chair

The most interesting line in the performance review is the title in the signature block. Broussard is the interim chief executive. HP's previous chief, Enrique Lores, left to become chief executive of PayPal, effective March 1, according to The Register's report from February 3, and the board named Broussard on an interim basis while a search firm looked for a permanent successor. I found no announcement of a permanent appointment.

Nine months on, an interim arrangement has produced a year of revenue growth, a cash flow target exceeded and a forecast below the current year. It would be unfair to blame the incumbent for the arrangement, and equally hard to call it a plan.

## Verdict

HP gets a favourable review on the sales line, a mixed one on profits, and a pointed note on the guidance. The tariff refund has done a favour to the quarter that cannot be repeated by guidance, and the supervisor is, as the performance system puts it, acting. Nobody has yet given the company a permanent manager to appeal to.
