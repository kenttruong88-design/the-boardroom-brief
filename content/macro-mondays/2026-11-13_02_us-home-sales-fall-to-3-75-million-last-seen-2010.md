# US Home Sales Fall to 3.75 Million, a Level Last Seen in 2010

Sales of existing US homes fell 5.5% in October to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million, the National Association of Realtors said on Thursday. That is the lowest pace since August 2010, according to Inman and HousingWire, and 8.8% below the level of October 2025. The gap between the previous month and the one before it is not the story. The story is that a country of this size is currently buying and selling homes at the pace it managed in the aftermath of the financial crisis.

## A frozen market with firm prices

The oddity is the price. The median existing-home price was $416,700, which HousingWire reported was up 0.4% from a year earlier, the 40th consecutive month of annual gains. Normally, when volume collapses, prices follow. Here they have refused. Inventory rose to 1.59 million homes, up 4.6% from a year earlier and equal to 5.1 months of supply, per HousingWire. Homes sat on the market a median of 35 days, up from 34 a year ago.

The explanation is mortgage rates. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun said in a written statement: "Homebuying is difficult when mortgage rates have climbed to their highest levels since 2000, aside from a single month in 2023." Many existing owners hold mortgages well below that level and have no interest in trading them for a new one. They are not selling, buyers cannot afford to buy, and the median price drifts upward because the only people transacting are those who can manage it.

## Who is still buying

HousingWire's breakdown shows the shape of the squeeze. First-time buyers made up 29% of sales, down from 31% a month earlier and 32% a year earlier. Cash purchases accounted for 29%, up from 27% in September. The West was the weakest region, with sales 14.5% below a year ago, and all four regions fell year on year. NAR's affordability index stood at 100.7, against 107.3 a year earlier, per the same report.

The pattern is familiar to anyone who has watched the sector in a high-rate period: first-time buyers, who need financing, give way to buyers with equity or cash, who do not. A market that is increasingly composed of people who do not need a mortgage is not a market that responds to interest rates in the usual way. It simply gets quieter.

## The mortgage rate that will not fall

Inman cited a Freddie Mac weekly rate of 7.11% after four consecutive weekly declines, and said rates had reached a three-year high of 7.40% on October 8. The easing may not last. Treasury yields, which set the floor for mortgage pricing, moved the other way on Thursday, with the 10-year yield finishing at 5.10%, up 0.10 percentage point, according to Yahoo Finance's market coverage. Lisa Sturtevant of Bright MLS told Inman: "There is little reason to expect rates to come down before the end of the year."

Realtor.com's Danielle Hale struck a slightly more optimistic note: "I expect to see more homebuyers come back into the market as we move away from recent mortgage rate highs, but the upside will be limited." That is an accurate sentence, and also a very careful one. It forecasts a recovery and then limits it twice.

## Sellers discover the discount

Yun was blunt about what comes next for those who need to sell. "If the homes are not selling at the listing price, it requires a discount," he said, and added: "If the home is sitting on the market for more than 60 days, it requires a 9 percent discount." He added that the inventory count would be "the highest since November 2019."

That is the economics textbook, finally turning up on schedule. A seller who has watched a neighbour's house reach a record price at the peak finds that the price in the listing and the price in the market have become different concepts. Still, the median figure rose.

## The closing observation

Governments tend to describe housing weakness in terms of confidence, as though buyers had simply lost heart. The more accurate description is arithmetic. At 7%, a $416,700 median home carries a monthly payment that has priced out a growing share of buyers, as the fall in first-time buyers suggests, and no amount of encouragement changes the payment. The housing market is not broken. It is waiting for a rate that does not arrive, and a Federal Reserve now being asked to consider raising rates further is not offering one.
