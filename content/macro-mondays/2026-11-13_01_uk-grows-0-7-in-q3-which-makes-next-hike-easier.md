# UK Grows 0.7% in Q3, Which Makes the Next Rate Hike Easier

The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the third quarter of 2026, the Office for National Statistics said on Thursday, up from 0.5% in the second quarter and 0.6% in the first. According to Reuters, the figure matched the consensus forecast in its poll and was the strongest calendar quarter since the first quarter of 2024. Annual growth was 1.9%, per Investing.com's report of the release, the fastest since the end of 2024.

## A better month than anyone expected

The more interesting number sat inside the quarter. GDP rose 0.2% in September, against a Reuters-poll consensus of no growth at all, according to Investing.com. Rob Wood of Pantheon Macroeconomics put it this way: "Something is stirring: monthly GDP beat consensus for the fifth straight month and the seventh in eight." When an economy beats the forecasters in seven of eight months, the sensible conclusion is that the forecasters are the ones with the problem.

Services did the work. City AM reported that services output rose 0.9% over the three months, while production rose 0.1% and construction fell 0.7%. Grant Fitzner, the ONS chief economist, said: "The economy grew robustly in the third quarter almost entirely due to strong and widespread growth in the services sector." On the cause, he added: "We continue to see evidence that AI-linked businesses helped to boost this sector." The statistics office, which is rarely given to rhetoric, said separately that there was "widespread strength in industries which may be related to artificial intelligence."

## Where the growth did not appear

Investing.com reported that business investment rose only 0.4% on the quarter, the weakest this year and half the pace economists had expected. It is an awkward detail for a story about technological transformation. Firms are apparently benefitting from artificial intelligence long before they have decided how much to spend on it. The growth is real, but it is not yet the sort that gets built in factories and on balance sheets.

The report also placed the UK second in the G7 for growth in the quarter, behind the United States. For a country whose economy has been described as "resilient" at roughly the frequency of weather forecasts, being second is acceptable. It is, at least, a ranking that does not require a footnote.

## The problem that growth creates

It is easy to read a stronger economy as unambiguously welcome, and ministers will do exactly that in their next statement to the House. Here it complicates things. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate by a quarter point to 4% last week, and Investing.com reported that the Bank had upgraded its 2026 growth forecast to 1.6%, against the Office for Budget Responsibility's 1.2%. The same article cited LSEG data that put the probability of a December rate hike at 73%, and noted the Bank's warning that inflation would approach 5% in 2027, with household energy bills set to rise by about 25% in January.

Wood said the data raised the chances of a December hike. In other words, the faster the economy grows, the more the central bank feels able to tighten, and the tighter policy becomes, the sooner the growth is likely to slow. Scott Gardner of J.P. Morgan Personal Investing offered a reasonable summary of the mood: "that strength will be tested in the months ahead."

## The forecasts disagree about next year

City AM noted that the OBR expects growth to fall to 1% in 2027, while the National Institute of Economic and Social Research expects 1.1%. A fall to roughly 1% from the 1.6% the Bank of England forecasts for this year is not a collapse, but it is the shape of a slowdown. Governments are fond of describing such trajectories as "moderating." The word has the advantage of sounding like a decision.

## The closing observation

The central bank wanted a strong economy and an excuse. It has been given both. The danger is that it will treat Thursday's number as a licence to keep tightening into a year that its own forecasters expect to be softer. Central banks are traditionally accused of being late. Occasionally, they have the opposite difficulty: they are so pleased with the quarter they just read about that they forget it is already history.
