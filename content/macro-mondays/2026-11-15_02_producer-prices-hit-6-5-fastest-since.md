# Producer Prices Hit 6.5%, Fastest Since 2022, as Fed Hawks Circle

US producer prices rose 6.5% in the twelve months to October, the largest annual increase since November 2022, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics release published on Friday and summarised by StockTitan. Economists had expected 6.6%, so the figure counts as a small relief, in the way that a slightly smaller bill is a relief.

The monthly numbers were somewhat gentler. The producer price index for final demand rose 0.3% in October against a consensus of 0.4%, per Dow Jones estimates cited by StockTitan. September was revised up to 1.4% from 1.3%, which is the sort of footnote that makes the following month look moderate by comparison. The annual rate in September was 6.3%.

## The core question

Core producer prices, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month, in line with forecasts, after a 0.8% jump in September. The annual core rate stood at 5.0%. A narrower measure that also strips out trade margins rose 0.5%, against an expected 0.3%, which suggests that the underlying cost pressure is not confined to the volatile categories.

Energy prices fell 0.1% in October, so energy is not the culprit this time. The BLS said that prices for final demand services moved up 0.2%, and StockTitan notes that truck freight rose 3.9%, a figure that tends to arrive at the supermarket in due course. The pattern is of a pipeline filled by goods and transport rather than by oil.

Producer prices are not consumer prices, and the lag between them is neither fixed nor guaranteed. But the consumer data this week did nothing to reassure. October consumer prices rose 0.4% overall and 0.3% on the core measure, against expectations of 0.3% and 0.2%, according to StockTitan's summary of that release.

## The central bank gets the message

Federal Reserve officials have not been shy. Governor Christopher Waller said on November 10 that he leans toward a quarter-point increase in December, and that a half-point move "should be considered if core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher", per StockTitan. Core CPI came in at 0.3%. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said at least two more quarter-point hikes are needed, Boston's Susan Collins said on Friday that further tightening is warranted soon, and San Francisco's Mary Daly said her bias is that more will be needed, according to Investrade and CappNotes.

Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman put her estimate of core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, at 3.3% for the twelve months to October, per StockTitan. The central bank held its policy rate at 3.75%-4.00% on October 28, with two officials voting for a hike. The next decision is on December 9.

Markets have taken the hint. Investrade puts the probability of a 25 basis point December hike at about 88%, with about 12% for a half-point move. One market summary also cites the prediction market Kalshi at 91.5% for a quarter-point hike. In central banking terms, that is as close to a pre-announcement as the institution permits itself. The ten-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.152%.

## Expectations are the other half of the story

The University of Michigan's preliminary November survey showed year-ahead inflation expectations at 4.3%, down from 4.7% in October, with long-run expectations at 3.4%, per Kiplinger. That is an improvement and not a cure. An economy where households expect inflation to remain above 3% over the long run is one in which the central bank's credibility is being quietly tested at every meeting.

The coming week will add evidence in both directions. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks on Monday, retail sales for October are due on Tuesday, and the minutes of the October FOMC meeting arrive on Wednesday afternoon, per Kiplinger. The minutes will reveal how many officials were prepared to hike last time, and how many are prepared to explain why they did not.

## Reading the room

Governments traditionally describe a 6.5% producer inflation rate as "moderating", provided it has fallen by a tenth of a point. The central bank, to its credit, has chosen not to use that vocabulary. It is using the vocabulary of hikes.

The honest summary is this. Prices at the factory gate are rising at a pace last seen in 2022, the Fed has signalled that it intends to respond, and the bond market has already done so on its own. The only participants still waiting for confirmation are those who plan to be surprised, and procurement departments, who will be informed by invoice.
