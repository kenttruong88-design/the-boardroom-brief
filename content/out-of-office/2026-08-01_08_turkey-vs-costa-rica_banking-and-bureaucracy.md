# Turkey Will Open Your Bank Account In An Afternoon. Costa Rica Will Take Three Months And Call It "Pura Vida."

🇹🇷 Turkey + 🇨🇷 Costa Rica

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Turkey's banking bureaucracy, against every stereotype you might hold about Mediterranean pace, is startlingly efficient — get a tax ID number, walk into a branch with your passport, and you can walk out with a functioning account and a debit card the same afternoon. Costa Rica, a country that has built its entire national brand around the phrase "pura vida" — pure life, relax, it'll sort itself out — applies that same unhurried philosophy to bureaucracy with results that will test even the most patient expat's grip on their own sanity.

I opened a Turkish bank account faster than I've opened accounts in countries with reputations for German-level efficiency. I waited three months for a Costa Rican residency document that was, at every stage, described to me as "almost ready." Both experiences taught me something about national character. Only one of them taught me actual patience.

## Do's & Don'ts

**🇹🇷 Turkey**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Get your tax ID (vergi numarası) first — it's the single prerequisite for almost everything | Assume you need residency before opening a bank account; you generally don't |
| Bring your passport and a Turkish phone number to the branch in person | Expect English-language service at every branch outside major cities |
| Use a Turkish bank's app immediately — mobile banking here is genuinely excellent | Be surprised by high inflation-driven interest rates; ask about them upfront |
| Shop around between banks — service and English support vary significantly | Assume all paperwork can be done online; some steps still require a branch visit |

**🇨🇷 Costa Rica**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Start residency paperwork the moment you decide to stay long-term — it takes months | Expect any government timeline estimate to be accurate; pad it by double, minimum |
| Get certified, apostilled document translations before you begin, not after | Lose your temper at officials; "pura vida" patience is expected and reciprocated |
| Use a lawyer or gestor (paperwork facilitator) for residency — it's worth the fee | Assume a completed form means the process is close to finished; more steps always follow |
| Keep physical copies of every document — digital submissions aren't always trusted | Try to open a full bank account before residency; most banks require it first |

## Turkey: Bureaucratic Efficiency Nobody Warns You About

Turkey has a reputation, mostly inherited from broader stereotypes about the region, for languid pace and stacks of red tape, and the actual experience of opening a bank account there demolishes it almost immediately. The prerequisite is a vergi numarası — a tax identification number obtainable within minutes at a local tax office, often without an appointment — after which most major Turkish banks will open a full account, complete with a debit card and functioning mobile app, within the same day. Compare this to the multi-week account-opening sagas common in supposedly more "efficient" countries, and Turkey starts to look less like the bureaucratic minefield outsiders expect and more like a genuinely well-oiled system, at least for this specific process.

Where it gets more complicated is everything adjacent to banking — residency permits, property registration, and vehicle paperwork can still involve the kind of multi-office, multi-stamp runaround that fits the regional stereotype more closely. But the banking system itself, mobile apps included, is modern, fast, and increasingly digital-first, and Turkish banks have become notably savvy about serving the country's large foreign resident population, particularly in Istanbul. The one genuine catch is inflation — Turkey's economic volatility means interest rates and currency stability are worth understanding thoroughly before committing significant funds, a conversation worth having explicitly with your bank rather than assuming standard European or American norms apply.

## Costa Rica: Pura Vida, Except For Your Paperwork

Costa Rica's national ethos of unhurried contentment is, without question, one of the most genuinely appealing cultural exports of the last few decades — and it is an absolute nightmare when applied to residency paperwork. Every stage of the process, from initial application to final approval, tends to take significantly longer than any official estimate suggests, and "almost ready" or "just a few more weeks" from an immigration official can mean, in practice, anywhere from one month to eight. This isn't incompetence so much as a fundamentally different relationship with time and urgency — the same cultural trait that makes Costa Rica a genuinely wonderful place to slow down and live also makes it a genuinely maddening place to need a document by a specific date.

The practical workaround, and nearly every long-term expat I've spoken to through Internations and expat.com eventually arrives at this same conclusion, is to hire a lawyer or gestor — a paperwork facilitator who understands exactly which office needs which stamp in which order, and who can navigate the system with a fraction of the frustration an outsider would experience alone. Banking, meanwhile, is tightly coupled to residency status; most Costa Rican banks require proof of legal residency before opening a full account, which means the banking wait is really just the residency wait wearing a different hat. Document translation and apostille requirements add another substantial layer, and getting these done correctly before submission, rather than scrambling to fix them after a rejection, saves months of frustration that "pura vida" will not make any less real.

## The Verdict

Turkey's banking bureaucracy is a genuine, pleasant surprise — fast, modern, and refreshingly free of the runaround its regional reputation suggests, at least for this one specific process. Costa Rica's residency bureaucracy is exactly as slow as its national philosophy implies, a system that has fully internalised "it'll happen when it happens" and applies that logic to your paperwork whether you're ready for it or not. If you need to bank fast, Turkey wins comfortably. If you need to actually relax about the process because losing your temper achieves precisely nothing, Costa Rica will teach you that lesson whether you signed up for it or not.

## Conclusion

Turkey will hand you a functioning bank account before you've even found your footing, a rare and genuinely underrated bureaucratic win. Costa Rica will hand you the phrase "pura vida" roughly forty times while your residency paperwork quietly gathers dust in an office you'll never see, and somehow you'll come out the other side more relaxed about it than you'd have thought possible. Get your Turkish tax number the first week you arrive. Hire a Costa Rican gestor the moment you decide to stay, and then, genuinely, try to mean it when you say pura vida — you'll be saying it a lot before this is over.
