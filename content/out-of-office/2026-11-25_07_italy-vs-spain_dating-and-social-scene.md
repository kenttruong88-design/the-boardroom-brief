# Italy Wants a Candlelit Commitment and Spain Wants a Late Afternoon Drink

🇮🇹 Italy vs 🇪🇸 Spain

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Everybody assumes that dating in southern Europe is one long Fellini film. It is not. It is a series of misunderstandings conducted in two beautiful languages, with the added complication that the people you are dating have a mother, a cousin, a flat they are not leaving and a profound conviction that you will be late.

Italy and Spain have an international reputation for romance, and both have done very little to correct it. But anyone who has actually tried to get a date out of either has discovered that these are two completely different games. One is a theatrical slow burn with a last-minute plot twist. The other is an afternoon at a terrace table that mysteriously becomes a relationship. I will explain, in order of how much your pride will suffer.

## Do's & Don'ts

| 🇮🇹 Italy ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Learn some Italian before you start messaging | Rely on a translation app to carry a conversation |
| Dress well, because effort is read as respect | Turn up in your gym kit and expect charm to rescue you |
| Expect family to be part of the picture sooner than you think | Assume your date's mother is an irrelevant detail |
| Take dinner seriously and order properly | Ask for a cappuccino after your meal and watch a waiter wince |

| 🇪🇸 Spain ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Try tardeo, the afternoon drinks that bleed into evening | Insist on a formal seven-thirty reservation |
| Expect to split the bill, and take it as normal | Insist on paying and read their offer to split as a rejection |
| Arrive fifteen to thirty minutes late and consider it punctual | Turn up exactly on time and sit alone for half an hour |
| Meet in public the first time and keep your wits | Send money or details to someone you met online |

## 🇮🇹 Italy

Italy is where romance goes to be judged. The country takes courtship seriously, and by that I mean it treats every first date as an audition for a longer play. Looks, clothes, manners, family, food: everything is on the table, including the table. Dinner is the preferred format, late, unhurried and involving a degree of mutual study that would embarrass a customs officer.

The first obstacle for a foreigner is language, and it is large. A Ghanaian student in language school told an Expat.com thread he joined an Italian-only dating site, could not read it, relied on a translation tool and ended up deleting the profile. A man who had just moved from London to Rome asked how to approach women while learning Italian, which is the most British question imaginable. Another confessed to speaking only English and wanting to learn. The lesson is blunt: dating in Italy in English is dating on hard mode, and Italians will notice the effort and reward it.

The pace is a surprise. Italians do not do casual with the ease of the Spanish, and the family dimension arrives early. It is common, as everyone who has tried to date a thirty-something in Milan can tell you, for the other party to still live near, or with, their parents, and for the mother to have views. I will not claim to measure it with figures, but I will say the speed at which a flirtation becomes a Sunday lunch invitation is alarming. Be warned: you are not just dating one person. You are dating a food culture, a dynasty and a very short list of acceptable pasta shapes.

The other Italian wrinkle is rhythm. Dinner is late, the evening stretches, and the passeggiata, the early evening stroll, is an old social machine in which looking and being looked at are part of the point. Nobody pretends this is accidental. It is courtship as a performance of presence. In Italy you do not simply meet someone; you appear, repeatedly, in the places where they are likely to appear, until the universe agrees to introduce you. I find this charming in principle and exhausting in practice, especially if you have a deadline and one pair of decent shoes.

## 🇪🇸 Spain

Spain has a different method, which is to remove the pressure by making everything late, loose and social. The great institution is tardeo, afternoon drinks that begin after lunch and quietly turn into an evening, and one forum member who lives there called it a normal way to meet new people through friends. The scene runs on groups. You meet someone at a language exchange, a terrace, a friend's birthday, and by the third drink you are in a conversation you did not plan. A long-term resident said it varies by region, with the south more affectionate and the north more reserved, and that Tinder and Bumble dominate, with Bumble popular among expats.

The etiquette is forgiving. People arrive ten to thirty minutes late and call it on time, according to both a forum member and Expatica, and splitting the bill is the usual practice. One guide even notes that some men may read an offer to split as a sign you are not interested, so the rule is not quite as egalitarian as it looks. First dates should be in public, and nobody should send money to a stranger. The country is also, statistically, very single: Expatica cites 36.5 per cent of residents aged 16 and over as single, and 58.6 per cent among 25 to 44-year-olds, a record.

What changes when love gets serious is law, not feelings. Couples often move in together after two or three years, and unmarried partners lack automatic rights to shared pensions, inheritance or hospital decisions, which is a deeply unromantic fact that nobody mentions on a terrace. Spain gets the fun part right and the paperwork wrong. But it is still the better country for the first six months, when you want to meet people and have no idea what you are doing.

One last fact about Spain, since this is the kind of thing nobody tells you at the bar. The guides note that dating websites such as Badoo, Meetic, OkCupid and Parship are widely used alongside Tinder, Hinge and Bumble, and that a matching app called Bumpy covers more than 150 countries. It is a very organised country behind its ease. Spain looks relaxed because it has outsourced the stress to the apps and the paperwork, and the paperwork, as we have seen, begins only when you stop having fun. The first half of the relationship is a terrace. The second half is a notary.

## The Verdict

If you are single, curious and not in a hurry, Spain wins by a distance. The social structure is built to help strangers collide, the rules are loose, and the lateness is so universal that it stops being rude. You can attend a tardeo with a stranger and leave with six new friends and one possible date. That is efficiency of a sort.

Italy is the better choice if you want something serious and are willing to learn the language, the family and the proper time to order coffee. It is a harder game, with higher stakes, and its rewards are deeper. The Italians are more traditional, more formal and more likely to ask what your intentions are before the dessert arrives.

I give it to Spain, because I believe a dating scene should make an anxious person feel welcome. Italy makes you work. Spain hands you a drink and lets you work it out. I admire Italy the way I admire an opera: beautiful, demanding and best seen from a good seat. Spain is the party after.

## Conclusion

Here is what both countries share. They both believe romance is a social activity, that food is a precondition and that a person who does not make an effort will be quietly sent home. Neither believes in the American idea of a perfectly timed, efficient date with a schedule and an exit strategy.

So learn the language in Italy, learn patience in Spain and learn to eat late in both. Take a friend. Bring an appetite. And if someone tells you they will be there in ten minutes, put your phone down and order a drink. They are going to be forty. It is not a lie. It is a time zone.
