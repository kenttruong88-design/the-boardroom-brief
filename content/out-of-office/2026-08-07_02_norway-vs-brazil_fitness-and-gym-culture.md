# Snow Squats and Sand Sit-Ups: Two Nations That Refuse to Work Out Indoors Like Normal People

🇳🇴 Norway vs 🇧🇷 Brazil — Fitness and Gym Culture

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

There is a particular smugness shared by Norwegians and Brazilians that has nothing to do with wealth, football, or fjords, and everything to do with the fact that both nations have decided the gym is optional and the outdoors is not. Everywhere else on earth, fitness culture eventually retreats indoors, to air conditioning and mirrors and the faint smell of eucalyptus spray. Not here. Norway will have you cross-country skiing to the office. Brazil will have you doing pull-ups on public beach equipment in front of several hundred extremely toned strangers. Neither nation will accept "it's cold" or "I'm self-conscious" as an excuse, because both have engineered entire societies to make excuses structurally impossible.

The difference, as ever, is temperature and ego. Norway's fitness culture is quiet, solitary, borderline monastic — you suffer alone, in silence, in minus twelve degrees, and you like it. Brazil's is a full-contact spectator sport where the beach is both gym and runway, and everyone is unbothered by being watched, because they assume, correctly, that they look magnificent.

## Do's & Don'ts

### 🇳🇴 Norway

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Invest in proper winter gear before you attempt any outdoor training | Assume a UK or US puffer jacket will survive a Norwegian January run |
| Try friluftsliv — hiking, skiing, just being outside — as a legitimate substitute for a gym membership | Expect small talk at the gym; Norwegians train in near-total silence |
| Join SATS or a similar chain if you want indoor options, but expect to pay for the privilege | Be shocked by the price — Norwegian gym memberships are not for the faint of wallet |
| Respect the unspoken queue for equipment; nobody will say anything, they will just judge you | Hover impatiently near someone's machine; wait it out like a Norwegian |

### 🇧🇷 Brazil

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use the free public gym equipment along Rio's beachfront — it's genuinely excellent | Assume it's empty or unwatched; you will be evaluated, kindly but thoroughly |
| Try functional or calisthenics classes on the sand at sunrise, when the city trains as one | Skip sunscreen; Brazilian beach workouts do not pause for weather |
| Embrace the social side — beach fitness is a community event, not a solo mission | Wear noise-cancelling headphones and expect to be left entirely alone |
| Learn a few words of encouragement in Portuguese; strangers will cheer you on mid-set | Take offense at unsolicited fitness advice from a stranger doing burpees next to you |

## Norway: Suffering as a Lifestyle Choice

Norwegian fitness culture cannot be separated from friluftsliv, the national concept of "open-air living" that treats being outside, in any weather, as a moral good rather than a hobby. Norwegians do not "go to the gym" so much as they occasionally deign to visit one when the alternative — skiing to work, hiking a fjord before breakfast, running along a frozen lake — isn't available. Children are put on skis before they can properly walk. Adults treat a casual weekend hike as something closer to a religious observance than exercise.

Indoor gym culture, where it exists, mirrors the national character: quiet, orderly, expensive, and almost aggressively unsocial. SATS and similar chains are clean and well-equipped, but do not expect a Norwegian gym-goer to make eye contact, let alone conversation. Everyone is there to do their reps and leave, ideally without acknowledging another human's existence. Membership costs will make you gasp audibly, which is the only sound you are likely to make in the building without disturbing someone.

What Norway does better than almost anywhere on earth is make fitness free, structural, and unavoidable. You don't need a membership to cross-country ski through a national park at dawn. You need boots, a total absence of self-pity, and roughly four layers of merino wool.

## Brazil: Fitness as Performance and Community

Brazil, and Rio de Janeiro in particular, has turned the beach into the world's largest and best-attended open-air gym. Municipal fitness stations line the Orla Rio boardwalk — pull-up bars, parallel bars, rings — free, public, and in near-constant use from sunrise onward. Nobody trains alone for long; strangers spot each other, correct each other's form, and shout encouragement mid-set with a familiarity that would feel invasive anywhere else and here feels like community.

Beach volleyball, footvolley, functional circuits on the sand, capoeira circles — Brazilian fitness culture treats exercise as inherently social and mildly theatrical. You are not hiding your effort behind headphones; you are part of a shared ritual that happens to also be an unofficial beauty pageant. The famous Brazilian confidence around bodies of every shape training in public isn't performative modesty, it's a genuine cultural comfort with being seen mid-effort, sweating, imperfect, and unbothered.

The tradeoff is heat, humidity, and a total absence of Norwegian solitude. If you want to disappear into your own head during a workout, Rio's beachfront will not allow it. Someone will talk to you. Someone will probably outperform you effortlessly while doing so.

## The Verdict

Norway wins on infrastructure for solitary self-improvement and sheer willpower; nowhere else makes suffering through the cold feel so wholesome. But Brazil wins on joy, community, and the fact that its free public gyms cost less and deliver more genuine human connection than a Norwegian gym membership ever will. If fitness is meant to make you feel alive rather than merely disciplined, Rio's beachfront beats Oslo's silent treadmills without breaking a sweat — which, admittedly, is more than can be said for anyone actually using them.

## Conclusion

Norway will teach you that comfort is optional and that a body can be trained to love the cold, eventually, against its will. Brazil will teach you that fitness is a communal act best performed loudly, publicly, and with an audience that will cheer for you regardless of your form. Pick Norway if you want to build character through suffering in silence. Pick Brazil if you want to build a body while making six new friends before 8 a.m. Either way, you will finish more capable than when you started, and considerably more tired than you expected.
