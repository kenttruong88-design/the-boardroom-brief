# Karachi Has a Two-Week Winter and Canada Has a Six-Month Personality

🇵🇰 Pakistan vs 🇨🇦 Canada

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Pakistan is a country of extremes in which winter is usually the polite one. Karachi's cold season is, in the words of one Pakistani writer in Canada, barely a two-week fling. A few chilly evenings, a borrowed sweater, a hot cup of tea in the open air, and then back to the heat. Canada's winter is a different category of event. It is a six-month installation, with an entire national culture built around surviving it.

I have spent a winter in Toronto and a winter in Lahore and I can tell you they are both called winter for administrative reasons only. One is a quiet pause in a warm year. The other is a way of life, a wardrobe, a vocabulary and a set of obligations to your car, your neighbours and your driveway.

## Do's & Don'ts

| 🇵🇰 Pakistan ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Enjoy the short cool season outdoors with tea | Expect it to last |
| Keep a shawl or light jacket for evenings | Pack a parka for Karachi |
| Plan travel in the cooler months | Plan anything outdoors at noon in summer |
| Learn what changes in the north, where winters are real | Assume the whole country is hot |

| 🇨🇦 Canada ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Buy proper winter boots, a coat and gloves before the first snowfall | Wear your home-country jacket in January |
| Fit winter tyres and keep a blanket, flashlight and road salt in the car | Assume the roads will clear themselves |
| Shovel your path and salt your steps | Leave ice on the pavement outside your home |
| Join the winter markets and outdoor rinks | Hibernate until April |

## 🇵🇰 Pakistan

Pakistan's relationship with winter is local and seasonal. Tazeen Rizvi, writing for the Express Tribune in 2013, called Karachi's winter barely a two-week fling. That is a perfect description. The city goes mild for a short stretch, people wear their cardigans with pride and the streets are suddenly pleasant for a morning walk. Then summer returns, heavy and humid, and the cardigans go back in the cupboard.

The north is another country. Mountain regions have snow, cold and a proper winter lifestyle, with heaters, blankets, hot drinks and a shared sense of enduring. But for most Pakistani city dwellers, cold is a brief novelty and heat is the real adversary. The coping rules are therefore about summer: shade, fans, cold drinks, early mornings and evening outings. The whole social calendar bends around the sun.

That is important for expats arriving in Canada, because the winter they imagine is the winter they have met at home. A chilly Karachi evening is not a preview of anything. A first Canadian January is a shock, because the cold does not arrive as an event. It becomes the background of life, along with darkness, ice and a long wait for light.

For Pakistanis who emigrate, winter is also an emotional marker. Rizvi's piece is called, in full, how she misses Karachi's winter season, and the nostalgia comes through. The cold is not just the weather. It is the first big test of whether a newcomer can build a life somewhere that tries to freeze them.

It is also worth remembering how many people in Pakistan are used to improvised comfort. The tea stall, the shawl, the shared blanket and the charcoal heater are all versions of the same instinct that gets a Canadian family through January. The tools are different but the habit is the same: gather, warm up and keep the kettle on. Pakistani newcomers often adapt faster than they expect, because the social half of winter, the half that involves feeding people, is something they already know how to do.

## 🇨🇦 Canada

Canada's winter is real, long and organised. Rizvi describes waiting for a bus in minus 30 degrees Celsius, a highway salted for safety and a determination to shovel until the last winter blast passes in late March. Her winter kit for the car includes winter tyres, washer fluid, a blanket, a candle, a flashlight and a huge bag of coarse road salt. That list is not paranoia. It is the standard Canadian packing list.

A forum poster called PEI Red, replying to a newcomer on Expat.com, says temperatures run from minus 3 to minus 45 or 50 and that summers can top 40 degrees with humidity, which is a reminder that Canada is not simply cold. The advice is to keep two wardrobes and to buy winter clothes locally because they come with temperature guarantees. The same poster adds that Canadians talk about the weather constantly, mostly in a good-natured way, and that complaints are rarely serious.

That is the part outsiders miss. Canadians do not suffer in silence. They talk about it, share tips, swap stories of near misses with black ice and then carry on. The whole country coordinates its response. Roads are ploughed, pavements are salted, buses run and schools almost never close. Winter is a challenge and also a shared project.

A Narcity writer named Janice Rodrigues, who moved to Toronto from Dubai in 2022, says her previous idea of winter was a balmy 15 degrees. She found the darkness after 5pm in November more discouraging than the cold itself, and she describes weather that cycles through sunshine, rain, freezing rain, flurries and snow in a single week. What kept her going were winter markets and outdoor ice rinks. That is Canada's secret: the cold is endured and also, deliberately, enjoyed.

The practical rules are worth repeating because newcomers learn them the hard way. Winter tyres are a safety measure, not an upsell. Layers beat one thick coat. Boots with proper grip matter more than boots with fashion. Sunlight in short winter days is a mood medicine, so you go outside even when you do not want to. None of this is glamorous. It is the quiet engineering of getting through a cold season intact, and it is exactly what the Canadian winter teaches anyone who stays long enough.

## The Verdict

Canada wins, which is not a statement of preference so much as one of design. A country that has a winter of that scale has to be good at it, and Canada is. It has built the equipment, the habits and the humour required to turn a hostile season into a social one.

Pakistan wins on warmth, in every sense. Nobody in Karachi needs a salt bag in their boot, and a winter that lasts two weeks is a winter you can love. But it does not demand much of its people, and as a result does not give them much of a coping culture to export.

If you are moving from one to the other, give the cold a fair hearing. It is miserable for the first month and surprisingly manageable by the third. Buy the right boots, join the rink and accept that the person who shovels their path before you do will be unbearably pleased with themselves. They have earned it.

## Conclusion

Winter is not a weather report. It is a test of what a place has taught its people to do with discomfort. Pakistan teaches its people to wait for the short cool and enjoy it. Canada teaches its people to go outside anyway.

So pack for the season you are walking into, not the one you are leaving. Carry a hat, keep your boots by the door and learn the Canadian art of talking about the weather as a form of friendship. And when it all gets too much, sit down with a cup of hot tea and think of Karachi in January, which will be about 20 degrees and entirely unaware of you.
