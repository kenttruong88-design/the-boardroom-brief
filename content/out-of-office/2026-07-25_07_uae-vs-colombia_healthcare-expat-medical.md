# One Country Hands You a Bill Like a Restaurant Menu, the Other Hands You a Waiting List

🇦🇪 UAE vs 🇨🇴 Colombia

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

In Dubai, I once received an itemised hospital invoice so detailed it listed the individual cotton swabs used during a minor procedure, priced with the confidence of a five-star hotel minibar. In Bogotá, I registered with the public EPS system, sat in a waiting room for three hours next to a grandmother knitting something enormous, and left having paid almost nothing at all. Both systems produced competent care. Only one of them made me feel like I was being quietly financially interrogated the entire time.

The UAE has built a healthcare system around private efficiency and eye-watering transparency of cost. Colombia has built one around a surprisingly functional public-private hybrid that, chaos and all, tends to work out fine for those who learn to navigate it.

## Do's & Don'ts

### 🇦🇪 UAE

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Secure mandatory health insurance before your visa is finalised | Assume basic plans cover much beyond emergencies |
| Compare private hospital networks carefully — quality varies widely | Skip reading the policy's exclusions list |
| Keep every receipt for insurance reimbursement claims | Expect public hospitals to be the default for expats |
| Ask about direct billing arrangements to avoid upfront payment | Assume all specialists are covered under a standard plan |

### 🇨🇴 Colombia

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Enrol in the EPS system as a resident — it's mandatory and affordable | Expect private-hospital speed from public EPS appointments |
| Consider supplementary private insurance (medicina prepagada) for speed | Assume "public" means low quality — it often isn't |
| Learn which EPS providers have the best reputations in your city | Ignore wait times for non-urgent specialist referrals |
| Use private clinics for urgent or complex needs if you can afford it | Assume the system works identically in every region |

## UAE: World-Class Care, Priced Like It Knows It

Healthcare in the UAE, particularly in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, operates almost entirely through private providers, and the quality reflects the investment — state-of-the-art hospitals, short wait times, specialists flown in from everywhere, and facilities that could pass for luxury hotels with better lighting. Mandatory health insurance for residents ensures nobody's technically uncovered, but the gap between "technically covered" and "actually comfortable" can be enormous depending on your policy tier.

The billing culture here rewards vigilance. Itemised invoices arrive for everything, insurance networks vary wildly in which hospitals and specialists they'll actually cover, and the basic plans many employers provide can leave you exposed to costs that arrive as genuine shocks. Direct billing arrangements between insurers and hospitals smooth this considerably when they exist, but confirming coverage before treatment, rather than after, is a habit every long-term expat eventually learns the hard way.

What's genuinely impressive is the speed. Specialist appointments that might take weeks elsewhere happen within days here, and the private system's efficiency means healthcare rarely becomes a source of scheduling anxiety, only financial anxiety. Public healthcare exists for citizens but is largely inaccessible or impractical for most expats, making the private system the default by necessity rather than preference.

The overall experience is one of genuine competence wrapped in constant, low-level cost awareness — every appointment carries a faint mental cash register sound in the background, tallying up as you go, however good the care actually is.

## Colombia: Public, Chaotic, and Somehow Fine

Colombia's healthcare system runs on the EPS model — a mandatory, income-based public-private hybrid that, despite its reputation for bureaucratic frustration, generally delivers solid basic care at a fraction of what expats from more privatised systems expect to pay. Enrolling as a resident is required and genuinely affordable, and while the system has real weaknesses, it's far more functional than its chaotic reputation suggests once you understand how to work within it.

The trade-off for affordability is patience. Wait times for non-urgent specialist referrals through EPS can stretch into weeks or months, and the system's efficiency varies significantly depending on which EPS provider and which city you're in — Bogotá and Medellín generally offer more robust options than smaller regional centres. This has given rise to a thriving supplementary private insurance market, medicina prepagada, which many expats and increasingly many locals layer on top of EPS to skip the queue for anything beyond basic care.

Private clinics, when paid for directly or through prepagada, offer speed and comfort closer to what UAE expats take for granted, at costs that remain dramatically lower than fully private Western or Gulf systems. This dual-track approach — affordable public floor, optional private ceiling — gives Colombia a flexibility that pure private or pure public systems often lack.

What foreigners consistently underestimate is how good the actual clinical care can be, public or private. Colombian doctors are well-trained, often internationally educated, and the stereotype of Latin American healthcare as somehow lesser dissolves quickly once you're actually inside the system rather than assuming from outside it.

## The Verdict

The UAE offers speed and luxury at a price that demands constant vigilance. Colombia offers affordability and genuine competence at the cost of your patience for queues. Neither system is objectively superior — they're built for entirely different national economics and entirely different expat budgets.

If money isn't the primary constraint and you want white-glove speed, Dubai's private hospitals will never once make you wait, though they will make you check your policy twice. If your budget matters and you're willing to learn a system that rewards patience with real savings, Bogotá's EPS will treat you fairly, eventually, and rarely bankrupt you in the process. Suki's verdict: Colombia wins on value and quiet competence, the UAE wins on pure convenience for anyone who values their time over their bank balance — and I've been grateful for both, usually while regretting my life choices in an airport lounge somewhere between the two.

## Conclusion

The UAE will care for you brilliantly and bill you for the privilege down to the last cotton swab. Colombia will care for you affordably and ask, mostly, for your patience instead of your credit card. Move to Dubai and read every line of your insurance policy before you need it. Move to Bogotá and enrol in EPS the moment you land, then consider prepagada the moment you can afford it. Both systems work. Only one of them will make you flinch when the invoice arrives.
