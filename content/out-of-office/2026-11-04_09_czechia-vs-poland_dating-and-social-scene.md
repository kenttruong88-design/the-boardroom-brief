# Czechia Is Having Its Fewest Babies Since 1785 and Blaming the Men. Poland Just Assumes You're Exclusive by the Second Date.

🇨🇿 Czechia vs 🇵🇱 Poland
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Czechia had its lowest birth count since records began in 1785 this past year — a fourth straight year of decline — and a demographer at Masaryk University has a specific, almost clinical theory: up to half of Czech men aged 30 to 39 reportedly admit they lack the basic ability to form a relationship. Poland has the opposite problem entirely: dating moves so fast that exclusivity is often assumed from the first date, no negotiation required, which means a newcomer fresh off a Western "talking stage" can accidentally become a cheater within the week without ever being told the rules changed.

One country can't get its dating culture started. The other starts it before you've finished your coffee. I've relocated fourteen times and never once whiplashed this hard between two neighboring countries.

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect "who pays" to be genuinely unsettled — a street survey found 45% favor splitting evenly, 31% say the man pays, 19% say whoever asked | Expect dating apps to be the main event — one Prague local calls them "a waste of time that only leads you to garage flats in Timbuktu" |
| Know the real local-friendship numbers before judging your own slow start — only 31% of expats say locals are friendly, and 59% find it hard to make local friends | Expect fast progress if you don't speak Czech — the country ranks last of 31 surveyed destinations for ease of learning the local language |
| Try the registered-cohabitation route if marriage feels premature — 238,000 people now do this, a fivefold rise over 50 years | Mistake Prague's nightlife reputation for warmth — the city is actively campaigning against its own "party capital" image and has banned organized pub crawls in its historic center |

## Czechia: A Demographic Crisis With a Named Culprit

Czechia's dating problem has graduated from cultural observation to measurable national statistic. Births fell to 77,636 in 2025 — the lowest annual figure since records began in 1785 — the fourth consecutive year of decline, down 8% from 2024 and a full 31% from the 2021 peak, with the total fertility rate sliding from 1.83 to 1.28 over that same span. Demographer Martin Kreidl of Masaryk University puts a specific, almost uncomfortable number on the suspected cause: up to half of Czech men aged 30 to 39 admit they lack the ability to form a relationship, with women increasingly describing male partners as feeling like "another child" rather than an equal. It's rare to find a country where the dating-app complaints and the national statistics office are, essentially, telling the same story.

The InterNations Expat Insider 2026 survey backs up the texture of that difficulty from the expat side: Czechia ranks 25th of 31 destinations overall and a dismal 27th specifically for "Ease of Settling In" — only 31% of expats find locals friendly against a 61% global average, 59% report real difficulty making local friends, and 53% say most of their friends end up being other expats. The language barrier makes this worse, not incidental to it — Czechia ranks dead last of all 31 surveyed destinations for ease of learning the local language, with 77% of expats struggling, which throttles the dating pool directly since so much genuine social life still runs in-language outside the expat bubble.

What's emerging instead of marriage is a quietly booming alternative: registered unmarried cohabiting couples have hit a record 238,000 in Czechia, roughly a fivefold increase over fifty years, more than half of whom have never married and about a third of whom are divorced. The lived texture, per Expats.cz's "Expat Voices" series, is a genuine mixed bag — an Italian respondent named Giulia says she can "never really tell" if a Czech man is interested at all; a Latino respondent named Roberto misses affection and struggles to find "warm" people; a Brazilian respondent named Alissa recounts a Czech date using a racial slur despite the country's progressive self-image, though she later found happiness with a different Czech-Canadian partner met online. Most tellingly, a Czech local herself, Adela, admits she "doesn't know where to meet people" in her own hometown and increasingly retreats into "my little bubble" — proof this isn't purely an outsider's complaint. Prague, meanwhile, was recently named Europe's best nightlife destination by a UK-based index, even as Prague City Tourism actively campaigns against that exact reputation, with Prague 1 banning organized nighttime pub crawls since October 2024 in favor of a rebrand toward "culture and gastronomy."

## Poland: Exclusive by Default, Family-Fast, and Quietly Changing Underneath

Poland's dating culture runs almost in the opposite direction, structurally fast rather than structurally stalled, though the official numbers show the same underlying shift away from marriage. Registered marriages fell from roughly 190,000 in 2015 to about 146,000 in 2023, even as cohabiting couples nearly tripled over a decade — 895,443 in the 2021 census versus 316,505 in 2011, cohabitation now making up almost 8% of all Polish families, up from roughly 3% a decade earlier, with western Poland running above 10% and the more traditional east sitting at just 5 to 6%.

What actually surprises a newly arrived professional is the pace of commitment assumed within an individual relationship, which runs almost opposite to the national marriage decline. Poles posting on Reddit describe exclusivity as the assumed default from essentially the first date, not something negotiated after a few weeks of ambiguity — seeing other people without an explicit agreement is treated outright as cheating, a real trap for anyone used to a Western "talking stage." Family involvement follows close behind: one dating-culture account describes meeting a partner's mother "by month two," especially around holidays, though big cities like Warsaw and Kraków run markedly more casual than small towns and traditional families. Older generational divides remain visible too — the same Reddit thread describes a rigid, if loosening, split where women handle cooking, cleaning and childcare while men handle cars and repairs, a division younger Poles are actively moving away from.

The two major cities offer genuinely different flavors of the social scene built around all this. Warsaw runs bigger and pricier — local beer at 12 to 18 złoty, cocktails 30 to 50, a real techno scene around Mazowiecka and Praga's indie venues, a metro that runs until roughly 3am on weekends. Kraków is cheaper and more walkable, beer at 10 to 16 złoty, a compact Old Town and Kazimierz bar-hopping scene, a medieval-cellar club called Prozak 2.0, heavier stag-party tourist traffic, and no metro at all. A Talesmag respondent in Kraków notes the city has "not a large expat community," most socializing happens via dinners and the Rynek's bar culture, and while gay and lesbian bars exist, they're "not widely advertised" in what remains, outside the large student population, a fairly conservative country overall.

## The Verdict

Czechia's dating scene is a genuine demographic mystery that even locals can't crack, documented in national birth statistics and personal confessions in equal measure. Poland's dating scene moves fast and assumes a lot, but at least the rules — however quickly applied — are consistent and learnable once you know them. Give me Poland's accelerated commitment over Czechia's stalled, mutually confused standoff — at least in Warsaw, somebody will actually ask you out.

## Conclusion

Both countries are, in their own statistically documented ways, moving away from marriage as the default — Czechia through cohabitation at scale, Poland through the same shift plus a falling marriage rate — and yet the day-to-day experience of dating in each couldn't be more different. Czechia will make you wait, wonder, and possibly give up entirely before anything resolves. Poland will make you commit before you've finished your second coffee. Pack patience for Prague. Pack a clear head about what "just dating" even means before you land in Warsaw.
