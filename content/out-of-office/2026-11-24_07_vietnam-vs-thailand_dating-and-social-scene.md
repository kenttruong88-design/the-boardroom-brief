# Vietnam Wants Your Family Tree, Thailand Just Wants Your Line ID

🇻🇳 Vietnam vs 🇹🇭 Thailand

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

I have relocated fourteen times and dated, with varying degrees of dignity, in about half of those places. Southeast Asia is where I learned that a country's love life is a better cultural education than any museum. Vietnam and Thailand sit next door to each other, share a monsoon and a worrying fondness for plastic stools, and could not be more different when it comes to meeting someone you actually like.

Here is the short version for the impatient. Thailand is easy to enter and hard to keep. Vietnam is hard to enter and, if you manage it, you will be introduced to an aunt within the month. Pick your poison, then read on for the paperwork.

## Do's & Don'ts

| 🇻🇳 Vietnam ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Learn a few phrases and show genuine interest in her family | Assume a first date means anything casual |
| Use Facebook, Zalo and Tinder, and be patient with the replies | Expect Western-style fast intimacy |
| Join language exchanges, hobby clubs and volunteering to meet people | Treat Saigon rules as the rules everywhere in the country |
| Dress decently and arrive on time, because first impressions travel | Make public displays of affection or loud jokes about her age or body |

| 🇹🇭 Thailand ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Swap Line contacts early, because everyone expects it | Send money to someone you only know from a screen |
| Discuss who pays before the bill arrives | Assume the man must always pay, or that he never will |
| Use social events, classes and conferences as well as bars | Get into a car with a stranger at 3am |
| Be clear about your intentions from the start | Mistake politeness and a smile for a yes |

## 🇻🇳 Vietnam

Dating in Vietnam is a slow-burn drama with a large supporting cast. Expat.com's Saigon forum threads describe a society where family comes first, gender roles stay fairly traditional, and a relationship is not really a private matter between two adults. It is a merger. One forum regular sorted local women into three rough camps: a traditional majority with little contact with foreigners, a smaller college-educated group who are curious, and a very small, properly westernised set who will happily date outside their own culture. If you are a foreigner, you are fishing in the second and third ponds, and the ponds are not large.

Be honest with yourself about the geography. Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi are far more open than anywhere else, and the "I dated a lovely woman in Saigon, so Vietnam is easy" story collapses the moment you leave a district with a craft beer bar. Outside the big cities, a foreigner holding hands in public with a local becomes the evening's main entertainment, and the commentary is not always kind. The forums are also blunt about the body-shaming, which is real. People will remark on weight, to your face, within seconds, and families do it to their own relatives.

Socially, though, Vietnam is a dream for anyone who likes being fed. Evenings revolve around pavement seafood, beer by the crate and a lot of "mot, hai, ba, yo". People invite you to things. A colleague's cousin's engagement party is a normal Tuesday. You will not be lonely in Vietnam; you may, however, be adopted by someone's grandmother, which is a different commitment.

The apps work, mostly. Tinder, Facebook groups and Zalo are the usual tools, and you will need patience, because replies arrive on a schedule known only to the sender. Be warned that the courtship tempo is genuinely slower than anything a Londoner would call acceptable: group outings first, then a coffee, then a motorbike ride that somehow involves three of her friends. Nobody is being coy. It is simply how trust is built here, in public and in company. The reward for effort is sincerity. When Vietnamese people say they like you, there is usually a plan attached, and the plan usually involves lunch with the relatives.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand

Thailand is the opposite sales pitch: friction-free at the front door, slightly baffling inside. Expat.com's guide notes that everyone swaps Line contacts almost immediately, that Tinder and Badoo are popular, and that nightlife districts in Bangkok, Phuket and Pattaya are enormous. You will meet people in Thailand with no effort at all. That is the problem. Meeting is not dating, and dating is not a relationship, and the three get confused here with spectacular regularity.

The dating pool tilts heavily. Foreign men with Thai women is a well-trodden path, and the guide says as much, while expat women report a thin and often disappointing selection. Pay for dinner rules are shifting, with more Thai women splitting costs, but the old expectation that the foreign man picks up the tab still lingers. Talk about it before the bill, not during. Also expect scam warnings to be a standing feature of the conversation. Anyone you have only met online who needs money for a ticket, a hospital or a sick buffalo should be reported and blocked, not pitied.

What Thailand does brilliantly is the social layer. Islands, rooftops, co-working spaces, muay thai gyms, language classes, night markets, and a nomad community in Chiang Mai and Bangkok that organises something every evening. Making friends is far easier than in Vietnam, and plenty of the friends are other expats on six-month tourist-visa rotations. That is the catch. Thailand's social scene is warm, fast and temporary, like a good hostel breakfast.

Traditional couples still involve family, holiday visits to the home province and a certain amount of "meeting the parents". Plenty of others prefer casual Western-style dating. In Thailand, you can find whichever you want, which also means you can be told one thing and discover another. Say what you want out loud, early, and pay attention to who listens. The honest upside is that nobody in Thailand will make you feel strange for wanting something simple, and the honest downside is that nobody will chase you down the street to correct a misunderstanding either. Grown-up dating, in other words, with the grown-up bits left for you to supply.

## The Verdict

Vietnam wins for anyone who wants a relationship that means something, and Thailand wins for anyone who wants a good time with no paperwork. That is not me being diplomatic. It is the actual trade. Anyone who tells you one country is simply better at romance has either never left the capital or has never been left in one.

Vietnam rewards effort and punishes tourism. You have to learn things, show up, meet the family and tolerate unsolicited weight commentary. In return, you get sincerity, loyalty and a city where people genuinely include you. Thailand rewards speed and punishes expectations. You will have a glorious social life by Friday and a deeply confusing romantic one by Sunday, and the ones who leave first are usually the ones who thought they were in love.

If you want to be wooed, wined and gently interrogated by an aunt, move to Vietnam. If you want friends, sunsets and a very low chance of anyone remembering your surname, move to Thailand. I know which one I would date in. I also know which one I would leave a pleasant note in.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *Expat.com forum (Saigon) — Posters agree that most Vietnamese women are traditional and rarely meet foreigners, so the dating pool is smaller than the Tinder screenshots suggest, and the more open ones are mostly in the big, western-leaning cities.*

> *Expat.com forum (Saigon) — Several members note that comments on weight and appearance are blunt and constant, and that families criticise their own relatives openly, so thick skin is part of the relocation package.*

> *Expat.com dating guide (Thailand) — Expat women report a limited pool of potential partners compared with foreign men, and the guide warns that anyone met online who suddenly needs money for tickets, shopping or medical bills may be running a scam.*

## Conclusion

Neither country is going to hand you a soulmate on a banana leaf, and anyone selling that fantasy is also selling a visa run. What they will do is expose how you behave when you are lonely, jet-lagged and far from anyone who knows you. Vietnam makes you slow down and be sincere, which some people find terrifying. Thailand makes it all effortless, which some people find even more terrifying once the effort stops.

My advice, after fourteen moves, is simple. Go where the social rules match what you can actually deliver. If you can commit, Vietnam will reward you. If you cannot, Thailand will let you pretend for a few months and send you off with a smile and a tidy Line contact list. Either way, learn the word for "no thank you" in both languages. You will use it on the karaoke invitations alone.
