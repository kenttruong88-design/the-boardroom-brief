# Athens Treats Parks as an Afterthought. Singapore Treats Them as Load-Bearing Infrastructure.

🇬🇷 Greece · 🇸🇬 Singapore
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Greece has some of the most staggering natural beauty on the planet and, somehow, some of the most neglected urban green space I've encountered anywhere — a National Garden in Athens that's genuinely lovely, surrounded by a city that otherwise treats parks as leftover space between buildings, dusty and under-watered by August. Singapore, a nation with almost no natural land to spare, decided decades ago that greenery wasn't optional, and built an entire "City in Nature" out of sheer bureaucratic willpower, stitching parks together with connector paths until the whole island functions like one enormous, meticulously maintained garden with a metropolis growing through it.

I have wilted in an Athens park in July, searching in vain for shade that wasn't already claimed by stray cats with better real estate instincts than me. I have also cycled for two uninterrupted hours through Singapore on park connectors without ever really leaving green cover, in a country smaller than most Athens suburbs put together.

## Do's & Don'ts

**🇬🇷 Greece**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Visit the National Garden early morning before the heat sets in | Expect much shade or upkeep in smaller neighbourhood parks |
| Seek out lesser-known green pockets like Pedion tou Areos for fewer crowds | Assume park infrastructure matches the beauty of the wider Greek landscape |
| Bring water — public fountains are inconsistent at best | Expect much in the way of organised park programming or events |

**🇸🇬 Singapore**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Download the NParks app and explore the park connector network | Litter — fines are real, enforced, and unsympathetic to "just this once" |
| Visit Gardens by the Bay early or late to avoid both heat and crowds | Assume all green space is "natural" — much of it is meticulously engineered |
| Try a park connector cycle route end-to-end at least once | Underestimate the humidity — even a "walk in the park" requires hydration planning |

## Greece: Natural Splendour, Urban Neglect

There's a peculiar irony to Greek green space: a country blessed with some of the most dramatic natural landscapes in Europe — the mountains, the coastlines, the light — somehow struggles to translate that abundance into well-maintained urban parks. Athens' National Garden, adjacent to the Greek Parliament and managed by the Municipality of Athens, is a genuine oasis — mature trees, ponds, peacocks wandering with an entitled air — but it's something of an exception rather than the rule. Venture into most neighbourhood parks and you'll find patchy grass, inconsistent maintenance, and shade that feels more accidental than designed.

The Greek Ministry of Environment and Energy has, to its credit, pushed urban green space initiatives in recent years, acknowledging that Greek cities lag behind European counterparts on per-capita green coverage. But policy ambition and lived reality remain some distance apart, particularly in the punishing heat of a Mediterranean summer, when a shadeless park becomes actively hostile rather than merely underwhelming. Pedion tou Areos, Athens' second-largest park, is a lovely, under-visited alternative to the tourist-heavy National Garden, but it too suffers from patchy upkeep that makes you wonder where exactly the maintenance budget goes.

What Greek green space lacks in polish, it makes up for in something harder to engineer: atmosphere. There's an unhurried, unselfconscious quality to how Greeks use their parks — elderly men playing tavli under whatever shade they can find, families picnicking well into the evening once the heat breaks, the whole scene feeling less like a managed civic amenity and more like an extension of neighbourhood life that happens to have some trees in it. It's charming. It's also, undeniably, a little threadbare compared to what's possible.

## Singapore: Nature as National Infrastructure Project

Singapore's approach to green space is the diametric opposite: a small, densely populated island with almost no spare land, deciding as a matter of deliberate policy that nature wasn't a luxury to be sacrificed to development, but core infrastructure to be engineered alongside it. NParks' "City in Nature" initiative isn't marketing language — it's a genuine, decades-long planning philosophy, and the results are honestly startling if you've come from almost anywhere else. Parks aren't isolated green squares; they're stitched together by an extensive network of park connectors, cycling and walking paths that let you traverse enormous stretches of the island through continuous greenery.

Gardens by the Bay is the most photographed result, all soaring Supertrees and climate-controlled domes, but the real triumph is less flashy: the sheer, obsessive consistency of maintenance across every single park, from the flagship attractions down to the smallest neighbourhood green space. The Land Transport Authority's park connector data reflects a network spanning hundreds of kilometres, purpose-built to let residents move through the city almost entirely under tree cover if they choose to.

The trade-off, and Singaporeans will tell you this themselves with a mix of pride and mild exasperation, is that virtually none of it is "wild" in any meaningful sense. Every tree is placed, every path engineered, every green corridor the product of careful urban planning rather than organic growth. Fines for littering are real and enforced without much sympathy for tourists claiming ignorance. It's nature, but nature as a civic utility — reliable, universal, and completely without the shaggy, unplanned charm of Athens' patchier green spaces. You will never find a park in Singapore mysteriously overgrown or forgotten. That's precisely the point, and precisely the trade-off.

## The Verdict

Singapore wins on pure infrastructure, consistency, and access — nobody in that country is more than a short walk from genuinely well-maintained green space, an achievement that borders on civic engineering marvel given the land constraints. Greece wins on soul and atmosphere, even if the upkeep can't compete. If I want a reliable park experience, Singapore, without question. If I want to sit under an imperfect tree with elderly men shouting at each other over a tavli board until sunset, Athens, every single time.

## Conclusion

Greece gives you natural beauty and lets the parks fend for themselves; Singapore has almost no natural beauty to speak of and built it anyway, meticulously, block by block. One is a gift of geography left slightly under-tended; the other is a triumph of sheer planning will over geographic scarcity. If you want a park that feels alive with unplanned human chaos, go Greek. If you want a park system that actually, reliably works, Singapore has already thought of everything, including the bin you'll need for your rubbish.
