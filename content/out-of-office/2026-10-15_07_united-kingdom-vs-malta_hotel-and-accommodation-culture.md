# One Country Will Make You Fight For A Flat. The Other Has Thousands Empty And Still Won't Make It Easy.

🇬🇧 United Kingdom vs 🇲🇹 Malta

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

I once viewed a London flat with eleven other prospective tenants standing in the same hallway, all of us pretending not to be competing for a studio with mould shaped vaguely like Belgium. I have also stood outside a Maltese apartment that had been "available" on three separate listing sites for over a year, phoned the agent, and been told, cheerfully, that it had just been rented — to someone else, apparently, several times.

Housing is where a country's bureaucracy shows its actual face, past the tourism-board smile. The UK and Malta have built two entirely different flavours of accommodation chaos, and I've been personally inconvenienced by both.

## 🇬🇧 United Kingdom: Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Have a UK credit history or a guarantor ready; without one, expect friction at every stage | Assume viewing a flat once guarantees it; competitive listings move within hours |
| Budget for a deposit, first month's rent, and referencing fees upfront, all at once | Expect "right to rent" document checks to be a formality; landlords take them seriously |
| Treat estate agents as gatekeepers to be charmed, not simply hired help to instruct | Be shocked that "good" areas in major cities now rival small-country GDPs in rent alone |
| Get everything in writing; UK tenancy law favours documentation over verbal agreement | Skip reading the small print on break clauses; they are where disputes actually live |

## 🇲🇹 Malta: Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect to view a property in person before trusting any listing is genuinely available | Believe a listing's "available now" status without calling to confirm, twice |
| Budget extra time and patience for opening a bank account and registering your tenancy | Assume a low advertised rent is real; many listings are stale "bait" from months ago |
| Ask locals and expat forums which agencies are reliable before committing to a viewing trip | Try to secure housing entirely from abroad without at least a short scouting trip first |
| Consider a 2-3 week hotel stay while house-hunting in person — widely recommended by residents | Expect speed; InterNations survey data ranks Maltese bureaucracy among the hardest globally |

## The United Kingdom: Scarcity As Blood Sport

The UK's accommodation culture is, at its core, a story about genuine scarcity meeting genuine demand, and the result is something closer to competitive sport than a housing market. In desirable areas of London, Manchester, or Edinburgh, a reasonably priced flat can attract a dozen viewings in a single afternoon, each prospective tenant performing a slightly unhinged version of themselves in the hope of being chosen — employment letters, guarantor forms, and references assembled in advance like a dating profile nobody asked to see but everyone now expects.

The paperwork alone can be a genuine barrier for newcomers. "Right to rent" checks are a legal requirement, not a courtesy gesture, meaning landlords must verify your immigration status before handing over keys, and a thin UK credit history — the kind every arriving foreigner has by definition — can sink an otherwise strong application unless a guarantor or several months' rent upfront smooths the gap. Add a deposit, often capped by law but still substantial, plus referencing fees, and the up-front cost of simply getting through the door can rival a small holiday.

What genuinely surprises newcomers isn't the competition itself — most big cities have some version of this — it's the sheer theatre of it. Estate agents in the UK function as gatekeepers with real discretionary power over who gets shown what, and a certain performative charm in dealing with them pays dividends that a purely transactional approach doesn't. It's a market that rewards persistence, paperwork, and a willingness to turn up to a viewing within the hour of the listing going live, because by the time you've finished reading the description properly, someone else has already signed.

## Malta: Abundance That Refuses To Behave

Malta's accommodation problem is the mirror opposite, and somehow just as maddening. There is no shortage of flats — quite the reverse. A building boom combined with an enormous stock of unused holiday properties has left the island with, by some expat estimates, tens of thousands of empty dwellings, and yet actually renting one from abroad can feel nearly impossible. Forum threads on the subject read like detective fiction: posters describing properties still listed as "available" two years after someone else had already moved in, agencies allegedly using attractive flats as "bait" to generate enquiries for less appealing ones, and a general consensus that nothing listed online can be trusted without an actual phone call, and ideally a second one to confirm the first wasn't wrong either.

Then there's the bureaucracy sitting on top of the housing confusion. An InterNations Expat Insider survey, covering expats across more than fifty countries, found Malta ranked as having the hardest public administration to deal with of any country surveyed — with opening a bank account, arranging home internet, and getting a residency visa singled out as the specific pain points. None of these are housing per se, but all of them sit directly in the path of actually settling into a flat, which means the rental hunt is rarely the end of the ordeal; it's just the first of several.

The genuinely good advice, repeated across expat forums by posters with names like matm911 and mantasmo, is almost defeatist in its practicality: don't try to arrange housing entirely from abroad, book a hotel for two or three weeks, and view properties in person once you've landed, because the gap between a listing's claims and its reality is wide enough to drive a Maltese bus through. Rents, once you do find something genuine, are often surprisingly reasonable. Finding something genuine is the entire problem.

## The Verdict

The UK's housing culture punishes you with real scarcity and real competition — you're fighting actual other humans for actual limited stock, and the system, while exhausting, is at least honest about what it's testing you on: paperwork, credit history, and speed. Malta's housing culture punishes you with abundance that refuses to materialise — the flats exist, genuinely, in large numbers, but the listings lie, the agencies play games, and the bureaucracy wrapped around the whole process has been independently ranked among the worst on earth by the people forced to live through it.

Given the choice, I'd rather compete honestly for a UK flat than chase phantom listings in Malta for a month before being told, cheerfully, that the flat I called about yesterday was never actually available. At least London's chaos is transparent about being chaos.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *expat.com Malta forum — matm911, paraphrased: estimated tens of thousands of unused dwellings across the island and said finding accommodation from abroad is "almost impossible, unless you have very huge resources," recommending a two-to-three-week hotel stay to search properly in person.*

> *expat.com Malta forum — GozoMo, paraphrased: said most advertised properties simply aren't actually available, and that their own rented home remained listed online two full years after they'd moved in.*

> *r/malta — paraphrased: a longtime resident explained that empty flats sitting unrented for years are often owned outright by families with no urgency to rent them out, which keeps supply technically high while availability stays functionally low.*

## Conclusion

The UK will make you prove, in triplicate, that you deserve a flat that's genuinely scarce. Malta will dangle thousands of flats in front of you that mostly don't exist in any usable sense, then rank its own bureaucracy as the hardest in the world almost as a point of pride. I've navigated both, badly, and I'll say this plainly: bring your paperwork to London, and bring your patience, plus a hotel booking, to Malta. Neither country is going to make this easy. At least one of them is upfront about why.
