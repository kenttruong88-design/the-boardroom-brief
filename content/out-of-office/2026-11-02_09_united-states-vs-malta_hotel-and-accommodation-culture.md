# One Country Invented The Resort Fee. The Other Turned An Island Into A Short-Let.

🇺🇸🇲🇹 United States & Malta
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

America gave the world the modern hotel industry, the loyalty points scheme, and the single most dishonest line item in global hospitality: the "resort fee," a charge that exists purely so the headline room rate can lie to you with a straight face. Malta, meanwhile, a genuinely tiny island nation that has spent the last decade converting what feels like every third residential building into an Airbnb, has managed its own distinct form of accommodation chaos — not through fee trickery, but through sheer volume, turning long-term rental for actual residents into a genuinely brutal competitive sport against an endless tide of tourists willing to pay short-term premiums.

Both countries will take your money with a smile. America will do it in instalments disguised as "resort amenities" you never asked for. Malta will do it by quietly pricing you, the mere resident who wants to live there longer than a week, out of your own neighbourhood.

## Do's & Don'ts: United States

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Read the full price breakdown before booking, every single time — resort fees, parking, and "amenity fees" are rarely in the headline rate | Trust the advertised nightly rate as the actual cost; always assume 15-30% more will appear at checkout |
| Join a major hotel loyalty programme if you travel domestically often — the perks genuinely compound over time | Expect tipping at hotels to be optional; housekeeping and bellhop tips are a real, expected part of the cost |
| Check cancellation policies carefully — they vary wildly between "fully refundable" and "non-refundable" rates at the same hotel | Book the cheapest rate tier without checking what flexibility you're giving up; the savings often aren't worth the risk |
| Call the hotel directly if a rate looks off — direct negotiation sometimes beats the online booking price | Assume online aggregator prices are final; direct booking occasionally unlocks better rates or perks |

## Do's & Don'ts: Malta

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Start long-term apartment searches well before you need to move — the rental market moves fast and competitively | Expect short-term tourist-season pricing logic to apply to long-term rentals; the two markets barely resemble each other |
| Use a local agent or well-connected contact for apartment hunting — informal networks still beat most listing sites | Rely solely on international listing platforms; many of the best long-term deals never make it there |
| Negotiate directly with landlords on lease length and deposit terms — there's more flexibility than listings suggest | Assume prices are fixed; Malta's rental negotiation culture rewards persistence and local language effort |
| Visit in low season if you want an honest read on a neighbourhood — peak summer completely changes the character of touristy areas | Judge a Maltese neighbourhood's liveability purely from a summer visit; the tourist crush distorts everything |

## United States: The Resort Fee As National Art Form

America's hotel industry is enormous, sophisticated, and has perfected a pricing architecture that the Bureau of Labor Statistics' own accommodation data can confirm has grown increasingly layered over the past decade: base room rate, resort fee, parking fee, sometimes a "destination fee" that is functionally identical to the resort fee but charged by hotels that don't consider themselves resorts. The American Hotel & Lodging Association's own trend reporting acknowledges these fees as a genuine, growing share of hotel revenue, which tells you precisely how committed the industry is to keeping them.

What America does brilliantly, credit where due, is loyalty infrastructure — points systems sophisticated enough to functionally subsidise years of travel for people who play the game properly, status tiers that deliver genuinely tangible upgrades, and a sheer density and variety of accommodation options, from roadside motels to five-star urban towers, that few countries can match for sheer choice. The tipping culture around hotel stays — housekeeping, bellhops, valet — adds another layer of cost most international visitors don't budget for, and discovering it at checkout, or worse, not discovering it and simply being quietly judged by housekeeping, is a rite of passage.

## Malta: An Island-Sized Rental Market Under Tourist Siege

Malta's accommodation story isn't really about hotels at all — it's about what unrestrained short-term letting does to a genuinely tiny housing market. The Malta Tourism Authority's own accommodation stock figures show the short-term rental sector expanding dramatically over the past decade, and the National Statistics Office's tourism data confirms what any Maltese resident will tell you unprompted: the island's finite housing stock is now in direct, year-round competition between long-term residents and a tourism economy that can simply outbid them.

For anyone trying to actually live in Malta rather than visit it, this reshapes the entire rental experience into something closer to a competitive sport — apartments get snapped up within days, landlords increasingly favour short-term tourist lets over long-term tenants because the margins are better, and the informal local network of contacts and word-of-mouth listings has become more valuable than any international platform, precisely because the best long-term deals never make it to a listing site built for tourists. It's a small, beautiful island paying a real structural price for its own tourism success.

## The Verdict

America's resort fee is an annoying, well-documented, entirely avoidable-if-you-read-carefully scam. Malta's housing squeeze is a genuine structural problem reshaping the lives of actual residents, not just inconveniencing tourists for one stay. Petty dishonesty versus structural displacement — there's no real contest here. America's hotels will irritate you for a weekend. Malta's rental market will reshape your entire plan to actually live somewhere.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *Reddit r/travel — paraphrased: a traveller calculated that a hotel's advertised $140 nightly rate became nearly $190 after resort and parking fees, and called the entire disclosure process "legally honest and practically a con."*

> *Internations Malta — paraphrased: a relocated marketing executive said they viewed eleven apartments before securing one, and lost at least three to landlords who simply switched to short-term tourist listings instead.*

> *expat.com Malta — paraphrased: a longtime resident noted that negotiating in even basic Maltese noticeably improved landlord responsiveness compared to English-only inquiries, in a market where every advantage counts.*

## Conclusion

America's accommodation culture is a masterclass in pricing theatre — technically disclosed, practically infuriating, ultimately avoidable if you're paying attention. Malta's is a quieter, more consequential story about what happens when a small island's housing stock becomes a tourism commodity before anyone properly planned for the residents still trying to live there. One will cost you an extra fifty dollars at checkout. The other might cost someone their neighbourhood.
