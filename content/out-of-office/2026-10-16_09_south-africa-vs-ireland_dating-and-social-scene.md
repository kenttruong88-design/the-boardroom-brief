# South Africa Will Feed You Before It Flirts With You. Ireland Will Flirt With You Before It Admits It.

🇿🇦 🇮🇪
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Two nations, both allegedly obsessed with "craic" and "connection," and both constitutionally incapable of simply asking someone out like adults. South Africa solves this by feeding you first — hours of smoke, meat, and rugby commentary before anyone admits they're interested — while the national grid periodically solves it for them by switching off the lights entirely. Ireland solves it by getting everyone slightly drunk in a pub that has operated, unchanged, since the Famine, and calling the resulting fumbled kiss "the shift," as though giving it a cute nickname absolves anyone of having to define the relationship afterward.

I have been propositioned at a braai by a man holding tongs like a sceptre, and I have been "shifted" against a jukebox in Cork by someone who then spent four months pretending it hadn't happened. Both countries will tell you, with absolute sincerity, that they are the friendliest place on earth to fall in love. Both are lying, slightly, in opposite directions.

## Do's & Don'ts

### 🇿🇦 South Africa
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Accept the braai invite — it's a four-hour audition, not a quick bite | Mistake "boredom swiping" for actual intent on Tinder; most locals admit that's exactly what it is |
| Get a backup power plan for date night before you get a partner | Walk or cycle somewhere alone at night to "keep it romantic" |
| Move the chat to WhatsApp and meet the first date somewhere public and well-lit | Assume load shedding schedules won't become a genuine third party in your relationship |

### 🇮🇪 Ireland
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Learn to read banter as the primary flirtation language, not small talk filler | Ask someone out directly on the street — you will become their anecdote, not their boyfriend |
| Treat the pub as the actual first date, not the warm-up to one | Assume a "shift" on a night out means anything has been agreed upon, defined, or will be mentioned again |
| Try a trad night, a running club, or — if you're feeling bold — Lisdoonvarna's matchmaking festival | Expect dating apps to deliver; even locals are publicly asking how anyone under 30 still meets people |

## South Africa

South African courtship does not begin with a question. It begins with smoke. The braai is the real first date venue, regardless of what Tinder claims, because nobody trusts a connection that hasn't survived several hours of someone else's uncle discussing the Springboks. It is, genuinely, one of the only social rituals in the country that reliably crosses language, race, and class lines — which makes it either the nation's great democratic triumph or its most elaborate excuse for not texting back promptly, depending on your mood.

Underneath the hospitality, though, is a dating culture that has been quietly reshaped by two forces nobody puts in the brochure: load shedding and fear. A 2021 academic survey of South African Tinder users, reported in The Conversation, found that 56% had downloaded the app purely out of boredom or curiosity and only 52% were actually hunting for love — meaning a genuine majority are, by their own admission, treating romance as a procrastination tool rather than a project. The same research found women reporting real sexual freedom via the apps, alongside a parallel instinct to withhold their workplace and home address, shift the conversation to WhatsApp, and insist on meeting somewhere public. That instinct isn't paranoia for its own sake — InterNations' 2024 Expat Insider survey ranked South Africa dead last of 53 countries on personal safety, with 54% of expats rating it negatively against a global average of 8%, and 65% saying it isn't safe or easy to simply walk or cycle somewhere. Dating here is not merely emotionally risky; it comes with an actual logistics plan.

Then there is Eskom, doing what no self-help book ever could: forcing South Africans to get creative. Load shedding kills the standard dinner-and-mood-lighting date outright, and one admittedly unscientific Sex Expo survey, covered by IOL, found 65% of adults over 30 in long-term relationships said the blackouts had a *positive* effect on their intimate life. Candles were, it turns out, doing some unpaid overtime. Date someone here and you will, eventually, plan an evening around a loadshedding schedule app with more sincerity than you've ever planned a wedding.

## Ireland

Ireland's entire dating infrastructure runs on one building: the pub. This isn't folklore — Amárach Research, polling for the Drinks Industry Group of Ireland, found that 32% of Irish couples met their partner in a pub, hotel, or nightclub, and that 47% of the population associates their local with "socialising" before anything else. Yes, the survey was commissioned by people who'd quite like you to keep drinking, but the number tracks with every Irish person I've ever interrogated on the subject. The pub isn't where Irish dating happens *instead* of a real date. The pub is the real date. Suggesting coffee instead is, to the Irish ear, slightly suspicious.

What the pub gives you in warmth, it withholds in clarity. Irish flirtation runs almost entirely on banter — a dry, deflecting, compliment-shaped insult that you're meant to correctly decode as interest — and genuine directness is treated less as confidence and more as a small public incident. One widely shared spunout.ie piece on Irish dating describes a woman being directly asked out by a stranger on the street and reacting with actual shock, later admitting she said no simply because the unfamiliarity of straightforwardness made rejection feel like the safer option. That is a nation's entire courtship ethos in one anecdote.

Apps have not fixed this; arguably they've made the fog worse. A widely discussed r/AskIreland thread titled, essentially, "how is anyone under 30 meeting people," is full of posters describing matches who vanish after one message and dates that get cancelled on the day. "The shift" — a drunken, pub-born kiss with zero contractual obligations attached — remains the actual unit of romantic progress for a frightening number of Irish adults in their twenties, which tells you everything about a culture that would rather invent a cute verb than have a conversation. Even the expat guides, written by people paid to be reassuring, quietly concede the point: they recommend running pub life and apps in parallel, because neither alone is reliable, and the Lisdoonvarna Matchmaking Festival — a literal institution, staffed by an actual professional matchmaker, running every September since the 1800s — exists precisely because flirting-by-subtext has never once been an efficient national strategy.

## The Verdict

If you want to know where someone stands, don't move to Ireland — you'll spend eighteen months decoding banter for a signal that was never sent. South Africa, for all its genuine, well-documented safety anxieties, is at least honest about desire: people say what they want at the braai, occasionally too loudly, usually near an open flame. Ireland makes you excavate interest like an archaeologist; South Africa hands it to you on a plate, with chops.

But neither country will give you an efficient dating experience, so stop expecting one. South Africa asks you to accept genuine risk and a national power grid as co-stars in your love life. Ireland asks you to accept that "the shift" might be the entire relationship, permanently, and nobody will ever bring it up again. Pick your inconvenience. Mine, these days, is the one with reliable electricity — though I'll concede that nothing in Dublin has ever once left me assessing an exit route before a second date, which is more than South Africa's safety statistics can currently promise anyone.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *Reddit r/AskIreland — paraphrased: a poster in her mid-twenties describes dating apps as largely a dead end, citing repeated ghosting and cancelled plans, and openly asks how anyone her age is supposed to meet someone without them.*

> *expat.com Ireland dating guide — paraphrased: newcomers are advised that chatting up a stranger at the pub counter works, but that overly direct or blunt approaches tend to read as pushy rather than confident, so warmth and banter land better than straightforwardness.*

> *spunout.ie — paraphrased: young Irish daters describe real uncertainty over what actually counts as "dating" versus texting, app-matching, or "the shift," with one contributor noting that a stranger's direct, old-fashioned ask-out felt so unfamiliar it read as startling rather than romantic.*

## Conclusion

Here is the uncomfortable truth both countries would rather you not notice: neither has solved dating, they've just built very different coping mechanisms around the same universal cowardice. South Africa outsources courage to fire, meat, and collective ritual, then lets the national grid provide dramatic lighting changes free of charge. Ireland outsources courage to alcohol and linguistic trickery so elaborate it requires its own vocabulary, a festival, and several centuries of practice. If you're relocating for either, pack accordingly — a power bank and a healthy risk tolerance for the former, a tolerance for ambiguity and a second pint for the latter. Go in expecting clarity from either nation and you'll leave disappointed and, quite possibly, still technically "shifted" rather than dated. Love, it turns out, survives load shedding and banter equally well. It just requires you to stop expecting either nation to simply say what it means, and to start reading the subtext, the smoke signals, and — in at least one country — the official outage schedule instead.
