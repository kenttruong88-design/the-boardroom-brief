# In Ecuador They Move Fast. In Italy They Just Move Loudly.

🇪🇨 Ecuador · 🇮🇹 Italy

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Two cultures, both convinced they invented romance, both wrong, both magnificent anyway. Ecuador courts you like it's already decided the outcome and is simply waiting for you to catch up — declarations early, nicknames by the second date, a mother you'll meet before you've learned anyone's surname. Italy courts you like a parliamentary debate with better lighting — passionate, theatrical, occasionally about something that happened to someone's cousin in 1994, and somehow still entirely about you.

I've dated, badly, in both places, which I consider a public service to this article. The lesson in both countries was the same: nobody is being subtle, and if you're waiting for subtlety, you will wait alone, confused, while everyone around you is having a significantly better time.

## Do's & Don'ts: Ecuador

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect to meet the family early — it's a sign of seriousness, not pressure | Mistake directness and fast-moving affection for a lack of seriousness |
| Let group outings — with cousins, friends, siblings — be the actual first dates | Expect one-on-one coffee dates to be the default social script everywhere |
| Accept that machismo-coded gestures (paying, opening doors) are still broadly the norm, especially outside Quito and Guayaquil | Assume every social circle will match big-city liberal dating norms — regional variation is real |
| Use WhatsApp, not dating apps, once you've actually met someone — it's the real social infrastructure | Ghost — in a tight-knit social scene, word travels through the group chat faster than you'd like |

## Do's & Don'ts: Italy

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Treat the aperitivo as the real first date — low stakes, good lighting, an exit built in | Rush straight to dinner — skipping the aperitivo skips the actual audition |
| Expect families to be vocally, immediately involved in your love life, often within earshot | Assume a grand romantic gesture reads as "attentive" rather than "overbearing" — read the room |
| Dress like you tried, even for something casual — effort is the baseline, not the bonus | Discuss "defining the relationship" too early — it can read as anxious rather than honest |
| Let conversation be loud, interrupted, and argumentative — that's engagement, not conflict | Mistake flirtation (everyone's, with everyone) for an actual signal — it's closer to a social reflex |

## Ecuador: Fast, Familial, and Entirely Unembarrassed

Dating in Ecuador moves at a pace that will genuinely alarm anyone raised on the slow-burn Anglo dating script of three non-committal coffees and a vague Hinge bio. Affection is declared early and often. Pet names appear by the second or third meeting. You will likely be introduced to someone's mother before you've settled on how to describe the relationship to your own friends back home, and this isn't love-bombing — it's simply the cultural default, inherited from a broader Latin American courtship pattern where seriousness is shown through speed and visibility, not withheld through caution.

The social scene itself is deeply group-oriented. Outside the largest cities, dating rarely starts as a solo one-on-one outing; it starts inside the existing friend group, at a family asado, at a cousin's birthday, somewhere you're both already present and the "date" part sort of assembles itself in plain sight of everyone's aunt. This has an upside foreigners underrate: you get vetted by an entire social ecosystem before things get serious, which is either reassuring or terrifying depending on how your last relationship ended.

Machismo, let's not pretend otherwise, is still structurally present — the expectation that men initiate, pay, and perform a certain protective chivalry remains the norm well beyond Quito's more cosmopolitan dating scene, particularly in the highlands and smaller coastal towns. Academic research on Andean courtship norms backs up what every expat eventually notices anecdotally: gender roles in dating here have liberalised unevenly, fastest in the cities, slowest everywhere else, and visitors who arrive expecting a single national "Ecuadorian dating culture" will find instead several, layered by region and class.

## Italy: Loud, Theatrical, and Not Actually in a Hurry

Italy's dating culture performs intensity while, underneath, moving rather more cautiously than the stereotype suggests. The aperitivo is the genius move here — a structured, low-commitment, early-evening ritual that functions as dating's screening round. Spritz, small plates, an hour, built-in exit. Nobody has to call it a date. Everybody knows it's a date. This is, frankly, better design than most Western dating cultures have managed, and I say that as someone who has sat through a truly punishing number of three-hour "coffee dates" elsewhere that neither party wanted.

What trips visitors up is the volume-to-intention ratio. Italians flirt as a form of general social lubrication — with the barista, the bus driver, a stranger at a bus stop debating the correct ragù recipe — and mistaking this ambient charm for a specific signal is the single most common foreigner error here, according to roughly every dating guide and forum thread on the subject. The actual romantic interest, when it exists, tends to show up not as escalating flirtation but as inclusion: being folded into Sunday lunch, meeting the mother (who will have Opinions, delivered at volume, with love), being discussed — approvingly or not — by an extended family network that considers your relationship collective business within about three weeks.

Beneath the theatre, Italian dating culture is also quietly traditional in its pacing on bigger milestones — moving in together, marriage talk, children — often slower and more family-consultative than the loud, expressive surface suggests. The passion is real. The timeline underneath it is more conservative than the opera would have you believe.

## The Verdict

Ecuador tells you exactly where you stand, immediately, loudly, with the whole family as witness. Italy tells you exactly where you stand too — you just have to learn to read theatre as sincerity rather than performance. If I had to pick which approach spares more heartbreak, it's Ecuador's: declared feelings are at least falsifiable. Italy's ambient charm-for-everyone approach means you'll spend your first few months mistaking the barista's warmth for a relationship status.

## Conclusion

Neither country does subtlety, and that, improbably, is the gift. Ecuador's directness and Italy's theatre both solve the same universal dating problem — not knowing where you stand — just with opposite tools: one tells you outright, the other performs it so loudly you eventually can't miss it either. Arrive expecting restraint from either and you'll misread everything. Arrive expecting volume, and you might actually have a wonderful time.
