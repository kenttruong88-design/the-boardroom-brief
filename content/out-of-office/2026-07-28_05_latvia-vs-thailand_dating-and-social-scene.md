# One Country Will Tell You Exactly What It Thinks of You, the Other Will Smile and Let You Figure It Out

🇱🇻 Latvia vs 🇹🇭 Thailand

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Dating abroad is where cultural assumptions go to die spectacular, embarrassing deaths, usually over a drink you paid too much for. Latvia hands you blunt honesty wrapped in several layers of reserve you'll need a crowbar to open. Thailand hands you warmth, charm, and a social smile so versatile it could mean genuine interest, polite disinterest, or mild horror, and you will not be told which.

I've been on dates in Riga that felt like job interviews conducted by someone who'd already decided not to hire me, and dates in Bangkok so pleasant I genuinely couldn't tell if I'd been rejected until three weeks later. Both systems work. Neither is designed for the impatient.

## Do's & Don'ts

### 🇱🇻 Latvia

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Take reserved body language seriously — warmth exists but is earned slowly, not offered upfront | Mistake a slow start for disinterest; Latvians commit hard once they actually decide to |
| Be direct about your intentions; vague, non-committal signals are read as untrustworthy | Overwhelm with excessive early enthusiasm; it tends to read as insincere rather than flattering |
| Expect dates to be low-key and unhurried — long walks and quiet bars over loud group outings | Assume small talk is going well just because it's polite; politeness and warmth are not the same signal here |

### 🇹🇭 Thailand

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Read context and indirect cues carefully — a smile can mean several very different things | Take "maybe" or a vague answer as a soft yes; it's frequently a polite no |
| Respect the importance of "face" — public criticism or confrontation on a date is a serious misstep | Push for a direct, explicit answer about feelings too early; it can feel confrontational |
| Understand that group outings often precede one-on-one dating as a natural, expected step | Assume Western-style direct dating apps behavior translates cleanly to in-person social norms |

## Latvia: Warmth Is Real, It's Just Rationed

Latvian dating culture confuses newcomers because the initial signal is so consistently reserved that it's genuinely difficult to tell whether someone is interested, mildly curious, or simply being polite because you're standing in front of them. Small talk is efficient, not effusive. Eye contact is measured. Smiles are dispensed like a limited resource. None of this means disinterest — it means Latvians, as a broader cultural pattern, do not perform enthusiasm they haven't actually earned yet, and expecting an American-style burst of early warmth is a fast route to disappointment.

The flip side is that once a Latvian actually decides they're interested, the shift is real and substantial. Reserve gives way to genuine directness — you'll generally know where you stand because Latvians tend to say what they mean rather than dressing it in ambiguity, which, after navigating more indirect cultures, comes as something of a relief. Dates tend to be understated: a walk, a quiet bar, something unhurried rather than a loud group event designed to fill silence. The silence, incidentally, is not a problem here. Comfortable quiet is read as a sign of ease, not a social failure that needs fixing.

The social scene beyond one-on-one dating leans smaller and slower too — Latvians build friend groups gradually and are famously slow to let new people all the way in, expats included. It can feel isolating for the first several months, watching warmth exist clearly among established friend groups while you remain politely on the outside. Stick with it, though, and the payoff is loyalty that runs deep and lasts, rather than the wide, shallow social circles more performative cultures tend to produce. Latvia isn't cold. It's just extremely careful about who gets to see it isn't.

## Thailand: The Smile Is Doing a Lot of Work

Thai social and dating culture runs on an entirely different operating system, one built around indirectness, harmony, and the concept of "face" — essentially, avoiding public embarrassment or confrontation for yourself or anyone else involved. The famous Thai smile isn't a single signal, it's a whole vocabulary, deployed for genuine happiness, polite discomfort, gentle refusal, and everything between, and learning to read the context around it is essential and, frankly, takes longer than most newcomers expect.

Dating typically moves through group settings before narrowing into one-on-one time, which can frustrate people used to direct, app-driven dating culture expecting a fast track to exclusivity. Being folded into a friend group first isn't a stalling tactic, it's often exactly how genuine interest gets tested and confirmed, at a pace that prioritises social harmony over speed. Direct, explicit conversations about feelings or relationship status, the kind that feel completely normal in more Western dating cultures, can land as unexpectedly confrontational here, pushing someone to give a direct answer before they're ready can cause them to lose face, and the relationship often cools as a quiet consequence.

Bangkok's social scene in particular is enormous, layered, and genuinely welcoming on the surface — rooftop bars, night markets, a huge and visible expat dating pool — but the ease of meeting people can mask how much slower it actually takes to build something real. Newcomers who mistake surface warmth for fast intimacy often find themselves confused months in, still unsure where they stand, having mistaken politeness for progress. The people who do well here learn patience, read context over content, and stop expecting a straight answer to a direct question.

## The Verdict

Latvia gives you honesty upfront but makes you wait for warmth. Thailand gives you warmth upfront but makes you wait, sometimes indefinitely, for a straight answer. If you need clarity more than comfort, Latvia will eventually reward your patience with real, unambiguous connection. If you can tolerate genuine ambiguity in exchange for constant social warmth, Thailand will keep you entertained and thoroughly confused in equal measure. Neither is going to hand you certainty quickly. Welcome to dating abroad; bring patience, leave your assumptions at customs.

## Conclusion

Latvia and Thailand are both, in their own way, teaching you the same lesson: don't trust the first read. Latvian reserve isn't rejection, and Thai warmth isn't automatically progress. Slow down, pay attention to what's actually being communicated underneath the surface signal, and stop importing your home country's dating shorthand into a culture that's operating on entirely different rules. You'll get there. Just don't expect either country to make it fast, and don't mistake either country's patience for a lack of genuine feeling.
