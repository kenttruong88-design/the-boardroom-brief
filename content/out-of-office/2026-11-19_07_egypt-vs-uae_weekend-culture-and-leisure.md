# Egypt Spends Its Weekend on the Nile and the UAE Spends It at a Buffet

🇪🇬 Egypt · 🇦🇪 UAE

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

There are two kinds of weekend. The first is a social act, a long loose gathering of friends, family and strangers who happen to be passing, and the second is a consumer event with a reservation. Egypt is the first. The United Arab Emirates, in its high-gloss way, has perfected the second. I have lived through both, and I can tell you that one leaves you with a sunburn and a conversation, and the other leaves you with a receipt.

The calendar itself has been a drama. Egypt's weekend remains Friday and Saturday. The UAE, in a decision announced in December 2021, moved its federal government to a Saturday and Sunday weekend from 1 January 2022, with a four-and-a-half-day week, to align with the rest of the global economy. Same region, different gravity: one country wanted to match the markets, the other to stay with its own rhythm. Both have somewhere to go on Friday, and both will make you pay for the privilege in different currencies.

## Do's & Don'ts

**🇪🇬 Egypt**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Hire a felucca on the Nile for a picnic or drinks | Assume a boat ride must be expensive; reports suggest about US$10 an hour |
| Try Zamalek for live music and Cairo's Opera House | Expect European prices for the arts; they run far cheaper |
| Join a club or class and choose friends carefully | Assume gym acquaintances will become close friends quickly |
| Check whether a restaurant serves alcohol; many do not | Assume dinner out always comes with a drink |

**🇦🇪 UAE**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Book Friday brunch, typically noon to 4pm | Expect a brunch for under about AED 185 |
| Remember the weekend is now Saturday and Sunday for the federal government | Assume the private sector always follows the same hours |
| Know the alcohol rules: available in six of seven emirates | Drink in Sharjah, where it is prohibited |
| Behave sensibly in public; intoxication can lead to arrest | Treat the brunch as a licence to misbehave |

## 🇪🇬 Egypt: The Social Weekend

An Egyptian weekend begins with a decision about who is coming over. Home entertaining, a Cairo post report notes, is the most common social activity, and everything else is arranged around it. The city offers a handful of public pleasures that cost almost nothing: renting a felucca or sailboat on the Nile for a picnic or drinks, reported at around US$10 an hour in 2016 and 2022 accounts, with boats holding as many as twenty people. Zamalek has the Jazz Club and other live-music venues at weekends, plus the Opera House for music and ballet, described in a 2010 report as much cheaper than European equivalents.

For expats, the Maadi clubs are the social centre: Maadi House, the embassy-run American club with pools and playgrounds, and the Maadi British Club, open to other nationalities. Facebook and WhatsApp groups cover everything from Hash House Harriers to book clubs and trivia nights, and InterNations is the platform most frequently recommended for events. The same Cairo post report cautions that many Western-style restaurants do not serve alcohol, that hotel restaurants are more likely to, and that a 2013 report said alcohol with restaurant dinners had been made illegal, with some restaurants allowing BYO for a fee. In other words, do not assume anything about the drink.

Friendship in Cairo is slow, and locals say so themselves. On the Expat.com forum, a resident of Giza advises joining a club and talking to people, then choosing your friends carefully because "nice friends are not easy to find". Another poster adds that real friendship takes time, so a gym or dance class mostly yields acquaintances. A visitor staying in Zamalek asked for company for drinks or shisha on a Friday evening, and others replied they were interested; one was already having shisha in the neighbourhood. That is the Cairo weekend in miniature: loose, spontaneous and entirely dependent on who happens to be around.

Expat reports disagree on mixing with Egyptians: a 2025 report says expats mostly don't, while a 2020 report says InterNations helped build local connections, and a 2021 report warns that women may receive unwanted messages through it. So treat the platform as a door, not a destination. The best Egyptian weekends are the ones where somebody you barely know insists you stay for dinner.

## 🇦🇪 UAE: The Brunch Industrial Complex

The Emirates have industrialised leisure, and I mean that as both praise and warning. The Friday brunch, typically running noon to 4pm, is the national institution, and the price menu is a masterclass in tiering: Long's Brunch at the Towers Rotana at AED 185 with house drinks or 245 premium, Jazz at Pizza Express in JLT at AED 189, Dusit Thani on Sheikh Zayed Road at AED 250, Park Hyatt's Thai Kitchen at AED 299, Armani Deli at the Burj Khalifa at AED 395 with a flexible three-hour slot between noon and 9pm, Café Belge at the Ritz-Carlton Dubai Festival City at AED 395, Park Hyatt Traiteur at AED 690 with premium sparkling, and Madinat Jumeirah's Al Qasr at AED 795 with bubbly. That is a pricing ladder with a prosecco rung at every level.

The working week changed, too. Federal and many local government staff now work 7:30am to 3:30pm Monday to Thursday and 7:30am to noon on Friday, with flexible and work-from-home Fridays possible, and Dubai and Abu Dhabi adopted the same hours. Friday prayers are at 1:15pm all year. The private sector was not required to follow, and the 48-hour weekly maximum still applies. The HR minister said the shift would "eliminate the weekend gap" with world markets, which is government language for: we would like to be able to take a call from Frankfurt on a Friday.

The rules of the game are legal, not just social. Alcohol is available in six of the seven emirates, and Sharjah prohibits it; public intoxication can bring arrest, fines and deportation. The brunch, in other words, has a regulatory perimeter. The history is worth a sentence: a six-day week with only Friday off from 1971 to 1999, Thursday added in 1999, and a move to Friday and Saturday in 2006, before the 2022 shift. The weekend is a policy decision, and it has been changed repeatedly in a few decades.

I could not find a Dubai expat forum thread to quote, because the brunch page is an article, so I will be honest that the UAE side of this piece rests on published facts rather than complaints. What I can say is that the Emirati weekend is bright, expensive and extraordinarily well organised. Everything you want exists, has a menu and takes a card.

## The Verdict

Egypt wins, narrowly, for the thing the UAE cannot buy: spontaneity. A felucca at dusk with a bag of food and ten acquaintances is a better afternoon than a three-hour package with unlimited bubbles, and I will argue that point with anyone holding a cocktail. Cairo's weekend is slower, poorer and more human, and its charm is that nothing is arranged.

But the UAE earns its respect. It has the discipline to change its calendar in order to align with the planet, the infrastructure to put on a brunch that works every single time, and the legal clarity to tell you precisely which emirate allows what. If you want comfort, schedule and a guarantee that the lights will be on, Dubai is the winner and I will not pretend otherwise.

My sting: Egypt gives you a weekend you will remember, and the UAE gives you a weekend you will have paid for. Decide which sort of regret you can live with.

## Conclusion

I have spent weekends in sixty countries, and the best ones have always been unplanned. Egypt understands this instinctively and the UAE understands it intellectually, which is the polite way of saying it has priced it.

Go to Cairo for the Nile and the long dinner. Go to Dubai for the brunch and the flawless service. And whatever you do, check the weekend before you book the flight, because in this region the calendar is a political statement and the only person who hasn't been told is you.
