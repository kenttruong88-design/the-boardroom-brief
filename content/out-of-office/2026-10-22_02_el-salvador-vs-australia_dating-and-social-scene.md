# Flattery Is Free In San Salvador And Apparently Banned By Law In Sydney

🇸🇻 El Salvador vs 🇦🇺 Australia

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Two countries, two entirely different theories of romantic effort. In El Salvador, a stranger will tell you that you have the eyes of a volcano sunset before you've finished crossing the street, and his mother will have an opinion about you by Thursday. In Australia, you can date someone for four months, meet all his mates, attend his sister's wedding, and still not technically know if you're "a thing," because nobody has said so and nobody plans to.

I have lived on both ends of this spectrum, and I can report that neither is healthier, just differently exhausting. One culture oversells affection in public and undersells commitment in private; the other does the reverse, mumbles it into a beer, and calls that intimacy. Both will leave a newcomer standing in a doorway at midnight, phone in hand, trying to work out what just happened and whether it was romance, ritual, or an elaborate misunderstanding about who was supposed to text first. Buckle up.

## Do's & Don'ts

**El Salvador:**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect to meet the family early — courtship here is a group sport | Mistake a piropo (street compliment) for a marriage proposal |
| Dress up, even for a casual coffee — effort is read as respect | Show up looking like you just left the gym, unless you did and are proud of it |
| Let conversations get warm, direct, and a little theatrical fast | Assume Western-style "no labels" dating will translate; it mostly won't |
| Be patient with chaperoning and family check-ins, especially outside the capital | Joke about machismo like it's a quirky footnote — it has a body count |

**Australia:**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Learn to read "we should catch up sometime" as a genuine, if vague, invitation | Expect a grand gesture; the grandest gesture is him remembering your shout |
| Offer to buy a round — reciprocity at the pub is basically a love language | Assume silence after a good date means disinterest; it might just mean Tuesday |
| Treat the group hangout as the actual first date; pairing off happens later | Push for "what are we" talk before the fourth or fifth casual catch-up |
| Match the self-deprecating humour — sincerity delivered straight can read as try-hard | Confuse laid-back with lazy; Australians plan, they just refuse to sound like it |

## El Salvador

El Salvador does not do subtlety, and frankly I respect the commitment. Social life is loud, outdoor, family-anchored, and allergic to ambiguity. You will be complimented on the street before breakfast, invited to a cousin's birthday before the second date, and asked your intentions by somebody's tía before you've worked out the Spanish word for "intentions." This is not overbearing so much as structural: dating in El Salvador has historically functioned less as a private negotiation between two people and more as a semi-public family audition, and that hasn't fully changed just because everyone now also has Instagram.

The flip side, and I will not pretend it's a charming cultural quirk, is machismo — a set of gender scripts so entrenched that grassroots groups have spent years running campaigns specifically to dismantle it, as Global Voices has documented. El Salvador has, at various points, posted some of the highest femicide rates in the Americas, a statistic UN Women has flagged repeatedly as symptomatic of a wider regional pattern where jealousy and control get rebranded as devotion. Al Jazeera's reporting on rising violence against women traces a direct line from "protective" boyfriend behaviour to something much darker when it curdles. None of this means every relationship is a horror story — plenty are warm, generous, and genuinely equal — but the cultural permission structure for controlling behaviour is real, and visitors (especially women) should clock it rather than romanticise it as passion.

Practically, this means courtship is fast, flattering, and family-supervised, especially outside San Salvador's more cosmopolitan pockets. Expat forums are full of foreigners trying to figure out whether a local partner's constant check-ins are affection or surveillance — the honest answer is usually "it's considered normal here, interrogate it anyway." Catholic and evangelical social norms still shape expectations around premarital seriousness, so "casual" is a harder sell than in, say, Berlin. If you want an actual casual fling, say so loudly and early, because nobody here is going to assume it.

The social scene outside dating is, to be fair, glorious. Sunday asados that last six hours, a genuine national obsession with football as a conversation-opener, and a hospitality instinct so strong that a neighbour you've met twice will feed you before asking your surname. San Salvador's bar scene has quietly gentrified in pockets like Zona Rosa, where you'll find a more international, egalitarian crowd closer to what you'd find in Mexico City or Bogotá. But step outside that bubble and the old rules reassert themselves fast, and foreigners who mistake the warmth for permissiveness tend to get a harder cultural lesson than they bargained for.

## Australia

Australia's social scene runs on the opposite fuel: understatement so aggressive it becomes its own personality. Nobody will serenade you on the street — that would be deeply, cosmically embarrassing for everyone involved — but they will absolutely remember that you're a shandy drinker and buy accordingly. The entire social contract is built around the pub "shout," the custom where you take turns buying rounds, a ritual so load-bearing that a recent survey found 70% of Australians now consider it financially unfair and younger Aussies are quietly ditching it, according to reporting from savings.com.au. Watching an entire national dating culture pivot around who's paying for the next round of beers is, I'll admit, one of the more honest things I've seen a society do.

Dating itself is almost comically indirect. "We should catch up" can mean anything from "I am obsessed with you" to "I will never contact you again," and Australians apparently enjoy keeping you guessing, judging by the endless Reddit threads — r/AskAnAustralian chief among them, recapped at length by outlets like BuzzFeed — of foreigners, particularly British expats, trying to decode Australian men's conversational style. Even the most basic etiquette question, whether men should pay on a first date, splits Australian Reddit clean in half, per Pedestrian.TV's coverage; there is no national consensus, only vibes.

The group-first social model doesn't help newcomers. Expat.com threads on cracking the Australian friend group describe a slow, pub-and-barbecue-mediated entry process where you're folded into "the mates" long before anyone clarifies whether you're dating one of them specifically. It's egalitarian, low-pressure, and allergic to melodrama — genuinely lovely once you're in. Getting in, though, requires a tolerance for ambiguity that borders on the Olympic.

What nobody tells you is how much of Australian social life happens sideways, through sport, through a shared obsession with the beach, through someone's housemate's birthday, rather than through anything resembling a planned "date." The official guidance from Smartraveller, Australia's own government travel advisory, mostly concerns itself with physical safety rather than romantic etiquette, which tells you plenty: the danger here isn't the people, it's the chronic, structural vagueness. You will have a wonderful time. You may also need to directly ask, in full sentences, whether you are in a relationship, because nobody is going to volunteer the information.

## The Verdict

If I'm grading on honesty, Australia wins by default — nobody's pretending to be more invested than they are, which at least spares you the theatrics. If I'm grading on warmth, El Salvador wins outright — the hospitality, the family-wrapping, the sheer unembarrassed enthusiasm for telling someone they're beautiful, is genuinely disarming in a way no amount of Antipodean understatement can touch.

But I'm grading on survivability, and here's the sting: El Salvador's romantic culture carries a documented darker edge that no amount of charm offsets, and Australia's romantic culture carries a documented flakiness problem that no amount of mateship offsets. One asks too much too fast and sometimes with menace; the other asks nothing at all and calls it respect. Neither is "better." Both will leave you, at 1am, texting a friend back home asking what on earth just happened. Pack accordingly, and lower your expectations of clarity to roughly zero in both postcodes.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *expat.com, Central America/El Salvador forum — paraphrased: foreigners dating locally describe being folded into family gatherings within weeks, with relatives openly weighing in on the relationship's seriousness before the couple themselves have discussed it.*

> *Reddit r/AskAnAustralian — paraphrased: a recurring thread theme is that Australian men will happily spend a whole evening with someone, introduce them to the entire friend group, and still never say anything that confirms it was a date.*

> *Quora — paraphrased: a frequent question format asks why Australians seem so casual about dating compared to other Western countries, with answers generally agreeing that commitment language arrives extremely late, if ever, relative to actual behaviour.*

## Conclusion

So which do you book a one-way ticket for: the country that flirts like it's an Olympic sport, or the one that flirts like it's mildly against council regulations? Honestly, go to El Salvador for the volcano-sunset compliments and the unbeatable family asados, and go to Australia for the mateship and the shout — just don't go to either expecting the dating culture to behave like the one you left. El Salvador will love you loudly and sometimes possessively; Australia will love you quietly and sometimes so quietly you'll need written confirmation it happened at all. The only universal rule, wherever you land: read the room, ask the direct question anyway, and never, ever let anyone call either country "exotic" within your earshot. I will end the friendship.
