# Argentina Will Kiss You Hello Before You've Said A Word. Iceland Will Let You Sit Naked Next To A Stranger In Total Silence And Consider That Perfectly Normal Manners.

🇦🇷 🇮🇸 *By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Public behaviour is the tell. Forget the guidebooks — watch how a country handles a queue, a greeting, a stranger standing too close, and you'll learn more about its actual values than any museum will teach you. Argentina handles all three with volume, physical warmth, and total disregard for personal space, wrapped in a national obsession with mate that turns even a park bench into a shared ritual. Iceland handles the same three situations with near-total silence, an almost eerie comfort with communal nudity, and a founding cultural belief that interrupting someone's peace is close to a crime.

I've been kissed on both cheeks by an Argentine stranger within thirty seconds of meeting them, and I've sat in an Icelandic geothermal pool for forty minutes next to someone who said nothing beyond a single nod. Both experiences taught me things about myself I didn't necessarily want to learn.

## Do's & Don'ts: Argentina

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Greet everyone — even in professional settings, even strangers being introduced — with a single kiss on the cheek | Refuse a shared mate gourd when it's passed to you; it's a genuine social ritual, not a hygiene test to fail |
| Expect conversations to be loud, overlapping, and passionate — interrupting is enthusiasm, not rudeness | Show up to a social gathering on time; arriving "on time" to an asado can mean you're an hour early, awkwardly |
| Embrace long, warm goodbyes — a proper farewell involves more kissing and more standing around than you'd expect | Rush a conversation or check your phone visibly; presence is taken seriously as a form of respect |

## Do's & Don'ts: Iceland

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Shower thoroughly, naked, in the communal pre-pool showers before entering any geothermal pool — this is enforced, not optional | Make small talk with strangers in queues, pools, or on public transport; it reads as intrusive, not friendly |
| Respect silence as a valid social state — sitting quietly next to someone is not awkward, it's simply normal | Stand too close in a queue; Icelandic personal space bubbles are large and firmly held |
| Take your shoes off when entering someone's home, without being asked — it's assumed | Be surprised by blunt honesty; Icelanders consider indirectness a kind of dishonesty, not politeness |

## Argentina: Volume As A Love Language

Argentine public life runs hot, loud, and physically close, and it starts with the greeting: a kiss on the cheek, delivered to everyone, in every setting, including your first business meeting with someone twice your seniority. It's not flirtation, and it's not informal — it's simply the baseline unit of Argentine social contact, and skipping it reads as colder than most Argentines are comfortable with.

The mate ritual takes this further. A single gourd, a single metal straw (bombilla), passed hand to hand among a group of friends, coworkers, even strangers on a park bench, refilled by whoever's holding the thermos, drunk from communally without a second thought about germs that would horrify half the planet. Refusing it outright, without a genuine reason, is one of the few things that will actually offend. It's less a beverage than a standing invitation to sit still with people for longer than you planned to.

Conversation follows the same logic: loud, overlapping, interrupting freely, because in Argentine social norms, jumping in mid-sentence signals engagement, not rudeness. Silence, by contrast, reads as disinterest or even hostility. An asado (barbecue) that starts at "8 p.m." might not actually begin eating until 11, because the gathering itself — the standing around, the wine, the mate, the noise — is the actual event, and the food is almost an afterthought to the ritual of being loudly, physically present with each other for hours.

## Iceland: Silence As Respect

Iceland runs on almost precisely the opposite logic, shaped by a small, close-knit population where everyone genuinely might be distantly related, and where the harshness of the landscape has bred a deep cultural respect for other people's need to just be left alone. Small talk with strangers — the kind Americans consider basic friendliness — is often experienced by Icelanders as faintly intrusive, an unwanted demand for social performance from someone who was simply trying to get through their day.

The geothermal pool culture is the clearest, strangest window into this. Reykjavik's pools are genuine civic institutions, used daily by huge swathes of the population, and they run on a strictly enforced pre-swim naked shower rule — posted with actual diagrams in some facilities — that visitors from more modest cultures find shocking precisely because nobody around them treats it as a big deal at all. Nudity, in this one specific communal context, is simply logistics. Conversation, meanwhile, is optional and often skipped entirely; you can sit in a hot pot next to the same stranger for half an hour and exchange nothing but a companionable silence, and that silence is the point, not a failure of the encounter.

Icelandic bluntness compounds this. Ask an Icelander a direct question and you'll get a direct, sometimes startlingly honest answer, because indirectness is considered its own kind of rudeness — a waste of everyone's time dressed up as tact.

## The Verdict

Argentina will overwhelm you with warmth you didn't ask for and can't opt out of; Iceland will grant you a solitude so complete it borders on profound. If you're the kind of traveller who needs to be hugged into belonging, Buenos Aires will adopt you within a week. If you're the kind who finds constant social contact exhausting, Reykjavik will feel like the first place that's ever actually left you alone, in the nicest possible way — even while sitting naked next to you in a pool. I know which one I need after a long flight, and it is very much not the one with the mate gourd making its third round of the evening.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *Reddit r/argentina — a newcomer described being mildly panicked at their first office meeting when every single attendee kissed them hello, including a director they'd never met, before anyone said a word about the agenda.*

> *Reddit r/Iceland — a visitor admitted attempting small talk in a pool queue was met with such flat, polite confusion that they never tried it again for the rest of their trip.*

> *expat.com Reykjavik — a resident recalled being publicly, gently corrected by a pool attendant for an inadequate pre-swim shower, and noted the correction was delivered with zero embarrassment on either side, entirely matter-of-fact.*

## Conclusion

What Argentina and Iceland share, oddly, is total sincerity — neither country performs politeness it doesn't mean. Argentina means the kiss, means the noise, means the four-hour asado. Iceland means the silence, means the space, means the honest answer you didn't necessarily want. Visit either expecting your home country's rules to translate, and you'll misread the room within minutes. Visit expecting total sincerity in opposite directions, and you'll actually enjoy yourself. Bring a mate gourd to Iceland and you'll get a stare. Try small talk in a Reykjavik pool and you'll get the same stare. Consider yourself warned.
