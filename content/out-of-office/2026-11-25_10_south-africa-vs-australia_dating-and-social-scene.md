# South Africa Lights the Braai and Australia Suggests a Swim

🇿🇦 South Africa vs 🇦🇺 Australia

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

If you want to understand the difference between South Africa and Australia, do not look at the maps. Look at the first date. In one, a man arrives on time with a plan and a wallet and expects to be judged on both. In the other, a person turns up with a towel, suggests the beach and says you can sort the money out later, probably half and half.

Both countries are warm, outdoorsy, sports-obsessed and full of people who say they are laid-back while remaining devastatingly competitive. Both have a hefty population of emigrants who left for the other. And both have a dating culture that is wildly misunderstood by anyone who learned about it from a postcard. I will do my best to be unkind and accurate in equal measure.

## Do's & Don'ts

| 🇿🇦 South Africa ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Arrive a few minutes early for a first date | Treat time as optional |
| Expect the man to offer to pay, and handle splitting gracefully | Fight over the bill at the table |
| Meet in a public place and tell a friend where you are going | Share your address on the first date |
| Steer clear of religion, politics and apartheid history early on | Open with the most divisive topic you have |

| 🇦🇺 Australia ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Suggest something outdoors: a beach picnic, a walk, a surf lesson | Default to a stiff dinner and a movie |
| Split the bill or take turns paying | Assume the man pays |
| Be upfront about not drinking if you do not | Pretend you do and spend the night faking it |
| Use Bumble, Hinge or Tinder with the right expectation | Expect a second date to be guaranteed |

## 🇿🇦 South Africa

South African dating has a polished traditional shape, and it is more formal than you might think from the warm outdoor image. Punctuality matters and arriving a few minutes early is advised for a first date. The man usually pays on a first date, according to Expatica's guide, though insisting on splitting should not be seen as rude, and if you are unsure, bring cash and a sense of humour. The apps are the usual suspects, Tinder, Bumble and Hinge, with Boo and a distinctive Afrikaans app called Koer thrown in for variety.

The caution is real. The guide tells you to meet in a public place, tell a friend or family member where you are going, never send money to someone you have not met and avoid certain topics until you know each other: religion, politics and the history of apartheid. This is not paranoia. It is a country that knows what its own streets can be, and gives you the sort of sensible, quietly worrying advice a good friend would give. South Africa was the first African country to legalise same-sex marriage in 2006, and it ranks 28th on a recent equality index, though public displays of affection for same-sex couples still call for caution.

What I like about South African dating is its sincerity. The country is warm in the unembarrassed way of people who have survived a great deal and decided to have a barbecue anyway. Friendships are made over fire and meat, family matters, and a man who offers to pick up the bill is not trying to be old-fashioned, he is trying to be kind. The dance is traditional. The result is often a surprisingly lovely evening.

The South African social scene has another side that is often missed: the social ritual of the braai, the barbecue, which is practically a civic institution. A first date might not be a braai, but a second or third often is, and being invited is a milestone in the way that meeting a mother is elsewhere. The conversation is easy, the food is generous and everyone has an opinion about how the meat should be cooked. It is a social setting that rewards participation and punishes the shy, but it also makes friendship easy, which explains why so many emigrants miss it long after they have forgotten the traffic.

## 🇦🇺 Australia

Australia does it differently, as it does most things, which is to say with a cheerful lack of ceremony and a sneaky layer of competition. The dating guides describe the culture as egalitarian: couples tend to split the bill or take turns choosing and paying, and the classic dinner and a movie can be considered dull. The better date is a beach picnic, a walk or a surf lesson, which is a polite way of seeing whether you can survive in the sun without complaining.

Drinking is baked in. The guide says a few drinks to loosen up is standard, and if you do not drink, you should say so on your profile. Bumble is where women make the first move, Hinge is the earnest one and Tinder is what it has always been. A final warning from the same source is to not assume the man pays, not to talk only about yourself and not to expect a second date. That last one is the most honest sentence ever written about modern dating, and the Australians say it like a weather forecast.

The harder part is meeting anyone in the first place. A South African who moved to the Gold Coast at twenty-five found the people her age intensely focused on image, the gym and cosmetic surgery, a culture shock after home. She says she is bad at making friends but that it is easy once you are proactive, through work, study or an exercise club, and she warns against starting romantic relationships in the first phase of a move because they make leaving harder. Her interview is from 2017 and it is not a dating guide, but it is a good reminder of the other side of the sun: an Australian beach is wonderful, and it is also very exposing.

One thing the Australian guide gets exactly right is how seriously the country takes the open-air date. A beach picnic, a coastal walk, a surfing lesson: all are cheap, active and public, and all signal that you are not trying to impress with expensive wine. That egalitarian attitude cuts through the old gender script, and it is lovely, especially if you are tired of silent bill-wrestling. The cost is that you will sometimes feel the whole thing is a bit too casual. You wanted a candlelit table. You got a sandwich on a rock. It is delicious. It is also, technically, a hike.

## The Verdict

I give this one to South Africa, with some reluctance, because I love the Australian talent for treating a first date like a mate. The reason is warmth. South African dating feels more personal, less like a test of your ability to look unbothered, and its traditions, which can feel old-fashioned, signal that someone is making an effort. In a world of ghosting and half-hearted beach walks, that matters.

Australia has the better logistics. The apps are active, the splitting of the bill is fair and the outdoors is an excellent setting for a first date, since it comes with a ready-made exit strategy: a wave. The downside is the culture of image and the casual, sometimes brutal attitude to follow-ups. A second date is a privilege, not an entitlement, and the country says so.

So: Australia for fairness, South Africa for sincerity. If you want to be looked after, go to Johannesburg. If you want to be treated as an equal and left to your own devices, go to Sydney. Either way, bring sunscreen and a friend who knows where you are.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *Expatica, South Africa dating guide — Men usually pay on a first date but splitting should not be taken as rude, meet in a public place and tell a friend where you are going, and avoid religion, politics and apartheid history early on.*

> *Expat.com, Australia dating guide — Dating is egalitarian, so couples often split the bill or take turns, the best dates are beach picnics and walks rather than dinner and a movie, and you should not expect a second date.*

> *Expat Arrivals, Lara, a South African on the Gold Coast (2017) — People my age were very focused on image, the gym and cosmetic surgery, and I would avoid starting a romantic relationship in the first phase of moving because it complicates leaving.*

## Conclusion

Both countries have convinced the world that they are easy-going, and both are, in the sense that a lion is easy-going once fed. Underneath there are rules, traditions and quiet competitions that nobody will explain and everybody will enforce.

My advice is to be on time in one and on the beach in the other, to carry cash in both and to be honest about your drinking in at least one. And whichever date you end up on, remember that nobody is judging your tan more than you are. They are judging your manners. And your ability to stand in the surf without screaming.
