# Switzerland Locks Your Door At 10pm. The Maldives Doesn't Have A Door.

🇲🇻 Maldives + 🇨🇭 Switzerland

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Here are two nations that have both built entire economies on the premise that rich people will pay absurd sums to feel like they're the only humans left alive. The Maldives does it with water so clear it looks Photoshopped. Switzerland does it with silence so total you can hear your own guilt. Both will happily charge you £900 a night for the privilege, and both will make you feel, in wildly different ways, that you have personally inconvenienced the entire nation by requesting a late checkout.

I have stayed in overwater villas where the butler introduced himself before I'd introduced myself to my own jet lag, and I have stayed in a family-run Swiss guesthouse where the owner inspected my shoes for grass before allowing me across the threshold. Neither experience was relaxing. Both were, in their own way, a form of theatre — one performed by staff trained to anticipate your every whim, the other performed by an entire culture that has decided competence is a personality trait.

## Do's & Don'ts

**🇲🇻 Maldives**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Tip your butler in cash, discreetly | Assume every resort is "eco" because it says so on the website |
| Book transfers (speedboat or seaplane) as part of your stay | Wander onto a local, non-resort island in a bikini |
| Ask about house reef quality before booking — it varies wildly | Expect fast Wi-Fi to mean fast anything else |
| Bring reef-safe sunscreen; some resorts fine you for the wrong kind | Bring alcohol onto a local island — it's illegal outside resorts |

**🇨🇭 Switzerland**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Register with the local Gemeinde if staying more than 90 days | Run a washing machine, vacuum, or flush loudly after 10pm |
| Pay the Kurtaxe (tourist tax) without complaint — it funds the trains you'll love | Expect a refund for late cancellation, ever |
| Confirm quiet hours (Ruhezeit) before you unpack anything | Assume "hotel" means what it means at home — many are half-board only |
| Bring cash; some mountain guesthouses still don't take cards | Haggle. You will be looked at like you've suggested arson |

## Maldives: Paradise With Someone Watching

The Maldives resort industry runs on one uncomfortable truth: the country is 99% water, which means the "local" experience most visitors have is entirely manufactured on a private coral speck that the actual Maldivian population never sets foot on unless they work there. This isn't a criticism so much as a business model, and it is executed with a precision that borders on unsettling. Your butler will know your coffee order by day two. Your villa will have anticipated your towel needs before you've finished swimming. There is a version of hospitality here that tips over from "attentive" into "faintly surveillance-adjacent," and after a week you start wondering whether the resort has a file on you.

What nobody tells you before you go is how segregated the whole system is. Locals live on separate inhabited islands governed by conservative Islamic social codes — no alcohol, no public bikinis, no PDA — while resort islands operate as legal exclaves where all of that is not just permitted but actively marketed. You can spend a fortnight in the Maldives and interact with exactly zero Maldivians beyond the ones paid to serve you cocktails, which is either extremely relaxing or a little bit sad depending on how much you like meeting actual people on your travels. I lean toward the latter, though I'll admit the infinity pool did most of the convincing otherwise.

Booking logistics are their own minor bureaucracy: most resorts require a speedboat or seaplane transfer that can cost more than your flight from London, and getting that wrong means arriving at Malé airport at 11pm with nowhere to sleep and a very smug-looking domestic terminal. Sort transfers before you land. And read the fine print on "all-inclusive" — it frequently excludes the one bottle of wine you actually wanted.

## Switzerland: Immaculate, And It Knows It

Switzerland has taken the concept of hospitality and run it through the same efficiency algorithm it applies to trains, banking, and cheese regulation. The result is accommodation that is spotless, punctual, and about as warm as the inside of a fridge. Check-in times are enforced with a rigidity that would make a Swiss train conductor blush, and the concept of "just showing up a bit early, they won't mind" does not exist here — they will mind, politely, in a way that makes you feel you've committed a small crime against the nation's scheduling infrastructure.

The quiet hours are not a suggestion. Ruhezeit is taken so seriously that hotels post it on laminated cards, and neighbours in apartment buildings have been known to call the police over a Sunday vacuum cleaner. If you're renting an Airbnb or long-stay flat, you'll also meet the great Swiss institution of the Kaution — a deposit so large and so ceremonially handled it feels less like a booking fee and more like collateral for a hostage negotiation. Expats on Internations forums swap Kaution horror stories the way other expats swap restaurant recommendations.

And yet — the trains run on time to the accommodation, the sheets are pressed within an inch of their life, and nobody will ever, ever overbook your room. Switzerland's version of luxury isn't indulgence, it's the complete absence of anything going wrong, which after a Maldives trip involving three transfer delays starts to look genuinely appealing.

## The Verdict

If you want to feel like a monarch being quietly monitored by a very attentive staff, book the Maldives. If you want to feel like a minor bureaucratic infraction waiting to happen, book Switzerland. Both will extract maximum money from you with maximum politeness, which is really the only throughline in luxury hospitality worldwide.

My money's on the Maldives for pure decadence, but Switzerland wins on reliability — you will never, not once, have an unpleasant surprise there, mostly because the entire country has been engineered to prevent surprises of any kind. That's either deeply reassuring or the most Swiss sentence ever written. I genuinely can't decide, and neither, I suspect, can they.

## Conclusion

Neither of these countries wants you to relax, not really — they want you to perform relaxation according to a very specific national script. The Maldives' script involves overwater villas and staff who materialise before you've finished a thought. Switzerland's script involves quiet hours, deposits, and a level of cleanliness that borders on accusatory. Pick based on whether you'd rather be pampered by strangers or judged silently by an entire nation's plumbing standards. Either way, pack the reef-safe sunscreen and the correct change for the Kurtaxe — you'll need both, and neither destination will forgive you for forgetting.
