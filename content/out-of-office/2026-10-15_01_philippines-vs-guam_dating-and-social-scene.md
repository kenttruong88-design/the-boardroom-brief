# Everyone On This Island Is Either Your Cousin Or Your Ex

🇵🇭 Philippines vs 🇬🇺 Guam

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

I have been proposed to in four countries, ghosted in seven, and set up on a blind date by a taxi driver exactly once, in Manila, with his own nephew. The nephew was lovely. It did not work out. But it taught me something I now consider a universal law of expat dating: the smaller the social pond, the more elaborate the choreography everyone performs to pretend they're not all swimming in it.

Nowhere proves this better than a tale of two Pacific-adjacent dating markets that look nothing alike on paper and turn out to be cousins underneath — one sprawling archipelago of 115 million people, one US territory you could jog around before breakfast if you really hated yourself. Grab a drink. Preferably not from someone's actual relative.

## 🇵🇭 Philippines: Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Expect the family to be present, emotionally if not physically, by date three | Mistake warmth for availability — "let's be friends" has load-bearing meaning here |
| Learn what "harana" was and why your date's lola still expects a version of it | Show up without being palabok-curious; food fluency is a prerequisite, not a bonus |
| Accept that courtship has phases with actual names (ligawan, manliligaw) | Rush the group-chaperone phase — pakikisama (group harmony) outranks your schedule |
| Treat a text reply within the hour as a genuine investment of effort | Assume "maybe" means no, or that "yes" means an actual commitment to a time |

## 🇬🇺 Guam: Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Assume your date already knows your coworker, your landlord, and possibly your cousin | Badmouth an ex on a first date — you will be dating their best friend by Tuesday |
| Treat a fiesta or rosary novena as a legitimate first-date venue | Expect anonymity anywhere, ever, including at 2am at the Chamorro Village |
| Lean into the mixed Chamorro-Filipino-American social fabric rather than fighting it | Act surprised when "the island" turns out to mean a population smaller than a London postcode |
| Learn the difference between a "boonie dog" joke and an actual insult before using either | Underestimate how seriously karaoke night functions as a courtship ritual |

## The Philippines: Courtship As Competitive Sport

The Philippines does not do casual. I want to be extremely clear about this before any starry-eyed newcomer wanders in assuming Southeast Asian dating apps work like the ones back home, just with better weather. They do not. Filipino courtship — and yes, it still has that shape even on Tinder — runs on a centuries-old operating system of ligawan, the formal, often theatrical pursuit phase in which a suitor is expected to demonstrate sustained, visible effort: showing up, running errands, enduring the scrutiny of aunties who have opinions about your shoes. The old serenade tradition, harana, is mostly extinct as an actual nighttime guitar performance under a window, but its ghost still haunts expectations. Effort is not optional; it is the entire point.

What nobody explains to newcomers is that the family isn't a hurdle you clear once — it's a standing committee with veto power indefinitely. Pakikisama, the deep cultural premium on group harmony, means your date's approval is necessary but not remotely sufficient. I watched a Danish contractor spend eight months "dating" a woman in Cebu without ever being alone with her for more than twenty minutes, because her mother had decided an unchaperoned coffee was a bridge too far. He found this maddening. I found it refreshingly honest about what everyone else's dating culture only pretends isn't also a family negotiation.

The flip side, and the part expats actually fall for, is the warmth. Filipino hospitality toward a foreign date is generous almost to the point of alarming — you will be fed by people who are not your date, welcomed by people who are not obligated to welcome you, and asked within one conversation whether you've thought about settling down "dito na lang" (just here). Take this as the sincere compliment it is, not a hostage situation. It isn't. It's just a culture that has never seen the point of being coy about wanting people to stay.

## Guam: Everybody Already Knows Your Business

Guam's dating scene operates on a principle I can only describe as mandatory transparency, enforced not by any rule but by raw demographic math. The island has roughly 150,000 residents stitched into a web of Chamorro extended family, Filipino immigrant networks going back generations (Filipinos have made up something close to a third of the island's population at various points, per Guampedia's own history of the community), and a rotating cast of US military personnel who arrive certain they're exotic imports and leave realizing they were, for exactly two years, the most gossiped-about demographic on the rock.

The defining fact of dating here is that six degrees of separation collapses to about one and a half. Date someone, and within a week you will discover they already know your barber, attended your coworker's daughter's quinceañera, and have strong, pre-formed opinions about your ex because your ex dated their cousin's best friend in 2019. I am not exaggerating for comic effect, which is rare for me. This is simply how an island this size functions when everyone's grandparents already knew everyone else's grandparents.

What this does to the actual dating itself is fascinating: it raises the social cost of being careless to something approaching medieval. Ghost someone in London and you vanish into eleven million strangers. Ghost someone in Guam and you will see them, and everyone who loves them, at the next fiesta, the next rosary novena, the next karaoke night at a bar that is, infuriatingly, the only decent bar. Courtship therefore trends toward a kind of enforced decency — people behave because misbehaving has consequences with names and addresses attached. It also means the island's famous hospitality toward outsiders comes with fine print: you are being evaluated by an entire community in real time, and the verdict travels faster than you do.

## The Verdict

Both places run on family-as-gatekeeper and hospitality-as-love-language, but they get there by entirely different mechanisms. The Philippines builds its romantic obstacle course deliberately, with named rituals, expected timelines, and a cultural vocabulary for every stage of the siege. Guam doesn't need rituals; geography and small-island demographics do the enforcing for free. One is a tradition you opt into. The other is a panopticon you cannot opt out of.

If you want the warmth without quite so much surveillance, the Philippines at least lets you pretend you have privacy while the aunties quietly decide your fate behind the scenes. Guam offers no such illusion — everyone already knows, and they knew before you did. I'd call that a point in Guam's favour, honestly. Nothing clarifies intentions faster than knowing your ex will hear about your new date from four independent sources by Thursday. Dishonesty simply isn't efficient enough to survive there. Pick your poison: elaborate choreography, or a small island with an excellent memory.

## What Nobody Warned You About

> *r/guam — paraphrased: a newcomer asked why everyone kept asking "who's your family" on a first date, and was told, not unkindly, that the island needs to know within one conversation how many people it would upset if things went badly.*

> *Internations Manila — paraphrased: a longtime expat described dating in the Philippines as "auditioning for a reality show where the judges are related to the contestant," and recommended simply accepting the family round as non-negotiable rather than resenting it.*

> *expat.com Philippines forum — paraphrased: several posters agreed that the fastest way to end a promising relationship was to appear impatient with the chaperone stage, with one calling it "the most effective filter for people who weren't serious anyway."*

## Conclusion

I've dated across three continents and been evaluated by in-laws in four languages, and I will say this without a shred of diplomacy: both of these places will tell you more about yourself than any dating app ever could, because neither one lets you hide. The Philippines does it with grace, patience, and roughly nine family dinners before anyone admits anything. Guam does it with the sheer physics of small-island living, where your reputation arrives at the bar before you do. Go into either expecting Western-style anonymity and you will be humbled fast. Go in understanding that you're not dating a person so much as auditioning for an entire community, and you might actually have a lovely time. Just don't badmouth anyone's cousin. Statistically, you're dating them next.
