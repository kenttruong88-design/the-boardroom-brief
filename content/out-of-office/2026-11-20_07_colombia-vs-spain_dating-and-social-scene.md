# Colombia Dances Its Way Into a Relationship and Spain Talks Its Way Out of One

🇨🇴 Colombia vs 🇪🇸 Spain

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Dating in Colombia is a contact sport conducted to a soundtrack. There is music, there is movement, there is a great deal of eye contact and, almost certainly, a family member watching from across the room. Courtship is warm, attentive and unhurried by the standards of countries that treat romance like a project plan. Compliments are offered freely, gestures are expected and nobody confuses sincerity with weakness.

Dating in Spain is a conversation that never quite ends. It starts at a bar, continues over tapas and moves to another bar, and by the time anyone has mentioned the word relationship, two months have passed and everyone has agreed that it is probably fine. The Spanish approach to love is relaxed, sociable and spectacularly allergic to formality, which makes it ideal for people who hate labels and a puzzle for those who need to know where they stand.

**Colombia**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |

| Learn to dance, or at least be willing to try | Stand at the wall and watch |
| Be respectful of family and ask about theirs | Treat the relatives as an inconvenience |

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Go out in a group first and let things develop | Expect a formal first date with a schedule |
| Join the late-night routine and stay for the second bar | Leave at ten and wonder why nothing happened |

## Colombia

Colombian dating culture is built on warmth, family and a firm belief that effort matters. Expat guides describe a scene in which men are expected to be attentive and sometimes traditional, women are confident and self-possessed, and both sexes dress well for the occasion. There is a courtesy to it, a formality of attention that can feel old-fashioned in the best sense, and an openness about attraction that can feel intense to the unprepared.

Dancing is a social language. Salsa, vallenato and reggaeton are woven into nightlife, and not dancing in a country where everyone dances is an act of isolation. You do not need to be good. You need to be willing, and a willing beginner is far more attractive than a skilled snob.

Family is central. A relationship in Colombia does not remain a private affair for long. Introductions to parents and relatives arrive earlier than many foreigners expect, and the family's opinion carries real weight. Expat discussions on intercultural relationships return to the same theme: the relationship is with the person and, to a degree, with everyone around them.

There are cautions. Expat threads mention misunderstandings about expectations, differences in how directly people state their intentions and the risk of being dazzled by attention. As anywhere, take time, meet friends and family, and be honest about what you want. The Colombian approach to romance rewards sincerity far more than strategy.

Safety and common sense matter in any dating scene. Expat advice for Colombia emphasises meeting in public places, telling a friend where you are and being cautious about anyone who seems to be moving too fast or asking for money. The same applies everywhere, but the warmth of the culture can make a newcomer lower their guard faster than they should.

## Spain

Spain makes social life the foundation of the dating scene. People rarely meet a partner through a formal introduction. They meet through friends, at bars, in the long, loose evenings that start with one drink and finish somewhere unrecognisable. The culture of going out in groups means a new person often enters a circle first and a relationship second.

The pace is relaxed. Spaniards tend to treat attraction as a natural by-product of shared company, and they will not rush it. Humour carries enormous weight, and the ability to be teased and to tease back is a basic requirement. Directness is common and usually friendly, and the line between flirting and conversation is intentionally blurry.

Apps are used, as everywhere, but the app is often a prelude to a drink with a friend present, not a candlelit dinner. Formal dates exist, though many Spaniards would find the word itself a little stiff. You see each other, you keep seeing each other, and one day someone says the word novio or novia and the matter is settled.

For a Colombian arriving in Spain, a lot is familiar: the warmth, the sociability, the language. What is different is the tone. Spain is less ceremonial and more casual, with less emphasis on gallantry and more on companionship. Expect equality in conversation, no expectation that someone pays for everything and an unspoken understanding that independence is attractive.

Language is a shortcut in both countries. A few sentences of decent Spanish transform your prospects, and the effort is noticed and appreciated. Spanish in Spain and Spanish in Colombia are not identical, and the vocabulary differences produce a rich supply of comic misunderstandings. Laugh at them. Laughter is the only currency that is accepted equally in both places.

## The Verdict

Colombia is for people who want romance with the volume turned up. It is expressive, attentive and rich in ritual, and it makes you feel noticed. If you enjoy being courted and enjoy courting, it is hard to beat.

Spain is for people who want a relationship that sneaks up on them. It is easier, lighter and less pressured, and it suits those who prefer a gradual path to commitment. The trade-off is that you may spend months unsure what you are to each other.

I give Colombia the edge for intensity and Spain the edge for ease. If I were single and sensible, I would choose Spain. Since I have never been either, I would choose Colombia and learn to dance badly in public, which is, in the end, the only reliable way to find out who actually likes you.

## Conclusion

Romance travels badly. The signals that mean interest in one country mean politeness in the next, and a gesture that melts one heart will bore another. Colombia and Spain share a language and a love of company, and they still disagree on almost everything that matters in the first three dates.

So watch, listen and ask. Dress for Colombia, relax for Spain and never assume. The one rule that survives every border is that sincerity, delivered with a sense of humour, will get you further than any strategy you have ever read about.
