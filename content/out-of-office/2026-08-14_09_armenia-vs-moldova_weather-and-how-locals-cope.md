# Two Ways to Survive a Winter Nobody Warned You About

🇦🇲 Armenia 🇲🇩 Moldova
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Armenia and Moldova both get lumped into vague "Eastern Europe, probably cold" mental categories by people who've never actually spent a January in either place, and both will correct that assumption fast, in very different registers. Yerevan sits in a mountain basin that swings from scorching, dry summers to genuinely brutal winters, with temperature drops that catch newcomers off guard every single year regardless of how much they were warned. Moldova, flatter and more exposed, gets a damper, greyer cold — less dramatic on a thermometer, more relentless in how long it drags on.

I've shivered through winters in both. Yerevan's cold has teeth, sudden and sharp against those gorgeous blue-sky mountain views. Chisinau's cold is more of a slow, grey siege that wears you down through sheer duration rather than intensity. Neither country complains about it much. Both have just quietly built entire cultures around enduring it.

## Do's & Don'ts: Armenia

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Budget seriously for winter heating — gas costs spike and older buildings lose heat fast | Assume Yerevan's sunny reputation means mild winters; the basin traps genuine cold |
| Invest in proper layering — the dry cold is deceptive and drops fast after sunset | Skip checking your apartment's heating system before signing a winter lease |
| Embrace the mountain views in winter; clear skies over snow-capped Ararat are genuinely stunning | Underestimate summer heat either — Yerevan swings from one extreme to the other |

## Do's & Don'ts: Moldova

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Check your building's centralized heating schedule — many older Soviet-era buildings run on fixed timing | Expect consistently sunny winter days; grey, damp skies are the default for months |
| Stock up on warm, practical layers for a persistent, low-grade cold rather than sharp drops | Assume rural Moldova has the same heating reliability as central Chisinau |
| Lean into the local rhythm of slower, indoor winter socialising over wine and home cooking | Underestimate how long the grey season actually lasts — it stretches well past what the calendar suggests |

## Armenia: Sharp Extremes in a Beautiful Bowl

Yerevan sits in a valley ringed by mountains, and that geography is directly responsible for both its beauty and its weather mood swings. Summers run hot and dry, regularly pushing past 35°C with a relentless, dust-dry heat that empties the streets by mid-afternoon. Winters flip hard the other direction, dropping well below freezing with a dry, biting cold that photographs beautifully against snow-dusted mountains and feels considerably less beautiful once you're standing in it without proper layers.

What catches newcomers off guard isn't just the temperature, it's the heating infrastructure underneath it. Many of Yerevan's older Soviet-era apartment buildings were never built with modern insulation standards in mind, and gas heating costs, which spiked notably in recent years, mean winter can become a genuinely expensive season fast if you haven't checked how well-insulated your building actually is before signing a lease. Locals have adapted with a mix of practical layering, space heaters as backup, and a general acceptance that January electricity bills simply cost more, full stop.

There's a resilience to how Armenians handle this that goes beyond mere tolerance. Winter markets stay lively, cafés fill with people determined not to let the cold dictate their social calendar, and the mountain views — Ararat catching the low winter light — genuinely soften the sting of the cold in a way that flatter, greyer countries simply can't offer. It's a harder winter than the sunny brochure photos suggest, but it comes with a payoff those photos also, for once, aren't exaggerating.

## Moldova: The Cold That Wears You Down Through Duration, Not Drama

Moldova's winter rarely hits the dramatic lows Yerevan sees, but it makes up for that with sheer persistence. The cold here is damp, grey, and long — months of overcast skies rather than sharp, photogenic extremes, and that consistency is, in its own way, harder to psychologically endure than a country that at least rewards you with blue skies between cold snaps. Chisinau's winter doesn't announce itself with drama. It just settles in around November and doesn't fully lift until April, wearing down morale through sheer duration.

Heating here carries its own particular quirk: many older, Soviet-era apartment blocks still run on centralized heating systems with fixed schedules rather than individual thermostat control, meaning residents sometimes find themselves too warm in October and shivering in a supposedly heated apartment in February, depending entirely on when the building's system was designed to switch on and off, a decision made decades ago by planners who never met you. Newer buildings and private homes have moved toward individual heating control, but plenty of the older housing stock hasn't caught up, and expats renting in Soviet-era blocks should ask very specifically about heating schedules before committing to a lease.

Moldovans cope the way people in long, grey winters generally do everywhere: by moving life indoors and making that indoor life genuinely warm. Wine culture, unsurprisingly given Moldova's status as one of the world's great under-the-radar wine producers, plays a real role here, alongside long, unhurried home-cooked meals and a social calendar that shifts almost entirely toward small gatherings rather than public outdoor life. The cold isn't fought so much as quietly waited out, glass in hand, until spring finally, eventually, shows up.

## The Verdict

Armenia wins on drama and payoff — a sharper, colder winter, yes, but one set against genuinely stunning mountain scenery that makes the suffering feel almost worth it on the right clear day. Moldova wins on nothing dramatic, but there's something to be said for its approach: a quieter, wine-soaked indoor culture that's made peace with grey skies rather than fighting them. If I had to choose a winter to endure, I'd take Yerevan's cold with Ararat on the horizon over Chisinau's endless grey, but I'd take Moldova's wine cellars over Armenia's space heaters without a second thought.

> *Internations Chisinau — someone described the Moldovan winter as "not cold enough to complain about loudly, but long enough to genuinely affect your mood by February."*

## Conclusion

Armenia and Moldova both prove that "Eastern European winter" is nowhere near a single experience — one is sharp, dry, and dramatic against mountains, the other is grey, damp, and quietly relentless across flatter terrain. Neither country makes a fuss about it publicly, because fussing has never once made a winter shorter. Pack real layers for Yerevan's cold snaps and real patience for Chisinau's grey stretch, and you'll survive both. Just don't expect either country to apologise for weather it's had centuries to get used to and you haven't.
