# Germany Will Tell You Exactly What It Wants. Morocco Will Make You Guess, Chaperone Included

🇩🇪 Germany 🇲🇦 Morocco
*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Germany approaches dating the way it approaches everything else: with a startling absence of small talk and a refreshing, occasionally brutal, commitment to stating intentions upfront. Morocco approaches dating as a negotiation between the individual, the family, the neighbourhood, and, depending on the city, a fairly conservative reading of what's publicly acceptable. Both systems produce relationships. Neither produces them the way a rom-com would have you believe.

I've had a German man tell me, on a first coffee, that he wasn't looking for anything serious and would appreciate clarity on my intentions before we ordered a second round. I've also sat in a Fez medina café watching a young couple maintain a scrupulous three feet of distance while an aunt "just happened" to be walking past for the fourth time. One of these is efficient. The other is a full-contact sport played at a respectful distance. Guess which.

## Do's & Don'ts: Germany

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Say what you want, clearly, early — ambiguity reads as dishonest | Play games or send mixed signals — it will be read as a red flag, not intrigue |
| Split the bill unless someone explicitly offers otherwise | Assume a coffee date implies romantic interest — it might just be coffee |
| Expect punctuality — five minutes late needs a text | Be offended by directness — it's respect, not rudeness |

## Do's & Don'ts: Morocco

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Understand that public affection is heavily frowned upon | Assume Western dating apps map cleanly onto local courtship norms |
| Expect family involvement, especially outside major cities | Push for a meeting alone early on without discussing context first |
| Recognise regional variation — Casablanca and rural towns differ hugely | Treat "getting to know the family" as optional or skippable |

## Germany: Efficiency, Applied to the Heart

German dating culture runs on a principle that would horrify most Anglophone daters: say the thing. Germans don't do the extended ambiguity phase, the will-they-won't-they, the deliberately vague texting cadence designed to keep options open. If a German asks you out for coffee, it might genuinely just be coffee, and they will tell you so, because leaving you to wonder feels, to them, actively unkind. If it's a date, they'll usually say that too, sometimes with a directness that lands like a foam dart to the sternum for anyone raised on more evasive courtship rituals.

This directness extends to logistics that other cultures leave charmingly unresolved. Splitting the bill isn't a power move or a statement about gender politics, it's just the default, tracked with an efficiency that borders on the administrative — I've watched a German date literally open a calculator app to divide a restaurant bill down to the cent, and nobody at the table found this remotely strange. Punctuality is similarly non-negotiable; five minutes late requires a text, ten requires an apology, and repeated lateness will simply end things, because it reads as disrespect for someone else's time, not romantic mystique.

Where it gets genuinely useful is the emotional labour saved. German dating culture largely eliminates the guessing games that eat up months of other systems — the "does he like me" spirals, the reading of texting response times as tea leaves. If a German isn't interested, they will, with startling frequency, simply tell you, sometimes before you've finished your sentence. It's a mercy, even when it stings. The apps (predominantly Bumble, Tinder, and the more relationship-oriented Parship) mirror this: profiles tend to state exactly what people are looking for — casual, serious, open relationship — with a specificity that would read as oversharing anywhere else and reads here as basic courtesy.

## Morocco: Courtship as a Family Production

Dating in Morocco isn't a two-person activity, it's a production with a supporting cast, and the size of that cast depends heavily on where you are. Casablanca and Rabat have visibly Westernised dating scenes, with young professionals meeting through apps, cafés, and university circles in ways that would look familiar to a European visitor. But step outside the major cities, or even just outside the most cosmopolitan neighbourhoods within them, and courtship reverts to something far more structured, more chaperoned, and far more conscious of what the neighbours will say.

Public displays of affection are a genuine risk, not a minor faux pas — holding hands can draw stares in more conservative areas, and anything beyond that risks real social, sometimes legal, consequences, since unmarried couples cohabiting or being visibly intimate in public can technically fall foul of local law, even if enforcement varies enormously by city and by how foreign you look. This isn't presented as oppressive by most Moroccans I've spoken to; it's presented as the water they swim in, the framework courtship has always operated inside.

Family involvement isn't a later-stage development, it's baked in from early on. Meeting the family isn't the "getting serious" milestone it is in Germany or the UK, it's often part of how a relationship becomes legitimate in the first place, particularly outside the biggest cities. Foreigners dating Moroccans, especially foreign women dating Moroccan men, often describe a courtship that moves at two speeds simultaneously: fast in terms of family integration, and deliberately, sometimes maddeningly slow in terms of anything resembling Western-style physical or public intimacy.

What nobody quite prepares you for is how much genuine warmth exists inside this structure. The chaperoning, the family scrutiny, the public restraint, none of it reads as coldness once you're inside it. If anything, it produces a kind of earnestness that Berlin's brutal efficiency doesn't always leave room for. It's just earnestness with an audience.

## The Verdict

Germany wins on clarity, on saving you months of interpretive labour, on simply telling you where you stand. Morocco wins on depth of ritual, on making a relationship feel like it means something to more than just the two people in it, whether or not you asked for that scale of involvement.

If efficiency is your love language, book a flight to Berlin. If you want your romance to come with a full extended cast, subplots, and at least one aunt with impeccable timing, Morocco delivers, but be honest with yourself about whether you actually want an audience for your love life. Some of us just want to know if it's a date or not without opening a calculator app to find out.

## Conclusion

Germany treats romance like a well-run meeting: agenda stated upfront, outcomes clear, nobody wasting anyone's time. Morocco treats it like an inheritance, passed through family, neighbourhood, and social expectation before it's fully yours to define. Neither is wrong, though one will save you a great deal of therapy and the other will occasionally make you feel like you're auditioning for a role you didn't know was open. Date accordingly, and in Morocco, budget extra time for the aunts.
