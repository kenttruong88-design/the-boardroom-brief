# A Country Where Dating Happens Through Three Intermediaries and a Country Where It Happens at 1am Over a Shared Plate of Jamón

🇵🇰 Pakistan vs 🇪🇸 Spain — Dating and Social Scene

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Somewhere around relocation number nine I stopped expecting "the social scene" to mean the same thing twice, and nowhere has that been truer than the distance between Lahore and Madrid. Pakistan's social life is rich, warm, and almost entirely mediated through family, introductions, and group gatherings where romance, if it happens at all, happens quietly in the margins. Spain's social life starts at 10pm, has no apparent closing time, and treats romantic pursuit as a loud, public, communal sport conducted over shared plates and far too much vermouth.

People love to flatten both into a single lazy narrative — "conservative" versus "passionate" — as if either country could be summarised in one adjective stolen from a travel brochure. It cannot. Pakistan's social warmth is some of the most genuine hospitality you will ever receive anywhere on earth. Spain's social stamina will outlast your liver, your sleep schedule, and your sense of what a reasonable Tuesday looks like.

## Do's & Don'ts: Pakistan

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Accept invitations to family gatherings — this is where most genuine social life actually happens | Assume Western-style public dating norms translate directly; they generally don't, especially outside major cities |
| Let friendships develop slowly through group settings before expecting one-on-one time | Discuss a budding relationship loudly or publicly; discretion is the norm, not the exception |
| Appreciate that hospitality here is a serious cultural value, not a performance | Mistake warm hospitality for romantic interest; it is offered to everyone, equally, constantly |
| Use introductions through trusted mutual contacts — they carry real social weight | Expect dating apps to work the same way they do elsewhere; usage exists but is far more discreet |

## Do's & Don'ts: Spain

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Embrace the late schedule — dinner at 9pm and plans continuing past midnight are completely normal | Suggest meeting for dinner at 6pm unless you want to eat alone in an empty restaurant |
| Join a tertulia or a regular bar group — repeated casual contact is how friendships actually form here | Expect a first date to mean a quiet one-on-one; group hangouts often come first and stay the norm |
| Learn to linger — a coffee can become three hours and nobody will rush you out | Be offended by directness; Spaniards will tell you exactly what they think, often loudly, often fondly |
| Use the terraza culture to your advantage — outdoor tables are where most real socialising happens | Assume August plans will go ahead as scheduled; half the country disappears on holiday simultaneously |

## Pakistan: Warmth as the Entire Operating System

Pakistan's social scene runs on an entirely different engine to what most Western arrivals expect, and the sooner you stop trying to force it into a familiar shape, the sooner you'll actually enjoy it. Hospitality here isn't a nice bonus, it's close to a civic religion — you will be fed, welcomed, and included with a warmth that can genuinely stun people from colder social cultures, and it will happen whether or not there is any romantic undertone whatsoever. Family and community gatherings are where social life actually concentrates: weddings that run for days, extended family dinners, and neighbourhood networks that know your business before you've told anyone yourself.

Romance exists, obviously, vibrantly, in every major city — but it operates with considerably more discretion than the Instagram-public courtships you might be used to, particularly outside the most cosmopolitan circles of Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad. Dating apps are used, quietly, by a growing urban population, but public displays and loud declarations are simply not the cultural grammar here. Group settings do the heavy lifting that one-on-one "dates" do elsewhere: you get to know someone through their friends, their cousins, their whole social orbit, long before anything resembling a formal pursuit begins. Mistake this slowness for coldness and you will miss one of the warmest, most genuinely connected social cultures you will ever be welcomed into. The Ministry of Human Rights' own public messaging around social norms reflects exactly this emphasis on family and community as the primary social unit, rather than the individual-first dating culture exported relentlessly by Western media.

## Spain: A Country That Treats Socialising as a Full-Contact Sport

Spain does not do quiet social lives. The entire rhythm of the day is built around delaying everything until it can be done properly, in company, outdoors, and preferably with a shared plate of something fried in the middle of the table. Dinner at 9 or 10pm is not fashionable lateness, it is simply dinner time, and plans routinely continue well past 1am on a weekday with the same casual energy most cultures reserve for a special occasion. The terraza — the outdoor café table — is less a seating option than the actual stage on which Spanish social life performs itself, loudly, visibly, and usually with strong opinions about football interrupting every third sentence.

Dating here is refreshingly direct by comparison: Spaniards will tell you plainly if they're interested, plainly if they're not, and will not spend six months dancing around an ambiguous signal the way some other cultures might. Group hangouts — a cuadrilla of friends who have known each other since childhood and absorb newcomers with surprising ease — often precede anything resembling a formal date, which can feel disorienting if you're used to the one-on-one coffee-date format, but tends to produce friendships and relationships built on a far broader foundation. August is its own separate social universe: much of the country simply leaves for the coast or the village, plans evaporate en masse, and anyone still trying to schedule a serious meeting in Madrid that month will be gently, repeatedly ignored. The Instituto Nacional de Estadística's own time-use surveys consistently show Spain's evening social hours running later than almost anywhere else in Europe, which tracks with literally every single night I spent there wondering how anyone functions the next morning.

## The Verdict

If you want to be folded into a warm, slower-moving social world where hospitality itself is the love language and romance is a quiet subplot rather than the headline, Pakistan offers something genuinely rare and deeply underrated. If you want to be thrown headfirst into a loud, late, communal social whirlwind where directness is king and nobody will let you go home before 1am, Spain will not just offer that, it will insist on it.

I'm calling it for Spain, but only because my liver has apparently forgiven me and my social calendar has never been fuller. Pakistan's warmth deserves enormous respect and considerably less lazy stereotyping than it gets from outsiders who've never actually sat at one of those family tables.

## Conclusion

Neither of these countries is going to hand you a social life that looks like a rom-com, and thank goodness for that. Pakistan will teach you that warmth doesn't need an agenda, and Spain will teach you that an agenda can start at midnight and still somehow work out fine. Stop waiting for either to behave like home. Accept the family dinner. Accept the 11pm plan. Your liver and your social calendar will both have opinions about this, and for once, they will not agree with each other.
