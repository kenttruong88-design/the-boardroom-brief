# Everyone In Both Cities Is Single, Tired, and Blaming the Hawker Centre

🇸🇬 Singapore vs 🇭🇰 Hong Kong

*By Suki Nakamura, Out of Office*

Let me save you some time, the one resource nobody in either city has to spare: Singapore and Hong Kong are the two most eligible, hardest-working, worst-dated populations in Asia, and both have independently decided the fix is to work even more. I have watched grown adults in both cities describe an 11pm conference call after a 90-minute commute as "just how it is," with the flat affect of someone describing weather. Then they wonder, genuinely baffled, why they haven't been on a date since the last decade.

Time Out's most recent love-and-sex survey ranked both cities dead last in Asia for romance, beaten by Macau — a place whose main industries are gambling and wedding photography. That is not a coincidence, it's a diagnosis. These are two extraordinarily efficient, extraordinarily well-run cities that have optimised the romance clean out of themselves, and their residents have responded, as people do, by building entire subcultures around complaining about it online instead of fixing it in person.

**Do's & Don'ts: Singapore**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Treat the hawker centre as a legitimate first-date venue — it works, and it's cheaper than whatever "concept dining" trap you were about to fall into | Act shocked when a real share of locals tell researchers they find chilli crab at a plastic table unromantic — some of them mean it |
| Download more than one app; Tinder, Coffee Meets Bagel and Paktor each cater to a different crowd and a different level of commitment | Lead with your BTO timeline, your five-year plan, or your parents' opinion on the relationship by date two |
| Use CMB if you're actually looking for something serious — that's precisely the niche it carved out and kept | Assume "shall we get bubble tea" is a non-committal suggestion; in Singapore it is a documented courtship ritual with its own etiquette |
| Accept that most courtships now happen over months of WhatsApp before anyone commits to a dinner | Ghost — the dating pool is small enough that your ex's new date is statistically your colleague's cousin |

**Do's & Don'ts: Hong Kong**

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Budget actual money for this — speed-dating nights and boutique matchmakers routinely run into the thousands of HKD, and that's considered normal, not desperate | Expect your date to have energy left after a 50-hour work week; the AIA Whitepaper found Hong Kong the hardest-working city on earth, and it shows in every half-asleep "sure, Lan Kwai Fong?" text |
| Try the rooftop bar circuit — Hong Kong's vertical geography means "going out" is an actual production, so dress for it | Schedule anything that competes with a client call; 77% of Hongkongers report taking work calls on holiday, and your date has almost certainly done it mid-date |
| Lean on expat societies and sports leagues if apps have gone stale — the social scene genuinely does reward showing up in person | Mistake "no standard working hours" legislation for a quirky fact; it is the entire reason everyone you match with cancels on a Tuesday |
| Take the matchmaker route seriously if you can afford it — SCMP reports it's become the default, not the backup plan, for busy professionals | Confuse "efficient" with "fast"; everything here moves fast except the courtship, which moves at the speed of two overlapping calendars |

## Singapore: Efficient, Wholesome, Quietly Exhausted

Singapore's dating culture is what happens when a famously efficient government decides declining birth rates are a planning problem, and a famously online population decides they're a meme opportunity — and both are simultaneously correct. The apps tell the story: YouGov's research shows Tinder dominates for volume, but Coffee Meets Bagel has carved out a dominant niche among Singaporeans looking for something that ends in a joint HDB application rather than a Saturday. That alone tells you the national character. Even the casual-dating app market gets colonised by people who are, deep down, extremely serious about finding a spouse on a reasonable, spreadsheet-friendly timeline.

Then there's the hawker centre question, now a genuine cultural fault line. A widely cited survey found a real minority of Singaporeans flatly will not accept a first date at a hawker centre — too casual, too loud, too much risk of an auntie reserving your table mid-conversation. The clear majority, correctly, think this is the best first-date format in the country: cheap, easy to abandon if it's going badly, free of the pressure of a candlelit restaurant where you can hear every word about their ex. I am firmly in that majority. Anyone who thinks laksa at a hawker stall isn't romantic has never had good laksa.

Underneath the chilli-crab banter is something grimmer: Singapore's own marriage statistics, tracked annually by SingStat, show a persistent pattern of rising singlehood among young adults — not a lack of apps, but a lack of time and energy after national service, exam culture, and an 8am-to-whenever work ethic take their cut. Singapore didn't kill romance. It just scheduled it for a slot that never comes up.

## Hong Kong: Vertical, Expensive, Allergic to Idle Time

Hong Kong's dating scene runs on one principle: everyone is busy, stressed, and willing to pay someone else to fix it. SCMP has repeatedly documented the rise of boutique matchmaking agencies charging thousands of Hong Kong dollars to professionals who've simply run out of hours to meet anyone the old-fashioned way. This isn't a niche lifestyle trend; AIA's 2025 Whitepaper named Hong Kong the hardest-working city on the planet at 50 hours a week, and a separate survey found Hongkongers report the worst work-life balance in Asia-Pacific, with 77% taking work calls on holiday. You cannot build romance on a population that answers Slack from the beach.

What's left is vertical and loud: Lan Kwai Fong, Soho's rooftop bars, a nightlife scene that compensates for lack of time with sheer intensity when it does happen. Hongkongers don't date slowly out of caution — they date in bursts, because bursts are all the calendar allows. SCMP's coverage of Hongkongers finding love "without Tinder and Bumble" notes a real swing toward sports clubs, hiking groups, and expat societies, less as romantic philosophy than triage: if the apps haven't worked after the eleventh matched-and-ghosted conversation, show up in person and hope gravity does the rest.

The honest version: Hong Kong has no shortage of eligible, interesting people. It has a shortage of hours in which two of them can be in the same room, sober, not checking a phone for a reply from their managing director.

## The Verdict

Want volume, intensity, and the thrill of never knowing whether your date cancels for a 9pm call with Zurich? Hong Kong. Want a slower, earnest courtship that ends, statistically, in a joint savings account and a hawker centre wedding dinner? Singapore. Neither city hands you romance; both make you fight your calendar for it, and only one charges a matchmaker's fee for the privilege.

My verdict: Singapore wins, on points, because its singles are merely exhausted, while Hong Kong's are exhausted *and* billing their social life to a credit card. A culture that turns courtship into a subscription service has lost the plot. Give me the aunties at the kopitiam eyeing my date over the rooftop bar where everyone's phone is lit up with Bloomberg alerts. At least in Singapore the exhaustion is free.

## Conclusion

Here's the part nobody puts in the brochure: both cities are world-class at almost everything, and both have engineered themselves into a corner where falling in love requires the kind of advance planning normally reserved for a corporate offsite. Singapore will at least feed you well while you're failing at it. Hong Kong will simply bill you for the attempt and call it a matchmaking fee. Move to either expecting dazzling, effortless romance and you'll instead find two extremely competent cities, full of extremely competent people, all quietly too tired to flirt properly. Which, if you think about it, is the most honest romance either place has left: showing up at all, after the hours they've put in, counts as a grand gesture now. Plan accordingly, bring snacks, and do not, under any circumstances, call it exotic.
