# Corporate Survival Mode: A Quarter of US Workers Took TikTok's Advice

A Careerminds survey of 1,000 full-time US workers, conducted in November 2025 and reported by Innovative Human Capital in December, found that 24.5% had used TikTok strategies at work at least once. It is a year old, but the advice has aged better than the economy it was written for. Among Gen Z the share was 48.22%. Millennials came in at 36.29%.

*Photo: Ono Kosuki via Pexels*

The tips are exactly what you would expect from a platform whose business model is anxiety. Creators named in the write-up offer "corporate survival tips you need right now", "5 behaviours employees adopt when layoffs are coming", and hacks that "help you, and the ones that will get you fired". One recommendation is keyboard slamming, a method of appearing busy that has the added benefit of annoying everyone in earshot.

The behaviour underneath is more telling than the advice. In the survey, 46.47% of workers had job-searched "just in case". About 41.18% were worrying more about job security. More than a third (33.53%) had avoided taking PTO or sick days, and 38.2% had taken on extra work. Around 15.29% had delayed asking for a raise or promotion, which is what confidence looks like after a year of headlines.

Raymond Lee, president of Careerminds, said workers should not have to rely on TikTok creators to understand how to protect their jobs. He is right, and also describing the market that currently exists.

Age made a difference. Gen X came in at 11.82% and Boomers at 4.04%, so the people with the most experience of surviving layoffs are the least likely to learn how from an app. Among Gen Z, 22.32% had avoided PTO and the same share had delayed asking for a raise. They are doing everything the video said, and nobody has told them the video is not a contract.

For managers, the finding is less about social media than about signal. If a quarter of your people are performing busyness, your dashboards are measuring theatre. If a third are skipping leave, your culture has a policy and a practice, and they are different.

The funniest detail is the survey's own caution: 21.4% of workers felt less confident about job security than a year earlier. The rest are presumably on TikTok, learning how to look as though they feel fine.
