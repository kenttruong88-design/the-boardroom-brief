# Canva Lets Leavers Keep Their MacBooks and Slack, Briefly

There is an etiquette to being laid off, and this week, Canva wrote a chapter. Chief executive Melanie Perkins published a memo on the company's website saying changes affect 1,271 employees, or 22% of the organisation, according to Ragan Communications' weekly roundup.

![Laptop on an office desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-07-02-canva-leavers-keep-macbooks-slack-hero)
*Photo: Zen Chung via Pexels*

The details are where it gets interesting. Some roles will change or move to centralised hubs; others will end. Anyone leaving receives at least six months' salary and, Ragan reported, may keep their company MacBook. Perkins also told staff that leavers keep Slack and Gmail access until the end of the week.

Consider that last item. For the remaining days of the week, a person who no longer works at Canva retains the one thing that made working at Canva exhausting: notifications. They can read the channel where everyone else is processing the news. They may even react to messages with the little thumbs-up. It is the corporate version of being allowed to stay at the party after the host has announced it's over.

The laptop is a better gesture. A company laptop is the single most useful thing a person can take with them on the way out, and it can be found in the "kept" column rather than the "returned in a prepaid envelope" column, where it usually lives. Credit where due.

Squarespace, meanwhile, laid off nearly 300 people, 19% of its workforce, and its chief executive Anthony Casalena wrote that the plan involves "smaller teams, more player coaches, and a simplified organizational structure." A player-coach is someone who manages people and also does the work. In other words, the job title you get when two jobs are merged and one salary stays.

People Inc. told staff it is cutting 195 jobs and has added 320 new employees so far in 2026, per Chief Executive Neil Vogel's memo, which Variety obtained. That is not a reassuring statistic if you are among the 195, but it makes a lovely sentence.

Taken together, this week's memos form a style guide. The kind approach: money, a laptop, a few days of Slack. The compressed approach: fewer managers and more "player coaches." The arithmetic approach: net hiring, still positive.

The best advice for anyone receiving one of these: take the laptop, take the six months, and mute the channel.
