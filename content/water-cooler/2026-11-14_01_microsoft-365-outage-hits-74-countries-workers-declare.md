# Microsoft 365 Outage Hits 74 Countries; Workers Declare Unofficial Holiday

Microsoft's Outlook, Teams, Exchange Online and Copilot went down late on Thursday and stayed down well into Friday across at least 74 countries, according to Kenya's Citizen Digital. Downdetector reports for Outlook peaked at about 138,000, India.com reported. Microsoft 365 had been down for more than 12 hours, Attitude said, citing Reuters.

Microsoft said the outage was not a cyberattack. Servers failed automated health checks and dropped out, and the remaining ones were overloaded, Citizen Digital reported. Microsoft also admitted its first restoration attempts had not gone as expected and that services would return gradually. In other words, the cure was also a bit unwell.

The internet responded as it always does: by deciding the outage was a gift.

"Dear Microsoft, there is really no rush. Rest, hydrate, and come back next week," one user wrote on X, according to Citizen Digital. Another said, per Gulf News: "If Outlook doesn't come back we are legally allowed to take the rest of the week off." A third observed that days like this remind you your entire job is just sending emails. It was not a joke. It was a performance review.

The brands joined in. KitKat's US account posted, "So we're all just signing off for the day now, right?" and Emirates told its followers that their email might be down, but the skies are always open, according to Gulf News. The marketing departments of the brands involved appeared to enjoy themselves, and they did it without a single email.

There was also a human cost. In the UK, workers could not reach Outlook or Teams for a 9am meeting, Attitude reported. In Kenya, banks sent customers notices, and IT helpdesks sent staff advisories, Citizen Digital said.

Consider what the outage revealed. One company's servers misbehaved, and a large share of global office work paused. No one was unable to do their job. They were unable to prove they were doing it, which is a different thing.

The coverage offers no figure for how much real work happened on Friday, and one suspects no one will produce one. The inbox returns, the unread count climbs back, and everyone resumes the great shared agreement that this was productivity all along.

A manager somewhere will draft a message about lessons learned. It will go out the moment Outlook works.
