# Logitech Gives 7,000 Staff a Day Off to Play Video Games

Logitech has told its 7,000 employees to take one day off to play a video game of their choice. The programme is called "Logi Game Day". It runs for about a year, covers everyone from full-time staff to interns, and can be saved until 2027, Fortune reported on 10 November.

![A video game controller on a desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-21-01-logitech-game-day-gta-hero)
*Photo: Cedric Fauntleroy via Pexels*

The timing is not subtle. Grand Theft Auto VI launched on 19 November, and chief executive Hanneke Faber said she would play that day, although she will still go into the office for the company's streaming event, where the space becomes a gaming lounge. Chief financial officer Matteo Anversa plans to play Forza on a sim racing rig. The chief people officer, Elaine Laird, will compete with colleagues at Block Blast, a mobile puzzle game, which is the only part of the plan that sounds like work.

The policy has a rival to defeat: the sick day. BambooHR data cited by Fortune shows requests for time off on the release date running nearly 13 percent above comparable days in previous years. The firm's survey found that 18 percent of salaried workers planned to take the day off, and that half of gamers planning time off for a release would call in sick rather than ask for PTO in advance. Logitech has therefore spotted a gap in the market for honesty.

Faber said it was time to retire the stereotype that gaming is a waste of time. Laird said play is not the opposite of work. A Logitech study of 900 adults and 100 gamers in nine countries found that four in five agreed gaming makes them more confident handling real-life challenges. Logitech, it should be noted, sells game controllers.

One estimate in the article suggests the US economy could lose up to $1 billion in productivity if 1.5 million workers skipped work for the launch. Economist Victor Matheson of the College of the Holy Cross called that loss tiny in the big picture, and said it is not necessarily a bad thing if people are happier.

The company's spokesperson summed up the policy: exploring Vice City on launch day, you won't hear any complaints from us. Somewhere in a nearby office, a manager is reading that sentence and quietly updating a calendar.
