# A Third of Bosses Trust AI Over Their Own Judgment

About a third of managers and executives say they trust AI more than their own judgment, according to a Charter survey reported by Fortune on 16 November. Among non-managers, the figure is 16.3 percent. The survey covered 5,002 full-time US desk workers who use AI at work.

*Photo: fauxels via Pexels*

The finding sits uneasily beside the rest of the data. Executives were 3.3 times as likely as other workers to use AI output they doubt. They are also more optimistic: 85 percent of executives were positive about AI's impact on the organisation, against 56 percent of employees. The people who will be least affected by an AI error are the people most ready to forward it.

To be fair to leadership, executives also check more. Some 81 percent of them said they always or usually fact-check AI output, against 71 percent of workers. That is a good number, and also a slightly alarming one, because it means roughly one executive in five does not.

Brian Elliott of Charter, who works on the research, put the likely explanation in one phrase: AI is great at summaries and high-level answers. That, he noted, is much of what a leader needs. It is also much of what a leader has historically paid a consultant for, and the consultant at least had slides.

There is a tidy logic to the arrangement. A manager asks a question, gets a confident summary in four seconds, and moves on to the next meeting. An analyst asks the same question, spends four hours finding where the summary is wrong, and is told the deck looks great.

There is a practical lesson for anyone on the receiving end. If your manager has just forwarded you a perfectly formatted summary with the instruction to "sanity check it", you are not being asked to check it. You are being asked to be the other 19 percent. Bring a highlighter, and a calm face.

The survey does not say which group is correct. It says only that confidence rises with seniority, and that the checking does not. Anyone who has worked in an office will recognise the pattern. The only new element is that the confident colleague who never read the report now has a very good ghostwriter.
