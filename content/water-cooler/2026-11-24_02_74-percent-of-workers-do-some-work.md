# 74% of Workers Do Some Work on Sunday, Owl Labs Report Finds

The four-day workweek has not arrived. The six-day one has, quietly, and nobody is advertising it. According to Owl Labs' 10th annual State of Hybrid Work report, covered by Fortune in September, 74 percent of employees do some work on a typical Sunday.

![A laptop on a sofa on a Sunday](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-24-02-sunday-work-laptop-hero)
*Photo: kaboompics via Pexels*

For 21 percent of them, Sunday is a full or near-full workday. That is roughly one in five people whose weekend is a Monday that starts early and has worse snacks.

Frank Weishaupt, the chief executive of Owl Labs, told Fortune: "We have completely shifted away from a conversation about the four-day workweek." The industry had a nice idea, then went back to the laptop on Sunday night. Weishaupt described Sunday work as flexibility, provided it is not additive to the rest of the week. That condition is doing a great deal of work, and so, apparently, is everyone else.

The report adds findings that fit the pattern. Fifty-three percent of workers have adopted or plan to adopt "career minimalism," which means doing the job and nothing beyond it. Weishaupt called that "one of the more concerning pieces of data." So employees are working seven days while minimizing their careers. Both things can be true. It is a diet where you eat dinner twice and skip breakfast.

Artificial intelligence was supposed to help. Sixty-nine percent of workers in the report say AI has made them more productive. Forty-six percent say it has hindered them at times. Weishaupt called the current stage a "trade-off period," a phrase that has probably been used for every technology since the stapler. Productivity, as ever, produces more work to be productive about.

There is a fair case for Sunday work. A parent who attends a school play on Tuesday afternoon and catches up on Sunday evening has traded hours, not added them. Whether that describes a given worker is something only that worker knows.

The Sunday number is the useful one. It will not appear in a flexible-work policy, a careers-page banner or a benefits deck. It will appear in a calendar, at 8 p.m., with a laptop on a sofa. Management calls that autonomy. Everyone on the sofa calls it Sunday.
