# AI Cut the Googling and Left the Meetings Alone, Gallup Finds

Artificial intelligence has changed how American workers spend their time, according to a new Gallup survey, with one conspicuous exception: the meeting.

![A video call meeting on a laptop](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-22-01-gallup-ai-cut-googling-meetings-hero)
*Photo: Jack Sparrow via Pexels*

Gallup surveyed 21,648 US employees in August and found that 32% now use AI at work at least a few times a week, up two points on the previous quarter. Among those frequent users, 88% said AI has changed how they spend their time. The details are a tidy portrait of the modern desk.

Fifty-two percent of frequent users spend less time gathering information. Forty-seven percent spend less time writing and drafting. Thirty-eight percent spend less time analysing data, and 29% less on administrative tasks. Machines, it turns out, are excellent at the parts of the job that involve opening fourteen tabs.

Now the other column. Gallup found that 83% of frequent users say the time they spend in meetings is about the same. So is the time they spend working with customers (82%) and collaborating with coworkers (78%). The robot has taken the research and the first draft. It has left behind the sync, the pre-sync and the follow-up to the sync.

There is a small cost, and it is not in the minutes. Thirty-six percent of frequent users say AI has raised expectations for their daily output, compared with 16% of infrequent users. A third of frequent users feel pressure to use AI at all, versus 17% of the rest. Sixty-five percent say the mental effort is unchanged, 21% say it fell and 14% say it rose.

So the arrangement, if you squint, goes like this. AI makes the work faster. Expectations go up to match. The meetings remain, presumably because nobody has found a way to automate the sentence "let's take that offline."

Gallup's own framing is more restrained. It says leaders are still working out what AI means for their teams. That is a polite way of describing a workplace where the tools have changed and the calendar has not.

The practical takeaway is modest. If your job now has more time for deep work, it is because someone else's AI summarised your reading. If it does not, your calendar is presumably working exactly as designed. Either way, the meeting is still on, and it could, in some cases, have been an email that a machine read for you.
