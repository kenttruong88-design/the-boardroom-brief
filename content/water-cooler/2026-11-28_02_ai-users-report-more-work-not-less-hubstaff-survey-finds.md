# AI Users Report More Work, Not Less, Hubstaff Survey Finds

Of workers who use artificial intelligence, 46% say their expected workload has increased since they started. Five percent say it has decreased, according to Hubstaff's State of the Workweek 2026 research, published on 27 October.

![Team working on laptops in an office meeting](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-28-02-ai-workload-fixing-colleagues-work-hero)
*Photo: fauxels via Pexels*

The survey, run through Pollfish, polled 1,068 full-time US employees in computer-based roles, with a margin of error of plus or minus three points. Seventy-nine percent said they use AI tools for work.

Do the sums. Nearly eight in ten people have adopted a technology sold as a time-saver, and nearly half of those say their workload has gone up. For every user whose workload shrank, roughly nine reported the opposite. That is not a productivity revolution. That is a very efficient way of finding more work.

## The cleanup crew

Forty-five percent of respondents spend an hour or more every week fixing colleagues' AI-generated work, the research found. Not their own work. A colleague's.

Somewhere in your office, a spreadsheet was generated in four seconds, and an adult is currently spending forty minutes working out why the totals contain a column that does not exist. They will not be thanked. They will be asked to do it again on Thursday.

Hubstaff's CEO, Jared Brown, put it plainly: AI gives time back to individual employees, but most organisations are not capturing it as capacity. Translation: you saved the hour, and then someone else spent it.

## Where did the hour go

This is the oldest trick in office life. Give a team a faster way to answer email, and the team receives more email. Introduce a quicker report builder, and the reports multiply. The saved time is never banked. It is spent immediately, usually by the person who did not save it.

The same survey found that 55% of workers have told a manager everything was fine while feeling overwhelmed. Add that to the 46% and you have an organisation in which the workload is growing, the tool is working and nobody is mentioning it.

A modest proposal for leaders: before the next AI licence is signed, ask one question. If this tool saves each of your people an hour a week, what, specifically, happens to that hour? If the answer is "more tasks", congratulations. You have bought a faster treadmill.
