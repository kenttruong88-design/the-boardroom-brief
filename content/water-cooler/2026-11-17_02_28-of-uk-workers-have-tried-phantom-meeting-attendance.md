# 28% of UK Workers Have Tried "Phantom" Meeting Attendance

You are in the meeting. Technically. Your name is on the list, your icon is in the grid and your mind is somewhere over a sandwich.

According to research published by Owl Labs, based on a survey of 2,014 full-time UK employees, 28% have tried "phantom meeting attendance." The habit is more common among managers, at 33%, than among non-managers, at 15%. Those with the most meetings are, naturally, the keenest to have fewer of them.

*Photo: Jack Sparrow via Pexels*

It gets better. Some 42% say they would consider sending an AI avatar to meetings on their behalf. Among managers the figure is 50%. Among non-managers it is 21%. Management, it seems, has found a way to attend even less than it already does. Somewhere, an avatar is being briefed on a project it will never understand, which is already the standard for most attendees.

The context is monitoring. The survey found that 59% of employees say their employer uses workplace monitoring software, including 71% of Gen Z but 38% of Gen X, which suggests either that younger workers are watched more or that older workers are better at not noticing. The tracked activities include login and logout times (32%), meetings attended (22%), screen and mouse movement (17%) and keyboard usage (15%).

Workers are not hostile to being measured, exactly. They want to know about it: 82% would like a legal requirement for employers to disclose the use of monitoring. The UK government, according to IT Brief, is considering workplace monitoring approaches as part of its Make Work Pay initiative.

Then there is the technology. Some 73% lose time to technical difficulties in hybrid meetings, 70% struggle to hear everyone and 69% report echo or distortion. Meetings take an average of six minutes to start, so a one-hour meeting is, in practice, a fifty-four-minute meeting with a six-minute prologue about whether anyone can hear.

Frank Weishaupt, chief executive of Owl Labs, said trust and reliable technology are what keep people productive. Which is fair, and also the kind of sentence nobody has ever argued with in a meeting, phantom or otherwise.

The pattern is familiar. The more a workplace measures presence, the more people optimise for presence. The mouse jiggler was not invented by laziness. It was invented by a dashboard.
