# 55% of Workers Told Their Manager "I'm Fine" While Quietly Drowning

"How's your workload?" "Totally manageable!" According to research from workforce software company Hubstaff, published October 27, 55% of surveyed workers have said something close to this to a manager when the honest answer was a muffled scream.

![An overwhelmed employee at a cluttered desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-16-02-55-percent-told-manager-fine-overwhelmed-hero)
*Photo: ANTONI SHKRABA production via Pexels*

The survey, run through Pollfish in October, polled 1,068 full-time US workers aged 18 to 64, mostly in computer-based jobs, with a margin of error of about three percentage points. Hubstaff sells time-tracking software, so a pinch of salt is advisable, but the numbers are hard to wave away. Sixty-nine percent said they had experienced burnout in the past twelve months.

The most devastating line is the follow-up. Among burned-out workers whose manager did know, 64% said nothing changed. So the workplace has created a two-step ritual: employees hide their stress because disclosure feels pointless, and managers stay blissfully unaware because they were told everything was fine. It is a perfectly closed loop, like a smoke alarm that has been politely asked to wait outside.

Then there is the AI angle, which every workplace survey now legally requires. Among AI users, 46% said the amount of work expected of them has increased, while only 26% said they take on more work with the time they save. In other words, the machine gave people their afternoon back, and the organisation immediately sent it a meeting invite.

Hubstaff also reported that 54% of employees say their employer sees half or less of their real workday. This is the quietest line in the release and possibly the saddest. Half of a person's effort is, on average, a rumour.

Hubstaff CEO Jared Brown said, in the release, that AI is returning time to individual workers but most organisations are not turning it into real capacity. Translation: your Tuesday is still full, just with better spelling.

There are limits. The results are unweighted, they come from a vendor with a product to sell, and "fine" is a word with many shades. Some of those 55% were probably just being polite in a lift.

But the pattern is familiar to anyone who has said "no worries!" while worrying heavily. The fix is not a wellness webinar. It is a manager who asks "how are you really?" and then, crucially, does something with the answer.

Until then, "I'm good, thanks" remains the most productive sentence in American business. It takes two seconds and covers everything.
