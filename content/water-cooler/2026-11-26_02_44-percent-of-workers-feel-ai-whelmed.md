# 44% of Workers Are Now "AI-Whelmed" and Their Boss Hasn't Asked

A new survey has given a name to the feeling of being asked to learn a technology that changes every Tuesday. The word is "AI-whelmed," and 44 percent of surveyed US workers say it describes them.

![A tired worker at a laptop on a desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-26-02-ai-whelmed-workers-overwhelmed-hero)
*Photo: Ketut Subiyanto via Pexels*

The research comes from Resume Now, an AI resume builder, which surveyed more than 1,000 employed American workers and released the results on November 24. The 44 percent breaks down as 9 percent extremely overwhelmed, 17 percent somewhat and 18 percent a little. The remaining 56 percent do not feel overwhelmed, and presumably have a calendar.

The more interesting finding is the gap between the pressure and the instruction. Only 17 percent of workers say they are expected to use AI daily. Another 23 percent say it is encouraged but not required. Eighteen percent say AI has come up at work without any real expectation attached. And 42 percent say their employer has no expectation about AI use at all.

So nearly half feel overwhelmed, and more than four in ten say nobody has asked. The deadline appears to be self-imposed, or at least delivered by someone with a newsletter.

The confidence numbers tell a muddier story. Some 58 percent say they are somewhat or very confident integrating AI into their work. Yet 44 percent say they are not clear on where to begin building AI skills. It is possible to be confident and lost. Most managers manage it before lunch.

The money question is sharper. Thirty percent say they are very or somewhat likely to spend their own money on AI skills. That is a course, a subscription or a certificate with a lightning bolt on it, bought to keep up with a requirement that, for four in ten, does not exist.

Keith Spencer, a career expert at Resume Now, tried to calm things down. Feeling AI-whelmed, he said, does not mean workers are against AI, and he advised people who feel behind to focus on one or two areas where it can make their work easier.

That is sensible. Pick one task. Try one tool. Do not buy the course on the first night.

Remember that the pressure reported here comes from surveys, feeds and posts, much of it from people selling something. The tool is real. So is the sales pitch. Learn to tell them apart, and the overwhelm gets smaller.

Your boss may not have asked. Your LinkedIn feed definitely did.
