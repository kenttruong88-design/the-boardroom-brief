# 88% of Job Seekers Ask AI About Employers; 53% Then Passed

Your careers page used to be the first word on what working for you is like. According to a report from Built In, covered by Allwork.Space on November 10, it is now the second.

![A job seeker using a laptop in a cafe](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-23-01-ai-employer-research-job-seekers-hero)
*Photo: Ono Kosuki via Pexels*

Built In's "Invisible Candidate Journey" report found that 88% of job seekers used AI to discover or research employers over the past year. The coverage does not give the sample size or field dates, so treat the headline numbers as a mood, not a census. It is a rather clear mood.

The decisions follow the answers. Some 66% of respondents applied to an employer they had not previously considered after reading what AI told them. And 53% decided against applying based on AI-generated information. The chatbot, in other words, is at once your best recruiter and your harshest reviewer, and it does not take lunch.

There is a modest note of caution in the data. Among AI users, 68% said they always or often verify what the tools tell them about an employer. That leaves about a third who apparently take the machine at its word, which is a great deal of trust to place in an unpaid intern.

The recruiters are in the room too. Among AI users, 24% were prompted to respond to a recruiter because of what they learned. A nearly identical 23% chose not to respond or continue interviewing. So the chatbot's research, once complete, sends a candidate toward the recruiter about as often as it sends one away. A coin has been tossed and it has opinions.

What follows for employers is awkward. The candidate arrives at the first conversation already briefed. They know the reviews, the news and whatever the model has stitched together from them, including the bits the company would rather have left out. The employer's job is no longer to introduce itself but to correct the record, in real time, in front of someone who has already decided what the record says.

A sensible company will do what it should have done anyway: check what the tools say about it, fix what is wrong, and stop calling things "like a family." An unsensible one will keep polishing the careers page, which the chatbot has summarised in four words and is reading charitably.

The interview, it turns out, was never going to be one-way for long. It has simply moved upstream, to a place where the interviewer cannot be seen and the employer is not asked to attend.
