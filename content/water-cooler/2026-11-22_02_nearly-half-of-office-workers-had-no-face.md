# Nearly Half of Office Workers Had No Face-to-Face Meetings on Their Last Office Day

Employers have spent years arguing that the office exists for in-person collaboration. A new survey suggests that, on the average office day, a large share of employees are collaborating with a screen.

![Empty desks in an open-plan office](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-22-02-ny-fed-office-no-face-to-face-meetings-hero)
*Photo: Mikhail Nilov via Pexels*

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York surveyed college-educated workers in September, according to a report on Allwork.Space that cites CNBC's coverage. It found that 48.8% of office-based workers had no face-to-face meetings with teammates or colleagues on their most recent day in the office. Not one. They commuted, badged in and found a desk.

Why? More than 70% of respondents had at least one teammate working somewhere else that day. The meeting that needed everybody therefore needed a link. And 37% attended at least one virtual meeting in which everyone was in the same office.

Read that again. Everyone in the same office. A video call. Possibly with a person on the other side of a partition, who could, with a minor adjustment to their posture, have heard the question directly.

Data from a Fortune 500 company, cited in the same report, pointed in the same direction: nearly half of corporate employees had at least one teammate absent on in-office days. This is the central flaw in the plan. An attendance mandate requires everyone to arrive. A meeting requires them to arrive on the same day. Nobody has a mandate for the second thing.

The honest reading is not that the office is pointless. It is that the office is a venue, and venues are only as good as the programming. A calendar full of video calls will fill the building with people wearing headphones, and the headphones, in turn, will be a useful signal to the colleague who was about to say hello.

There is an obvious fix, which is to schedule the in-person work for the in-person days. It requires a manager to know who is in the building on Wednesday. Most firms discover that this is harder than adding a tab to a calendar. It is also, in fairness, harder than a mandate memo.

Until then, the contemporary workday has a new shape. Commute. Sit. Unmute. Wave at a colleague through a pane of glass, and a second time on screen for the record.
