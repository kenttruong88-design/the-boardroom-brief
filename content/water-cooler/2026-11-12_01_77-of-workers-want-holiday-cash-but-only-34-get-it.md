# 77% of Workers Want Holiday Cash, but Only 34% Get It

Ask American workers what they would like from their employer for the holidays and the answer is not subtle. A cash bonus was the most wanted gift among 77% of respondents in a new Zety survey of 1,040 US workers. The share who usually receive one is 34%.

![Wrapped gifts on an office desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-12-01-holiday-gift-exchange-cash-office-hero)
*Photo: Pavel Danilyuk via Pexels*

Zety, the resume-builder, ran the poll through Pollfish on October 19, according to CPA Practice Advisor. The gap between the wish and the delivery runs through the whole list. Extra paid time off was wanted by 24% and received by 8%. A team meal, by contrast, is wanted by 9% and received by 24%. Company-branded items were wanted by 5% and delivered to 15%. The festive season, in other words, is a distribution problem in which supply is a lunch and demand is a bank transfer.

Nor is the exchange confined to the top of the org chart. Some 35% of workers said they had bought or contributed to a gift for their manager out of obligation. For a coworker the figure was 45%, and for the CEO, 8%. Work is the one place where a gift flows upward and is classed as appreciation.

The effect is not trivial. A total of 43% said workplace gift expectations cause financial stress, while 53% said they do not expect a gift from their employer at all. Some 26% said that $50 to $99 is the minimum for a gift to feel meaningful, and 8% said it takes $500 or more.

Zety's career expert, Jasmine Escalera, put the dynamic gently: "The pressure to join a workplace gift exchange often comes from wanting to fit in." Nobody has ever organized a whip-round for a manager's present out of pure joy, but the survey suggests a lot of people have contributed to one in a spirit of good citizenship.

The respondents were 26% Gen Z, 28% Millennials, 28% Gen X and 18% Boomers. Four generations, one demand.

The takeaway for HR is not complicated. Nobody is asking for a ritual. They are asking for the one item on the list that nobody, in the history of the office party, has ever regifted. Employers keep opting for the team meal, because a team meal is a budget line that photographs well and cannot be spent on rent.

So the season will arrive as it does each year. A bowl of fruit will appear in the break room. A card will circulate for signatures. And somewhere, a hand will go into a pocket for a manager's scented candle.

Cash is 77%. The gift card is not on the list. It will, nonetheless, be in the post.
