# HR Emails 'Anonymous' Survey Answers to Entire 84-Person Company

An employee on Reddit's r/office says the full export of their company's anonymous engagement survey, every open-text comment included, was sent to the entire mailing list: 84 people. The post, shared by user u/cuteyasmine on 8 June and collecting more than 3,000 upvotes, was written up by Upworthy's Scoop. The poster put it down to HR hitting the wrong button or misconfiguring the survey platform.

Then came the silence. Slack, per the write-up, went quiet for three hours. Anyone who has worked in an office will recognise the sound: 84 people reading the same document in separate rooms.

When HR did respond, it sent an apology and asked staff to delete the email. The poster's assessment of the recall effort is the best line in the story: a recall notice, they wrote, mostly reminds people to save the attachment before it stops working. It is hard to improve on that, and this column will not try.

Commenters supplied the rest of the evidence. One said they had always been told they had an easily recognised writing style. Another said management at a large hospital system had simply removed the option to write comments at all, which is one way to solve a data-security problem.

The underlying issue is old. The write-up cites a 2010 Harvard Business Review piece by James Detert, Ethan Burris and David Harrison, which studied 439 employees and found 42% held back information because they feared losing something or saw nothing to gain. Nearly 20% feared consequences. Honest feedback was already a rare commodity. Now there is a case study in what happens to the supply.

A fair-minded footnote: the date on the write-up is unclear, as the page says it appeared "1 month ago" while carrying a September stamp, and this column has only the poster's account. The company is not named. It may have handled the aftermath better than the story suggests.

Still, the lesson travels. If your survey is anonymous, make sure it is. If it is not, say so. And if you ever find yourself sending a recall notice, reflect on the reminder it provides: everyone has already saved the attachment.
