# A Quarter of Workers Now Do Their Own Office Repairs

One in four office workers has made a do-it-yourself fix for ergonomics, temperature or visual privacy, according to Gensler's 2026 Global Workplace Survey. Two thirds hack their workspace in some way. The survey covered more than 16,400 office workers in 16 countries. The dataset behind the survey spans nearly 125,000 respondents over two decades. In other words, a meaningful share of the surveyed workforce has been quietly doing facilities management without the title.

![An office chair at a desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-18-02-workers-diy-fix-office-chair-hero)
*Photo: aboodi vesakaran via Pexels*

Think about what the "fixes" are. A stack of printer paper under a monitor. A jacket draped across the back of a chair that the company paid for. A bag propped between a screen and the walkway to prevent the colleague behind from reading your email. Somewhere a facilities team has a ticket system that explains why none of this was raised.

The survey also found that employees spend 55% of their workweek in the office, 18% at home and 26% elsewhere. They spend 39% of their time working alone and 27% collaborating in person. The office, then, is a place where a person goes to work alone, near other people, in a chair they have adjusted themselves.

Then comes the twist. Gensler found that 30% of employees are what it calls "AI Power Users", and that these workers are the more connected ones. They spend 37% of the week working alone against 42% for late adopters, and 11% socialising against 9%. They also spend 12% of the week learning, against 8% for late adopters. Janet Pogue McLaurin, Gensler's global director of workplace research, said: "We often assume that more technology means less connection."

The answer, apparently, is that it does not. The people who talk to a machine all day talk to colleagues more than the people who do not. Possibly this is because they have finished their work faster. Possibly it is because they need someone to tell about the chatbot.

Either way the headline stands. Picture the AI power user at their desk: chatbot open, laptop on a stack of paper, chair adjusted by hand. The future of work was supposed to arrive with a design brief. It came with a roll of tape.
