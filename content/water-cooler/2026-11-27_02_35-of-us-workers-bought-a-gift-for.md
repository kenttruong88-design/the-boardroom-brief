# 35% of US Workers Bought a Gift for Their Boss Out of Obligation, Zety Finds

More than one in three American workers has bought, or chipped in on, a gift for their manager because they felt they had to, according to a survey by resume company Zety published by PR Newswire on November 9. The figure is 35%. The boss, one assumes, will receive a candle.

![Wrapped gifts piled on an office desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-27-02-office-gift-exchange-obligation-hero)
*Photo: kaboompics via Pexels*

The poll covered 1,040 US workers and was fielded on October 19 through Pollfish. It also found that 43% say workplace gift expectations cause them financial stress. That is a lot of anxiety for an economy in which one of the participants is paid to be there.

Obligation runs in every direction. Some 45% felt obligated to buy for a coworker, 11% for a direct report and 8% for the CEO. Eight per cent are buying the chief executive a present, so somewhere a person with a mortgage is gifting a scented diffuser to a person with a very large one.

Meanwhile, what workers receive and what they want have parted ways. According to the survey, 77% would prefer a cash bonus, yet only 34% typically get one. Company-branded items made up 15% of gifts received but only 5% of those wanted. Extra paid time off was wanted by 24% and received by 8%. The holiday party, received by 25%, was wanted by just 12%. The office has, in effect, perfected the art of giving employees what the employer finds easiest to buy.

Expectations are sensibly low: 53% don't expect an employer gift this year. A gift would still make 53% feel more valued, and 18% more loyal. Loyalty, at the price of a mug, is the best deal in management.

Zety's career expert Jasmine Escalera said: "The pressure to join a workplace gift exchange often comes from wanting to fit in." Which is true, and also the sentence that explains every group chat that has ever asked for a Secret Santa budget.

A fair note: this is a survey released by a resume company, so read it with the usual allowance. The pattern, though, is hard to argue with. Employees are paying to be generous upward, while the people with budgets are handing out stationery with their own logo on it.

The cheapest fix is the one nobody has tried. Skip the gift exchange, remove the obligation, and give people a day off, which 24% wanted and only 8% got. It even arrives gift-wrapped, in an email that says "enjoy".
