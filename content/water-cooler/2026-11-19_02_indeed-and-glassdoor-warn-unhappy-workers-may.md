# Indeed and Glassdoor Warn Unhappy Workers May Finally Quit in 2027

Good news for anyone who has spent 2026 clinging to a job they quietly resent: the economists have noticed.

![Tired office worker looking out of a window](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-19-02-unhappy-workers-may-quit-in-2027-hero)
*Photo: Ketut Subiyanto via Pexels*

In their 2027 US Hiring and Worklife Trends Report, published Wednesday, Indeed Hiring Lab and Glassdoor Economic Research say the unhappy employee who stayed put in 2026 may be the first to leave in 2027. Their summary of the year ahead is that something is about to give.

Managers have spent three years in what the report calls a low-hire, low-fire market. Nobody moved, so everyone assumed everybody was happy. That is the corporate equivalent of believing a guest is having a lovely time because they have not left the party. They simply could not find their coat.

The evidence for the mood is not subtle. Advertised wages are growing at about 2.5% a year, while prices are rising 3.7%. Employer health insurance costs are up 25% over four years. Worker ratings of pay and benefits on Glassdoor are the lowest since 2017.

Then there is AI, the technology that was supposed to make everyone's day easier. According to the report, only 45% of AI mentions in Glassdoor reviews are positive, down from 81% in 2019. The authors call its impact "broad but shallow", which is also a fair description of most all-hands presentations about it.

Younger workers are not having a better time. Postings for senior roles are up 19% since January 2025, while entry-level postings are down, and more than half of 2025 graduates were underemployed a year after graduating. Experience is in demand. Getting some is the problem.

There is one small ray of light for employers. Job postings grew year on year in September for the first time since 2022, so the market may be thawing. For workers, that is less a ray of light than an open door.

The report was signed by Indeed's chief economist, Svenja Gudell, and Glassdoor's chief economist, Daniel Zhao, who stop short of predicting a mass resignation. They do note, politely, that a workforce which stays out of caution can leave out of confidence.

So, for any manager reading: check in on the quiet ones. Not the loud ones. The quiet ones who have started updating their profile photo.
