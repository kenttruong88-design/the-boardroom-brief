# Almost Half of Workers Now Pick Office Days to Be Seen

Forty-eight percent of employees have chosen specific days in the office so that senior leaders or clients will see them there, or plan to, according to Owl Labs' 10th annual State of Hybrid Work report, as covered by Fortune in September. The report calls the practice "billboard days". Fortune's write-up gives no sample size, which is the only thing in this story that is not being advertised.

![An empty open-plan office](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-18-01-billboard-days-office-seen-hero)
*Photo: Mikhail Nilov via Pexels*

Here is the detail that makes it better. Of the people doing billboard days, 40% are managers. The people who are supposed to be checking whether staff are present are also arranging to be present, for the benefit of someone further up. It is attendance all the way up, like a pyramid of people waving at each other.

Meanwhile, 76% of respondents say the real driver of return-to-office policy is leadership oversight. That beats the 74% who cite productivity and collaboration, and the 71% who cite client service and culture. Oversight wins by two points. In the usual corporate telling, the office exists to enable collaboration. In the telling of the people who work in it, the office exists so that someone can see it enabling.

The same report found that 74% of employees do some work on a typical Sunday, and 21% call it a full or near-full workday. Owl Labs CEO Frank Weishaupt told Fortune: "We have completely shifted away from a conversation about the four-day workweek." The four-day week did not die. It simply found out that the other days were available.

Add it up and the modern schedule is: Sunday for the real work, Tuesday for the audience, and the remaining days for the video calls about both. The report also found that 53% have adopted or plan to adopt "career minimalism", a phrase that, in this context, sounds like the opposite of a billboard.

If you were wondering why the office on Tuesday is so full and so quiet, that is why. It is full of people who came to be seen. It is quiet because everybody is working out who is looking.

The best advice available is also the oldest. If your manager is picking a billboard day, there is no need to join the campaign. Just do a good job on the Sunday, and let the Tuesday look after itself.
