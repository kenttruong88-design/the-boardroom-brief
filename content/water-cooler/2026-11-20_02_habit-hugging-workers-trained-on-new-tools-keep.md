# Habit Hugging: Workers Trained on New Tools Keep Using Old Ones

There is now a name for attending the training, nodding at the demo, and going straight back to the spreadsheet you have used since 2019. It is "habit hugging," and a TalentLMS survey reported by HR Dive on October 30 suggests most of us are doing it.

![Office worker with a notebook at a desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-20-02-habit-hugging-old-tools-new-training-hero)

*Photo: Ivan S via Pexels*

The headline finding: fewer than half of U.S. employees consistently use new ways of working after they have been trained on them. The article gives no exact figure, which feels about right for a finding about vagueness.

The more telling number is 73%. That share of employees mixed old and new ways of working, according to the survey of 1,500 employees. The article's wording is ambiguous about whether this applies to everyone or only to those expected to use AI, so take it as a rough guide. More than a third of the AI-using respondents said they sometimes return to their old methods, the professional equivalent of a gym membership.

Why? Partly because the old way works. Some 60% said their organizations care more about getting results than about which method delivers them. That is a rational reading of the situation. If nobody checks how the report was built, the report built the old way is, in effect, the approved version.

Partly it is about the boss. Among employees whose managers also adopted the new methods, 49% did so consistently. Among those whose managers did not, just 22% did. The training session, it turns out, matters less than whether the person who approves your time off is still using the old template.

TalentLMS's parent company chief, Dimitris Tsingos, said in a press release that businesses should make new ways of working an integral part of everyday work. This is excellent advice, and also the thing the training was supposed to do.

A caveat: the survey comes from a training vendor, with no independent second outlet found, and the article does not give the field dates.

The practical takeaway is gentle. Stop scheduling a second workshop. Ask the senior people to use the new tool in front of everyone, badly if necessary. Habits are hugged because they are warm. Nobody has ever hugged a learning module.
