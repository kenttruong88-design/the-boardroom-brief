# Holiday Party Budgets Rise 13% and 84% of Staff Plan to Attend

Good news for anyone who enjoys a buffet: holiday party budgets are projected to climb 13% on average this season, according to a survey released Thursday by ezCater, a company that sells catering to offices. Yes, the source sells catering. Season accordingly.

![Food laid out for an office holiday party](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-13-02-holiday-party-budgets-hero)
*Photo: RDNE Stock project via Pexels*

The numbers, for what they're worth. Ninety-six percent of planners expect to match or exceed last year's spending. Eighty-four percent of employees say they plan to attend, up from 82% in 2025. The survey covered 1,000 full-time employees and 605 workplace decision-makers who help plan the parties, fielded in July.

Why do planners do it? Boosting morale, said 80%. Company culture, said 65%. Team bonding, said 64%. Why do employees show up? Seventy-one percent said free food and drinks motivate them to attend. Eighty-one percent also said holiday parties help them bond, so everybody gets to feel good about the sandwiches.

Here is the quiet insight: morale is the stated purpose and food is the working mechanism. Nobody has ever been moved to loyalty by an agenda. Plenty have been moved by a slider tray.

Gen Z makes up 24% of planners, up 69% from last year. This means, with all due respect, that the person choosing the playlist is now younger than the chairs.

Robert Kaskel, ezCater's vice president of people, said: "companies understand they can't leave connection to chance." That's a charming way of saying that companies have scheduled the fun, catered it and put it in the calendar. It is still a better offer than a mandatory quarterly all-hands.

There is also a gentle management lesson in the 84%. Attendance is up even though nobody has been ordered to enjoy themselves, which suggests that people will gladly show up for something they actually want. Compare this with the average mandatory-fun event, where attendance is a performance review in a party hat.

A few humble tips, then. Pick a date people can actually attend. Feed them properly. End before the speeches. And remember the unwritten rule: the party's job is to be pleasant, not to fix the year.

A budget up 13% is not a cure for engagement. But it will, at least, guarantee that the croissants arrive.
