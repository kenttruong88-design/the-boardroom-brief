# 55% of Workers Have Seen Their Job Posted for More Money

You are scrolling a job board at 11 p.m. and there it is. Your job. Your title. Your responsibilities, lightly reworded. A salary range that would have changed your year.

You are not alone. A Paychex survey of 1,034 full-time US employees, conducted online by Centiment from August 21 to 31 with a margin of error of about 3%, found that 55% have seen their own role advertised at a higher pay rate. For 21%, the listing was at their own employer. For 37%, it was at another company.

*Photo: Pixabay via Pexels*

So what did they do? Mostly nothing. According to the survey, 64% of those who saw a higher posting did not ask for a raise. Thirty-one percent started job hunting instead, and 26% did nothing at all, which is the corporate version of seeing a spider and moving to another room.

The ones who did ask were more likely to be the ones whose own employer was doing the advertising. Among workers who saw their role posted for more at their own company, 46% asked for a raise. Among those who only saw it at another company, the figure was 30%. Evidently it is easier to confront your manager about a document your manager posted than about a rival's.

The cost of silence is real, at least for employers. Workers who saw a higher posting were about twice as likely to have quit over a pay problem, 24% versus 13%. And 61% of them have seriously considered leaving over pay, compared with 30% of those who had not seen one.

Younger workers are the most alert. Among 18- to 29-year-olds, 64% have seen a higher-paying listing for their role and 42% of them started job hunting. For workers aged 46 to 61, the figures were 46% and 21%. The generation that grew up comparing prices online has applied the habit to itself.

Meanwhile, only 21% of employees say their employer shares pay range data. Seventeen states and Washington, D.C. have pay transparency laws, according to the article, which means salary information keeps surfacing in the one place employers did not intend: the job ad.

One caveat: the article is Paychex content distributed by Stacker, and the quotes in it are paraphrased. The numbers, however, will feel familiar.

The takeaway is not complicated. If a company is willing to tell strangers what your job is worth, it can probably tell you.
