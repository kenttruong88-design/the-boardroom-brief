# Half of Office Days Are Spent on Video Calls, NY Fed Finds

You got up early. You commuted. You tapped your badge, found a desk, opened your laptop and joined a video call. The Federal Reserve Bank of New York would like a word.

![A worker on a video call at an office desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-13-01-half-office-days-video-calls-hero)
*Photo: Jack Sparrow via Pexels*

A study by the bank's economists Natalia Emanuel and Emma Harrington, reported by HR Dive on November 12, surveyed 1,835 full-time US workers in September. The headline finding: about half of in-office days are spent entirely on video calls or working alone. Which is, to be clear, what the same people were doing at home, minus the better coffee.

The reason is not mysterious. On in-office days, seven in ten workers had at least one teammate who was not in the office. On average, teams were missing about a third of their members. When one person dials in, nearly half of respondents said, the whole meeting goes virtual. And for 37% of workers, in-office collaboration turns remote because the meeting is held online even when everyone is in the building.

Read that last one again. Everyone is in the building. The meeting is online. Somewhere, a facilities manager is weeping into a floor plan.

The researchers were polite about it. The office, they wrote, "has become less effective in delivering on its promise of co-location with colleagues." They added that seeing colleagues is a prominent draw of the office, but that "since the rise of remote work, the office increasingly fails to deliver this benefit." That's economist for "you've paid to move your laptop across town."

The fix proposed by management is usually more attendance. The fix suggested by the data is synchronised attendance, which is a harder sentence to put in a memo. If your team is in on Monday and Thursday and the other half is in on Tuesday and Wednesday, the building is full and the room is empty.

Nothing here argues that offices are pointless. People still go to see colleagues, and the survey says so. It just argues that being in the same postcode is not the same as being in the same room.

So next time someone announces a mandatory return, ask one question: which day, and which people? Otherwise, enjoy your commute and your video call.
