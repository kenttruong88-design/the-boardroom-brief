# 96% Look Forward to the Holiday Party; Budgets Rise 13%

Ninety-six percent of workers say they look forward to their company's holiday party. Eighty-four percent plan to attend, up from 82 percent last year. The 12 percent who are keen but not attending are presumably the ones with a reason. That is according to the ezCater 2026 Workplace Holiday Party Report, which surveyed 1,000 full-time employees and 605 workplace decision-makers, and was reported on November 19 by The Daily Upside. A caveat for the sceptical: ezCater is a catering company, and a catering company has views on parties.

![Colleagues celebrating at an office holiday party](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-25-01-holiday-party-survey-hero)
*Photo: RDNE Stock project via Pexels*

Twenty-seven percent get FOMO when they cannot attend. Seventy-four percent would take a smaller party over no party. Eighty-one percent say the party helps them bond and make friends at work. Fifty-six percent of remote workers say it plays a major role in connecting with colleagues, which means the one day a year they put on trousers is also the day that matters.

Budgets are projected to rise 13 percent. "Holiday party budgets are rising 13% this year, backed by clear organizational priorities," said Cindy Klein Roche, chief growth officer at ezCater. The clear organizational priority, as far as one can tell, is cake.

Seven in ten workers want alcohol available. Among those who do not plan to drink, 36 percent want mocktails or premium non-alcoholic drinks. The report cites Gallup in noting that 54 percent of American adults drank in 2026, matching the 2025 record low and down from 62 percent in 2023, which is the sort of fact that gives a facilities manager sleepless nights.

The upshot is that the drinks menu is no longer a drinks menu. "It's a risk, culture and cost decision that lands on their desk," Nick Araco Jr., founder and CEO of CFO Alliance, told The Daily Upside, referring to finance chiefs. The mocktail has a line item. Somewhere, a controller is approving a ginger-beer variance.

A year of reorganisations, return-to-office memos and mandatory fun later, workers still want one evening in December where the manager is also holding a paper plate. The party does not require an agenda, a deck or a breakout room. It requires snacks and a place to stand.

That is a low bar. Most companies will clear it, and a few will add a team-building exercise anyway.
