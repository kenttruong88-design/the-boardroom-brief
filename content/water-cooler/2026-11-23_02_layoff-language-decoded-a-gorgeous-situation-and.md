# Layoff Language Decoded: "A Gorgeous Situation" and Other Corporate Poetry

Companies cutting jobs rarely say "we are cutting jobs." They have developed a dialect. Here is a field guide, using sentences companies actually said.

![A tired office worker at a laptop](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-23-02-layoff-language-glossary-hero)
*Photo: kaboompics via Pexels*

**"A gorgeous situation."** ServiceNow's chief executive, Bill McDermott, used this phrase on the company's third-quarter call to describe a smaller workforce, according to People Matters. Translation: fewer people, same software.

**"Making necessary changes to how we are structured."** ServiceNow's statement on its latest round of cuts, per People Matters. Translation: some of you are no longer on the chart. The word "necessary" does heavy lifting, because it implies that someone had no choice, and that someone has not been named.

**"Continually assess our workforce."** An Intel spokesperson told Business Insider, as relayed by Stocktwits, that the company does this so that priorities and resources are "appropriately aligned." Translation: assessment is continuous, and so is the possibility of a result. Intel had 81,100 employees at the end of September, down from 85,100 at the end of 2025, per filings Stocktwits cites. The statement contained no number. The filings did.

**"Fewer roles across Nike."** Chief executive Elliott Hill wrote this in an October announcement reported by Fast Company, adding that news like this "creates uncertainty." Translation: yes, and you are now in the uncertainty. He also said he did not yet know the number of roles. Honesty about not knowing is rare, and it is not much of a comfort at 9 a.m.

**"Hard decisions."** Cisco's Chuck Robbins used it in May, when the company announced cuts of fewer than 4,000 jobs alongside record revenue, per Fox Business. Translation: the decision was hard for the person who made it. The effect on the other people was unrelated to the effort.

The pattern is easy to spot. Every phrase is grammatically about the company. None has a subject who is a person, and none has an object who is also a person. Things are aligned, structured and assessed. Roles are fewer. Employees do not appear, in the sentence or often afterwards, in the building.

A suggestion for the next announcement: try "we are cutting 400 jobs, and we are sorry." It is fewer words, it is true, and it has never once been described as gorgeous.
