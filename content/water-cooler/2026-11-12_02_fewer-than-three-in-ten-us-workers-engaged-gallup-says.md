# Fewer Than Three in Ten US Workers Are Engaged, Gallup Says

Only 29% of American employees are engaged at work, according to Gallup's third-quarter 2026 data, down from 31% in the previous quarter and the lowest since 2011. The Journal of Accountancy reported the finding on November 6.

![A bored employee at an office desk](https://res.cloudinary.com/dnpcbx89m/image/upload/c_fill,w_1200,h_630/boardroom-brief/water-cooler/2026-11-12-02-employee-engagement-gallup-manager-hero)
*Photo: cottonbro studio via Pexels*

The rest of the workforce is distributed as follows. About 53% are not engaged, which means they do the job and, in the technical term, nothing more. A further 18% are actively disengaged. That figure is also up, the article said. Put differently, nearly three in four American workers are on the payroll but not on the project.

The cost is large, even by the standards of figures that begin with a dollar sign. Gallup's estimate of the annual productivity lost to non-engaged and disengaged workers is about $2 trillion, according to the report. That is a very large amount of lost enthusiasm.

The cause, Gallup says, is not the snacks. The factor most strongly associated with engagement is "manager support." Managers account for at least 70% of the variance in team engagement, the article said. Manager engagement itself fell 9 points between 2022 and 2025, including 5 points from 2024 to 2025.

Read that twice. The people employers rely on to engage everyone else are themselves less engaged. Somewhere, a middle manager is being asked to inspire a team while managing a larger one and doing the work of a person who left in March and was not replaced.

There is a ray of light, and it comes with a slide deck. Organizations that combine frequent AI use, a clear AI strategy and active manager support reach engagement of 53%, the report found. Two things to notice. First, it is the manager support again, wearing a hat. Second, 53% is the same number as the share of workers who are currently not engaged, which is either a coincidence or the most efficient reorg in history.

The conclusion is not subtle. Companies that have spent a decade on pulse surveys, wellness weeks and pizza Fridays are being told that the variable that matters is a human being who returns messages. It costs nothing, which is perhaps why it has never been tried at scale.

Gallup did not report whether the findings will be discussed at an all-hands.
